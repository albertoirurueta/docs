Elements, Shapes, Lines and Graphics

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers the building blocks that Canva calls elements: graphics and icons, shapes, lines and connectors, stickers, 3D objects, signatures and the other library items that you drop onto a page. Elements are part of the editor of Canva (web, desktop app and mobile apps) and the Elements tab is available on every plan: Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits. What differs by plan is the content: part of the library is paid content that needs Pro or a higher plan, and the AI ways of making an element (AI shapes, Magic Media, Canva AI) draw on an AI allowance whose Standard, Premium and Ultra tiers are explained on Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance. Prices are not repeated here; they are published at https://www.canva.com/pricing/. The Help Center describes AI shapes as a gradual rollout, so treat them as a feature that may not yet appear in your account. The conversational Canva AI 2.0 launched as a research preview on 2026-04-16 and is now documented in the Help Center without a preview label; no general-availability date is published and its availability varies by account and region.

The running example is Bookshelf, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the Figma sub-section. Its launch campaign uses elements everywhere: a flowchart of the reader journey for the launch deck, and a "Book of the week" Instagram post decorated with graphics, a recoloured sticker and a badge. Where each tool sits in the editor is described in the Editor Tour; this page goes deep on the Elements tab and its neighbours.

What counts as an element

The Help Center defines elements as pre-made design assets that you use to build or enhance a design. They include graphics and icons, photos and videos, shapes, lines and frames, and charts, stickers and audio clips. Elements are different from uploads: elements come from Canva’s content library, while uploads are files or media that you add yourself (see Uploads, Stock Media and Imports). Photos and their editing tools have their own page, Photos and Image Editing, and frames and grids that hold photos are on Frames, Grids, Guides and Print Layout.

A short vocabulary helps when searching the library.

Kind What it is Typical use in a design

Graphic or icon

A vector or raster illustration, often tagged by artist

Decoration, spot illustrations, iconography for lists

Shape

A geometric figure you can fill, outline, round and type inside

Backgrounds, badges, call-outs, flowchart boxes

Line or connector

A stroke with adjustable endings, weight and style

Dividers, pointers, diagrams that stay connected

Sticker

A playful pre-made illustration meant to be dropped on top of a design

Social posts and stories, emphasis

3D element

A rotatable 3D object from the library or generated with AI

Depth in presentations, videos, whiteboards, social posts and websites

Signature

A drawn, typed or uploaded personal signature

Letters, resumes, print designs

Where elements live in the editor

Open the Elements tab in the editor side panel. Its search bar searches Canva’s content library, and under Browse categories you choose a category and select See all to view everything in it. You can also add content from other side panel tabs, including Text, Uploads, Photos, Videos and Apps, and the Tools tab holds Shapes, Lines, Sticky Notes, Draw, Text and Tables (see Drawing and the Tools Tab). Which categories appear depends on the design type and on brand controls set by an administrator, so the figure below shows typical categories rather than a fixed list.

Source: Canva Help Center: Add, duplicate, and delete elements

On a phone, tap Elements in the editor toolbar and then Shapes (or another category) under Browse categories; the same search works.

Handling an element once it is on the page

Adding an element is a click or tap on its thumbnail. After that you reposition it by dragging it and resize it with the resize handles. To duplicate, select the element (hold Shift to add more to the selection), right-click and choose Duplicate, or press Command + D on macOS and Ctrl + D on Windows. To delete, use the Delete icon on the floating toolbar, or press Delete on macOS and Backspace on Windows. Undo an accidental deletion with Command + Z on macOS and Ctrl + Z on Windows.

Hovering over an element in the library shows a More icon with View more by artist; using several elements from the same artist keeps a consistent visual style. The same menu lets you star an element: in the editor, open Elements, select Photos or Graphics, hover, choose More and then Star. Starred photos and elements are collected in the Starred content section of the Templates tab on the homepage, whereas fonts can only be starred from the editor.

Source: Canva Help Center: Star your favorite photos, elements and fonts

Shapes

Shapes organise information, guide attention and connect ideas. The Elements tab has basic shapes, polygons, arrows, flowchart shapes, speech bubbles, banners and more, and the Help Center says shapes are available to every plan. To add one, open Elements, choose Browse categories, then Shapes, browse or search, and select the shape.

Know the limits before you plan a layout. Shapes cannot be flipped, images cannot be dropped into a shape (use a frame instead), and an uploaded SVG used as a shape can only be resized or recoloured, not given new corners or strokes. You can also draw a custom shape in design software such as Affinity and upload it as an SVG.

Source: Canva Help Center: Add and edit shapes

AI shapes

AI shapes generate a custom vector shape from a plain-text description, for example a minimalist mountain range with three peaks. Open Elements, go to Shapes, type the description, select Generate shapes and pick a result, or generate again. Specific wording with a style word such as simple, geometric or cartoon-style gives better results. You can change colours, borders and styling afterwards, but not the shape’s path or nodes. The Help Center states that AI shapes are being gradually rolled out; if you cannot see the field, use the shape library and combine shapes instead.

Source: Canva Help Center: Generate custom shapes with AI

Lines and connectors

Lines connect ideas, guide attention and add structure to layouts. Press L to insert a line, or choose Elements, Browse categories, Shapes, Lines, See all, or open Tools and select Lines. After adding a line, drag its endpoints to resize or rotate it.

Property What it does

Weight

Thickness, to emphasise or play down the line

Style

Solid, dashed or dotted

Line endings

Arrows, circles, bars or no end point, set separately for the start and the end; Swap line ends exchanges them

Line type

Straight, curved or elbow; curved suits loose links, elbows suit structured diagrams

Connector

A line that stays attached to the shapes at both ends

Transparency

Lower values make a line less prominent, which suits background guides

Colour

Any colour, or a Brand Kit colour for consistency

To label a line, double-click it and type; on a phone tap the line twice. Drag the pill handles on the label text box to change its width.

Connectors and flowcharts

To connect a line to a shape, drag the line’s endpoint to the shape’s border; when it snaps, the line is connected and follows the shape when you move it. The Elements tab has a Flowchart shapes section under Shapes (terminator, process, decision, sub-process and data shapes). Switching a shape type, for example from a rectangle to a diamond, keeps the connected lines attached. Duplicate shapes to keep styling consistent, align and distribute them, avoid diagonal lines, place decision shapes along the main path with a label on every branch, and group finished sections to protect their structure.

Quick Flow builds the diagram for you: add a shape, right-click it and choose Enable Quick Flow (on mobile, tap the shape, then More). Arrows appear around the shape; choosing a directional arrow adds the next shape, connects it, matches your current styles and keeps everything aligned. Quick Flow is available in every design type except Canva Docs.

Source: Canva Help Center: Create flowcharts fast with Quick Flow in Canva

Graphics, stickers and 3D elements

Graphics and stickers are found by searching the Elements tab or browsing its categories. Search with concrete nouns and style words, and use View more by artist to keep a set consistent. Library content follows the Content License Agreement (https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/), which, among other limits, does not let you trademark library content other than fonts, simple shapes and lines; for a logo, use your own artwork or the brand kit described on Brand Kit and Brand Voice.

3D elements bring depth to a design. The library holds over 10,000 curated 3D elements, and you can generate custom ones with Magic Media (its 3D tab) or from the Generate dropdown of the Elements search. Drag a 3D element on the canvas to rotate it in 3D space; some library elements let you change up to five colours from the colour tile. 3D elements work in presentations, videos, whiteboards, social media designs and websites, and are not supported in Docs, Sheets and Email. The limits are five 3D elements per page and 50 per design, and in present mode an element returns to its saved starting angle when you change slides, so set the starting angle while editing. Exports contain 3D elements as static images, and uploading your own 3D files is not supported. A published website must be republished to show new 3D elements.

Source: Canva Help Center: Add 3D elements to your design

Making a custom element with AI

For visual content that the library does not hold, the Help Center points to Canva AI (from the homepage or the editor side panel, choose a content type such as Image or Video clip) and to Magic Media (Apps, then search for Magic Media; the Images, Graphics, Videos and 3D tabs each take a description). Generated results vary between attempts, so refine the prompt and regenerate. Ownership and safety of AI output are covered on the Canva AI assistant page.

Source: Canva Help Center: Add, duplicate, and delete elements

Signatures

A signature is an element that you draw, type or upload and then save for reuse. In the editor, open the Tools tab and select Signature, choose Draw, Type or Upload (or a saved signature) and select Add signature; the Elements tab also offers it when you search for Signature and select Create. In Canva Docs and email designs the signature lives in the Quick Actions menu instead: use the plus button or type / on the design and search for Signature. Once placed, it moves, resizes and edits like any other element. Whether an electronic signature is legally binding depends on the jurisdiction and document type, so check the requirements before relying on it. Asking other people to sign is a separate feature for Business and Enterprise.

Source: Canva Help Center: Add your signature to a design

Element colour

Select an element and choose its colour tile in the toolbar; the Color panel opens at the side. Pick a colour, use Add a new color (the rainbow tile) for others, or use the eyedropper to pick a colour from your design. A Current page or All pages choice replaces a colour across the design. Some solid fills show a Transparency control that affects only the fill, while Transparency on the editor toolbar affects the whole element. If no colour tile appears, the element’s colours cannot be changed. Palettes, gradients and brand colours are covered on Color, Gradients and Palettes.

Source: Canva Help Center: Change element colors

Procedure: sketch the Bookshelf reader journey as a flowchart

The launch deck needs one slide that explains how a reader uses Bookshelf: pick a book, read it, decide whether it is finished, review it and share it.

  1. Open the launch presentation and select the slide.

  2. Open Elements, choose Shapes under Browse categories, scroll to Flowchart shapes and select See all. Drag a terminator shape for the start, process shapes for the steps and a diamond for the decision onto the slide.

  3. Press L to add a line. Drag one endpoint to the border of the first shape until it snaps, then drag the other endpoint to the next shape. Repeat for each step.

  4. Double-click the connector leaving the diamond and type Yes; label the other branch No.

  5. Select the shapes, then align and distribute them, and duplicate a finished box with Command + D (Ctrl + D on Windows) to keep styling consistent.

  6. Recolour the final box with the colour tile, using a Bookshelf brand colour, and group the finished flow.

Procedure: decorate the Book of the week post

The Instagram post for the weekly pick needs a book illustration, the brand colours and a small badge.

  1. Open the "Book of the week" post and open the Elements tab.

  2. Search for books and review the Graphics, Stickers and 3D results. Hover over a result you like and choose More, then Star to find it again later.

  3. Select the graphic to add it, drag it into place and use the corner handles to resize it proportionally.

  4. With the graphic selected, choose its colour tile and replace the colours with the Bookshelf indigo and amber from the brand kit.

  5. Add a rounded rectangle from Elements, Shapes, double-click it and type "New pick". Use Advanced settings, then Padding, to balance the space around the text.

  6. Check the post: the badge sits inside the safe layout, and the graphic comes from the library, so keep to the licence terms.

Desktop and mobile differences

  • The Elements tab is in the side panel on desktop and in the editor toolbar on mobile; the shape toolbar entries are named Stroke style and Colour on desktop and Style and Color below the editor on mobile.

  • Rulers are desktop only, which matters when you align elements precisely; see Layers, Alignment and Arrangement.

  • Quick Flow is enabled from the right-click menu on desktop and from the shape’s More menu on mobile.

  • Dragging a 3D element to rotate it works in the editor, in present mode and on a published website.

Common pitfalls

  • Looking for flip on a shape: shapes cannot be flipped.

  • Trying to place a photo inside a shape or border: use a frame or grid instead.

  • Expecting to edit the path of an AI shape or an uploaded SVG: only size and colour can change.

  • Missing colour tile: the element has no editable colours.

  • Overusing 3D elements: the limits are five per page and 50 per design, and they export as static images.

  • Treating library graphics as a logo: library content cannot be trademarked, apart from fonts, simple shapes and lines.

  • Adding decorative graphics without alt text: mark purely decorative items as decorative, as described on Accessibility in Canva.
