Brand Templates, Controls and Approvals

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers the governance side of Canva’s brand tooling: publishing a design as a Brand Template, locking the parts of it that must not change, restricting colours and fonts with Brand Controls, requiring review before publishing, and the two checkers that help people stay on brand (Brand Assist and the Template Assistant). All of it is generally available in Canva on the web, the desktop app and, for using templates and requesting approvals, the mobile apps. Most of the setup is limited to team administrators and brand designers and, for the Brand Controls settings, to a computer.

Plans are the main difference between the features. The pricing grid lists Brand Templates for Pro, Business and Enterprise, and Brand Controls and approvals for Business and Enterprise only. Within a team, Brand Templates are published by administrators and brand designers (Business replaced Teams for new sign-ups; existing Teams keep their plan). The Help Center states that Brand Controls are available to Teams and Business administrators and not to Education teams or organisations, that Brand Assist is available for Teams and Enterprise plans, that the Template Assistant is available to Teams owners, administrators and brand designers, and that several approval features (group reviewers, several approvers, rules for specific people, approval history export) are Enterprise only. Replace across designs is listed for Pro, Teams, Business, Education and Nonprofits. Prices are not repeated here; see https://www.canva.com/pricing/ and Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance. None of these features is a Canva AI feature with its own allowance, although Brand Templates and their locks also guide Canva AI when it generates designs (see Locks and Canva AI).

The running example is Bookshelf, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the Figma sub-section. The Bookshelf Brand Kit is built on Brand Kit and Brand Voice. This page turns the "Book of the week" Instagram post into a locked Brand Template that the marketing team can reuse, gets its first version approved, and keeps colours and fonts on brand. Filling that template in bulk is covered on Resize, Bulk Create and Translate, and the team roles behind "brand designer" and "admin" are explained on Teams, Roles and Groups.

The big picture

Three layers work together. A Brand Kit holds the approved colours, fonts and logos. A Brand Template is a ready-made layout built on that kit, with locks on the elements that must stay fixed. Brand Controls and design approval decide how strictly people must follow the kit and whether a design needs a reviewer’s agreement before it can be published or downloaded. Brand Assist and the Template Assistant sit alongside as advisers: they flag problems but do not decide anything.

Brand Templates

A Brand Template is a design that an administrator or brand designer has published for the team to start from. Everyone else opens it, adds content and leaves the protected parts alone. Templates are owned by the brand rather than by an individual, so they survive staff changes, although some older templates still show a personal owner while Canva updates ownership.

Where Brand Templates live

On the homepage, the Brand tab in the side panel lists Brand Kits and an All Brand Templates view. In the editor, the Brand tab of the side panel has an All Brand Templates section with a See all link. On a phone, the homepage Brand tab sits under More, and in the editor the Brand tab is at the bottom. If a template is missing from a Project or Team Space, look under Brand, All Brand Templates: removing a template from a Team Space does not delete it.

Using a Brand Template

From the homepage, open the Brand tab, choose All Brand Templates, select a template and choose Use this Brand Template. From the editor, open the Brand tab, choose See all under All Brand Templates, select a template and then pick Apply all pages or Brand Template Style to finish. If elements in a template cannot be moved, locks were applied; ask a team owner or administrator to adjust them. If you cannot choose other colours or fonts, Brand Controls are probably on for the design.

Publishing a design as a Brand Template

Administrators and brand designers can publish from four places: Share, then Brand Template (select See all first if it is not shown); the homepage, hovering a design and choosing More, then Save as Template; the editor, File, then Save as Brand Template; and the Make a copy flow, choosing Proceed to publish. All four open the same Publish Brand Template panel, so the choices below do not depend on the entry point.

Panel option What it does

Description

Tells the team what the template is for

Share with

Brand designers and admins only, specific people or groups of the team, or the whole team

Move (optional)

Stores the template in a chosen folder

Add to Brand Kit (optional)

Links the template to a Brand Kit, which also helps Canva AI generate on-brand designs

Design approval

Requires approval for designs made from this template; shown only when approvals are enabled for specific Brand Templates

Send notification

Notifies users about the new template; notifying is optional

Any comments and comment history are deleted when a template is published or republished. Tags can be added when publishing or editing so that people find templates faster; consistent names such as social, sales, event and internal work best. Brand Templates are for internal use: a public template link no longer works and embedded templates are no longer supported, so to share something outside the team, make a design from the template and share it as view-only.

Building a template that is easy to reuse

The Help Center gives practical rules for the layout. Use placeholder text such as a "headline goes here" line and placeholder images rather than empty frames. Use frames and grids for pictures so that a replacement keeps its crop. Prefer SVG icons, which scale cleanly and can be recoloured with Brand Kit colours (select the element, choose Color, pick a kit colour and use Change all). Leave room between text boxes so they can grow, match text box widths with Command + Shift + J on macOS or Ctrl + Shift + J on Windows before grouping, and choose Top, Center or Bottom in the text box’s advanced settings to control how text reflows. Group items that must move together, such as a headline and subheading. Put transparency on an overlay rather than on the photo and keep the photo above the overlay, so that a picture stays easy to replace by drag and drop.

Source: Canva Help Center: Brand Templates best practices guide

Procedure: lock and publish the Book of the week template

  1. Open the finished "Book of the week" Instagram post that uses the Bookshelf Brand Kit, and replace the real title, author and cover with placeholder text and a placeholder cover image inside a frame.

  2. Select the Bookshelf logo, open the More icon on the floating toolbar and choose Lock, the full lock.

  3. Select the title and author text boxes and choose the position-only lock, then keep the formatting locked so that teammates can change the words but not the font, size or colour.

  4. Select the cover frame and choose the position-only lock, so that the picture can be replaced, cropped or resized inside its frame but the frame cannot be deleted or duplicated. Lock the call-to-action pill with a full lock so the required wording stays.

  5. Test the locks yourself: try to move, restyle and delete each element before publishing.

  6. Choose Share, then See all, then Brand Template. Add a description, choose Your Team under Share with, choose the Bookshelf Brand Kit under Add to Brand Kit, tick Send notification and select Publish and close.

  7. Open Brand on the homepage, choose All Brand Templates and confirm that the template is listed.

Template locks

Locks protect the elements that define a template. They are set by administrators and brand designers before publishing, and they only affect people who use the template; the owner can still edit everything when editing the template itself.

Figure 3. A "Book of the week" template with full locks and position-only locks marked

Lock (full lock)

Any edit to the selected element, for example resizing, moving or deleting the logo

Page lock, position only

Deleting the page or moving it

Some edits to the page content

Background lock

Choose what to lock by asking what would damage the brand if it changed: logos, brand colours in key areas, legal disclaimers or required text, layout structures and contact details are the Help Center’s own examples. Locks must be set before publishing. To change them later, an administrator or brand designer edits the template, adjusts the locks and republishes. The Help Center’s menu wording differs slightly between articles (a "Lock position only" entry in one table and a "Lock position and appearance" entry in the steps), so rely on what the More menu on your element offers.

On desktop, locks are applied from the floating toolbar. The Help Center does not publish a dedicated keyboard shortcut for locking in the Brand Template articles, so use the menu; the general lock-and-unlock behaviour of the editor is described on Layers, Alignment and Arrangement. On a phone, the locks of an existing template still apply, but templates are normally prepared on a computer.

Locks and Canva AI

When Canva AI generates a new design from Brand Templates linked to a Brand Kit, it treats locked elements as guardrails: it keeps locked elements unchanged, keeps brand logos by default even without a lock, follows the template’s colours, fonts and layout structure, and may adjust unlocked elements to fit the generated content. Editing an existing design with Canva AI behaves differently: the Help Center says Canva AI currently treats all locks the same, so content inside a partially locked element cannot be updated by Canva AI after the first generation, and a Lock All or a design lock is treated as a complete lock. If you want Canva AI to edit, lock only the specific elements to preserve. Static design types such as presentations, Instagram posts, Facebook stories, posters, business cards and invitations work well; websites, Docs, Sheets and videos are not yet supported. See the Canva AI assistant page for the assistant itself.

Source: Canva Help Center: Apply locks to Brand Templates

Updating and managing Brand Templates

Edits to a template never go live automatically. An administrator or brand designer edits the template, Canva saves the changes as a draft, teammates keep using the last published version, and the changes go live when someone republishes. The same article series explains that only administrators, brand designers or people invited to collaborate can edit a template and see unpublished changes, that any administrator or brand designer can delete any template, and that organisation brand designers can edit a template owned by another team of the same organisation through Edit in owning team.

Procedure: update the template for a new campaign

  1. From the homepage, open Brand, then All Brand Templates.

  2. Hover over the "Book of the week" template, select the More icon and choose Edit original.

  3. Change the design, for example update the call to action, and adjust any locks that need to change.

  4. Select Republish with changes on the menu bar above the editor.

  5. Review the panel, choose whether to send a notification and select Publish and close. Teammates now start from the new version; designs they already made are not changed.

Source: Canva Help Center: Edit Brand Templates

Templates that had open comments or recent edits when Canva upgraded the system were moved into an Unpublished changes section: open Brand, All Brand Templates, Unpublished Changes, review the edits and choose Republish with changes, or delete the copies you no longer need.

Source: Canva Help Center: What’s new: Brand Templates

Brand Controls

Brand Controls limit how team members use brand assets. At team level an administrator can restrict designs to the Brand Kit’s colours (Color control), to its fonts (Font control), and can require design approval before publishing. Brand Controls are available to Teams and Business administrators, are not available to Education teams or organisations, and can be set only on a computer. A Brand Kit must exist first.

Figure 4. Team-level controls and the folder-level override

Brand Controls do not work like locks. A lock protects an element of one template, whereas a control applies to a person’s whole design: with colour control on, the colour panel offers only the kit’s palette, and the option to switch kits disappears. If a design is saved in a controlled folder, the assigned kit cannot be changed in the editor; to use another kit, move the design to a folder without controls. Controls cannot be set up in the mobile app, but they still apply when a controlled design is edited there.

Folder-level Brand Controls

A group of designs can follow one specific Brand Kit by adding that kit to a folder. Administrators, brand designers and teachers (and team members with edit access to the folder, for the first step) can add the kit; then an administrator, brand designer or teacher opens the folder’s Brand Kit tab and switches on only the colours and fonts of that kit. Controls applied directly to a folder overwrite the team-level controls for designs in that folder, and removing the kit returns the team’s controls. The first time you enable folder controls it can take up to an hour to take effect. Team members who cannot see the switches should ask an administrator, teacher or brand designer to be added as a folder editor. Folders themselves are covered on Projects, Folders and File Management.

Procedure: turn on controls for the Bookshelf team

  1. On a computer, open the homepage and select Brand in the side panel.

  2. Select the dropdown beside the Bookshelf Brand Kit name and open Brand Controls.

  3. Switch on Color control and Font control so designs use only the Bookshelf indigo, amber and ink palette and its fonts.

  4. Switch on Design approval if the team wants review before publishing (see the next section).

  5. Create a folder called "Bookshelf Launch", add the Bookshelf Brand Kit to it from the kit’s More menu and, in the folder’s Brand Kit tab, switch on the colour and font controls. Wait up to an hour for the first activation.

Source: Canva Help Center: Setting up Brand Controls

Design approval

Design approval requires a reviewer to agree before designs are published or downloaded. Canva Teams supports individual reviewers only; Canva Enterprise adds several approvers, group reviewers and finer rules. Approval can be required (a design must be approved before it can be published) or optional (members may ask, but it is not mandatory).

Settings for administrators

Open Brand on the homepage, select the dropdown beside the kit name, open Brand Controls and toggle Design approvals on. Select the settings icon to configure three things.

Setting Choices

Who can submit designs for approval

Everyone, Members only, or Selected people or groups only (Enterprise only)

Who can be assigned to review

Everyone, Admins and brand designers only, Selected people only (Teams), or Selected people or groups (Enterprise)

Which designs can be submitted

All designs, or designs created from designated Brand Templates only; Enterprise adds All designs except selected Brand Templates

Is approval required to publish

Yes or No on Teams; Optional or Required on Enterprise

On Enterprise, a group reviewer notifies every group member but only one of them needs to approve. When you choose a template-based option, a confirmation window lets you require approval for all existing templates, skip it for all, or leave them unchanged. Changes are not retroactive: existing templates must be republished once approvals are enabled, and designs published earlier are not affected. Enterprise administrators can also export the approval history as a CSV file from the Approvals tab with Download data. Their best-practice list recommends a short approver list, clear naming, specific feedback such as a page-level instruction, stated turnaround times and regular reviews of approver permissions.

Approvals per Brand Template

With template-based approvals enabled, an administrator or brand designer opens Brand, All Brand Templates, the template’s More menu and Edit Brand Template, chooses Republish with changes and ticks Allow design approval requests for designs created from this Brand Template (and optionally Send notification) before selecting Publish and close. Members must then seek approval on designs made from that template before they can download or publish them.

Procedure: request approval and review the launch post

  1. As the designer, open the finished Instagram post, select Share and choose Request approval.

  2. Select a reviewer, optionally set a due date and add a note, then select Request approval. To cancel, choose Withdraw submission.

  3. The reviewer receives an email titled "You’ve been asked to review" and a notice in the homepage Notifications tab. They can also open the Approvals tab (on mobile, More, then Designs to review) and select Review.

  4. In the editor the reviewer checks content, branding and design, optionally comments on pages or elements, then selects Awaiting review on the menu bar and chooses Review.

  5. The reviewer adds optional notes and chooses Approve, or Request changes. Change decision reverses an earlier choice.

  6. The designer follows progress in Approvals, Your submissions, filtered by All approvals, Approved, Awaiting review or Needs changes. After feedback, the designer edits and requests approval again.

  7. Once approved, the publish options unlock. If the designer edits the design afterwards it needs approval again, whereas approvers can edit at any time without reapproval.

Brand Assist and the Template Assistant

Brand Assist gives members real-time suggestions while they design. An administrator or brand designer switches it on under Brand, the kit’s dropdown, Brand Controls; members then open the editor’s Brand tab and, when suggestions appear, select the option under Brand Assist. Its panel lists issues, each of which can be opened for a fix or handled with auto-fix. It checks several areas: colours not in the kit, fonts not in the kit, logos (rotated, flipped, faded, cropped, bordered or filtered), and photos, icons and graphics with altered brightness, saturation, filters or effects. Its rules are defined by Canva and cannot be customised, it switches between several Brand Kits automatically, it does not support Docs or Sheets, and it flags problems without blocking publishing: members may bypass a suggestion. A complete kit (logos, fonts and a wide range of colours) makes it work better.

The Template Assistant is for the people who build templates. It is available to Teams owners, administrators and brand designers, on a computer only. Open the design, choose File, Settings and Show Template Assistant. It checks print designs (no unwanted white edges, nothing near the trim, no animation or video), text (defined, non-overlapping, legible, consistent with the kit), elements (easy to select, no stacked or hidden elements) and media (high quality, easy to replace). Run it on the Book of the week template before publishing.

Source: Canva Help Center: Activate Brand Assist to ensure on-brand designs

Source: Canva Help Center: Check the quality of designs and prints

Replace across designs

When a logo or recurring photo changes, administrators and brand designers with edit access to both the image and the designs can replace it everywhere at once on desktop. Open Brand, select the kit, choose Logos (or Photos), hover over the asset, select More and Replace across designs. Select the designs, choose Continue, supply the replacement, select Preview and then Replace in the chosen number of designs. Logos can be replaced only with PNG or JPG files. For photos, the image must be identical (the same file) across designs; the option does not appear if the image is not a reusable kit or shared asset, is used in only one editable design, or you lack edit access. Designs where the image was cropped, filtered or resized may not update. Keep recurring images in the Brand Kit or in shared folders and duplicate from a master template to keep file identity consistent. On mobile, replacing images is limited to social media designs.

Source: Canva Help Center: Replace all logos or images in multiple designs

Common mistakes

  • Locking after publishing: locks cannot be added to a published template; edit, lock and republish instead.

  • Locking everything: a full lock on text stops teammates from entering the book title. Use position-only locks for content that changes.

  • Expecting Canva AI to edit partially locked elements after the first generation.

  • Forgetting to republish: edited templates stay as drafts and teammates keep using the previous version.

  • Sharing a template link externally: public and embedded Brand Template access is no longer supported.

  • Switching on approval without republishing templates: changes are not retroactive.

  • Looking for Brand Controls on a phone, or on an Education account where they are not offered.

What changed and where older guides are outdated

  • Older Canva guides describe Teams as the paid team plan and lock features as "template locking" on any shared template. Canva Business replaced Teams for new customers on 2025-10-30, and locks now offer full, position-only, page and background types, are set by administrators and brand designers before publishing, and also guide Canva AI.

  • Older guides describe team templates as editable live by anyone with folder access, shared by links. The Help Center’s "What’s new: Brand Templates" article describes the current model: only administrators, brand designers or invited people edit, changes go live on republish, templates stay inside the team account, comments are deleted on publish, tags exist, notifying users is optional and public or embedded access ended.

  • Older guides do not mention design approval, Brand Assist, the Template Assistant or Replace across designs, which are all documented in the current Help Center. A July 2026 summary in the Help Center’s release notes also lists Brand Kit approval workflows and one-click Brand Template publishing; check your account for availability.

  • Older guides describe a single "Brand Kit" and brand-wide controls. Teams can now hold several Brand Kits, and controls can be set for a folder.

For the full list of corrections across the sub-section see What Changed Since Older Guides. Enterprise administration, SSO and security settings are on Administration, Enterprise and Security, and sharing rules are on Sharing and Permissions. The same templates can also be driven by code through the Brand Template endpoints described on REST APIs and the tools on the Canva MCP server page.
