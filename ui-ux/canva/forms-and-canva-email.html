Forms and Canva Email

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers two Visual Suite features that turn a design into a conversation with an audience. Canva Forms are form, poll and quiz elements that you place in a design and share by link, so that readers can answer you. Canva Email is a visual email design type that exports clean HTML, so that a newsletter can be sent from an email platform. The Help Center article on forms says that forms are added to designs from the Elements tab or created with Canva AI, and the article on Canva Email says that Canva does not send emails itself.

Plans and AI tiers: the Help Center articles used here do not restrict forms or email design to a particular plan. Adding a form from the Elements tab counts as a Premium AI feature toward the AI allowance, and creating a form with Canva AI draws on the same allowance (the Standard, Premium and Ultra tiers are explained on Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance). Generating a quiz from selected text is powered by Magic Write and is included in all plans, except in China. Publishing an email design to Mailchimp is available on Free, Pro, Business and Enterprise. Prices are published only at https://www.canva.com/pricing/ and are not repeated here. Canva Forms and Canva Email are generally available. The conversational Canva AI 2.0 launched as a research preview on 2026-04-16, has no published general-availability date, and is not required for anything on this page.

The running example is Bookshelf, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the Figma sub-section. The launch campaign needs a reader survey that asks which genres people want first, and a newsletter that announces the "Book of the week" and links to the launch website (see Websites).

Canva Forms

A form in Canva is an element, not a separate product. It sits on a page like a shape or a photo, can have up to 10 questions, and collects answers while the design is shared through a Canva link, for example as a public view link or as a published website (see Sharing and Permissions). Polls and quizzes are forms with different question types, and the quiz type lets you mark correct answers. The step-by-step basics of the element, including the quiz storyboard, are on Interactive Elements; this section concentrates on responses and on forms used as part of a campaign.

Three ways to get a form

  • From the library. In the editor side panel select Elements, then under Browse categories select Forms, browse the form types (use See all for a type) and select one to add it. Library forms are presets with common fields and layouts.

  • With Canva AI. From the homepage, or from the editor side panel for supported design types, open Canva AI and describe the form, poll or quiz in a prompt, such as a post-event poll. The plus icon adds context: uploads, files from Canva, your brand and connected apps. Canva AI adds the form to your design as a new page.

  • From text. Select two or three full sentences of text, choose Magic Write in the floating toolbar and then Generate quiz. If there is not enough content, select more text and try again; failed attempts do not affect AI usage.

The Help Center documents these steps for the desktop browser and for the mobile app. To select several elements at once, hold Cmd on macOS or Ctrl on Windows while you click, as the quiz article describes.

Customising a form

Select the form and choose Edit in the quick-access menu. The Questions tab lets you add or remove questions (up to 10), change the question type, make fields required, add helper text and mark the correct answers of a quiz. The Settings tab lets you show or hide the header field, change the submit button text and decide whether to collect respondent names. The same quick-access menu changes colours, border style, corner rounding, transparency, animation and position, and the text toolbar styles the text.

Limits to know before you build

  • Up to 10 questions per form and up to 10 options per question.

  • Up to 10 forms per page and up to 20 forms per design.

  • Forms and quizzes can be added to all design types except Docs and Email, so a form cannot live inside a Canva Email; link from the email to a design or website that carries the form.

  • The design must be shared by a Canva link to collect responses; forms do not collect responses once the design is downloaded as an image, video or PDF.

  • If name collection is off, responses are anonymous, even from logged-in users. If it is on, names are collected from logged-in users and may be asked of others. On Canva Websites responses are always anonymous and names cannot be collected.

Reading responses and getting notified

Select the form and then Responses to open the responses panel. Its More menu offers Download as CSV. Edits after responses exist have visible effects: deleting an option labels earlier submissions as inactive, changing the correct answer of a quiz re-scores earlier submissions, and editing an option label updates it for all earlier submissions. Canva Pro accounts can also see data insights.

Notifications work by email. The form owner receives a message when someone submits a response, and you cannot choose or add other recipients. Each recipient receives at most five notification emails per day, and several quick submissions may be batched into one message. To switch them on or off for a form created in Canva, select the form, choose Edit, open the Settings tab and toggle Form submission notification under Notifications.

Forms that save into Sheets

Forms built with Canva Code behave differently from the form element: each creation connects to its own Canva Sheet automatically, and submissions are written there. The Help Center states that data flows one way, from the creation to the Sheet, that edits made in the Sheet do not sync back and may be overwritten, that a Sheet holds up to 999 rows and 104 columns, and that each Version of a creation has its own Sheet. Notifications for Canva Code forms are switched in the connected Sheet under Data, then Settings. Responses collected in a design are stored separately from those collected in the version in your homepage thread. Custom data connectors for a Canva Code form were described as coming soon at the time of checking.

Canva Email

Canva Email is a design type that opens an email canvas with blocks instead of a free-form page. The Help Center describes it as a way to design brand-consistent emails without writing code, and to download the result as an HTML file for platforms such as Gmail, Mailchimp or any platform that supports HTML. Because Canva does not send the message, the workflow has four steps: structure the layout, add content, test and export, and send from an email platform.

Figure 1. The parts of the Bookshelf newsletter: header, hero, columns, button and footer

Layouts and building blocks

Start from the homepage with the Email icon, which opens a new email design. You can type a description of the email in the Templates tab for a pre-made layout, or click the page and start from a blank design. A good layout follows logical sections: a header or hero, a short introduction, the main content, a call to action and a footer, with clean spacing, one or two columns and consistent alignment.

The elements the Help Center recommends are:

Element What it does in an email

Design blocks (header, footer)

Pre-made sections found in the Elements tab under Headers and Footers. A header or footer can be pinned to the edge of the design with More, then Set to top or Set to bottom.

Icon blocks

Clickable icons with short text for social links or feature highlights; recolour them from the toolbar colour tiles.

Buttons

Add from Elements, then Buttons (or the plus icon, then Button), enter the button name and the URL, and select Done.

Columns

Basic, styled or pre-designed columns from Elements, or from the plus icon next to the text cursor. Drag the divider to resize; columns stack vertically on mobile.

Images

From Elements or from Uploads. Compress large images before uploading so the email loads fast.

Text

Only email-safe fonts are guaranteed to display; the default is Arial. The Help Center suggests 14 to 16 pixels for body text and 20 to 30 pixels for headings. Paragraph, line and letter spacing are under Advanced settings in the text toolbar, and block spacing under Spacing.

A page background colour comes from the document colours or the Brand Kit (see Brand Kit and Brand Voice).

What does not survive in an email

Email clients handle HTML and CSS differently, so Canva states its limits explicitly:

  • Only solid, dotted and dashed borders are supported, and dotted or dashed patterns may differ by client.

  • Gradient text usually falls back to a solid colour.

  • Embeds, sheets, charts and other complex elements are converted to images on export or send, so they are not interactive and do not update.

  • Polls and quizzes appear as static content, and videos are converted to silent GIFs that may be large.

  • Layout items support a single row; use tables for several rows. To add a column to a single-column layout, select the layout cell, press Escape to leave text editing and use the layout controls.

  • Gmail removes media queries and some Outlook-specific optimisations, so a layout may not stack correctly on small screens when sent or viewed through Gmail.

  • Banners, grouped graphics and text saved as part of an image export as one flat image; build with text boxes, frames and layout blocks so that platforms can read them as HTML.

Desktop and mobile

The Help Center documents the layout, element and export steps for both the desktop editor and the mobile app. Checking the exported ZIP on your computer, by extracting it and opening the HTML file in a browser, is documented for desktop only. Keyboard shortcuts are not published for email blocks beyond the Escape key mentioned above.

Test, export and send

Test           Share > Send test email (only to your Canva login address)
Copy HTML      Share > Copy HTML > Copy to clipboard
Download       Share > Download > File type: HTML and images
Gmail          Share > Gmail > Connect Gmail > Export to Gmail
Mailchimp      Share > Mailchimp > Publish now

Test emails can only be sent to your Canva login email address and arrive from the sender no-reply@preview.canva.email; check your spam folder if it does not show up. To export for an email service provider (ESP) you either copy the HTML into the platform’s code editor, or download a ZIP file that contains the HTML and its images. Extract the ZIP before you open the HTML, otherwise the images will not load.

Platforms fall into two groups. An email inbox provider such as Gmail or Outlook is for one-to-one or small sends. An ESP such as Mailchimp, HubSpot, Klaviyo, Brevo, Constant Contact or Flodesk manages lists, automation, analytics and deliverability. The Help Center has separate articles for Gmail, Outlook, Mailchimp, Constant Contact, Klaviyo and Flodesk. For any other ESP, use its option to import HTML, code your own or custom HTML, upload the HTML file (and the image folder if it asks for one), preview, add the required fields and send a test.

Figure 2. The routes from a finished email design to an inbox provider or an email service provider

Three facts catch people out. First, an Unsubscribe placeholder in a premade footer is not a working link in exported HTML, so add or confirm the real unsubscribe link in your ESP before you send, which is required by laws such as CAN-SPAM and GDPR. Second, Canva does not yet support ESP-specific merge tags, so recipient names and similar fields are added in the ESP after upload. Third, once imported as HTML, most ESPs no longer allow drag-and-drop editing, so make design changes in Canva and publish again.

Direct publishing to Mailchimp is available for one email page at a time, as Email (HTML), and the design must be under 100 KB. It is not available in China. Compliance links you added to the design are converted into Mailchimp merge tags on publish. Gmail is not an ESP: the Export to Gmail route is for one-off or small-group messages, not for bulk campaigns.

Procedure 1: add a reader survey to the Bookshelf launch design

The goal is a short form that asks which genres the launch should feature first.

  1. Open the Bookshelf Launch Flyer or the launch website design and add a page for the survey.

  2. In the editor side panel select Elements, then under Browse categories select Forms, and select a survey-style form. Alternatively open Canva AI and ask for a three-question poll about favourite book genres.

  3. Select the form, choose Edit and in the Questions tab keep three questions: favourite genre, how often people read and an optional free-text suggestion. Mark the first two as required.

  4. In the Settings tab change the submit button to "Send my picks" and leave name collection off to keep answers anonymous.

  5. Under Notifications confirm that Form submission notification is on.

  6. Share the design with a public link or publish the website, submit a test answer yourself, then select the form and Responses to confirm it arrived.

  7. When the survey closes, choose More, then Download as CSV and open the file in a spreadsheet.

Source: Canva Help Center: Add forms and quizzes

Procedure 2: design, test and send the Bookshelf newsletter

The goal is a "Book of the week" newsletter that links to the survey and the catalogue.

  1. On the Canva homepage select the Email icon. In the Templates tab describe a "basic newsletter layout" and select one, or click the blank page to start from scratch.

  2. In the Elements tab add a header with the Bookshelf logo, then replace the colours and fonts with the brand kit and an email-safe font.

  3. Add a hero image, the headline "Book of the week" and a two-column block with the cover on one side and three lines of text on the other.

  4. Add a button named "Read the catalogue" and paste the web address of the launch website; add a second button for the survey page.

  5. Add the footer and an icon block for the social links, and change the placeholder unsubscribe text only as a reminder: the working link is added in the sending platform.

  6. Select Send test email, enter a subject, open the message on a desktop and on a phone, and fix broken links or fonts.

  7. Select Share, then Mailchimp, choose the campaign page and select Publish now; or for a few friendly readers choose Gmail and Export to Gmail.

  8. In the sending platform add the unsubscribe link and merge tags, send a final test and schedule the send.

Common mistakes

  • Putting a form in a Canva Email: forms are not available in Email designs, and a poll or quiz appears as static content. Link to the form instead.

  • Downloading a design as a PDF and expecting the form to work, since forms do not collect responses outside a shared Canva link.

  • Expecting a form element to write into Sheets: that is how Canva Code forms behave; for a form element use the Responses panel and the CSV download.

  • Using a decorative font in an email, which is replaced by a fallback font in the recipient’s client.

  • Sending a campaign without replacing the placeholder unsubscribe text.

  • Judging the layout only in the editor. Always send a test email and check Gmail and a phone.
