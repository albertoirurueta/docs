Business and Marketing Materials

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center and Canva’s design-type pages on 2026-11-07. It covers the paper and screen materials that a small product team needs around a launch: logos, business cards, letterheads, email signatures, reports and proposals, brochures and flyers, posters and signage, ads, event promotion, infographics, résumés, book covers and YouTube thumbnails. All of them are made in the same Canva editor, on every plan (Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits); what differs by plan is the content and controls you can use around them, for example brand controls and Brand Templates, which are explained on Brand Templates, Controls and Approvals. Prices are not repeated here; they are published at https://www.canva.com/pricing/. Nothing on this page needs a Canva AI tier, although the template search can offer to generate a template with Canva AI, an experience the Help Center says may not be available to everyone yet (Canva AI tiers are on Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance). Everything described is generally available.

The running example is Bookshelf, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the Figma sub-section. Its launch needs a business card for the founder, a flyer for the book-club meetup, an email signature for the community lead, a YouTube thumbnail for the launch video and a reusable template so that teammates can produce more of the same materials without breaking the brand. The pieces covered elsewhere are linked rather than repeated: the colours, fonts and logos come from Brand Kit and Brand Voice, printing and ordering is on Print and Mockups, social posts and scheduling are on Social Media and the Content Planner, resizing one design into many formats is on Resize, Bulk Create and Translate, and forms and newsletters are on Forms and Canva Email.

One workflow for every material

Whatever the material, the workflow is the same, and most mistakes come from skipping the first two steps: choosing the size and the output before designing.

Starting a design: type, custom size or template

You can begin a material in three ways. From the homepage you can search the Create tab for a design type, which opens a blank design with the preset size. You can search for or browse templates from the homepage or from the editor’s side panel and select Customize this template. Or you can select Custom size on the homepage banner, choose the unit (px, in, mm or cm) and enter a width and height. The Help Center states that a custom size must be at least 40 pixels and at most 8000 pixels on a side, and that a design outside the allowed limits has to be started at a supported size and then resized within the limits. Designs open in a new browser tab by default; you can change that under Settings, in Your profile, with the Link opening toggle. On a phone the same actions are reachable from the mobile app.

Canva’s template search can also offer a generated starting point: with a search of four or more keywords, the message Can’t find something? offers Try Canva AI, and you can attach context, apply your brand and submit the prompt; the result is added to the design as a new page. The Help Center notes this experience may not be available to everyone yet.

If the design is going to be printed, the Help Center suggests CMYK colours so that the printed colours are printer-friendly, and recommends it for Canva Print orders. Colour handling for print is explained on Print and Mockups.

Source: Canva Help Center: Create designs using templates

Margins, bleed and safe zones

Print materials need a bleed (artwork that runs past the trim line) and a margin (an inner zone where text stays). Screen materials have no bleed, but they do have safe areas, such as the corner that a video player covers with the duration badge on a thumbnail. The five zones of a print page and the exact rules, with the default 0.125 inch (3.175 mm) bleed for products that are trimmed, are explained and illustrated on Print and Mockups; guides, grids and frames are on Frames, Grids, Guides and Print Layout. Rulers, margins and bleed are tools of the computer editor; they are not available in Canva Docs.

The practical rule is simple. Fill backgrounds into the bleed, keep text, logos and QR codes inside the margins, and judge text size at 100 percent zoom. Canva generally advises not going below size 7 for print text, and recommends images of 300 dpi.

Source: Canva Help Center: Use margins, bleed, rulers, and crop marks

Material by material

Logos and brand marks

Logo work in Canva covers three jobs: drawing or generating a mark, storing the variants, and placing them correctly. Canva offers a logo maker page and an AI logo generator page, and the Brand Kit keeps the final variants (full colour, single colour, icon only) so that anyone can insert them from the Brand tab. Draw the logo once at a large size, keep a transparent-background copy, and never stretch it; resize by holding the corner handles. A logo is not a trademark registration; check the licence terms of any element you use on Licensing, Responsible AI and Canva Shield.

Source: Canva: Logo maker

Business cards, letterheads and stationery

A business card is the smallest print product, so the margin matters most: a few millimetres decide whether a name is cut off. Choose the card format in the Create tab or from Print Shop, put the logo and name on the front and the web address and a QR code on the back. Canva’s QR code generator makes static or dynamic QR codes; for a dynamic code you can change the destination URL later, set an expiry date with a fallback URL, and download PNG, JPG, SVG or PDF versions. Customising the colour, corners and pattern is allowed, and the page says that with Canva Business or Enterprise you can replace the default Canva logo on the code with your own icon. Always scan the final code with a phone before ordering.

A letterhead is a template page with the logo, address block and footer fixed and the body empty. Make it once as a Brand Template (see the reuse section) rather than copying a design.

Email signatures

An email signature in Canva is an image. The Help Center says to download it as a common image format such as JPG or PNG, because exporting an email signature as HTML is not yet available in Canva. Clickable social icons work by downloading each icon separately, uploading the signature and the icons as separate images in the email client and adding a link to each image there. Mail clients differ, so use the documentation of Gmail, Outlook or Yahoo Mail for the exact settings.

Reports, proposals and brochures

Long documents are usually better in a Canva Doc or a presentation than on a poster-style design: a Doc keeps text editable and flowing, and a presentation suits a proposal that will be read on screen. Charts and tables are described on Charts, Tables and Data Visualization and presentations on Presentations. For printed brochures choose the fold first (bi-fold, trifold, gate fold or accordion), because the fold decides the number of panels and where the artwork must not be cut. Keep the call to action on the panel that readers see first.

Flyers, posters and signage

A flyer has one job: send a reader to one action. Use a headline, one image, the date and place, and one call to action. Posters and signage are read from a distance, so the headline must be much larger than for a flyer and contrasts must be higher; see Accessibility in Canva for the contrast checks.

Ads, product launches and event promotion

Ads and event promotion are the same designs in different formats. Build one master design and resize it for each network with the resize tools on Resize, Bulk Create and Translate; the exact pixel sizes, safe areas and file limits come from the ad platform, not from Canva, so read the platform’s own specification before exporting. Social posts and their schedule are on Social Media and the Content Planner, campaign analytics are on Canva Grow and Marketing Analytics, and newsletters and sign-up forms are on Forms and Canva Email. For an event, the same colour and type system on the flyer, the invitation, the slides and the signage is what makes the event look planned.

Infographics

An infographic is a tall design that tells one story in sections. Choose the destination first, because the size changes with it: roughly 663 x 2000 px for a blog post, 1200 x 628 px for a Facebook post and 1104 x 736 px for a LinkedIn image, according to Canva’s infographic page. Give each section one number or fact, keep a consistent icon style and cite the data source on the design. Data visualisation tools are on Charts, Tables and Data Visualization.

Résumés

Canva’s résumé page suggests professional fonts, single line spacing, clear section headings and half-inch margins, with the most relevant and recent experience first. Export as PDF when a recipient asks for a document, and keep a plain-text copy of the content for application forms that do not accept a design.

Book covers

A book cover is a print design with three parts, the front, spine and back, and its dimensions depend on the page count and paper of the printer or retailer. Download that provider’s template or specification first and enter its dimensions as a custom size, within the 40 to 8000 pixel limits, or use a standard cover size from the Create tab. Keep the title large enough to read at thumbnail size, because most readers first see the cover as a small image in a store. The finished file goes to the provider, not to Canva Print, unless the product exists in the Canva Print catalogue.

YouTube thumbnails

A thumbnail is a screen design in the 16:9 ratio, 1280 x 720 px, under 2 MB, according to YouTube Help. Canva’s thumbnail page lists best practices: a clean readable font for the title, colours and graphics that match your brand, strong colour contrast and an image that accurately reflects the content of the channel. Keep the lower right corner free because the player draws the duration badge over it.

Shortcuts and desktop versus mobile

Printing from the editor uses Command + P on macOS and Ctrl + P on Windows, and the side panel toggles with Command + / or Ctrl + /. The complete list is on Keyboard Shortcuts and Quick Actions. Rulers, print margins and bleed guides are computer tools; on a phone or tablet you can still set guides from More on the mobile toolbar, but rulers are not offered. Downloading and sharing work in every app. File formats for each output are on Downloading and File Formats.

Procedure 1: design the Bookshelf business card

  1. On the homepage, select Custom size, choose mm as the unit and enter 85 by 55, or open the Create tab and search for a business card format.

  2. Open the Brand tab in the side panel and apply the Bookshelf colours, fonts and logo from the Brand Kit.

  3. Turn on margins and bleed in the File menu under Rulers and guides, put the logo, name and role inside the margins and extend the background colour into the bleed.

  4. Add a second page for the back, with the web address and a QR code that opens the Bookshelf launch page. Download the QR code from Canva’s QR code generator in a vector or high-resolution format if you need a larger copy.

  5. Check text size, scan the QR code and order the cards, or download a PDF Print with crop marks and bleed. Ordering is described on Print and Mockups.

Figure 4. The five steps of the business card from size to order

Source: Canva Help Center: Design from scratch

Procedure 2: design the Bookshelf meetup flyer

  1. Start a flyer from the Create tab, or choose a flyer product in Print Shop so the size matches what you will order.

  2. Build the hierarchy: a short headline, one image of the Bookshelf shelf logo or a cover collage you own, and a call to action such as "Join the book club".

  3. Add the date and place in the next-largest text and a QR code to the sign-up page.

  4. Turn on margins and bleed, stretch the background into the bleed and keep the text inside the margins.

  5. Open Share, then Download, and choose PDF with the print preset (crop marks and bleed) if you print outside Canva, or use the Print entry to order. The ordering steps and the proofing check are on Print and Mockups.

Figure 5. The five steps of the flyer from blank page to print-ready PDF

Source: Canva Help Center: Get started with Canva Print

Procedure 3: make the community lead’s email signature

  1. Create a custom size of about 600 by 200 px and apply the Bookshelf brand.

  2. Place the logo on the left, then the name and role on two lines and the contact details below.

  3. Download the signature as PNG or JPG. Download each social icon you want clickable as its own PNG or JPG.

  4. In the email client’s signature settings upload the signature image and the icons, then add a link to each image.

  5. Send a test message to yourself on a phone and on a computer to check that the layout and links survive.

Source: Canva Help Center: I’m having issues with my email signature design

Procedure 4: build the launch video thumbnail

  1. Create a YouTube thumbnail design (1280 x 720 px).

  2. Place one focal image, such as the Bookshelf logo on a shelf or a cover, on one side.

  3. Add a title of three to five words in the brand heading font with strong contrast against the background.

  4. Keep the lower right corner empty. Export as PNG or JPG and check that it is under 2 MB.

  5. Upload it in YouTube Studio as the video’s custom thumbnail. Promoting the video in social posts is on Social Media and the Content Planner.

Source: Canva: YouTube thumbnail maker

Reusing materials with Brand Templates and locks

The way to keep dozens of materials on brand is to make each recurring material a Brand Template and lock the parts that must not change. The locked parts (logo, brand strip, footer) stay fixed, while the editable placeholders (title, cover image, author line) can be changed by the teammate who starts from the template. Where locks, controls and approvals are available, and what an administrator can restrict, is covered on Brand Templates, Controls and Approvals; filling a template from a spreadsheet is on Resize, Bulk Create and Translate; finding and starting from templates is on Templates, Layouts and Magic Design.

Common pitfalls

  • Text or logos placed in the bleed or too close to the trim are cut off. Keep them inside the margins.

  • White edges appear when a background does not reach the bleed.

  • Bright screen colours print duller. Choose print-safe colours or CMYK-friendly values from the start.

  • A signature or thumbnail exported at the wrong size is rescaled by the email client or the player; export at the intended size.

  • Choosing the size after the design leads to rework; if you must, resize, then check every element.

  • A QR code that was never scanned before printing is a risk; test the printed proof.

  • Third-party specifications (ad networks, printers, retailers) override any size on this page.
