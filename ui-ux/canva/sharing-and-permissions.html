Sharing and Permissions

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers the Share button of the Canva editor (web, desktop app and mobile apps) and its counterpart on the homepage: who can open a design, what they can do with it, and how you take that access back. Sharing a design is available on every plan, Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits, and it is a generally available feature with no AI tier. Some options are gated by plan: several public view links, template links, design analytics and the administrator controls differ between plans, and each such option below names the tiers that the Help Center lists for it. For the full comparison of plans see Plans, Pricing and the AI Allowance and the official pricing page.

Sharing is the first step of every feedback loop in a design team, so this page sits between the single-user editor tour and the pages on working together. Once people are inside a design, comments, live co-editing and version history are described on Collaboration, Comments and Version History; who counts as a team member, an administrator or a brand designer is described on Teams, Roles and Groups; sharing folders is part of Projects, Folders and File Management.

The running example is Bookshelf, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the Figma sub-section. In this page the "Book of the week" Instagram post is sent to the launch team for review, a public view link is created for a partner newsletter, an access request is answered, and the post is finally reset to private. The drawings are deliberately schematic and are not screenshots.

Two settings: who, and what they can do

Every share link or invitation is the combination of two choices.

  • The access level answers "who can open it". A new design is set to Only you can access. The other values are Only your team, offered to people in a team workspace, and Anyone with the link.

  • The permission level answers "what can they do". The three values are can view, can comment and can edit, and you can change them at any time after sharing.

Figure 1. The three access levels crossed with the three permission levels, using the Book of the week post

Source: Canva Help Center: Sharing designs via email or links

If you give anyone a comment-only link, they can read every comment in the editor without logging in; to reply, add a comment or change the design they must log in or sign up. In a team workspace the Share panel also has a Visible in search results setting, with the choices For all team members and Only for members with the link, so that a team-wide design does not have to appear in everybody’s search. Shared designs show profile pictures on the homepage and in Projects: click one to see who has access; a design without a picture is private to you, and a second picture with a plus sign means that you and two or more people or teams have access.

Share with a link

The link is the fastest route and the only one that works for people who do not have a Canva account. The Share button sits in the menu bar above the editor, and on the homepage you reach the same panel by hovering over a design thumbnail and selecting More, Share. On a phone the steps are the same through the mobile editor.

Share a design or a single page

  1. Open the design and select Share in the menu bar above the editor.

  2. Under Access level, open the dropdown and choose Only you can access, Only your team (team workspaces only) or Anyone with the link.

  3. In the second dropdown choose Can edit, Can comment or Can view.

  4. In a team workspace, under Visible in search results choose For all team members or Only for members with the link.

  5. Select Copy link. A short link is generated for the design.

  6. To share one page rather than the whole design, select More on that page and choose Copy link to this page.

Source: Canva Help Center: Sharing designs via email or links

Guests: edit access for anyone with the link

Setting Anyone with the link to Can edit lets people change the design without signing up. When such a visitor is not logged in, Canva shows them as an animal illustration with a name instead of initials or a photo; seeing guests in a design means that someone gave edit access to everybody with the link. The Help Center lists what guests can and cannot do.

A guest can A guest cannot

View, present and play the design

Upload content or drag files into the design

Edit: use the side panel, add, edit or delete elements and text, and add, edit or delete pages

Add, edit or resolve a comment without signing up or logging in

Copy the design link that was sent to them

Share by any route other than the link they received, or change link permissions

See comments from other users and live updates

Copy, print, publish or download the design

Source: Canva Help Center: Share your Canva design and collaborate with anyone

Share with specific people

Inviting people by email gives each person their own permission level, so a copywriter can edit while the whole launch team only comments. Inside a team you can also search for a name, or for a group, and pick it from the results.

Invite reviewers by email

  1. Open the design and select Share.

  2. In the field under People with access, type the email addresses, separated by commas. In a team, you can search names instead.

  3. Use the dropdown beside the field to choose Can edit, Can comment or Can view.

  4. Select Share.

Source: Canva Help Center: Sharing designs via email or links

When you share with a team, a building icon next to the avatar means that the whole team has access, and a people icon marks a group, with the number of members shown under its name. You can also invite someone to your team at the moment you share, by ticking the option to invite the user to your team under People with access. The Help Center notes that on Canva Pro you cannot invite people to a team while sharing: creating or growing a team needs Canva Business, and you can still collaborate with people who are not on your plan or do not use Canva. Canva Business replaced Canva Teams for new sign-ups on 2025-10-30, so older articles may still say Canva Teams where the current plan name is Business.

Source: Canva Help Center: Share designs with your team

Walkthrough: send the Book of the week post to the launch team

  1. Open the "Book of the week" post and select Share.

  2. Under Access level, leave the post on Only your team, or choose Anyone with the link if an external illustrator needs it.

  3. Choose the permission: Can comment for reviewers, Can edit for the designer.

  4. Select Copy link and paste it into the launch channel, or type the reviewers' emails under People with access and select Share.

  5. Check the avatars on the design: the people and teams with access are now visible from the homepage.

Figure 2. Storyboard: share the post with the launch team

Source: Canva Help Center: Sharing designs via email or links

Team sharing and the Shared with you page

Inside a team workspace you can share with the whole team through an access level of Only your team, or with selected members and groups through email-style invitations. Team admins and owners with at least View access to a folder can move and delete it. The Help Center lists these limits for team sharing: unique links are only available to paid plans; anyone with a view or watch link can forward it, and you cannot change permissions after it is shared; for template links, recipients with the same work email domain must be on a paid plan; and hidden content (cropped images, hidden pages) becomes visible if the design is copied. Shared designs also cannot be removed from the homepage or Projects, but you can filter to show only designs you own.

The Shared with you page is deliberately narrow. It lists designs, folders and Brand Templates shared directly with you, shared with a group you belong to, reached through a link you opened, or obtained by selecting Use this Brand Template from a shared Brand Template link, plus imported files converted into editable designs. It does not list individual files inside a shared folder (only the folder itself, unless a file was also shared with you directly) nor uploaded media. Content that disappeared from the page when this rule changed has not been deleted: search for it or open the folder that contains it, using the Type filter in Projects if needed.

Source: Canva Help Center: Share designs with your team

Folders have their own permission levels, which apply to everything inside: Can edit and share, Can edit, Can view, or Remove access, changed under Projects with Share or the Access icon. A parent-level folder notice means that a folder above controls access, so change it there or ask a team admin.

Source: Canva Help Center: Update folder permissions in Canva

Public view links

A public view link lets anyone view the design in the experience that suits its type, without editing. It is separate from the collaboration link above: you create, name and delete it on its own.

Create a public view link for the partner newsletter

  1. Open the design and select Share.

  2. Select Public view link, then Create public view link.

  3. Name the link, for example "Partner newsletter", and select Save and copy.

  4. To create another link for a different channel, select Add another link. Adding more links is available on paid plans.

  5. Select Copy to copy the short link. The word Live appears at the top of the link when it is active.

Source: Canva Help Center: Share your design as a public view link

To remove public access, open Share, select Public view link, then More, Delete. Deleting a link also deletes its URL; a new link gets a new URL. Selecting Visitors beside a link opens its analytics: the average time spent and the time on each page. Named links let you tell the newsletter audience from the social audience.

Source: Canva Help Center: Share your design as a public view link

Prepare a design before sharing publicly

Depending on how you share a design publicly, people may reach content that is not visible on the canvas: hidden pages, cropped content, presenter notes and content from earlier revisions. The Help Center recommends this order.

  1. In the original design, remove hidden pages, presenter notes, cropped content and anything else you do not want to share.

  2. Make a copy of the design; the copy does not carry the earlier revisions of the original.

  3. Review the copy so that it holds only what you want to publish.

  4. Share the copy with the option you prefer.

Template links, which let people use a design as a template, are only offered on Canva Pro, Canva Teams, Canva Enterprise, Canva for Education and Canva for Nonprofits according to the same article; see Templates, Layouts and Magic Design for what they do.

Source: Canva Help Center: Sharing designs via email or links

Access requests

When someone opens a link to a design that is not shared with them, they cannot see it. They log in to Canva, open the link and select Request Access; the owner is notified and, after granting access, the requester receives an email with an Open design button. Owners can answer from the bell icon on the homepage: find the request, choose Can edit, Can comment or Can view, and select Share. The notification then moves from the To do to the Completed tab.

If a person still cannot open a design that was shared with everyone in a team, they are usually signed in to another team: from the profile icon on the homepage, under Switch team, check which team has the check mark and change to the right one.

Source: Canva Help Center: Request design access

Restrict and revoke access

You can take access back at two scales. Revoking resets one design; restricting is an organisation-wide control for administrators.

Revoke all access to a design

Remove access removes everyone, including people who had inherited access through a shared folder. Use it when a design moved into a shared folder you do not control, when unexpected people can view or edit it, when you want to reset sharing, or when the content is sensitive.

  1. Open the design and go to the Share menu.

  2. Beside the share design control, select the share settings icon.

  3. Select Remove access.

  4. Choose Yes, remove access, or Cancel.

Figure 3. Storyboard: reset the post to private

Source: Canva Help Center: Revoke all access to your design even from shared folders

Consequences to know in advance: all users lose access, shared links stop working, the design stays in your folders and only you keep access. The action cannot be undone, so re-sharing means adding people again; nobody is notified, and people simply see an access denied message. Only editors and design owners see the option, and it can be greyed out when the design is part of a template or restricted by an administrator. Published designs and templates use separate access settings and are not affected. Afterwards you can re-share with specific people, create new links with updated settings, move the design to a folder you control, or keep it private.

Source: Canva Help Center: Revoke all access to your design even from shared folders

Restrict who can share outside the organisation

Restricted design sharing is available to Canva Enterprise and Canva Education. A team owner chooses who can share designs outside their brand; changes apply only to new designs, and designs already shared externally stay reachable. The default is team admins and brand admins only.

  1. From the homepage open your account profile, then Settings, then Permissions.

  2. Open the Share and publish tab.

  3. For Who can share designs externally via links and Who can share designs to external emails, choose Everyone, Team admins and team brand designers, Team admins only, or No one.

  4. Optionally add domains to the allowlist to enable private embeds on secured domains.

Restricted team members can still share with peers in the same brand, but cannot create a public view link, create a website, share by email, copy the design to another team, use Q&A on presentations, use embeds, or use present and record. Admins and brand admins can still share their designs externally, and the members can see that the design is shared but not the public view links created. For LMS sharing in Education, restriction means it only works between teachers and students of the same school. Who may switch the restriction off depends on whether the organisation has one school or team, or several; the request goes through an admin.

Source: Canva Help Center: Restrict design sharing

Secure sharing outside the organisation

For clients, vendors and partners outside an organisation, the Help Center describes a workflow for Canva Enterprise users. Share a link with Anyone with the link set to Can view or Can comment, or invite people by email and give Can edit only to those who need it. Enterprise adds two options: restricting links to approved email domains, so that only trusted partners can open a forwarded link, and protecting a PDF download with a password under Share, Download, PDF Standard or PDF Print, Protect PDF with a password.

External users cannot access your workspace, teams or Brand Kits, and administrators can restrict or turn off external sharing. To revoke access, use the share settings and Revoke all access. Note the limits: public links can be forwarded unless domain restrictions apply, Can view links show real-time design updates, and a static export (PNG, JPG or PDF) is the right choice for one-way sharing. If you shared through a public link and want to drop one person, revoke all access and invite only the people you want. If Anyone with the link is missing, an administrator may have disabled it in Brand Controls. The administrator side is covered in Administration, Enterprise and Security and Brand Templates, Controls and Approvals.

Source: Canva Help Center: Securely sharing designs outside your organization

Design insights

Once a design is shared or embedded, the Analytics item in the top menu (some Help Center articles still call it Insights) shows how people interacted with it. The Help Center article says these metrics are available for Canva Pro, Canva Teams, Canva for Education and Canva Nonprofits; check your account for other plans. A visit counts only while someone actively views the design, and reopening a hidden tab starts a new visit.

Section What it reports

Activity: visitor activity

Total unique visitors, visitors with their access level and last viewed time (names only for team members), total visits and daily unique visits

Activity: time spent

Average time on the design and on each page

Activity: page performance

Top-performing pages, average pages visited and pages visited by percentage

External traffic

Public view links created, visitors per named link, last visit time, and for social posts impressions, clicks, reactions and engagements

Source: Canva Help Center: Viewing design analytics and insights

Pitfalls

  • Edit access for Anyone with the link lets unsigned-in guests change the design; choose Can comment or Can view unless you trust everyone who may receive the link.

  • A view or watch link can be forwarded, and its permissions cannot be changed after it was shared; delete the public view link, or revoke access, to stop it.

  • Hidden pages, cropped images, trimmed videos and presenter notes remain reachable; delete them, or share a clean copy, before public sharing.

  • Restricting sharing affects new designs only; earlier external shares remain reachable.

  • Remove access is permanent and silent: nobody is told, and you must re-add people by hand.

  • On Canva Pro you cannot invite people to a team while sharing.
