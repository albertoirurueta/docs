Uploads, Stock Media and Imports

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers every way that content other than your own typing gets into Canva: files uploaded from a device, files imported from cloud storage and other apps, the stock library of photos, video and audio, and the converters that turn a PDF, a PowerPoint deck, a Figma export or an Illustrator file into an editable design. These features belong to the Canva editor on the web, the desktop app and the mobile apps. Uploading and the stock library are generally available on every plan (Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits), with storage, premium content, fonts and tags differing by plan as described below. The PowerPoint, Illustrator and Photoshop converters are documented as beta releases that work only in a desktop browser. None of this page depends on an AI tier, although Smart Tags and some search features use AI to suggest results. Plans and limits change; see Canva pricing and Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance.

The running example is the Bookshelf launch campaign. By the end of this page the campaign has its cover images, logo and promo clip uploaded and tagged, the Figma frames from the design sub-section imported as an editable design, and a flyer that began as a PDF reopened for editing. Organising these files afterwards is covered in Projects, Folders and File Management, and editing the photos in Photos and Image Editing.

Where content comes from

Every piece of media in a design arrives by one of three routes. You upload it from your device, you import it from cloud storage or another app, or you pick it from the stock library in the Elements tab. The first two end up in your account, under the Uploads tab of the editor and the Uploads folder of Projects, and they count toward your storage limit. The third stays in Canva’s library and is licensed to you, with rules that depend on the item and your plan.

Figure 1. The three routes into an account and the two outcomes of an upload

An uploaded file is private. Only you can access it; to share it with a team, add it to a folder and share the folder (see Sharing and Permissions). If the upload button is missing or disabled, a team restriction may be the cause, and the team admin can lift it or update your role.

Original or converted

What Canva does with a file depends on its type. Images, video, audio, fonts, Affinity files and files such as ZIP, INDD and HTML are stored as originals: you can organise, download or share them, although Canva cannot open or edit ZIP, INDD or HTML files yet. PDF, PPTX and CSV files are converted into Canva designs, so the original file is not kept; you export the design to PDF, PNG or MP4 instead. Every file counts toward storage, whether it is stored as an original, converted to a design or both. For an Affinity file you can use Open in editor from the preview to create an editable design while the original stays stored.

Uploading media from your device

Open the Uploads tab in the editor side panel and choose Upload files, or drop files straight onto the tab. To upload a folder, open the menu beside Upload files and choose Upload folder (not available on mobile). On the homepage you can drag a file onto the page, use the Upload button on the banner, or use Projects and Add new. After an upload finishes, select the file to place it on the page; the Uploads folder in Projects, reached through the Owner dropdown set to your name or Any owner, lists everything you have uploaded and offers download and delete.

Large batches can cause errors, so split them. Files with unsupported embedded images should be fixed or converted first, and a folder should be checked against the format limits before it is uploaded.

Storage

Storage differs by subscription: Free accounts have 5 GB, Pro accounts 100 GB, Business accounts 500 GB per team and Enterprise accounts 1 to 5 TB per team, according to the upload and format articles. The upload article also lists a 1 TB figure for the legacy Teams plan. You cannot check the remaining space, but a Free account is notified when it passes 80 percent. Business replaced Teams for new sign-ups on 2025-10-30.

Organising uploads and finding them again

Folders inside the Uploads tab keep uploads tidy: open the Folders tab, choose Create folder, name it, and optionally share it with your team. Move a file by hovering over it, opening its menu and choosing Move; in a folder you can also add designs or upload straight into it. Manage uploads shows how to download one file or bulk-delete several; a deleted upload goes to Trash (see Projects, Folders and File Management).

Smart Tags help you search. For an image, Details offers Add tags for your own words and shows AI-suggested Smart Tags that you can edit or remove; videos accept tags but no Smart Tags. The article limits tags to Pro, Teams, Nonprofits and Education accounts. Search by tag from the homepage search bar, choosing Projects so that results show your own media. Keyword search of private media is documented for Pro, Teams, Enterprise, Nonprofits and Education accounts in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand, so availability depends on region.

Procedure: upload the Bookshelf cover images

The campaign needs three book covers, the Bookshelf logo and a short promo clip, all used by later pages.

Figure 2. Uploading and tagging the Bookshelf covers

  1. Open the Bookshelf "Book of the week" Instagram post and select the Uploads tab in the side panel.

  2. Choose Upload files, select the cover images, the logo SVG and the promo clip, and confirm. You can also drag them from a folder onto the tab.

  3. Wait for the progress bars under each file to finish; the files then appear under Images, Videos or Audio.

  4. Hover over a cover, open its menu, choose Details, then Add tags and enter "book" and "cover". Save.

  5. Select a cover to place it on the page, or drag it onto an image frame to replace the placeholder.

Source: Canva Help Center: Upload media from your device

Importing from cloud storage and apps

Cloud files can be added without downloading them first. In the editor, open Apps, choose a source, select Open, connect your account when prompted and pick the image or video. From the homepage, choose Projects, then Add new and Import for Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive. The Help Center lists Facebook, Instagram, Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, Box, HubSpot, SharePoint and Acquia DAM (Widen) as import sources, and some of these apps are built by third-party developers whose terms and privacy policies apply. You can revoke access in Settings.

Whole folders can be imported from Google Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive with the folder structure preserved: up to 300 items, 50 folders and 10 nested levels per import, with file names up to 200 characters. The import continues in the background if you close the browser. A missing app is usually a team or organisation setting. Separately, Canva AI can take files from Google, Dropbox and OneDrive as context for a prompt. A Chrome extension also saves images from the web to Canva.

Source: Canva Help Center: Upload media from cloud storage or apps and Canva Help Center: Upload multiple files and folders from Google Drive, Dropbox, and OneDrive

Stock photos, video and audio

The Elements tab holds Canva’s library of images, video, audio and graphics; the image article describes adding an image by searching or browsing Elements and selecting it. Free items are available to everyone. Premium items carry a crown icon, and the Help Center says an extended library of premium images is free with Canva Pro, Teams, Education or Nonprofits accounts. Items you can generate with Canva AI are covered in AI Photo Tools.

How licences work

Figure 3. How free items, premium elements and Pro audio are licensed when you download

  • Premium elements show watermarks until they are purchased. You can download the design to buy all of them, or select Remove watermarks on one element. They are bought with a Pro Content licence, and using them in several designs means a new licence for each use unless your plan includes the extended library.

  • Pro audio is marked with a crown in the Audio tab. When you download a design that contains it, Canva creates a licence for that design, which covers business use including videos on YouTube, Facebook and Instagram. Each design generates one licence, so reusing a track in another video means File, Make a copy, and downloading the copy. Connect the social account before you download to prevent automatic copyright claims.

  • Popular music is a separate library, limited to personal and educational use, with tracks previewing for 30 seconds and playing for 60 seconds in a design; it is not available on Free accounts and some other plans, and it depends on region.

  • Your own music is fingerprint-checked. If it infringes copyright, you cannot publish the design with audio.

  • Editorial use of Canva images (for example in a news article) requires crediting the creators.

Audio is added from the Audio tab (or Elements, then Audio), where you can layer up to 50 tracks, mute or delete tracks and trim volume in the timeline; see Video Editor, Audio and Captions. The legal terms are in the Content License Agreement; read it for what each licence permits.

Source: Canva Help Center: Add images to designs, Canva Help Center: Premium elements, Canva Help Center: Purchase and get licenses for Pro audio tracks and Canva Help Center: Add audio tracks and sound effects

Bulk photo edits

The Help Center groups photo editing under "Edit and enhance images": image settings, filters, effects, instant crop, expand, blur, Auto Focus, shadows, AI upscale and colour restore. Each of these is applied to the selected image or video in the editor; the Help Center describes no one-click batch editor for an entire set of uploads. For the Bookshelf covers, the efficient pattern is to build one design, replace the image in each frame with Replace, and use Bulk Create for repeated layouts (see Templates, Layouts and Magic Design). Details of each adjustment are in Photos and Image Editing.

Source: Canva Help Center: Edit and enhance images

PDF files

Drop a PDF on the homepage or editor, use the Upload button, or choose File, Upload files. A PDF editor or converter page on the Canva site does the same. Canva keeps the original layout as close as possible and makes supported text and images editable. A scanned PDF becomes one flattened image, so its text cannot be edited, unsupported fonts are replaced by similar ones, and a PDF over the limits may import incompletely. Brand Controls and team permissions can restrict image replacement or editing. After editing, Share, Download offers PDF Standard, PDF Print, PNG or JPG, and MP4 if the design contains video. An older line on the formats page says PDF pages become single images in an A4 document; the dedicated PDF article describes the newer editable import and is the one followed here.

Procedure: edit the Bookshelf flyer PDF

Figure 5. Reopening a flyer PDF for editing

  1. Start from the exported Bookshelf flyer PDF.

  2. Drag it onto the Canva homepage, or choose Upload on the homepage banner, then Upload files.

  3. Wait for the import to finish and open the imported design.

  4. Select the title text box and change the font, size or spacing in the toolbar; drag a new cover from Uploads onto the old image to replace it.

  5. Choose Share, then Download, pick PDF Print and download the corrected flyer.

Source: Canva Help Center: Import and edit PDFs in Canva

PowerPoint and Google Slides

PowerPoint import works on canva.com in a desktop browser and is described as an early beta. It accepts .pptx and .ppt files (limits in the figure), up to 300 slides and 1,400 elements and images per file. Charts, SmartArt, 3D objects and WordArt are ignored, and a scanned deck becomes a flat image. Fonts are matched when possible, so text may look different. To import a Google Slides deck, connect Google Drive under Projects, Add new, Import; the presentation appears as a Document, and uploading it makes a design.

Source: Canva Help Center: Import PowerPoint presentations and Canva Help Center: Import Google Slides presentations

Figma files

The recommended path is the Figma to Canva app, a free third-party app from PixelTinkers. In Figma, run the Export to Canva plugin, sign in with Canva, generate a pairing code, then paste it in the Figma to Canva app under Apps in Canva. Select the frames in Figma, export, and open the export from Recent exports in Canva; you may be asked to match Figma fonts to Canva fonts. Pairing codes are valid for 24 hours. The app aims for editability over pixel fidelity, so some complex elements arrive as images. Manually, export frames as PNG or SVG and upload them, or export a FigJam board as a PDF. The Figma side is described in Importing and Migrating.

Procedure: import the Bookshelf frames from Figma

Figure 6. Moving Figma frames into Canva with the Figma to Canva app

  1. In the Figma file that holds the Bookshelf design, open Plugins, search for Export to Canva and sign in with Canva.

  2. In the plugin, select Get connection code; in Canva open Apps, search for Figma to Canva and paste the code.

  3. In Figma, select the home and detail frames and choose Export slides.

  4. Select Open in Canva app, then pick the export under Recent exports.

  5. Match each Figma font to a Canva font if asked, then continue. The design opens in the editor.

Source: Canva Help Center: Import Figma files

Illustrator, Photoshop, Lottie and Markdown

Illustrator (.ai saved as PDF compatible) and Photoshop (.psd) files import as designs in a beta feature limited to desktop browsers. Layers, gradients and masks are supported, although some elements may be rasterised, and up to 100 fonts are imported. Lottie files are dropped onto the homepage, or imported from File, Import files in the editor; Lottie is a JSON-based animation format. Markdown (.md) files up to 5 MB open as Canva Docs only, some elements may not import as expected, and a Doc cannot be exported back to Markdown. A TXT file becomes a Doc or a Sheet, decided by Canva from its content.

Source: Canva Help Center: Import Adobe Illustrator or Photoshop files, Canva Help Center: Import Lottie files in Canva and Canva Help Center: Import Markdown files

Pitfalls

  • A design with many images has a documented ceiling of 1,400 images, and imports over 1,400 elements can be incomplete.

  • A PDF or deck from a scan cannot be edited as text; recreate it with Canva text.

  • The default All projects view on the homepage shows folders, designs and Brand Templates, so a fresh upload may need the Type filter to appear.

  • Premium audio licences are per design, and a copied design is needed for a second video.

  • Fonts must permit embedding or the upload may fail.
