Grafana: Sharing and Reporting

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Checked on 2026-11-09 against Grafana 13.3.x.

To do this by hand, follow Reuse, Versions and Sharing.

A dashboard is only useful if the right people can see the right view at the right time, and "sharing" covers very different needs. An engineer pastes a link into the incident channel; a manager wants a weekly PDF; an operations room wants a wall display; a customer wants a read-only status page; a vendor wants a screenshot of a spike. Each need maps to a different Grafana feature with different security consequences, and a wrong choice either blocks the need or leaks data. This page lists the options, what each exposes, and how to verify them in the lab. Who may do what inside Grafana is covered by Users, Teams and Permissions.

flowchart TD q["Who needs to see it?"] --> a["People in the organisation"] q --> b["Anyone, read-only"] q --> c["A frozen moment in time"] q --> d["Scheduled recipients"] q --> e["A wall display"] a --> a1["Internal link (lock time range, short URL)"] b --> b1["Externally shared dashboard (Share externally)"] c --> c1["Snapshot (local or external)"] d --> d1["Report: PDF, CSV (Enterprise or Cloud)"] e --> e1["Playlist in kiosk mode"] a --> a2["Embed in another site (iframe)"] a --> a3["Export as code or image"]

Source: Grafana: Share dashboards and panels.

Internal links

Share > Share internally produces a link for users who already have access to the dashboard. Options are Lock time range (on by default, so the recipient sees what you saw), Shorten link (on by default) and a theme (current, dark or light). The Share button itself copies a shortened link that keeps your saved customisations, which are stored in the browser. Save dashboard changes before sharing; a link points at the saved dashboard, not at your unsaved edit. In an incident the locked time range matters more than anything else: a link without it opens on "last 6 hours" and the evidence has scrolled away.

Source: Share an internal link.

Externally shared dashboards

Externally shared dashboards (formerly called public dashboards) let people without a Grafana account view a dashboard read-only. They are created from Share > Share externally and listed under Dashboards > Shared dashboards, and a Public label appears in the dashboard header once shared. Creating one needs the Admin role, the dashboards.public:write permission or the fixed:dashboards.public:write fixed role.

Access options. Anyone with the link needs you to confirm the checkbox and accept; whoever has the URL can view it. Specific people is available in Grafana Enterprise and Grafana Cloud and is in private preview: each invitee gets a one-time link valid for one hour, after which access lasts 30 days, and re-inviting a viewer invalidates their earlier links. Enable time range lets viewers change the range and Display annotations shows annotations. Pause access blocks viewers but keeps the link, Resume access restores it, and Revoke access ends it (a new link is needed to share again).

What does not work. The page lists the limits, and each one is a design decision to check against your dashboard: only the queries stored in the dashboard run (no arbitrary queries); variables and queries that include variables are not supported; panels that use frontend data sources fail; exemplars are omitted; annotations work only for the -- Grafana -- data source with the Annotations & Alerts query, and tag-based annotations return matches from all dashboards in the organisation; Grafana Live, library panels and data sources that use reverse-proxy functionality are unsupported. Prometheus, Loki, Elasticsearch, PostgreSQL, MySQL, InfluxDB and CloudWatch are among the data sources confirmed to work; Tempo and Graphite are listed as unsupported.

Risk. Every view of a shared dashboard runs queries against your data sources, and the page documents no rate limit; use caching and rate limiting (Enterprise) or place a static status page in front of anything that could be popular. Share dashboards that contain only information that is acceptable to publish: label values, route names and service names often reveal more than expected. Review Dashboards > Shared dashboards regularly and revoke what is no longer needed. The Shared dashboard users tab (Administration > Users and access > Users) shows people who were invited and lets you revoke access to all shared dashboards for a user.

Source: Grafana: Externally shared dashboards.

Snapshots

A snapshot is an instantaneous, sanitised copy of the visible metrics: queries are stripped and the data is frozen, so it is ideal for attaching evidence to an incident report or a bug report. Choose Share > Snapshot, set the expiration (1 hour, 1 day, 1 week or never) and publish locally with Publish snapshot or to snapshots.raintank.io. The external option is disabled by default in Grafana Cloud and can be switched on or off in the configuration; snapshots with custom panels cannot be published externally. Dashboards > Snapshots lists the organisation’s snapshots; to delete one, open it with View and click the red x (deletion is immediate).

[snapshots]
enabled = true
external_enabled = false

enabled switches the feature off entirely when false, external_enabled controls the external publish endpoint (default true), external_snapshot_url can point to your own Grafana instance acting as snapshot server (public_mode = true on that instance allows unauthenticated create and delete requests, which is a real exposure) and external_snapshot_name renames the button. For most teams the safe default is to keep local snapshots and turn off the external endpoint.

Source: Share a snapshot and Configure Grafana, snapshots.

Panel links and embedding

A panel’s menu offers Share link (lock time range, shorten link, theme, and optional PNG generation with width, height and scale), Share embed (an iframe whose HTML can be edited before copying) and Share snapshot. An embedded panel is an iframe such as:

<iframe
  src="https://grafana.example.org/d-solo/bookshelf-service-health/bookshelf-service-health?panelId=1&from=now-6h&to=now"
  width="650"
  height="300"
  frameborder="0"
></iframe>

Viewers must be signed in unless anonymous access is enabled (Enterprise and OSS only, not Grafana Cloud). Panel embedding is itself not available in Grafana Cloud. Grafana refuses to be framed by default: the allow_embedding setting in the [security] section is false, which sets the X-Frame-Options: deny header to mitigate clickjacking, so set it to true on a self-managed instance that must be embedded, and understand the trade-off (anonymous access plus embedding exposes the dashboard to any page that frames it).

Source: Share dashboards and panels (the embedding and panel sharing sections) and Configure Grafana, allow_embedding.

Export as code

Exporting a dashboard as code produces Classic JSON or as the V2 Resource model (JSON or YAML), with an option to strip instance-specific details so the file can be imported elsewhere; the formats and the import side are described in Library Panels, JSON Model and Versions. A dashboard can be published to the grafana.com catalogue from a Grafana Cloud account, exported with the Classic model.

Source: Export a dashboard as code.

Images and the Image Renderer service

PNG export, the panel image option and server-side render URLs need the Grafana Image Renderer, which runs as a separate service (the old plugin is deprecated and no longer updated, and Grafana 13.0 removed plugin support). Run it as a container next to Grafana and point Grafana at it:

services:
  renderer:
    image: grafana/grafana-image-renderer:latest

  grafana:
    image: grafana/grafana-enterprise:latest
    ports:
      - "3000:3000"
    environment:
      GF_RENDERING_SERVER_URL: http://renderer:8081/render
      GF_RENDERING_CALLBACK_URL: http://grafana:3000/

Pin a specific renderer version in production rather than latest. The renderer launches a browser, so size it generously: the documentation asks for at least 16 GiB of memory and 4 CPU cores for the service. A render URL (/render/d-solo/<uid>?panelId=1&width=1000&height=500&tz=UTC) takes width and height (default 1000 by 500 pixels, which are also the minimums on Grafana Cloud), scale (higher gives higher DPI) and timeout (default 30 seconds, to be raised for slow queries). Viewing a rendered image needs a signed-in, authorised user.

Source: Grafana: Set up image rendering and Export a dashboard as an image.

PDF and scheduled reports

Export as PDF and Reports are Grafana Enterprise features (reporting is generally available in Grafana Cloud; on self-managed Enterprise the redesigned experience needs the newShareReportDrawer feature toggle). A PDF has an orientation (landscape or portrait), a layout (Grid matches the dashboard, Simple gives full-width panels) and a zoom. Reports are created under Dashboards > Reporting or Share > Schedule report, in five sections: dashboards (with optional time range and variable values that override the dashboard’s), schedule (once, hourly, daily, weekly, monthly or custom; optional end date; weekday-only option), email settings (subject, message, reply-to, embedded images and links), recipients (comma or semicolon separated) and attachments (PDF, and a CSV per table panel; a separate PDF of table data is in public preview behind the pdfTables toggle).

Operational points that decide whether reports work:

  • Prerequisites. SMTP must be configured, and the Image Renderer service must be set up.

  • Time zone. The report uses the dashboard’s time zone; a dashboard set to browser time uses the server’s, so save the dashboard with a fixed time zone.

  • Variables. A query variable saved in a report can become stale; select All or keep the original selection so the report stays current.

  • Size. Attachments above 10 MB are not sent by default; the limit is max_attachment_size_mb. Temporary files live under the csv, pdf and png folders of the data directory and are cleaned by a job that runs every 10 minutes (temp_data_lifetime).

  • Permissions. By default only organisation admins create reports; with RBAC enabled users need the reporting permissions.

  • Troubleshooting. Enable debug logging under [log] with the filters rendering, report.api, report.render, report.scheduler, report.sender and report.service.

A scheduled report is a good fit for management summaries and capacity reviews and a poor one for operational alerting: it arrives at a fixed time and tells nobody about a fault that began an hour later. Use alert rules for that, as in Designing Actionable Alerts.

Source: Grafana: Create reports and Export a dashboard as PDF.

Playlists

A playlist cycles through dashboards in a fixed order, for a wall display or a presentation. Creating and editing needs at least the Editor role. In Dashboards > Playlists choose New playlist, give it a name and an interval (how long each dashboard shows), add dashboards by title or by tag (the order added is the play order) and save. Using tags keeps a playlist current as dashboards come and go. When you start a playlist you choose a mode and whether to show Time and refresh, Variables and Dashboard links:

Mode Behaviour

Normal

Main menu and navigation bar stay visible, dashboard controls are hidden, playlist controls show at the top.

Normal (auto fit panels)

As Normal, with panels resized to use the screen.

Kiosk

Menu, navigation bar and dashboard controls are hidden.

Kiosk (auto fit panels)

As Kiosk, with panels resized to use the screen.

Esc stops the playlist (in kiosk modes it first shows the controls briefly). The share icon of a playlist copies a link for the chosen view mode. For a wall display, use a dedicated low-privilege user or an externally shared dashboard rather than an administrator session left logged in, set a refresh interval that the data sources can sustain, and prefer a handful of overview dashboards built as in Dashboard Design Principles.

Source: Grafana: Create and manage playlists.

Usage insights

In Grafana Enterprise and Grafana Cloud, usage insights record dashboard views (aggregated and per user), data source errors and data source queries. The dashboard insights icon shows queries and errors per day and views per day for the last 30 days, last activities and up to 20 recent users; the Insights tab of a data source shows queries, errors and average query load time per day. A presence indicator shows avatars of users who recently interacted with the dashboard, by default within the last 10 minutes (recent_users_age in [analytics.views], 0 disables it). Dashboard search can sort by errors and views (total and last 30 days), computed once a day. Logs can be exported to Loki and visualised with the prebuilt "Usage Insights overview" (ID 13785) and "Data source details" (ID 13786) dashboards.

For monitoring work, the insights answer two questions: which dashboards nobody opens (candidates for deletion, in the spirit of the pruning advice in Dashboard Design Principles) and which dashboards produce errors or heavy query load (candidates for tuning, or for a recording rule).

Source: Grafana: Assess dashboard usage.

What can go wrong

  • A shared dashboard shows nothing. It uses variables, a frontend data source or an unsupported data source; rebuild it without them.

  • A shared dashboard shows other teams' events. A tag-based annotation query returns annotations from the whole organisation.

  • Embedding shows a blank frame. allow_embedding is false, or the viewer is not signed in and anonymous access is off.

  • Images are blank or time out. The renderer is unreachable from Grafana (check GF_RENDERING_SERVER_URL and the callback URL, which the renderer uses to call back into Grafana) or the queries need more than the default 30 seconds.

  • Reports never arrive. SMTP or the renderer is missing, or the attachment is above the size limit; enable the report log filters.

  • A snapshot keeps living. Its expiration was set to never, or it was published externally and has to be deleted there.

  • A playlist wall stops updating. The kiosk browser session expired or the user lost access.

Verify in the lab

  1. Use Share > Share internally on "Bookshelf service health" with the time range locked and open the link in a private window after signing in with another user.

  2. Share the dashboard externally with Anyone with the link only after removing variable-dependent panels, open the link signed out, then Pause access and confirm it stops working.

  3. Take a local snapshot with a one-hour expiry and confirm it shows under Dashboards > Snapshots.

  4. Add the renderer service from the compose snippet above, then download a PNG of a panel.

  5. Create a playlist from two tagged dashboards and start it in kiosk mode.

  6. On Enterprise or Cloud, schedule a report to a test mailbox, and open the dashboard insights.

In the books

Salituro (Learn Grafana 10.x) shows sharing a dashboard link, exporting a dashboard and publishing a snapshot (chapter 10), and building, displaying and editing playlists in normal, TV and kiosk modes (chapter 14). Since then public dashboards were renamed externally shared dashboards and gained access options and limits, the playlist modes were reduced to normal and kiosk with optional auto fit, the image renderer plugin was removed in favour of a separate service, reports were redesigned, and usage insights were added to Enterprise and Cloud. The books are right about the principle (a snapshot is a copy, a shared link is access), but follow the current pages for steps.
