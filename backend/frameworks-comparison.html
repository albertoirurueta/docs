Backend Frameworks Comparison

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page compares 48 backend frameworks on the same metrics: the frameworks documented on this site, plus others that matter because they are popular, because they are growing, or for historical context. Every number carries a source and a date, and anything that could not be sourced is shown as n/a instead of being estimated. The data snapshot date is 2026-10-27: all sources were read on that day, and each caption names the edition or measurement date of its data. Downloads, survey shares and stars change every month, so treat the figures as a dated snapshot.

The raw data (CSV files), the chart and table generators and the scoring rubric are kept in the repository under scripts/framework-charts/; npm run charts:frameworks regenerates every figure and table on this page. The view layers of full-stack frameworks (Razor and Blazor, Django templates, Blade, Livewire and Inertia, Thymeleaf) are compared on the Web Frameworks Comparison; language-level metrics (indices, benchmarks of the languages themselves, salaries) are on the Programming Languages Comparative. Jump to the Summary and recommendations if you only need the conclusions.

Scope and inclusion criteria

A framework is included when at least one of these holds:

  1. It has its own section on this site: Spring Boot, Quarkus, Jakarta EE (through the JSF and JSP and Struts sections), ASP.NET Core, ASP.NET MVC 5 and Web API 2, ASP.NET Web Forms, Express and Fastify (in Node.js), NestJS, Django, FastAPI, Flask, Tornado, Pyramid, Laravel, axum and Vapor.

  2. It appears in the framework lists of the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025, State of JS 2025 or the JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2023, or ranks in its registry’s downloads: Micronaut, Helidon, Ktor, Vert.x, Javalin and Play (JVM); Hono, Koa, AdonisJS and Elysia (Node.js and Bun); Litestar, Sanic, Django REST framework and Django Ninja (Python); Symfony, Slim and CodeIgniter (PHP); Actix Web, Rocket and Loco (Rust); Hummingbird (Swift); Ruby on Rails, Gin, Echo, Fiber, Go net/http as the baseline, and Phoenix (Elixir).

  3. It matters for history: ASP.NET Web Forms, Apache Struts, J2EE and EJB 2, Zend Framework and Laminas, CakePHP.

Frameworks without a section on this site get fewer metrics where data is scarce: the feature matrix, the deployment-target coverage and some concurrency rows are only assessed for the documented ones. Runtimes (Node.js, Deno, Bun) and content-management systems (WordPress, Drupal) appear in some surveys but are not frameworks and are excluded; WordPress is documented in WordPress, Drupal in Drupal, and they are compared with other CMSs on the CMS Comparison. Every framework name links to its section on this site, or to its official website when the site has no section.

How metrics, scales and scores were assigned

Metrics. A value is recorded only when it was read in the source named for it; otherwise it is n/a and charts draw a gap or an "n/a" label, never a zero. Radar scores (0 to 10, higher is better). Each radar axis is either computed ©, a formula over the data, or editorial (E), a judgement with a written basis per framework. Scores are relative to the frameworks on this page: 10 is the best in this comparison, not a theoretical maximum, and an axis without inputs is n/a.

  • Popularity ©: mean of the survey usage relative to the most used framework, registry downloads on a log scale within one registry (a framework on several registries is scored in the one with the most frameworks on this page), and GitHub stars on a log scale.

  • Growth ©: change in survey usage from 2023 to 2025, in npm downloads and Packagist downloads year over year, and in State of JS usage, each scaled between the slowest and fastest grower.

  • Talent pool and jobs ©: professional usage in the survey and Hacker News job-posting share, relative to the best framework.

  • Ease of use (E): concepts to learn before the first endpoint, configuration needed, conventions, and quality of the official tutorial, supported by the admired and retention scores.

  • Ecosystem and maturity (E): age, release cadence, support policy and LTS, governance, extension ecosystem and official integrations.

  • Productivity (E): batteries included (the feature matrix), scaffolding and hot reload.

  • Throughput ©: TechEmpower round 23 fortunes, 10 × (1 − log10(best / rps) / 2); 100 times slower than the best scores 0.

  • Memory and Startup ©: from the like-for-like Quarkus comparison only, JIT variant; 10 times more than the best scores 0.

Registry downloads count CI runs, mirrors and caches, not people, and the units of the registries differ (npm counts per tarball, Packagist per install, PyPI without mirrors). They are therefore only compared within one registry, never on one axis. The full formulas, the rubric and the method of every dataset are in scripts/framework-charts/data/NOTES.md.

Overview and timeline

The oldest frameworks still in wide use date from the late 2000s: Django, Flask and Tornado reached PyPI in 2010, Express in 2010 and Ruby on Rails on RubyGems in 2009. The JVM frameworks built for containers (Quarkus, Micronaut, Helidon) arrived in 2018 and 2019, and the newest frameworks here are Hono (2021), Elysia (2022), Litestar (2.0 in 2023) and Loco (2023). Registry dates are the first publication on the registry, which for older projects can be later than the first release; the timeline uses them so that every date has a source.

Popularity

Three independent signals are used: what developers say they use (surveys), what build systems download (registries) and what developers star (GitHub). In the Stack Overflow survey 2025, the most used backend frameworks are Express (19.9% of respondents), ASP.NET Core (19.7%), FastAPI (14.8%), Spring Boot (14.7%) and Flask (14.4%); classic ASP.NET still has 14.2%. The highest admired scores belong to the less used Phoenix (79%) and axum (76.4%), followed by ASP.NET Core (61.3%) and FastAPI (55.5%).

The JetBrains survey asks each language community separately, so its shares only compare frameworks of the same language: 72% of Java developers use Spring Boot, against 4% for Quarkus and 2% for Micronaut; 61% of PHP developers use Laravel and 21% Symfony; Django (40%) and Flask (38%) are close among Python developers in 2023, with FastAPI at 25% (20% in 2022).

Figure 4. Framework use within each language community. Source: JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2023.

For Node.js frameworks, State of JS 2025 adds usage among JavaScript developers: Express is used by 80.8% of the respondents to that question, NestJS by 32%, Fastify by 20.2% and Hono by 15.5%.

Registry downloads must be read within one registry. On npm, Express had 3,513 million downloads in the 12 months to 2026-06, Hono 841 million, NestJS 362 million, Koa 301 million and Fastify 237 million. Maven Central publishes no download counts, so JVM frameworks have no registry signal; .NET’s ASP.NET Core ships with the SDK, so its NuGet counts are a proxy.

Ease of use and learning curve

Ease of use is an editorial score (rubric above, basis per framework in the radar tables). Micro-frameworks (Flask, Express, Hono, Gin) need the fewest concepts before the first endpoint; convention-driven full-stack frameworks (Ruby on Rails, Laravel, Django) need more concepts but guide every step; frameworks built on dependency injection and modules (Spring Boot, NestJS) and on new runtime models (axum, Actix Web, Phoenix) take longest to learn. Supporting signals are the admired scores (Phoenix 79%, axum 76.4%, ASP.NET Core 61.3%) and State of JS retention (Hono 95%, Elysia 91.1%, Fastify 86.5%). The site’s own comparisons go deeper for some pairs: Quarkus versus Spring Boot, Express and Fastify (and the Express.js section), axum and the other Rust frameworks, Vapor and other Swift servers and choosing an ASP.NET framework.

Concurrency model

The concurrency model decides what happens when a handler blocks on I/O or computes for a long time. Thread-per-request stacks (Spring MVC, Struts) can switch to Java virtual threads (Spring Boot concurrency alternatives); event-loop stacks (Node.js frameworks, Vert.x, Quarkus’s reactive core) must never block the loop (Node.js concurrency models); async Python (FastAPI async and concurrency) and Rust frameworks run async handlers on an executor; PHP runs one request per worker process; Go starts a goroutine per request and Phoenix one BEAM process.

Summary and recommendations

These recommendations are an editorial judgement built on the data above; each cites the metric it relies on. The snapshot date is 2026-10-27, and surveys and downloads change every month, so re-check the numbers before a decision that will last years.

Safe mainstream choices, by language.

  • JVM: Spring Boot. 72% of Java developers use it (JetBrains 2023), 14.7% of all survey respondents (2025), the most complete feature matrix and support dates per minor version. Choose Quarkus when startup time and memory matter (574 ms and 98 MiB native, vendor-reported).

  • .NET: ASP.NET Core. 19.7% survey usage, 61.3% admired, top-tier TechEmpower throughput and LTS releases.

  • Python: Django for full applications, FastAPI for APIs. Django has an admin, ORM and LTS releases; FastAPI doubled its survey share between 2023 and 2025 and generates OpenAPI from type hints.

  • PHP: Laravel. 61% of PHP developers (JetBrains 2023), rising Packagist downloads, yearly majors with dated support.

  • Node.js: NestJS for structured services, Fastify for lean ones (in Node.js). Express (see the Express.js section) remains the most downloaded but provides the least out of the box.

Trends to watch. The growth data support three: FastAPI (survey share 7.42% to 14.8%, 2023 to 2025), Hono (841 million npm downloads in 12 months, 95% State of JS retention) and ASP.NET Core (16.57% to 19.7%). axum and Phoenix are niche (2.8% and 2.4% usage) but the most admired frameworks in the 2025 survey.

Avoid for new projects, keep for legacy maintenance.

  • ASP.NET Web Forms and ASP.NET MVC 5 run on .NET Framework (current cycle 4.8.1 on endoflife.date), not on modern .NET; MVC 5’s last NuGet release is 5.3.0 (2023-10-23) and classic ASP.NET’s survey usage fell from 26.3% (2019) to 14.2% (2025). See choosing an ASP.NET framework.

  • Apache Struts has the fourth-highest advisory count on this page (60) and 1,370 GitHub stars; new Java web applications fit Spring Boot better.

  • J2EE and EJB 2 are historical (their successor is Jakarta EE), and Zend Framework continues as Laminas.

Hiring perspective. The largest talent pools are ASP.NET Core, Express, Spring Boot, FastAPI and Flask (the highest professional survey usage); Ruby on Rails, FastAPI and Django lead the 2025 Hacker News postings. Niche but growing choices (Hono, axum, Phoenix, Litestar) are easier to sell to developers than to staff quickly: plan for training.

The strongest conclusion is the least surprising one: for most teams the right framework is the most used one of the language the team already knows, and the metrics above matter mainly at the edges (startup-sensitive serverless work, extreme throughput, hiring in a small market).
