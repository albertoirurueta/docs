A WooCommerce store is a WordPress site plus about a dozen decisions: where you sell and in which currency, whether prices
include tax, which zones you ship to and what it costs, how customers pay, which e-mails they get, what the shop looks like
and what you do every week to keep it safe. This page walks through those decisions in the order that avoids rework, for
*WooCommerce 11.2.1* on *WordPress 7.1.3* (checked on 2026-10-27), using the Settings screens as the reference and WP-CLI
to make the result repeatable. It covers the free plugin and the payment, tax and shipping features it ships with; paid
extensions are named only to say what core cannot do. Where a step needs a WordPress.org, WordPress.com or Woo account,
or a payment provider's onboarding (WooPayments, PayPal, Stripe), the page says so and does not run it.

Everything shown with a command was run on a clean WordPress 7.1.3 site with WooCommerce 11.2.1 (see
xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/installation-and-local-environments.adoc[Installation and Local Environments] for how the
site was built), and the complete script of the *Pocket Coffee* shop was run end to end on a second clean site. Pocket
Coffee is the running example of the section: single-origin beans in 18 variants, a starter kit, a Coffee Club discount,
two shipping zones and euro prices that include Spanish VAT. The sibling pages go deeper into each area:

== Decide before you click

The Settings screens ask for facts that are hard to change once orders exist, so settle these first. They are generic
store-planning questions (the books about WooCommerce spend most of their pages on them) and the Pocket Coffee answers
show what a decision looks like.

[cols="2,4,3",options=header]
|===
|Decision |Why it matters later |Pocket Coffee

|Selling and shipping countries
|Limits the address fields, the tax rules and the shipping zones; a country you do not sell to cannot check out
|Spain plus eight EU countries (ES, DE, FR, IT, PT, NL, BE, AT, IE)

|Currency
|One shop currency per site; orders store it, so changing it later does not convert old orders
|EUR; a second currency needs an extension (core has none)

|Prices with or without tax
|Decides how every price is entered and how tax is shown; changing it re-interprets the prices you typed
|Entered and shown with 21 % VAT, because Spanish consumers expect it

|What the catalogue looks like
|Simple versus variable products, SKU scheme, attributes and categories drive filters, stock and reports
|`PC-<ORIGIN>-<GRIND>-<SIZE>` SKUs, 18 variants, categories Beans and Equipment

|Shipping promise
|Zones, a free-shipping threshold and parcel limits must be supported by a method you can actually configure
|Spain 4.90 EUR flat, free from 40 EUR; other EU 9.90 EUR flat

|Payment methods
|The gateway decides fees, refunds, recurring payments and the PCI burden
|Bank transfer in the examples; a card gateway is a provider decision

|Who sees the store
|Guest checkout and account creation change conversion and what you must say about personal data
|Guest checkout on; accounts optional

|Legal pages and policies
|Terms, privacy policy and refund policy have to exist before the first order
|Pages created from drafts before launch
|===

None of this is legal or tax advice. The tax rates, the VAT scheme for sales to other EU countries (the *One Stop Shop*
scheme) and the legal pages depend on where the business is established; the page uses the standard Spanish rate of
21 % as an illustration only.

== Onboarding: the Core Profiler and the task list

Activating WooCommerce starts the *Core Profiler* in the browser (it replaced the Setup Wizard of the 3.x era). It
collects store details and offers recommended extensions and a theme; the answers are stored in the option
`woocommerce_onboarding_profile`, and the WooCommerce home screen then shows a task list. The tasks are classes under
`src/Admin/Features/OnboardingTasks/Tasks/` in 11.2.1 with the ids `store_details`, `products`, `payments`, `tax`,
`shipping`, `marketing`, `appearance` (the theme step), `extend-store`, `launch-your-store` and `get-mobile-app`, plus
further ids (`customize-store`, `review-shipping`, `woocommerce-payments`, `shipping-recommendation`,
`tour-in-app-marketplace`) that the source defines for some configurations. A task is only a link to the
screens below, so skipping the Core Profiler loses nothing that cannot be set by hand.

The flow needs a browser, an administrator session and, for some of its offers, a connection to a WordPress.com or Woo
account; it was not run for this page. The scripted route below does not depend on it. The legacy class
`WC_Admin_Setup_Wizard` still ships in `includes/admin/class-wc-admin-setup-wizard.php`, and books that describe a
page-by-page wizard (location, currency, payments, shipping, recommended extras) describe that older flow.

Source: https://woocommerce.com/document/configuring-woocommerce-settings/[WooCommerce: configuring WooCommerce settings]

== The Settings tabs

All store settings live under *WooCommerce > Settings*. In 11.2.1 the tab ids and labels, read from
`WC_Admin_Settings::get_settings_pages()`, are `general` (General), `products` (Products), `tax` (Tax), `shipping`
(Shipping), `checkout` (Payments), `account` (Accounts and Privacy), `email` (Emails), `integration` (Integration),
`site-visibility` (Site visibility), `point-of-sale` (Point of Sale) and `advanced` (Advanced). The Tax tab only exists
when tax calculation is on (`woocommerce_calc_taxes` is `yes`). The tab id is the value of the `tab` parameter in the URL:
`/wp-admin/admin.php?page=wc-settings&tab=shipping&section=options`.

image::ecommerce-woocommerce-setup-settings-map.svg["Eleven WooCommerce Settings tabs with their sections and main options: General, Products, Tax, Shipping, Payments, Accounts and Privacy, Emails, Integration, Site visibility, Point of Sale and Advanced",width=1000]

Every setting is a WordPress option whose name is the field id, so a value can be read and changed from WP-CLI with
`wp option get` and `wp option update`, and the same fields are exposed by the REST API under `/wp-json/wc/v3/settings`
(listing a group, then reading or updating one field; the REST settings route was not exercised for this page). Changing
an option directly skips the screen's validation and, for a few settings, the side effects that run on save, so use
WP-CLI for the simple fields below and the screen when a setting has dependencies.

Section *Inventory* (`&section=inventory`) holds `woocommerce_manage_stock` (`yes`), `woocommerce_hold_stock_minutes`

`woocommerce_notify_backorder` (`yes`), `woocommerce_stock_email_recipient` (the store e-mail address),

stock tracking on because every variant has its own stock (25 units each in the script below).

=== Tax

The Tax tab (`woocommerce_calc_taxes` must be `yes`) has these options on its first screen and one section per tax class:
`woocommerce_prices_include_tax`, `woocommerce_tax_based_on` (`shipping`, `billing` or `base`), `woocommerce_shipping_tax_class`
(`inherit`), `woocommerce_tax_round_at_subtotal`, `woocommerce_tax_classes` (additional classes, one per line),
`woocommerce_tax_display_shop`, `woocommerce_tax_display_cart` (`incl` or `excl`), `woocommerce_price_display_suffix` and
`woocommerce_tax_total_display` (`itemized` or `single`). The sections are *Standard rates*, *Reduced rate rates* and *Zero
rate rates*, the three classes that exist after the install. The shop and the cart display settings default to
`excl` (prices shown without tax), which is wrong for a shop that sells to consumers in the EU, so set both to `incl`.

=== Shipping

The first screen of the Shipping tab lists the shipping zones; the sections are *Shipping options*
(`woocommerce_enable_shipping_calc`, `woocommerce_shipping_cost_requires_address`, `woocommerce_shipping_hide_rates_when_free`,
`woocommerce_ship_to_destination` with `billing` as default, `woocommerce_shipping_debug_mode`), *Classes* (shipping
classes, for example "bulky") and *Local pickup* (pickup locations). Zones and methods are described in their own section
below.

=== Payments (tab id `checkout`)

The Payments tab lists the installed gateways in the order in which they are offered; in the core install the gateways are
`bacs` (Direct bank transfer), `cheque` (Check payments) and `cod` (Cash on delivery), none of which charges a fee. Gateway
suggestions, including WooPayments, are offered from this screen; their onboarding was not run. See the Payments section
below.

=== Accounts and Privacy

The options are `woocommerce_enable_guest_checkout`, `woocommerce_enable_checkout_login_reminder`,
`woocommerce_enable_signup_and_login_from_checkout`, `woocommerce_enable_myaccount_registration`,
`woocommerce_registration_generate_password`, `woocommerce_enable_delayed_account_creation` (create the account after the
order), the personal data options (`woocommerce_erasure_request_removes_order_data`, `woocommerce_erasure_request_removes_download_data`,
`woocommerce_allow_bulk_remove_personal_data`), the privacy policy text options
(`woocommerce_registration_privacy_policy_text`, `woocommerce_checkout_privacy_policy_text`) and the retention options
(`woocommerce_delete_inactive_accounts`, `woocommerce_trash_pending_orders`, `woocommerce_trash_failed_orders`,
`woocommerce_trash_cancelled_orders`, `woocommerce_anonymize_refunded_orders`, `woocommerce_anonymize_completed_orders`).
The retention options are the GDPR housekeeping that the books do not cover; decide the periods with the person
responsible for data protection and write the privacy policy page that the text options point to.

=== Integration, Site visibility, Point of Sale

*Integration* lists the integrations registered by core and extensions; the only one in 11.2.1 is *MaxMind Geolocation*
(`maxmind_geolocation`). *Site visibility* controls the coming-soon mode described in the launch section. *Point of Sale*
holds the store name, address, phone, e-mail and refund and returns policy that point-of-sale receipts print
(`woocommerce_pos_store_name`, `woocommerce_pos_store_address`, `woocommerce_pos_store_phone`, `woocommerce_pos_store_email`,
`woocommerce_pos_refund_returns_policy`); it matters only if you use the point-of-sale feature.

=== Advanced

The Advanced tab has the sections *Page setup*, *REST API keys*, *Webhooks*, *WooCommerce.com* (usage tracking
`woocommerce_allow_tracking`, default `no`, and marketplace suggestions `woocommerce_show_marketplace_suggestions`, default
`yes`), *Blueprint (beta)* and *Features*.

* *Page setup* maps the Cart, Checkout, My account and Terms pages (`woocommerce_cart_page_id`, `woocommerce_checkout_page_id`,
`woocommerce_myaccount_page_id`, `woocommerce_terms_page_id`) and the endpoint slugs such as `order-pay`, `order-received`,
`orders`, `view-order`, `downloads`, `edit-account` and `edit-address`. The option ids `woocommerce_force_ssl_checkout`
and `woocommerce_unforce_ssl_checkout` still appear in the 11.2.1 field definitions; the books' "HTTPS only on checkout"
advice is outdated, and a store should serve every page over HTTPS.
* *REST API keys* and *Webhooks* are covered on the xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/rest-api-and-webhooks.adoc[REST API and
Webhooks] page.
* *Features* lists the toggles of the features that can still be switched: `woocommerce_custom_orders_table_enabled` (HPOS,
off after a scripted install), `woocommerce_custom_orders_table_data_sync_enabled`, `woocommerce_analytics_enabled`
(`yes`), `woocommerce_feature_order_attribution_enabled` (`yes`), `woocommerce_feature_rate_limit_checkout_enabled`
(`no`), `woocommerce_feature_block_email_editor_enabled` (`no`), `woocommerce_feature_email_improvements_enabled` (`no`),
`woocommerce_feature_cost_of_goods_sold_enabled` (`no`), `woocommerce_feature_product_gallery_videos_enabled` (`no`),
`woocommerce_hpos_datastore_caching_enabled` (`no`), `woocommerce_feature_remote_logging_enabled` (`yes`) and
`woocommerce_feature_blueprint_enabled` (`yes`). Some of these exist for a release or two and then become permanent;
treat the screen as the source of truth for the version you run.

== Tax

Tax is where a launch usually goes wrong, so the decisions are explicit. With `woocommerce_prices_include_tax` set to
`yes` the price you type is the price the customer pays and WooCommerce extracts the tax from it; with `no` tax is added
on top. `woocommerce_tax_based_on` decides which address selects the rate: `shipping` (the default), `billing` or `base`
(the shop address). Rates are rows of country, state, postcode, city, rate, name, priority, *compound* and *shipping*
(whether the rate also applies to shipping), grouped by tax class. A product's *Tax status* (taxable, shipping only or
none) and *Tax class* choose the class; the classes after install are *Standard rate*, *Reduced rate* and *Zero rate*.

Pocket Coffee sells with tax included and one Spanish rate:

[source,bash]
----
wp option update woocommerce_calc_taxes yes
wp option update woocommerce_prices_include_tax yes
wp option update woocommerce_tax_display_shop incl
wp option update woocommerce_tax_display_cart incl
wp --user=shopadmin wc tax create --country=ES --rate=21.0000 --name=IVA --priority=1 --shipping=true --class=standard --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc tax list --fields=id,country,rate,name,shipping,class --format=table
wp --user=shopadmin wc tax_class list --fields=slug,name --format=table
----

[source,text]
----
1
id	country	rate	name	shipping	class
1	ES	21.0000	IVA	1	standard
slug	name
standard	Standard rate
reduced-rate	Reduced rate
zero-rate	Zero rate
----

The same data goes through *Settings > Tax > Standard rates > Insert row* or the import CSV
(`sample-data/sample_tax_rates.csv` in the plugin shows the columns). Rates entered by hand are not a tax-compliance
system: they do not know about EU One Stop Shop thresholds, VAT numbers for business customers or changing rates. Extensions
or the automated tax calculation of the WooCommerce Shipping & Tax extension (it needs a connected account and was
not run here) cover that, and the
xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/taxes-coupons-currencies-and-i18n.adoc[Taxes, Coupons, Currencies and i18n] page goes through
the calculation.

Source: https://woocommerce.com/document/setting-up-taxes-in-woocommerce/[WooCommerce: setting up taxes in WooCommerce]

== Products and variations

Products live under *Products*. The classic product editor is the editor in 11.2.1: the block-based product editor was
deprecated in 10.9.0 and removed in 11.0.0, and old guides that show its screens no longer match. The product types are
simple, variable, grouped and external (affiliate); *virtual* and *downloadable* are checkboxes on a simple or variable
product. A product has a name, description, price, SKU, stock, weight and dimensions, categories, tags, attributes, a
tax class, a shipping class and images; the xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/products-and-product-types.adoc[Products and
Product Types] page covers each tab. A *variable* product has *attributes* flagged "used for variations" and one
*variation* per combination; each variation has its own SKU, price, weight and stock.

Pocket Coffee's catalogue is one simple product and one variable product with three attributes (origin, grind, size), 18
variations, per-variant stock and the SKU pattern `PC-<ORIGIN3>-<GRIND3>-<SIZE>`. The prices are 8.90 EUR for 250 g and
29.90 EUR for 1 kg, with Ethiopia 1.00 EUR dearer per 250 g. Created from WP-CLI (the loop at the end of the script
below creates the variations):

[source,bash]
----
wp --user=shopadmin wc product_cat create --name=Beans --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc product_cat create --name=Equipment --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc product create --name="Starter kit" --type=simple --regular_price=24.00 --sku=PC-KIT \
  --weight=1.2 --manage_stock=true --stock_quantity=30 --categories='[{"id":17}]' --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc product_variation list 11 --per_page=30 --fields=id,sku,regular_price,weight,stock_quantity --format=table
----

attributes to stay short. A variation's `attributes` must name the exact attribute and option strings of the parent, or
the variation is created but never matches a selection. And creating hundreds of variations through the editor is slow;
use the CSV importer (*Products > Import*, a `Type` of `variation` and a `Parent` SKU) or the batch REST endpoint for
large catalogues.

== Shipping zones

A *shipping zone* is a set of locations (countries, states, postcodes) with shipping methods attached; a customer gets the
methods of the *first zone, in the zone order, that matches the address*, and the built-in "Locations not covered by your
other zones" zone is the fallback. Methods are *Flat rate*, *Free shipping* and *Local pickup* in core; carriers' methods
come from extensions. The facts below were measured on the Pocket Coffee zones.

[source,bash]
----
wp --user=shopadmin wc shipping_zone create --name="Spain" --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc shipping_zone create --name="European Union (excluding Spain)" --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc shipping_zone_method create 1 --method_id=free_shipping \
  --settings='{"requires":"min_amount","min_amount":"40"}' --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc shipping_zone_method create 1 --method_id=flat_rate \
  --settings='{"cost":"4.90","tax_status":"taxable"}' --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc shipping_zone_method create 2 --method_id=flat_rate \
  --settings='{"cost":"9.90","tax_status":"taxable"}' --porcelain
wp eval '
$es = new WC_Shipping_Zone( 1 ); $es->add_location( "ES", "country" ); $es->set_zone_order( 0 ); $es->save();
$eu = new WC_Shipping_Zone( 2 ); $eu->set_zone_order( 1 );
foreach ( array( "DE", "FR", "IT", "PT", "NL", "BE", "AT", "IE" ) as $c ) { $eu->add_location( $c, "country" ); }
$eu->save();
foreach ( WC_Shipping_Zones::get_zones() as $z ) {
	echo $z["zone_name"], ": ", $z["formatted_zone_location"], " -> ";
	foreach ( $z["shipping_methods"] as $m ) { echo $m->id, "(", $m->get_option( "cost" ) ?: $m->get_option( "min_amount" ), ") "; }
	echo "\n";
}'
----

Source: https://woocommerce.com/document/setting-up-shipping-zones/[WooCommerce: setting up shipping zones]

[source,text]
----

2
1
2
3
Spain: Spain -> free_shipping(40) flat_rate(4.90)
European Union (excluding Spain): Germany, France, Italy, Portugal, Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Ireland -> flat_rate(9.90)
----

The calls to `WC_Shipping_Zone` are needed because the `wp wc shipping_zone_location` group only has `list`
(there is no create or update command); the REST route `PUT /wc/v3/shipping/zones/<id>/locations` does the same with a
JSON array. Four behaviours are worth knowing before they surprise a customer.

. *The continent code `EU` means Europe, not the European Union.* A first attempt that added the location type
`continent` with code `EU` listed the zone as "Europe", which includes countries outside the EU. The zone above lists
the member states one by one.
. *A flat rate cost is entered without tax.* With prices that include tax, a flat rate of 4.90 with *Tax status:
Taxable* costs 4.90 plus 21 % VAT, which the cart showed as shipping 4.90 and shipping tax 1.03 (5.93 in total). To charge
4.90 all-in, enter the net amount (4.0496) or set the method untaxable. The cost field also takes formulas such as
`10.00 * [qty]`, `[cost]` and `[fee percent="10" min_fee="20"]`.
. *The free-shipping threshold follows the cart display setting.* The minimum order amount is compared with the subtotal
as it is displayed. With `woocommerce_tax_display_cart` at its default `excl`, a cart with two starter kits (48.00 with
VAT, 39.67 without) did not get free shipping against a 40.00 minimum; after switching to `incl` it did. The method's
*Coupons discounts* checkbox (`ignore_discounts`) decides whether coupons reduce the amount that is compared.
. *The first method in the zone is preselected.* With free shipping created after the flat rate, a cart that qualified for
free shipping showed both rates and selected the flat rate; reordering the methods so that free shipping comes first
(the drag handles on the zone screen) made it the selected rate. In the REST and CLI route, create the free method first,
as the script does.

[source,bash]
----
wp wc shipping_zone_method list 1 --user=shopadmin --fields=instance_id,method_id,order --format=table
----

[source,text]
----
instance_id	method_id	order
2	free_shipping	1
1	flat_rate	2
----

The output is from the first test site after its two methods were reordered; the order is the `method_order` column of
`wp_woocommerce_shipping_zone_methods`. The setting *Hide shipping rates when free shipping is available*
(`woocommerce_shipping_hide_rates_when_free`) removes the other rates once the free one qualifies, which makes the
default question moot; Pocket Coffee leaves it off so that customers can still choose a faster method later.

Core has no per-parcel weight limit. The 5 kg limit of the Pocket Coffee spec is therefore expressed through the
structure of the catalogue (a shipping class for bulky items, quantity-based cost formulas) or an extension; it cannot
be set as a single number on the flat-rate method. Local pickup, shipping classes and the rate pipeline are covered on the
xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/shipping-methods.adoc[Shipping Methods] page.

== Payments

A gateway is an object that decides which payment methods the checkout offers and what happens to the order afterwards.
In the core install there are three, and none talks to a provider:

[source,bash]
----
wp --user=shopadmin wc payment_gateway update bacs --enabled=true --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc payment_gateway list --fields=id,title,enabled --format=table
----

Source: https://woocommerce.com/document/payments/[WooCommerce: payments]

[source,text]
----
bacs
id	title	enabled
bacs	Direct bank transfer	1
cheque	Check payments
cod	Cash on delivery
----

Enabling a gateway sends the administrator an e-mail through the `admin_payment_gateway_enabled` message, so the command
printed `+sendmail: can't connect to remote host (127.0.0.1): Connection refused+` on a machine with no mail server; the
gateway was still enabled. `bacs` takes the account details shown to the customer on the order confirmation and in the
e-mail, and an order paid by bank transfer is created as *On hold* until you mark it paid. `cheque` behaves the same way.
`cod` needs a shipping method that supports it: a checkout with `cod` before any shipping zone existed returned
`woocommerce_rest_checkout_payment_method_disabled`. For card payments, the choices are the WooPayments plugin (the
official one, with an in-context onboarding since 9.9.0 that needs a WooPayments account), other provider plugins from the WordPress.org directory or the Woo Marketplace, and a hosted payment
page. Their onboarding was not run for this page. Prefer a provider whose card fields are hosted by the provider
(hosted fields or a redirect): then card numbers never reach your server, which keeps the PCI DSS burden at the lightest
self-assessment. A gateway must also support the Checkout block; the
xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/payment-gateways.adoc[Payment Gateways] page explains how, and why an old gateway that only
registers a classic payment method does not appear in the block checkout.

The setup is tested with a real order. The Store API request below uses the same endpoints as the block checkout, with a
guest cart, the coupon and bank transfer; the shipping and totals are computed by WooCommerce, not by the script:

[source,bash]
----
B="http://localhost:8095/?rest_route="
H=$(curl -s -D - -o /dev/null "${B}/wc/store/v1/cart")
TOKEN=$(echo "$H" | grep -i '^cart-token' | awk '{print $2}' | tr -d '\r')
NONCE=$(echo "$H" | grep -i '^nonce:' | awk '{print $2}' | tr -d '\r')
curl -s -X POST -H "Cart-Token: $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"id":11,"quantity":2,"variation":[{"attribute":"Origin","value":"Colombia Huila"},{"attribute":"Grind","value":"Espresso"},{"attribute":"Size","value":"1 kg"}]}' \
  "${B}/wc/store/v1/cart/add-item" > /dev/null
curl -s -X POST -H "Cart-Token: $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"id":10,"quantity":1}' \
  "${B}/wc/store/v1/cart/add-item" > /dev/null
curl -s -X POST -H "Cart-Token: $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"billing_address":{"email":"ada@example.test","country":"ES","first_name":"Ada","last_name":"Lovelace","address_1":"Calle Ejemplo 1","city":"Madrid","state":"M","postcode":"28001","phone":"600000000"}}' \
  "${B}/wc/store/v1/cart/update-customer" > /dev/null
curl -s -X POST -H "Cart-Token: $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"code":"COFFEECLUB"}' \
  "${B}/wc/store/v1/cart/apply-coupon" > /dev/null
----

Source: https://developer.woocommerce.com/docs/apis/store-api/resources-endpoints/checkout/[WooCommerce docs: Store API checkout endpoint]

The order is then placed with a `POST` to `/wc/store/v1/checkout` carrying the `Cart-Token` and `Nonce` headers and a body
with `billing_address`, `shipping_address` and `payment_method` set to `bacs`. The order that came out:

[source,bash]
----
wp --user=shopadmin wc shop_order list --fields=id,status,total,discount_total,total_tax,shipping_total,payment_method --format=table
----

[source,text]
----

36	on-hold	77.82	4.94	13.51	0.00	bacs

== The Coffee Club discount

Pocket Coffee wants "10 % off beans for Coffee Club members, applied automatically". WooCommerce core has no customer
groups and no automatic role-based discounts. What it offers:

* *A coupon* (*Marketing > Coupons*, or `wp wc shop_coupon create`) with a discount type (`percent`, `fixed_cart`,
`fixed_product`), an amount, product and category restrictions, an *Allowed emails* list (`email_restrictions`), usage
limits and an expiry. It is applied by code, not automatically.
* *A user role* to tag members (`wp role create coffee_club "Coffee Club"` and `wp user add-role <user> coffee_club`),
which gives you a list of members and something for extensions and code to test, but a role alone changes no price.
* *An extension or custom code* for an automatic discount keyed to the role, such as a membership extension (see
xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/subscriptions-memberships-and-bookings.adoc[Subscriptions, Memberships and Bookings]) or a
filter on the cart.

[source,bash]
----
wp role create coffee_club "Coffee Club"
wp user add-role 2 coffee_club
wp --user=shopadmin wc shop_coupon create --code=COFFEECLUB --discount_type=percent --amount=10 \
  --product_categories='[16]' --email_restrictions='["ada@example.test"]' --porcelain
wp --user=shopadmin wc shop_coupon get 30 --fields=id,code,discount_type,amount,product_categories,email_restrictions --format=json
----

[source,text]
----
Success: Role with key 'coffee_club' created.
Success: Added 'coffee_club' role for ada (2).
30
{"id":30,"code":"coffeeclub","amount":"10.00","discount_type":"percent","usage_limit_per_user":null,"product_categories":[16],"email_restrictions":["ada@example.test"]}
----

WooCommerce stores coupon codes in lower case (`coffeeclub`), whatever case you typed. Two behaviours of the e-mail
restriction came out of the tests: applying the coupon to a guest cart whose customer has no e-mail returned
`Please enter a valid email at checkout to use coupon code "coffeeclub"`, and applying it after `update-customer` had set
the billing e-mail to `ada@example.test` worked. The restriction is therefore checked against the e-mail the customer has
entered, not against a login. A coupon restricted to the Beans category did nothing to a cart with only the starter kit
(`coupon is not applicable to selected products`).

image::ecommerce-woocommerce-setup-cart-math.svg["A Pocket Coffee cart: two kilos of Colombia Huila espresso beans at 29.90 and one starter kit at 24.00 with VAT included, the Coffee Club coupon takes 10 percent off the beans, and the total is 83.75 with the flat rate preselected or 77.82 with free shipping first",width=1000]

The figure shows the verified cart: 59.80 of beans plus the 24.00 kit is 83.80; the coupon takes 5.98 from the beans only
(4.94 plus 1.04 VAT); that leaves 77.82. With the flat rate preselected the customer would have paid 77.82 plus 5.93 =
83.75 although free shipping was available, which is why the order of the methods matters.

Source: https://woocommerce.com/document/coupon-management/[WooCommerce: coupon management]

== Choosing a theme and extensions

A theme decides the look and the templates; extensions add behaviour. Both are choices you live with, so the checks below
matter more than the demo.

*Theme.* A new WordPress 7.1.3 site comes with the default block themes (Twenty Twenty-Five 1.5, Twenty Twenty-Four 1.6,
Twenty Twenty-Three 1.7) and the first one was active in the test site. WooCommerce works with block themes through
`theme.json` and the Woo block templates, with classic themes through PHP templates, and the Cart and Checkout blocks work
in both. *Storefront* 4.6.2 (2025-12-09) is the long-standing classic theme from the WooCommerce team; *Purple* (version
1.0.3, updated 2026-10-09) is the block theme and needs WordPress 7.1 or newer. Prefer a block theme for a new store, keep
customisations in a child theme or in the Site Editor, and expect a classic theme's `yourtheme/woocommerce/` template
overrides to break when WooCommerce bumps a template version (WooCommerce > Status lists outdated overrides). The theme
section of the xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/themes-block-and-classic.adoc[Themes: Block and Classic] page covers this in
detail; the books' advice to design around a Customizer and widget areas describes the classic era.

*Extensions.* WooCommerce core is deliberately small, so most stores add a few extensions. Before installing one, check:

[cols="3,6",options=header]
|===
|Check |What to look for

|Maintenance
|Last update date, `Tested up to` (WordPress) and `WC tested up to` in the plugin header, and the number of active installs
|Compatibility declarations
|HPOS and Cart and Checkout block compatibility (`wp wc hpos compatibility-info` lists what each active plugin declared)
|Source
|The WordPress.org directory or the Woo Marketplace; never "nulled" copies of paid plugins, which are a common malware route
|Marketplace vetting
|Woo Marketplace extensions pass the Quality Insights Toolkit tests (activation, API, end-to-end, security, PHP compatibility); that is a floor, not a guarantee
|Overlap
|Does it duplicate a core feature, the theme, or another extension? Every extra plugin is code that runs on each request and an update to track
|Exit cost
|Where its data lives (custom tables, post meta) and what happens to it on uninstall
|===

`wp wc hpos compatibility-info` is the quick HPOS check; a plugin that has not declared compatibility makes WooCommerce warn
before HPOS is enabled. Keep the number of extensions small, remove the ones you stop using instead of deactivating them,
and test updates on a copy of the store first. Advice that named specific plugins in the books (SEO, security, backup,
caching) is dated; evaluate current options with the checks above.

Source: https://developer.woocommerce.com/docs/extensions/getting-started-extensions/building-your-first-extension/[WooCommerce docs: extensions]

== The complete Pocket Coffee script

The script below assembles the commands of this page into one file that creates the settings, tax rate, shipping zones,
payments, catalogue and coupon. It was run on a clean WordPress 7.1.3 and WooCommerce 11.2.1 site, finished in about 45
seconds and produced the zones, the 18 variations (with the IDs, SKUs and prices of the table above), the tax rate and the
coupon listed in this page. It uses an administrator called `shopadmin` and WP-CLI as `$WP`.

[source,bash,subs=-attributes]
----
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# pocket-coffee-setup.sh: settings, tax, shipping, catalogue and coupon of Pocket Coffee.
# WP-CLI is called through $WP (default: wp) inside the WordPress directory.
set -euo pipefail
WP=${WP:-wp}
ADMIN=${ADMIN:-shopadmin}
wcli() { "$WP" --user="$ADMIN" wc "$@"; }
opt()  { "$WP" option update "$@" > /dev/null; }

# 1. Store details, selling countries, currency, units
opt woocommerce_store_address "Calle Ejemplo 1"
opt woocommerce_store_city Madrid
opt woocommerce_store_postcode 28001
opt woocommerce_default_country ES:M
opt woocommerce_currency EUR
opt woocommerce_weight_unit kg
opt woocommerce_dimension_unit cm
opt woocommerce_allowed_countries specific
opt woocommerce_specific_allowed_countries '["ES","DE","FR","IT","PT","NL","BE","AT","IE"]' --format=json

# 2. Tax: prices include VAT, shown with VAT in the shop and the cart, one rate for Spain
opt woocommerce_calc_taxes yes
opt woocommerce_prices_include_tax yes
opt woocommerce_tax_display_shop incl
opt woocommerce_tax_display_cart incl
wcli tax create --country=ES --rate=21.0000 --name=IVA --priority=1 --shipping=true --class=standard --porcelain

# 3. Shipping: free shipping is created first so that it is the preselected method
ES=$(wcli shipping_zone create --name="Spain" --porcelain)
EU=$(wcli shipping_zone create --name="European Union (excluding Spain)" --porcelain)
"$WP" eval '
$es = new WC_Shipping_Zone( '"$ES"' ); $es->add_location( "ES", "country" ); $es->set_zone_order( 0 ); $es->save();
$eu = new WC_Shipping_Zone( '"$EU"' ); $eu->set_zone_order( 1 );
foreach ( array( "DE", "FR", "IT", "PT", "NL", "BE", "AT", "IE" ) as $c ) { $eu->add_location( $c, "country" ); }
$eu->save();'
wcli shipping_zone_method create "$ES" --method_id=free_shipping --settings='{"requires":"min_amount","min_amount":"40"}' --porcelain
wcli shipping_zone_method create "$ES" --method_id=flat_rate --settings='{"cost":"4.90","tax_status":"taxable"}' --porcelain
wcli shipping_zone_method create "$EU" --method_id=flat_rate --settings='{"cost":"9.90","tax_status":"taxable"}' --porcelain

# 4. Payments
wcli payment_gateway update bacs --enabled=true --porcelain

# 5. Categories and products
BEANS=$(wcli product_cat create --name=Beans --porcelain)
EQUIP=$(wcli product_cat create --name=Equipment --porcelain)
wcli product create --name="Starter kit" --type=simple --regular_price=24.00 --sku=PC-KIT --weight=1.2 \
  --manage_stock=true --stock_quantity=30 --categories="[{\"id\":$EQUIP}]" --porcelain

PID=$(wcli product create --name="Single-origin beans" --type=variable --categories="[{\"id\":$BEANS}]" \
  --attributes='[{"name":"Origin","position":0,"visible":true,"variation":true,"options":["Ethiopia Yirgacheffe","Colombia Huila","Brazil Cerrado"]},{"name":"Grind","position":1,"visible":true,"variation":true,"options":["Whole bean","Espresso","Filter"]},{"name":"Size","position":2,"visible":true,"variation":true,"options":["250 g","1 kg"]}]' --porcelain)
for o in "Ethiopia Yirgacheffe:ETH:1" "Colombia Huila:COL:0" "Brazil Cerrado:BRA:0"; do
  IFS=: read -r oname ocode extra <<< "$o"
  for g in "Whole bean:WHL" "Espresso:ESP" "Filter:FIL"; do
    IFS=: read -r gname gcode <<< "$g"
    for s in "250 g:250:8.90:0.25:1" "1 kg:1KG:29.90:1:4"; do
      IFS=: read -r sname scode price weight mult <<< "$s"
      total=$(awk -v p="$price" -v e="$extra" -v m="$mult" 'BEGIN { printf "%.2f", p + e * m }')
      wcli product_variation create "$PID" --regular_price="$total" --sku="PC-$ocode-$gcode-$scode" \
        --weight="$weight" --manage_stock=true --stock_quantity=25 \
        --attributes="[{\"name\":\"Origin\",\"option\":\"$oname\"},{\"name\":\"Grind\",\"option\":\"$gname\"},{\"name\":\"Size\",\"option\":\"$sname\"}]" \
        --porcelain > /dev/null
    done
  done
done

# 6. Coffee Club: a role to tag members and a coupon restricted to Beans and to one member e-mail
"$WP" role create coffee_club "Coffee Club"
wcli shop_coupon create --code=COFFEECLUB --discount_type=percent --amount=10 \
  --product_categories="[$BEANS]" --email_restrictions='["ada@example.test"]' --porcelain
----

Source: https://developer.woocommerce.com/docs/getting-started/development-environment/[WooCommerce docs: WP-CLI and the development environment]

The script is not idempotent: run twice, it creates a second tax rate, second zones and a second catalogue. For repeatable
environments, create the site from scratch (a fresh container or `wp db reset --yes`) each time, as the local-environment
page describes. The coupon code is taken by the first run, and a second `shop_coupon create` for the same code fails with a
duplicate-code error.

== Security, backups and maintenance

Most store compromises come from outdated or untrusted plugins and weak administrator accounts, not from WooCommerce core;
Patchstack's 2026 report counts 91 % of the new WordPress vulnerabilities of 2025 in plugins and 9 % in themes (a
third-party source). The routine below is what a small store can sustain. The commands were run on the test site.

[cols="2,5,3",options=header]
|===
|When |Task |How

|Every week
|Apply WordPress, WooCommerce, theme and plugin updates, after testing on a copy; remove unused plugins
|`wp plugin list --fields=name,status,version,update,auto_update`, `wp core check-update`

|Every week
|Check scheduled actions and failed jobs (*WooCommerce > Status > Scheduled Actions*) and the log
|The xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/action-scheduler.adoc[Action Scheduler] page

|Every day
|Back up the database; back up uploads and code after every change
|`wp db export`, a file-system copy, an off-site copy

|Every month
|Restore a backup into a throw-away site and place a test order
|The restore is the test; an untested backup is a hope

|After each update
|Verify the files of core and plugins against the published checksums
|`wp plugin verify-checksums --all`, `wp core verify-checksums`

|Every quarter
|Review administrator and shop-manager accounts, API keys (*Advanced > REST API*), webhooks and application passwords
|`wp user list --role=administrator`

|Always
|HTTPS on every page, strong passwords and two-factor authentication for every administrator
|WordPress 7.1.3 core contains no two-factor code; use a maintained plugin
|===

[source,bash]
----
wp plugin verify-checksums --all
wp core check-update
wp plugin auto-updates enable woocommerce
wp plugin list --fields=name,status,version,update,auto_update
wp db export /backups/pocket-coffee-2026-10-27.sql
wp config set DISALLOW_FILE_EDIT true --raw
wp config set WP_ENVIRONMENT_TYPE production
wp cron event run --due-now
----

[source,text]
----

Success: WordPress is at the latest version.
Success: Enabled 1 of 1 plugin auto-updates.
name	status	version	update	auto_update
akismet	inactive	5.7.2	none	off
hello	inactive	1.7.2	none	off
woocommerce	active	11.2.1	none	on
Success: Exported to '/backups/pocket-coffee-2026-10-27.sql'.
Success: Added the constant 'DISALLOW_FILE_EDIT' to the 'wp-config.php' file with the raw value 'true'.
Success: Added the constant 'WP_ENVIRONMENT_TYPE' to the 'wp-config.php' file with the value 'production'.

Automatic updates for WooCommerce are a trade-off: security releases arrive fast (the July 2021 SQL injection was patched
in a forced update within a day), but an automatic update can break a customised checkout, so many stores enable them for
core and security releases and test major updates by hand. `DISALLOW_FILE_EDIT` removes the theme and plugin editors from
wp-admin, which stops an attacker with an administrator session from writing PHP there. WordPress's cron runs on page
visits; a store with low traffic or a caching layer should call `wp-cron.php` or `wp cron event run --due-now` from the
system cron and set `DISABLE_WP_CRON`, because Action Scheduler (orders, e-mails, subscriptions) rides on it.

*WooCommerce > Status > Tools* offers maintenance actions that are safe to run when a symptom matches. The ids in 11.2.1
(shown by `wp wc tool list --user=<administrator>`) include `clear_transients`, `clear_expired_transients`,
`delete_orphaned_variations`, `clear_expired_download_permissions`, `regenerate_product_lookup_tables`,
`repair_coupons_lookup_table`, `recount_terms`, `reset_roles`, `clear_sessions`, `clear_template_cache`, `install_pages`,
`db_update_routine`, `verify_db_tables`, `regenerate_product_attributes_lookup_table`,
`clear_woocommerce_analytics_cache`, `hpos_legacy_cleanup` and, destructive, `delete_taxes` and
`delete_custom_orders_table`. A tool runs with `wp wc tool run <id> --user=<administrator>`; read the description before you
run the destructive ones. The *Status > System status* report is what support asks for first: it lists versions, active
plugins, database tables, outdated template overrides and the HPOS state.

*Payment security.* A store that never sees card numbers (hosted fields or redirect) completes the lightest PCI DSS
self-assessment; a store that handles card data itself takes on the full burden. Keep API keys and gateway secrets out
of the code and rotate them after any suspected compromise. The incident history, the 21-release security backport window
and hardening beyond this routine are on the
xref:ecommerce/woocommerce/performance-and-security.adoc[Performance and Security] page, and the developer-side checklist is
in the official security best practices (PHPCS and Semgrep clean code, WordPress hardening, 2FA, data protection).

Source: https://developer.woocommerce.com/docs/best-practices/security/security-best-practices/[WooCommerce docs: security best practices]

== Launching

WooCommerce can hide the store until you are ready. *Settings > Site visibility* (the option names are
`woocommerce_coming_soon` and `woocommerce_store_pages_only`) has two modes: *coming soon* for the whole site, or for
the store pages only, so that a blog or marketing site stays public while the shop is private. In the 11.2.1 source,
`woocommerce_coming_soon` set to `yes` with `woocommerce_store_pages_only` not `yes` puts the whole site in coming-soon
mode, and both set to `yes` limits it to the store pages; administrators and shop managers (`manage_woocommerce`) always
see the real site. A scripted install leaves `woocommerce_coming_soon` unset, so the store is live from the moment it is
installed; check the setting before you point a domain at the site.

On a test site, with the whole site in coming-soon mode, an anonymous request to the home page returned HTTP 200 with the
header `Cache-Control: max-age=60`, and the page head contained `<meta name='woo-coming-soon-page' content='yes'>` and a
robots meta of `max-image-preview:large`. The coming-soon page is therefore a normal 200 response, not a 503, and it
carried no `noindex` directive; search engines can index it if they find it. WordPress's own *Discourage search engines
from indexing this site* switch (*Settings > Reading*, option `blog_public`) is a separate control. A page cache in front of
the site keeps serving the page for as long as its own lifetime, so purge it when you switch the mode.

Search and marketing are launch topics in every book: use readable permalinks (the default product base is `/product/` and
the product category base `/product-category/`, both editable in *Settings > Permalinks*), give each product a unique
title and description, keep one URL per product (variations use query parameters on the same URL), and let WooCommerce
emit its product structured data. Analytics and advertising pixels must respect consent rules in the countries you sell
to, so add them through a consent-aware plugin and mention them in the privacy policy. The order of the final steps is:
finish the content, run the test orders, switch the site visibility to live, purge caches, place a real order yourself,
and watch the first days of orders and e-mails.

== Legacy and book notes

The books on this subject describe an older WooCommerce and are background only. Sims (2018, WooCommerce 3.x and
WordPress 4.9) walks the Setup Wizard, the Customizer and widget areas of Storefront, shortcode-based Cart and Checkout
pages and PHP 5-era hosting; today the Core Profiler and the task list replace the wizard, block themes replace the
Customizer for new stores, the Cart and Checkout blocks are the default and PHP 7.4 is the floor (PHP 8.3 is
recommended). Rauland (2015, WooCommerce 2.2 and 2.3) edits the checkout with `woocommerce_checkout_fields` and enforces
HTTPS on the checkout only; those filters do not affect the Checkout block and HTTPS now belongs on every page.
Ravensbergen (2015, WooCommerce 2.4 and 2.5) builds a Storefront child theme with `storefront_*` hooks and shortcodes and
refers to WooThemes and Simplify Commerce, which no longer exist as described. The store-planning, launch, security,
backup and maintenance advice survives, which is why it is rewritten above for the current screens, options and commands.
