= B2B
:description: Selling wholesale on Shopify with the Admin GraphQL, Storefront and Customer Account APIs: companies, locations and contacts, B2B catalogs, price lists and quantity rules, payment terms and deposits, draft orders, themes and headless storefronts, written against API version 2026-10.
:keywords: Shopify, B2B, wholesale, company, companyCreate, company location, company contact, catalog, price list, publication, quantity rules, payment terms, net terms, deposit, draft order, purchasingEntity, Customer Account API, Storefront API, Shopify Plus

include::partial$ecommerce-disclaimer.adoc[]

Shopify B2B lets a merchant sell to other businesses from the same store that sells to consumers: a buyer logs in as a
contact of a company, picks one of the company's locations, and then sees the products, prices, quantity limits and
payment terms that the merchant agreed for that location. This page was written against the Admin GraphQL API and the
Storefront API *2026-10*, the Customer Account API schema *2026-04* (the newest schema that could be downloaded without
a store, shipped in `@shopify/hydrogen-react` 2026.4.4) and Shopify CLI *5.0.0*, and every fact was checked on
2026-10-27. B2B features depend on the plan, and the plan rules changed with the Winter '26 and Spring '26 Editions, so
the section <<plans,Plans, availability and limits>> lists what is known and where it comes from.

The page covers the four building blocks and the places where they surface: the data model (companies, locations,
contacts and roles), catalogs with their price lists, publications and quantity rules, payment terms and deposits, and
draft orders with imported orders; then the storefront side (Liquid in themes, and the Customer Account and Storefront
APIs for headless), checkout customization with Shopify Functions, events, and a list of the mistakes that cost the most
time. It builds on the xref:ecommerce/shopify/admin-graphql-api.adoc[Admin GraphQL API] page (versioning, cost-based
throttling, pagination), on xref:ecommerce/shopify/authentication-and-access-scopes.adoc[Authentication and Access Scopes]
for the tokens that sign the requests, and on xref:ecommerce/shopify/markets-and-internationalization.adoc[Markets and
Internationalization], because a company location is also a market condition and catalogs are shared between Markets and
B2B. The running example is Pocket Coffee, a small specialty-coffee shop that opens a wholesale channel: the invented
company Cafe Brasa SL (a cafe with a Madrid and a Berlin location) buys 1 kg bags of single-origin beans at a
negotiated price, in multiples of two bags, on 30-day terms with a 20 percent deposit.

Every Admin GraphQL, Storefront and Customer Account document on the page was validated against the downloaded schema,
and every set of variables was checked against the variable definitions of its operation. The Liquid snippets passed
`shopify theme check` and were rendered with a stand-in engine. The Shopify Function was built and run offline with the
CLI's Function runner. Nothing on this page was executed against a Shopify store: B2B resources need a shop on a plan that
supports them and an access token, and no account or credential was created for this page. The section
<<what-was-run,What was run and what was not>> lists exactly what each check covered.

== B2B on Shopify at a glance

Shopify B2B is not a separate product line with its own API. It is a set of objects and settings that sit on top of the
ordinary store model, and each layer has its own entry point:

[cols="2,4,3",options="header"]
|===
|Layer |What it does |Where you reach it

|Companies, locations, contacts
|Identify the business customer, its branches and the people who buy for it. A contact is a customer record linked to a
company.
|Admin GraphQL: `Company`, `CompanyLocation`, `CompanyContact`, `CompanyContactRole`

|Catalogs, price lists, publications
|Decide which products a location sees and what it pays, including fixed prices, volume breaks and quantity rules.
|Admin GraphQL: `CompanyLocationCatalog`, `PriceList`, `Publication`, `QuantityRule`, `QuantityPriceBreak`

|Payment terms and deposits
|Decide when the company pays: at once, within a number of days, on a date or on fulfillment, with an optional deposit.
|Admin GraphQL: `PaymentTermsTemplate`, `BuyerExperienceConfiguration`, `PaymentTerms`; Functions: `paymentTermsSet`

|Orders and draft orders
|A merchant or an app quotes and invoices a purchase, or imports history; the order records the purchasing company.
|Admin GraphQL: `DraftOrder`, `Order`, `PurchasingCompany`

|Storefront
|Show the buyer's location, prices and rules in a theme or a headless storefront.
|Liquid objects `company`, `company_location`, `customer.b2b?`; Storefront API `@inContext(buyer:)`; Customer Account API

|Checkout
|Apply the location's terms, review rules and validation to the B2B checkout.
|Shopify Functions and checkout UI extensions
|===

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/b2b[Apps and B2B]

The developer guide describes a typical lifecycle in four steps. A merchant creates a company with its contacts and
locations, and may create draft orders for a contact to review. A contact logs in with an account attached to the
company, selects products, reviews draft orders and completes checkout. Shopify creates the transaction and the order.
An app then uses the Admin API for the integrations around it: order management interfaces, B2B fulfillment, loyalty,
or CRM synchronization.

A store can serve consumers and companies together or only companies. The developer guides use the names "blended" and
"dedicated" for the two setups; in code the difference is whether the storefront hides prices, the cart and the order
buttons from visitors who are not logged in as a B2B buyer. Both setups use the same objects, and the section on
<<theme,Themes>> shows the Liquid that implements a dedicated store.

appear only in the Help Center pages for B2B are not relied on beyond what the developer documentation and the Plus page
repeat.

|Capability |Plans |Source and notes

|Company profiles, volume pricing and up to three B2B catalogs
|Basic, Grow and Advanced (as of the Spring '26 Edition); Plus

Two things in the documentation do not line up, and a project should resolve them with a development store of the
target plan before promising a feature to a merchant. First, the B2B overview guide still states that only development
stores, Shopify Plus partners and Shopify affiliates can access the Admin API's B2B resources, and that Plus partners
need a development store created on the Plus plan; the Spring '26 Edition says the same capabilities are available on
more plans. Second, the 2026-10 reference says a publication on a catalog is optional (without one, availability follows
the sales channel), while the older catalog guide says a B2B catalog without a publication shows no products. The
<<catalogs,Catalogs>> section describes both and how to avoid depending on either.

B2B does not support purchase options. The overview guide lists subscriptions, pre-orders and try before you buy as
unsupported for B2B, and the Storefront API reports a cart line that violates this with the warning code
`MERCHANDISE_SELLING_PLAN_NOT_APPLICABLE_ON_COMPANY_LOCATION`, which was added on 2025-07-30.

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/usage/access-scopes[Shopify API access scopes]

=== Customer accounts

B2B only works with the new customer accounts. A store still on legacy customer accounts cannot give buyers a B2B
session, and a headless storefront must replace its legacy login code with the Customer Account API authorization flow
before it can contextualize requests. Legacy customer accounts are deprecated in favour of new customer accounts, as the
xref:ecommerce/shopify/authentication-and-access-scopes.adoc[Authentication and Access Scopes] page explains.

=== A development store

Create a development store with `shopify store create dev` or from the Dev Dashboard, add products to it, and give the
app the scopes above in `shopify.app.toml`. The store has to be on a plan with B2B capabilities for the examples to work.
This page has no development store behind it, so the app configuration was only validated offline, in the section
<<events,Events and integrations>>.

== The data model

image::ecommerce-shopify-b2b-data-model.svg["The B2B objects: a company has locations and contacts; a role assignment joins contact, location and role; a location holds addresses, tax settings, buyer experience settings and catalogs; orders point to a purchasing company",width=780]

The B2B objects of the Admin API and how they hang together.

Three objects model the customer, and one more joins them:

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Object |Meaning

|`Company`
|The business that buys. Fields include `name`, `note`, `externalId` (the key of the company in an ERP or CRM),
`customerSince`, `mainContact`, `defaultRole`, `contactRoles`, `contacts`, `locations`, `orders`, `draftOrders`,
`ordersCount`, `totalSpent` and `metafields`.

|`CompanyLocation`
|A branch of the company, and the object that carries almost all commercial settings: `billingAddress`,
`shippingAddress`, `currency` (derived from the shipping address), `locale`, `externalId`, `note`, `taxSettings`,
`buyerExperienceConfiguration`, `catalogs`, `roleAssignments`, `staffMemberAssignments`, `storeCreditAccounts`,
`orders` and `draftOrders`.

|`CompanyContact`
|A person who buys on behalf of the company: `title`, `locale`, `isMainContact`, the linked `customer`, and its
`roleAssignments`. The contact is not a separate login; it points at an ordinary `Customer`, which is the account that
logs in.

|`CompanyContactRoleAssignment`
|One row of "this contact has this role at this location". A contact can act only at locations where it holds a role, so
creating a contact is not enough to let it order.

|`CompanyContactRole`
|A named role defined on the company (`id`, `name`, `note`). `Company.contactRoles` lists them and `Company.defaultRole`
is the one proposed by default.
|===

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/Company[Company object reference] (fields read from the 2026-10 introspection)

Orders and draft orders refer to all three through the `PurchasingEntity` union, which is either a `Customer` or a
`PurchasingCompany` (company, location and contact). A B2B order can be tied to the company, to one location or to one
contact. Catalogs, however, attach to a location only, which is why price differences between branches of the same
company are modelled with several locations, not several contacts.

Several fields of `CompanyLocation` are deprecated in 2026-10, and the Storefront API and Customer Account API have
smaller versions of the same types:

[cols="3,4",options="header"]
|===
|Deprecated field or mutation |Replacement

|`CompanyLocation.taxExemptions`, `CompanyLocation.taxRegistrationId`
|`CompanyLocation.taxSettings` (`taxExempt`, `taxExemptions`, `taxRegistrationId`), introduced with the `tax_exempt`
field on 2025-01-01

|`companyLocationAssignTaxExemptions`, `companyLocationRevokeTaxExemptions`, `companyLocationCreateTaxRegistration`,
`companyLocationRevokeTaxRegistration`
|`companyLocationTaxSettingsUpdate`

|`CompanyLocation.orderCount`, `Company.contactCount`
|`ordersCount`, `contactsCount` (both return `Count`)

|`CompanyLocation.market`
|None: "will be removed in a future version"; resolve markets with the Markets API

|`BuyerExperienceConfiguration.payNowOnly`
|`checkoutToDraft` (must be false) together with `paymentTermsTemplate` (must be null), from which the value is derived

|`Company.metafieldDefinitions`, `CompanyLocation.metafieldDefinitions`
|`QueryRoot.metafieldDefinitions`
|===

Source: https://shopify.dev/changelog/introduce-a-tax_exempt-field-associated-to-a-companylocation[Introduce a tax_exempt field associated to a CompanyLocation]

On the storefront side the types are deliberately small. The Storefront API `Company` has `id`, `name`, `externalId`,
timestamps and metafields; `CompanyLocation` adds `locale`; `CompanyContact` has only `title` and `locale`; and
`PurchasingCompany` ties them to a cart. The Customer Account API exposes `Customer.companyContacts`, and from a contact
its `company`, `locations`, `orders`, `draftOrders` and `status` (`DISABLED` or `ENABLED`), and from a location its
addresses, tax identifier, `buyerExperienceConfiguration`, payment instruments and orders.

== Companies, contacts and roles

=== Creating a company

`companyCreate` creates a company and, in the same call, its first location and first contact. The tutorial stresses that
a location must be added to attach information such as tax exemptions, and that a contact must exist before any order or
draft order can be created for the company. `CompanyCreateInput` has three parts: `company` (`name`, `note`,
`externalId`, `customerSince`), `companyLocation` (`CompanyLocationInput`, below) and `companyContact`
(`firstName`, `lastName`, `email`, `title`, `locale`, `phone`).

[source,graphql]
----
mutation CompanyCreate($input: CompanyCreateInput!) {
  companyCreate(input: $input) {
    company {
      id
      name
      externalId
      mainContact {
        id
        customer {
          email
        }
      }
      locations(first: 5) {
        nodes {
          id
          name
        }
      }
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      code
      message
    }
  }
}
----

Variables for Cafe Brasa SL, with the Madrid location as the first location and its purchasing manager as the first
contact. The reserved domain `example.test` stands in for a real address, and the tax ID is a placeholder.

[source,json]
----

`userErrors` on all company mutations are `BusinessCustomerUserError` objects with a `code` from
`BusinessCustomerErrorCode`: `RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `REQUIRED`, `NO_INPUT`, `INVALID_INPUT`, `INVALID`, `BLANK`, `TAKEN`
(for example a duplicate contact email), `TOO_LONG`, `LIMIT_REACHED`, `FAILED_TO_DELETE`, `UNEXPECTED_TYPE` and
`INTERNAL_ERROR`. Always select `code` besides `message`, because `message` is meant for people and may be translated.

=== Roles

A contact has no permission anywhere until it is assigned a role at a location. Read the available roles from the
company, then assign one per location:

[source,graphql]
----
query CompanyContactRoles($companyId: ID!) {
  company(id: $companyId) {
    defaultRole {
      id
      name
    }
    contactRoles(first: 10) {
      nodes {
        id
        name
      }
    }
  }
}

mutation ContactAssignRoles($contactId: ID!, $rolesToAssign: [CompanyContactRoleAssign!]!) {
  companyContactAssignRoles(companyContactId: $contactId, rolesToAssign: $rolesToAssign) {
    roleAssignments {
      id
      role {
        name
      }
      companyLocation {
        name
      }
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      code
      message
    }
  }
}
----

first query):

The schema describes role names such as `admin` or `buyer`; read the names and IDs from `contactRoles` instead of

the order, or the import returns an error.

=== Listing and maintaining companies

`companies` is a normal connection. Its `query` argument accepts the search syntax of the Admin API, and the sort keys
are `CREATED_AT`, `ID`, `NAME`, `ORDER_COUNT`, `SINCE_DATE`, `TOTAL_SPENT` and `UPDATED_AT`. Count fields return a
`Count` object, not an integer.

[source,graphql]
----
query CompaniesPage($after: String) {
  companies(first: 50, after: $after, sortKey: UPDATED_AT, reverse: true, query: "name:Cafe*") {
    pageInfo {
      hasNextPage
      endCursor
    }
    nodes {
      id
      name
      externalId
      ordersCount {
        count
      }
      totalSpent {
        amount
        currencyCode
      }
      locationsCount {
        count
      }
    }
  }
}

mutation CompanyUpdate($companyId: ID!, $input: CompanyInput!) {
  companyUpdate(companyId: $companyId, input: $input) {
    company {
      id
      note
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      code
      message
    }
  }
}

mutation ContactWelcome($contactId: ID!) {
  companyContactSendWelcomeEmail(companyContactId: $contactId) {
    companyContact {
      id
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      code
      message
    }
  }
}
----

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/queries/companies[companies query]

To export every company with its locations and payment terms, use a bulk query, which avoids paginating and throttling by

[source,graphql]

mutation BulkCompanies {

    query: """
    {

        edges {
          node {
            id
            name
            externalId
            locations {
              edges {
                node {
                  id
                  name
                  buyerExperienceConfiguration {
                    paymentTermsTemplate {
                      name
                    }
                  }
                }
              }
            }
          }
        }
      }
    }
    """
  ) {
    bulkOperation {
      id
      status
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      message
    }
  }
}
----

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/usage/bulk-operations/queries[Perform bulk operations with the GraphQL Admin API]

Use `externalId` on companies and locations as the join key with the ERP or CRM, and keep it stable. A bulk company
importer is also a natural fit for an app: the Winter '26 Edition even shows Sidekick generating one from a CSV file
and creating companies from a prompt, which is a convenience for merchants, not an API.

== Company locations

=== Adding a location

A company needs one location per place that buys on different terms. `CompanyLocationInput` takes `name`, `phone`,
`locale`, `externalId`, `note`, `billingAddress` and `shippingAddress` (`CompanyAddressInput`: `address1`, `address2`,
`city`, `zip`, `zoneCode`, `countryCode`, `recipient`, `firstName`, `lastName`, `phone`), `billingSameAsShipping`,
`taxRegistrationId`, `taxExemptions`, `taxExempt` and `buyerExperienceConfiguration`. The location's currency follows
its shipping address; without one it follows the shop.

[source,graphql]
----
mutation LocationCreate($companyId: ID!, $input: CompanyLocationInput!) {
  companyLocationCreate(companyId: $companyId, input: $input) {
    companyLocation {
      id
      name
      currency
      billingAddress {
        formattedAddress
      }
      shippingAddress {
        formattedAddress
      }
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      code
      message
    }
  }
}
----

The addresses are not copied onto draft orders automatically. The start-building tutorial points out that billing and
shipping information are not populated from the company location, so an app that creates draft orders should read the
addresses from the location and pass them in.

=== Tax settings

Tax exemptions belong to the location, not the company, because two branches may be taxed differently (the guide's
example is a company with branches that follow different billing and taxation rules). Set the exemption before the
location places its first order. The mutation takes the exemptions to add and remove and, since the 2025 `tax_exempt`
change, a `taxExempt` flag and a `taxRegistrationId`; `TaxExemption` has 74 values in 2026-10, mostly Canadian and US
provincial and state certificates such as `CA_BC_RESELLER_EXEMPTION` and `CA_STATUS_CARD_EXEMPTION`.

[[catalogs]]
== Catalogs, price lists and publications

=== Three objects, one idea

A *catalog* answers two questions for a context: which products can the buyer see, and what do they cost. It does so by
pointing at two optional objects. A *publication* is the set of products and collections visible in the catalog. A
*price list* holds prices for variants: a percentage adjustment of the base price (`parent`), plus fixed prices for
individual variants, plus quantity rules and price breaks. `Catalog` is an interface with three implementations, chosen
by the context:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Type |Context |Created with

|`CompanyLocationCatalog`
|One or more company locations (`CatalogContextInput.companyLocationIds`)
|`catalogCreate`, `catalogUpdate`, `catalogContextUpdate`

|`MarketCatalog`
|One or more markets (`marketIds`); the B2B way to serve whole regions or all locations of a region
|Markets API (`marketCreate`, `marketUpdate`)

|`AppCatalog`
|An app or channel, such as the Online Store
|Created by Shopify
|===

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/interfaces/Catalog[Catalog interface] and https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/markets/catalogs-different-markets[About catalogs for different markets]

Do not confuse these B2B catalogs with Shopify Catalog and the Catalog API, which are unrelated products for product
discovery by AI agents. Their changelog entries appear in the same feed, but they have nothing to do with price lists.

`CatalogContextInput` accepts exactly one kind of context per call (`MUST_PROVIDE_EXACTLY_ONE_CONTEXT_TYPE`), and
`catalogContextUpdate` must match the existing kind (`CONTEXT_DRIVER_MISMATCH`). A location that is already in the
catalog cannot be added again (`CONTEXT_ALREADY_ASSIGNED_TO_CATALOG`). The catalog guide recommends managing B2B
catalogs through markets when the shop uses the Markets API, instead of linking them to individual company locations.

Cafe Brasa locations:

A catalog is created without a price list and without a publication when you leave both out. The 2026-10 reference says
a company location catalog that only needs custom pricing should be created with a price list and without a
publication, and that without a publication availability is determined by the sales channel. The older catalog guide
warns that customers logged in to a B2B account see no products for a location whose catalog has no publication. The two
statements describe different generations of the same feature. For a catalog that must restrict products, create a
publication explicitly and test with a buyer on the target plan; for a pricing-only catalog, check on a development
store that the products you expect are visible before relying on the reference wording.

=== Step 2: the price list

A price list belongs to one catalog (error `CATALOG_TAKEN` otherwise) and has a currency. Its `parent` gives a
percentage adjustment of the prices it inherits, and `settings.compareAtMode` decides what happens to the compare-at
price: `ADJUSTED` applies the same adjustment, `NULLIFY` removes it. The adjustment type is `PERCENTAGE_DECREASE` or
`PERCENTAGE_INCREASE`; the schema notes that in `PriceListAdjustment.value`, positive numbers reduce prices and negative
numbers increase them, so read the type and the value together.

[source,graphql]
----
mutation PriceListCreate($input: PriceListCreateInput!) {
  priceListCreate(input: $input) {
    priceList {
      id
      name
      currency
      parent {
        adjustment {
          type
          value
        }
        settings {
          compareAtMode
        }
      }
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      code
      message
    }
  }
}
----

----
{
  "input": {
    "name": "Wholesale EUR",
    "currency": "EUR",

If a catalog has no price list, buyers get the initial variant prices converted to the market currency. To move an
existing price list to another catalog, use `priceListUpdate` with a new `catalogId`; the price list is detached from
its previous catalog, and if that catalog depended on it, it becomes a publishing-only catalog on store default prices,
so give the old catalog a new price list or accept the change. The `PriceList.contextRule` field was deprecated in API
version 2023-04 and is not in the 2026-10 schema (querying it is rejected, see the checks at the end of the page);
migrate any app that still uses it to catalogs.

=== Step 5: quantity rules and volume pricing

Wholesale usually comes with order-size rules. A `QuantityRule` has a `minimum`, an optional `maximum` and an
`increment`: only quantities divisible by the increment are valid, the minimum must be a multiple of the increment, and
the maximum too. A `QuantityPriceBreak` gives a lower price from a minimum quantity. `quantityPricingByVariantUpdate`
changes fixed prices, rules and breaks in one request, runs the deletions before the additions, and applies nothing if
any change fails. Quantity rules and breaks are accepted only for company location catalogs and for catalogs on
compatible markets (`CATALOG_CONTEXT_DOES_NOT_SUPPORT_QUANTITY_RULES`).

      sku

      message

=== Several catalogs on one location

A company location can be in several catalogs, and the rules for combining them are simple. A buyer sees the products
that are published in at least one applicable catalog, and when several catalogs price the same variant, the lowest
listed price applies. The developer docs build a scaling pattern on this: keep pricing-only catalogs (one per price list)
and publication-only catalogs (one per assortment) and assign several to each location. A merchant with 50 pricing tiers
and 10 assortments then manages 60 catalogs instead of 500 full ones. The pattern is shared with Markets, and on plans
below Plus the limit of three B2B catalogs applies, so it matters most on Plus.

image::ecommerce-shopify-b2b-catalog-resolution.svg["Two catalogs on the Madrid location: one with a publication and a 20 percent price list, one pricing-only with 30 percent; the buyer sees the union of publications and pays the lowest price, subject to the quantity rule",width=780]

How visibility, price and quantity rule are resolved for a location with two catalogs.

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/b2b/manage-catalogs#managing-multiple-catalogs-per-company-location[Manage B2B catalogs: managing multiple catalogs per company location]

The model below encodes only the documented rules, so a configuration can be reasoned about before it is sent to a
shop. It is a local illustration, not Shopify's engine: it does not know about rounding, exchange rules or market
inheritance, and how price breaks of one catalog combine with the lower price of another is not documented, so it takes
the lowest result.

[source,javascript]
----
const base = { 'PC-COL-WHL-1KG': 29.9, 'PC-BRA-WHL-1KG': 29.9, 'PC-ETH-WHL-1KG': 33.9 };

const catalogs = [
  {
    title: 'Wholesale beans 1 kg',
    locations: ['Madrid', 'Berlin'],
    publication: ['PC-COL-WHL-1KG', 'PC-BRA-WHL-1KG'],
    priceList: {
      decreasePercent: 20,
      fixed: { 'PC-BRA-WHL-1KG': 22.0 },
      breaks: { 'PC-COL-WHL-1KG': [{ min: 10, price: 21.5 }, { min: 20, price: 20.0 }] },
      rules: { 'PC-COL-WHL-1KG': { minimum: 4, increment: 2, maximum: 100 } },
    },
  },
  {
    title: 'Spring promotion (pricing only)',
    locations: ['Madrid'],
    publication: null,
    priceList: { decreasePercent: 30, fixed: {}, breaks: {}, rules: {} },
  },
];

const round2 = (n) => Math.round(n * 100) / 100;

export function resolve(location, sku, quantity) {
  const applicable = catalogs.filter((c) => c.locations.includes(location));
  const visible = applicable.some((c) => c.publication && c.publication.includes(sku));
  if (!visible) return { sku, location, visible: false };

  const rule = applicable.map((c) => c.priceList.rules[sku]).find(Boolean)
    ?? { minimum: 1, increment: 1, maximum: null };
  const quantityAllowed =
    quantity >= rule.minimum &&
    (rule.maximum === null || quantity <= rule.maximum) &&
    quantity % rule.increment === 0;

  const candidates = applicable.map((c) => {
    const pl = c.priceList;
    let unit = pl.fixed[sku] ?? round2(base[sku] * (1 - pl.decreasePercent / 100));
    for (const b of (pl.breaks[sku] ?? []).sort((x, y) => x.min - y.min)) if (quantity >= b.min) unit = b.price;
    return { catalog: c.title, unit };
  });
  const best = candidates.reduce((a, b) => (b.unit < a.unit ? b : a));
  return { sku, location, visible, quantity, quantityAllowed, candidates, unit: best.unit, from: best.catalog,
           line: round2(best.unit * quantity) };
}
----

== Payment terms and deposits

Payment terms say when a company pays. Shopify models them in two places: a *template* chosen by the merchant and
attached to a location as the default for its orders, and an instance of *payment terms* with *payment schedules* on an
individual order or draft order.

=== Templates and their types

`paymentTermsTemplates` lists the templates of the shop, optionally filtered by `PaymentTermsType`. A template has an
`id`, a `name`, a `translatedName`, a `description`, a type and, for net terms, `dueInDays`. The template IDs are specific
to a shop, so read them instead of copying the ID from a tutorial.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|`PaymentTermsType` |Meaning |Schedule field required on the order

|`RECEIPT`
|Due on receipt
|None

|`NET`
|Due a number of days after issue
|`issuedAt` on the schedule

|`FIXED`
|Due on a given date
|`dueAt` on the schedule

|`FULFILLMENT`
|Due when the order is fulfilled
|None

|`UNKNOWN`
|Type not recognised
|n/a
|===

[source,graphql]
----
query PaymentTermsTemplates {
  paymentTermsTemplates {
    id
    name
    translatedName
    description
    paymentTermsType
    dueInDays
  }

=== The default terms of a location

Set the terms through the location's `buyerExperienceConfiguration`. Besides `paymentTermsTemplateId` it holds
`checkoutToDraft`, `editableShippingAddress` and `deposit`:

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Field |Effect

|`paymentTermsTemplateId`
|The template applied to B2B orders from the location. With terms set, a buyer can pay later; without them the buyer
pays at checkout.

|`checkoutToDraft`
|When true, the checkout creates a draft order for merchant review instead of an order. This is how a merchant puts a
human approval step in front of fulfilment.

|`editableShippingAddress`
|Whether the buyer can use a different shipping address at checkout.

|`deposit`
|A percentage (`DepositInput.percentage`, 1 to 99) of the order total paid at checkout, with the balance on the terms.
The Plus Help Center page lists deposits as a Plus capability.

|`payNowOnly` (deprecated)
|Derived from the other two; do not write it.
|===

=== Terms on one order or draft order

A draft order can carry terms at creation (`DraftOrderInput.paymentTerms` and `deposit`), and `paymentTermsCreate`,

schedules. The result exposes `due`, `overdue` and the `paymentSchedules` with `issuedAt`, `dueAt`, `totalBalance` and
`balanceDue`, which are the fields to poll for accounts-receivable dashboards.

=== Setting terms from a Shopify Function

A payment customization Function can set terms per checkout instead of per location, which is how rules such as "net 30
only above 500 EUR" are expressed. On 2025-06-18 the target `cart.payment-methods.transform.run` gained the
`paymentTermsSet` operation, which defines net, fixed or event-based terms for the order, with an optional deposit. The
operation is only available on Plus stores. The three term types have these inputs:

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Variant |Fields

|`net`
|`dueInDays` (7, 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90), optional `issuedAt` (the current time when omitted), optional `deposit`

|`fixed`
|`dueAt` (a date), optional `deposit`

|`event`
|`trigger` (`FULFILLMENT_CREATED` or `INVOICE_SENT`), optional `deposit`
|===

Returning no operation leaves the checkout alone, and returning `paymentTermsSet` with no `paymentTerms` removes terms.
The Function below requests net 30 with a 20 percent deposit for company orders of 500 EUR or more, and does nothing for
retail buyers or smaller company orders. It reads the buyer's `purchasingCompany` from the cart.

[source,graphql]
----
query RunInput {
  cart {
    cost {
      subtotalAmount {
        amount
        currencyCode
      }
    }
    buyerIdentity {
      purchasingCompany {
        company {
          name
        }
        location {
          name
        }
      }
    }
  }
}
----

[source,javascript]
----
// @ts-check

const MINIMUM_SUBTOTAL = 500;
const NET_DAYS = 30;
const DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE = 20;

/**
 * @param {import("../generated/api").RunInput} input
 * @returns {import("../generated/api").CartPaymentMethodsTransformRunResult}
 */
export function run(input) {
  const company = input.cart.buyerIdentity?.purchasingCompany;
  const subtotal = parseFloat(input.cart.cost.subtotalAmount.amount);

  // Retail buyers and small company orders keep the default behaviour: pay now.
  if (!company || subtotal < MINIMUM_SUBTOTAL) {
    return { operations: [] };
  }

  return {
    operations: [
      {
        paymentTermsSet: {
          paymentTerms: {
            net: {
              dueInDays: NET_DAYS,
              deposit: { percentage: DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE },
            },
          },
        },
      },
    ],
  };
}
----

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/functions/latest/payment-customization[Payment Customization Function API]

== Draft orders, invoices and imported orders

=== Quote, invoice and convert

Draft orders are the B2B "quote": a merchant or an app prepares an order for a purchasing entity, checks the totals,
sends an invoice and, when the company agrees or pays, completes it into an order. The tutorial is a four-call sequence:
`draftOrderCalculate`, `draftOrderCreate`, `draftOrderInvoiceSend` and `draftOrderComplete`. The purchasing company goes
in `purchasingEntity.purchasingCompany`, which needs all three of `companyId`, `companyLocationId` and
`companyContactId`. The input also carries `poNumber`, `paymentTerms` and `deposit`, `lineItems` (by `variantId` and
`quantity`, or custom items), `billingAddress` and `shippingAddress`, `tags`, `note`, `taxExempt` and
`reserveInventoryUntil`.

=== Importing B2B orders

A merchant moving from another system can import history so that buyers see their full order list in their account.

for the original purchase order number, `processedAt`, `financialStatus`, `currency`, `transactions`, `sourceName` and

Two practical points follow from the guide. In a dedicated B2B store, hide prices, the cart icon, add-to-cart buttons and
other calls to action from visitors who are not B2B buyers, either with the theme editor's store contextualization
settings or with `customer.b2b?`; in a blended store keep the default prices for consumers. And for a Theme Store theme
that must stay eligible for automatic upgrades, do not edit core theme files: hide the existing section or block and add

must treat these responses as private.

== Headless storefronts

image::ecommerce-shopify-b2b-buyer-flow.svg["A B2B buyer's path through a headless storefront: log in with customer accounts, list company locations, select a location, contextualize queries, create the cart with the location, check out",width=780]

The buyer's path from login to a B2B checkout in a headless storefront.

A headless B2B storefront has four jobs, taken from the developer guide: obtain the buyer's access token, obtain a
company location ID, contextualize product queries to get B2B prices, volume prices and quantity rules, and contextualize
the cart so that checkout is a B2B checkout. Without contextualization the store's base prices and publishing are
used, and the buyer sees none of the catalogs, payment terms or other B2B setup.

=== Step 1 and 2: the token and the buyer's locations

The Customer Account API authorization flow produces an `access_token` for the customer; this is the
`customerAccessToken` of the later steps. With that token, a Customer Account API query returns the company contacts of the
customer and the locations they can buy for. If the result has one location, keep its ID. If it has several, build a
location selector, store the chosen ID and redirect there after login. A location is shown with its address and the
payment terms it will use, so the buyer knows what the checkout will do.

[source,graphql]
----
query BuyerLocations {
  customer {
    id
    displayName
    companyContacts(first: 5) {
      nodes {
        id
        title
        company {
          id
          name
        }
        locations(first: 20) {
          nodes {
            id
            name
            shippingAddress {
              countryCode
              city
            }
            buyerExperienceConfiguration {
              paymentTermsTemplate {
                name
                dueInDays
              }
            }
          }
        }
      }
    }
  }
}
----

This query was validated against the Customer Account API schema 2026-04 from `@shopify/hydrogen-react` 2026.4.4, because
the schema for 2026-10 is not served for download; the fields it uses are in the 2026-10 reference pages as well.

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/headless/bring-your-own-stack/b2b#step-2-get-a-company-location-id[Headless with B2B, step 2] and https://shopify.dev/docs/api/customer/latest/objects/CompanyContact[Customer Account API CompanyContact]

=== Step 3: contextualized Storefront API queries

Pass a `buyer` to the `@inContext` directive to contextualize any query. `BuyerInput` has two fields,
`customerAccessToken` (required) and `companyLocationId`. A contextualized product query returns the price for the
buyer's location, the `quantityRule` and the `quantityPriceBreaks` of each variant, which are the fields a quantity
selector and a volume table need.

[source,graphql]
----

  products(first: 5, query: "tag:wholesale") {
    nodes {
      id
      title
      variants(first: 5) {
        nodes {
          id
          sku
          price {
            amount
            currencyCode
          }
          quantityRule {
            minimum
            maximum
            increment
          }
          quantityPriceBreaks(first: 5) {
            nodes {
              minimumQuantity
              price {
                amount
                currencyCode
              }
            }
          }
        }
      }
    }
  }
}

depends on the token and the location, so a server that caches responses by URL would serve one buyer's prices to another;
the guide's caution says to disable caching on routes that return buyer-specific data, and it links to the caching guide
of the legacy Hydrogen framework for how to do that. The `@inContext` directive has other arguments (`country`, `language`,
`channelId`, `preferredLocationId`, `visitorConsent`); in cart operations `buyer` and `country` are ignored, and only
`language` still applies.

Changing the buyer identity of a cart that already has lines can make it invalid. Products not published for the new
location are removed, and products that stay but fall under different quantity rules are left unchanged. Reading the
cart does not fail, but updating it may, and a buyer who goes to checkout with an invalid cart sees the problems as an
error. The codes to handle in `userErrors` and `warnings`:

[cols="3,4",options="header"]
|===
|Code |Meaning

|`MISSING_CUSTOMER_ACCESS_TOKEN`
|The customer access token is required when a company location is set.

|`INVALID_COMPANY_LOCATION`, `BUYER_CANNOT_PURCHASE_FOR_COMPANY_LOCATION`
|The location was not found or is not allowed, or the buyer cannot purchase for it (the second code was added to
`CartErrorCode` on 2025-05-10).

|`MINIMUM_NOT_MET`, `MAXIMUM_EXCEEDED`, `INVALID_INCREMENT`
|A cart line breaks the variant's quantity rule.

|`PRODUCT_UNAVAILABLE_IN_BUYER_LOCATION` (warning)
|A line is not published for the buyer's location.

|`MERCHANDISE_SELLING_PLAN_NOT_APPLICABLE_ON_COMPANY_LOCATION` (warning)
|Only one-time purchase is available for B2B orders, so a subscription line is flagged.
|===

Headless also allows a gated store, where visitors can browse products but see no price and cannot order. Include the
price in the query, and render the add-to-cart button, only when there is a valid session with a stored company location
ID. The same principle as the Liquid `customer.b2b?` check applies, enforced in your own code. Hydrogen is a
framework-agnostic toolkit as of 2026-10; the xref:ecommerce/shopify/storefront-api-and-headless-hydrogen.adoc[Storefront
API and Headless] page explains how it holds the Storefront and Customer Account clients that these queries run on.

== Checkout and Functions

The B2B checkout is the standard Shopify checkout with the location's settings applied: the terms and deposit from
`buyerExperienceConfiguration`, the quantity rules and catalog prices of the cart, tax exemptions of the location, and,
with `checkoutToDraft`, the creation of a draft order for merchant review in place of an order. Customization goes
through the same extension points as any checkout, with B2B hooks in each:

* Functions receive `cart.buyerIdentity.purchasingCompany` in their input, as the payment terms Function above shows, so
  discounts, delivery customizations, payment customizations and validations can depend on the company or the location.
* Cart and Checkout Validation Functions can validate the billing address and the PO number since API version 2026-04
  (changelog of 2026-04-01). They return errors on the field targets `+$.cart.billingAddress.{field}+` and
  `+$.cart.poNumber+`, so a rule such as "company orders require a PO number" no longer needs client-side code. The
  inputs are null outside Cart and Checkout Validation.
* A Function can also impose a review requirement with the `orderReviewAdd` operation; the checkout is then submitted as
  a draft that the merchant approves. The Winter '26 Edition presents this as rules on order value and products, for Plus.
* Checkout UI extensions can read metafields owned by the `COMPANY` and `COMPANY_LOCATION` of a B2B checkout, besides
  those on the cart, customer, product and shop; `owner_type` is optional in 2026-10 and becomes required in 2027-01.
* The Winter '26 Edition also lists checkout and customer account customization per market and for B2B buyers in the
  theme editor, and pickup in store as a delivery option for B2B.

Source: https://shopify.dev/changelog/cart-and-checkout-validation-adds-billing-address-and-po-number-error-targets[Cart and checkout validation adds billing address and PO number error targets] and https://shopify.dev/docs/api/checkout-ui-extensions/latest/configuration[Checkout UI extension configuration]

[[events]]
== Events and integrations

Company data changes through the admin, the API and apps, so an integration that mirrors it to an ERP listens to
webhooks and reconciles with a bulk query. The topics are:

[cols="3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Topic |Fires when

|`companies/create`, `companies/update`, `companies/delete`
|A company is created, changed or deleted

|`company_locations/create`, `company_locations/update`, `company_locations/delete`
|A location is created, changed or deleted (including its settings)

|`company_contacts/create`, `company_contacts/update`, `company_contacts/delete`
|A contact is created, changed or deleted

|`company_contact_roles/assign`, `company_contact_roles/revoke`
|A role is assigned to or revoked from a contact at a location

|`payment_terms/create`, `payment_terms/update`, `payment_terms/delete`
|Payment terms on an order or draft order change

|`draft_orders/create`, `draft_orders/update`, `draft_orders/delete`
|A draft order is created, changed or deleted
|===

The company topics need `read_customers` or `read_companies`; the payment terms topics need `read_payment_terms`; the
catalog objects have no webhook topics of their own in 2026-10, so poll catalogs or react to the Markets topics.

The app configuration for these subscriptions goes in `shopify.app.toml`. This one was validated with `shopify app build
--no-input` using a placeholder client ID and the reserved host `example.invalid`; the build printed
`pocket-coffee-b2b-app built!`. The offline validation does not check topic names, so a mistyped topic still builds, and
only a deploy against Shopify finds it.

[source,toml]
----
client_id = "00000000000000000000000000000000"
name = "pocket-coffee-b2b-app"
application_url = "https://example.invalid"
embedded = true

[access_scopes]
scopes = "read_companies,write_companies,read_customers,read_products,write_products,read_publications,write_publications,read_draft_orders,write_draft_orders,read_orders,write_orders,read_payment_terms,write_payment_terms"

[auth]
redirect_urls = ["https://example.invalid/api/auth"]

[webhooks]
api_version = "2026-10"

  [[webhooks.subscriptions]]
  topics = [
    "companies/create",
    "companies/update",
    "company_locations/create",
    "company_locations/update",
    "company_contacts/create",
    "company_contact_roles/assign",
    "company_contact_roles/revoke",
    "payment_terms/create"
  ]
  uri = "/webhooks/b2b"

  [[webhooks.subscriptions]]
  topics = ["app/uninstalled"]
  uri = "/webhooks/app/uninstalled"

  [[webhooks.subscriptions]]
  uri = "/webhooks/customers/data_request"
  compliance_topics = ["customers/data_request"]

  [[webhooks.subscriptions]]
  uri = "/webhooks/customers/redact"
  compliance_topics = ["customers/redact"]

  [[webhooks.subscriptions]]
  uri = "/webhooks/shop/redact"
  compliance_topics = ["shop/redact"]
----

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/webhooks/subscribe[Subscribe to webhooks] and https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/enums/WebhookSubscriptionTopic[WebhookSubscriptionTopic]

The same subscriptions can be created over the API, with the topic as an enum value (`COMPANIES_CREATE` for
`companies/create`):

[source,graphql]
----
mutation SubscribeCompanies($uri: String!) {
  webhookSubscriptionCreate(topic: COMPANIES_CREATE, webhookSubscription: {uri: $uri, format: JSON}) {
    webhookSubscription {
      id
      topic
    }
    userErrors {
      field
      message
    }
  }
}
----

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/mutations/webhookSubscriptionCreate[webhookSubscriptionCreate]

The ecosystem around B2B is mostly apps. The Winter '26 Edition names eleven apps compatible with B2B for quote
requests, custom buyer roles and shopping lists, and the Spring '26 Edition says QuickBooks syncs B2B orders, PO numbers
and company details and Mailchimp can segment B2B customers. An app that adds those capabilities builds on the objects of
this page.

== A Pocket Coffee wholesale walkthrough

Putting the parts together for the example, an app or a script does the following, in this order, because each step
needs an ID from the one before:

[cols="1,4,4",options="header"]
|===
|Step |Call |Result

|1
|`companyCreate` with the Madrid location and Marta Ruiz
|Company, first location and main contact exist

|2
|`companyLocationCreate` for Berlin
|Second location with its own shipping address and the Madrid billing address

|3
|`companyContactAssignRoles` for both locations
|Marta can act at both locations

|4
|`paymentTermsTemplates(paymentTermsType: NET)`, then `companyLocationUpdate` with the template, `checkoutToDraft: false`
and a 20 percent deposit
|Both locations default to net 30 with a deposit

|5
|`catalogCreate` for both locations, then `priceListCreate` with 20 percent off and `publicationCreate` with the two 1 kg
beans
|Wholesale catalog with a price list and a publication

|6
|`priceListFixedPricesAdd` (Brazil 22.00) and `quantityPricingByVariantUpdate` (Colombia: minimum 4, step 2, maximum
100, breaks at 10 and 20)
|Negotiated prices and order-size rules

|7
|Optional: a second pricing-only catalog for a campaign on Madrid
|Madrid gets the lowest of two prices

|8
|`draftOrderCalculate`, `draftOrderCreate`, `draftOrderInvoiceSend`, `draftOrderComplete` for the first PO
|Order with `purchasingEntity` and net 30 terms

|9
|Storefront: login, `BuyerLocations`, `@inContext(buyer:)`, `cartCreate` with `companyLocationId`
|Cafe Brasa orders on its own from the shop
|===

== Mistakes that cost the most time

* Creating a contact and expecting it to order. Without a role assignment at a location there is no permission; the
  Storefront cart reports `BUYER_CANNOT_PURCHASE_FOR_COMPANY_LOCATION`.
* Giving the app only `write_products` and `write_draft_orders` and then wondering why company mutations fail. Company
  objects need `read_companies` and `write_companies`, or `read_customers` and `write_customers`.
* Copying a payment terms template ID from a tutorial. IDs are specific to a shop, so read them with
  `paymentTermsTemplates`.
* Skipping the publication on a catalog and finding that buyers see nothing (older guide) or whatever the sales channel
  makes available (2026-10 reference). Decide on purpose, and test with a real buyer on a development store of the target
  plan.
* Using several catalogs on a location and assuming the first wins. The lowest listed price wins, and a campaign catalog
  that is cheaper than the negotiated one silently overrides the negotiation.
* Sending `priceListFixedPricesAdd` with more than 250 prices, or expecting it to merge: it replaces the fixed price of a
  variant that already has one.
* Changing a catalog's price list or publication and leaving the previous object orphaned. Delete it, or use
  `deleteDependentResources` when deleting the catalog.
* Importing orders without `priceSet`. The API fills the current variant price and the history is wrong.
* Rendering buyer-specific data from a shared cache.
* Customer accounts: B2B does not work with legacy customer accounts. Headless login code must move to the Customer
  Account API.
* Building a subscription or pre-order flow for B2B. It is not supported, and the cart returns a warning.
