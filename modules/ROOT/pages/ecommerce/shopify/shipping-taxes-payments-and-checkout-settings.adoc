== The money path of a checkout

A Shopify checkout builds the amount a buyer pays in stages. The cart holds the variants, quantities, weights and the
delivery address. Delivery options are produced from the shipping configuration (delivery profiles, or shipping options
attached to markets), from carrier services, local delivery and pickup, and are then optionally reshaped by a Delivery
Customization Function. Taxes are calculated by the tax service selected for each region (Shopify Tax, Basic Tax, Manual
Tax or a tax app) and displayed as included or added. Payment methods come from Shopify Payments, third-party gateways,
wallets and manual methods, and are optionally reshaped by a Payment Customization Function. The order records the
transactions and the capture setting decides when the money is taken.

image::ecommerce-shopify-checkout-money-flow.svg["How a Shopify checkout builds the amount a buyer pays, and where merchants and apps change each stage",width=860]

Buyers never see these stages as separate steps; one-page checkout shows them on a single page. The stages matter to

=== Delivery profiles in the admin

Shipping is configured in Settings, Shipping and delivery. Each store has a General profile that applies to all products;
all existing and new products are added to it, and a product or variant that is not in a custom profile uses the General
rates. A store can create up to 99 custom profiles when different products need different rates, for example fragile
products that need a more expensive service. A product or variant belongs to exactly one profile at a time: adding it to
a second profile removes it from the first, and removing a variant from a custom profile returns it to the General
profile (the API field `variantsToDissociate` is documented the same way).

When a profile is created, every location that can fulfill online orders is added to it in one location group, so that
all those locations share the same zones and rates. A location can be moved into its own location group to give it
different zones and rates, removed from a group (shown under "Not fulfilling from"), or added to an existing group. If
the last location of a group is removed, the group's zones and rates are deleted. Locations that are deactivated are
labeled Inactive: rates can be added to them, but they are not used at checkout until the location is activated. A
location created after a profile is added to every profile as not fulfilling. Some apps that manage fulfillment, such as
on-demand printing apps, are added to the profile as locations automatically and can change the setup.

A new store gets default rates created from the store location, domestic and international, and the Help Center warns
that these frequently include a free shipping rate; they should be reviewed before the store accepts orders. Staff or
collaborators need the Products and Manage settings permissions to work with shipping profiles.

=== Rate types

Within a zone, a rate has one of four types:

[cols="2,5",options=header]
|===
|Rate type
|Behavior

|Flat
|One price for the zone. By default a flat rate applies once per order, not once per product; charging per product needs separate rates or an app.
|Order amount
|Price-based ranges, for example 24.99 USD from 0 to 100 USD, 9.99 USD from 100.01 to 200 USD and free above 200.01 USD. The maximum of the last range can be left empty.
|Weight
|Weight ranges evaluated on the combined weight of the items that ship from the same location with the same option. Every product needs a weight, or the rate does not calculate correctly; the store default package weight is added to each product.
|Carrier or app calculated
|The carrier or an app returns the price from weight, dimensions and destination. Built-in carrier integrations are available on all plans; connecting your own carrier accounts (UPS, FedEx, USPS, Canada Post, Australia Post) is third-party carrier-calculated shipping, which needs a plan or fee as described above.
|===

Any rate can be made free by leaving the price empty or entering 0, and the "Offer free shipping" option adds a minimum
order amount above which the rate costs nothing. Flat rates can show a transit time (a suggested value or a custom range
such as 3-5 days) that feeds the delivery estimate at checkout. Flat rates in local currencies exist only for stores
that use Shopify Payments: a rate set per market currency is shown as set, whereas automatic conversion of a EUR rate
shows a converted amount (the Help Center gives roughly 120 SEK for a 10 EUR rate); separate zones per region give fixed
prices instead of converted ones.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/shipping-rates/setting-up-shipping-rates[Shopify Help Center: Setting up shipping zones and rates]

=== How rates combine when a cart spans profiles and locations

Carts whose items sit in different profiles or ship from different locations get a single combined rate. The rules, as
documented, are these:

* Rates with the same name are added together and shown under that name, even if they are in different profiles. If the
  rate names differ, the cheapest options are added together and shown as "Shipping".
* Price-based rates apply to the cart total; weight-based rates apply to each product's weight plus the store default
  package weight, and the resulting rates are then combined.
* If an order must ship from several locations of the same location group with a flat rate, one flat fee is charged: a
  5 USD group charged once even when two locations are needed.
* If several locations in the same group could fulfill all items, fulfillment priority picks the location, and the other
  location's rate is free.
* If an order needs different location groups, their rates are added.

The consequence is that the way profiles and rate names are organized changes what the buyer pays, and the Help Center
states that a combined rate can lead to higher shipping costs. Two Pocket Coffee consequences: the Standard rate in the
Spanish zone and the same-named Standard rate in a second profile for the Starter kit would be added together unless the
profiles are deliberately named and priced as one rate, and the shop should keep every variant of the same parcel in
one profile.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/shipping-profiles/combined-shipping-rates[Shopify Help Center: Calculating shipping rates from different shipping profiles]

=== Backup rates

When all app or carrier calculated rates of a shipment fail and no other eligible rate exists, backup rates are shown so
that checkout does not stall. They apply to the whole store by default and need no setup, only when at least one app or
carrier-calculated rate exists. Shopify-supported carriers provide them where available (USPS, Canada Post, Evri,
Chronopost, Poste Italiane and Correos in the listed countries), country-specific rates built from historical data cover
the other zones, and legacy custom backup rates by order value or weight still work. Flat rates for the same destinations
are always shown when eligible, so they can be chosen even while calculated rates work. A product without a weight is
treated as weight 0 when backup rates are chosen, which can produce wrong prices. Since 2025-05-22 a 3xx or 4xx response
from a carrier also triggers backup rates instead of a hard failure.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/shipping-rates/backup-rates[Shopify Help Center: Backup shipping rates]

Inside a location group, `locationsToAdd`, `locationsToRemove` (up to 250 each per request) and `locations` (move to

5 per request). A zone takes `countries` (each with `code`, `provinces`, `includeAllProvinces` or `restOfWorld`) and
`methodDefinitionsToCreate`. A method definition has `name`, `description`, `active`, a `rateDefinition` with the price,
or a `participant` for a carrier or app rate, and `priceConditionsToCreate` or `weightConditionsToCreate` whose
`operator` is `GREATER_THAN_OR_EQUAL_TO` or `LESS_THAN_OR_EQUAL_TO`. A condition is either a money amount or a weight
with the unit `KILOGRAMS`, `GRAMS`, `POUNDS` or `OUNCES`.

Two changes to the shape of these objects are published and affect integrations that read merchant-managed profiles.
Starting with the unstable API version, a shipping option can contain several tiered rates, and
`DeliveryMethodDefinition.rateGroups` and `DeliveryRateDefinition.conditions` are meant to replace the legacy

deprecation and become obsolete when merchants use multiple tiered rates. The 2026-10 schema validated for this page
still contains only the legacy fields. Apps that write merchant-managed profiles have to move to the new write input
(`rateGroupsToCreate`, `rateGroupsToUpdate`, `freeConditions`, `currencyCode`), whereas apps that read and write
profiles that they own need no action.

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/mutations/carrierServiceCreate[Admin GraphQL API: carrierServiceCreate]

The requirements and behaviors of the callback are:

* The store must be on the Advanced plan or higher, on a lower plan with yearly billing or with the feature added for a
  monthly fee, or be a development store that is not on the Basic plan. If the store later stops meeting this, the
  association is deactivated.
* Shopify sends a POST with a `rate` object (origin, destination, items with grams, price and `requires_shipping`,
  `currency`, `locale`). Since 2025-11-04 the payload also contains the order totals (`subtotal_price`, `total_price`,
  `discount_amount`) and the customer's id and tags. For API-created services only the address fields `address1`,
  `address2`, `city`, `zip`, `province` and `country` are filled.
* The response is `+{"rates": [...]}+` where every rate has `service_name`, `description`, `service_code`, `currency` and
  `total_price` (as a string in minor units, so 1290 for 12.90 EUR), and optionally `phone_required`,
  `min_delivery_date` and `max_delivery_date`.
* An empty array with a 2xx status means that the service cannot handle the request; a 40x or 50x status forces backup
  rates; redirects are followed only on the same domain; there is no retry.
* The read timeout depends on the requests per minute of the shop and app pair: 10 seconds under 1,500, 5 seconds from
  1,500 to 3,000 and 3 seconds above 3,000.
* Shopify caches identical requests (same variants, quantities, weights, origin and destination) for 15 minutes after a
  success and 30 seconds after an error.
* From API version 2026-10, `carrierServiceCreate` only registers the service; it is no longer added automatically to
  the General shipping profile or its zones. The rates must be added to a profile by the merchant in the admin (the
  recommended path) or programmatically through the profile APIs, or merchants see no rates from the new service.
  Older supported API versions keep the automatic behavior until they are retired.

A minimal callback for Pocket Coffee that offers an Express rate (12.90 EUR) inside Spain for parcels up to 5 kg:

[source,javascript]
----
import { createServer } from "node:http";

const EXPRESS_PRICE_CENTS = 1290; // 12.90 EUR
const MAX_GRAMS = 5000; // parcel limit

export function ratesFor(rate) {
  const grams = rate.items
    .filter((item) => item.requires_shipping)
    .reduce((sum, item) => sum + item.grams * item.quantity, 0);

  // Express is offered inside Spain only, and only below the parcel limit.
  if (rate.destination.country !== "ES" || grams > MAX_GRAMS) {
    return []; // empty array with a 2xx status: "cannot handle this request"
  }

  // Delivery dates use the format shown in the carrier service documentation.
  const formatDate = (date) => date.toISOString().replace("T", " ").replace(/\.\d+Z$/, " +0000");
  const tomorrow = new Date(Date.now() + 24 * 3600 * 1000);
  const dayAfter = new Date(Date.now() + 48 * 3600 * 1000);
  return [
    {
      service_name: "Express",
      service_code: "pc_express",
      description: "Next working day",
      currency: "EUR",
      total_price: String(EXPRESS_PRICE_CENTS), // subunits, as a string
      min_delivery_date: formatDate(tomorrow),
      max_delivery_date: formatDate(dayAfter),
    },
  ];
}

const server = createServer((req, res) => {
  if (req.method !== "POST" || req.url !== "/carrier-rates") {
    res.writeHead(404).end();
    return;
  }
  let body = "";
  req.on("data", (chunk) => (body += chunk));
  req.on("end", () => {
    try {
      const { rate } = JSON.parse(body);
      res.writeHead(200, { "Content-Type": "application/json" });
      res.end(JSON.stringify({ rates: ratesFor(rate) }));
    } catch {
      res.writeHead(400).end(); // a 4xx makes Shopify fall back to backup rates
    }
  });
});

server.listen(Number(process.env.PORT ?? 8787), "127.0.0.1");
----

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-rest/latest/resources/carrierservice[Admin REST API: CarrierService (rate request and response format)]

The callback was started locally and called with three sample requests built from the documented request format. A
Spanish destination with two 250 g bags returned the rate (`"total_price":"1290"`, status 200). A German destination and
a Spanish cart of 8 kg both returned `+{"rates":[]}+` with status 200, and a request body that is not JSON returned 400,
which Shopify turns into backup rates. The REST page documents the wire format; the REST Admin API itself is legacy since
2024-10-01 and new public apps must use GraphQL since 2025-04-01, so registration goes through the GraphQL mutation
above.

=== Local delivery, pickup in store, pickup points, split shipping

Besides shipping, a store can offer other delivery methods:

* Local delivery is activated per location, within a radius or a list of postal codes. The maximum radius is 160
  kilometers or 100 miles and does not cross borders. A location has up to 10 delivery zones, each with up to 3 extra
  price-based rules, and a postal-code list is at most 3,000 characters. The option name "Local delivery" cannot be
  changed. Eligibility requires that all physical items come from one location with enough stock, that Google can verify
  the address, and that the buyer has not chosen an accelerated checkout other than Shop Pay in the first step. B2B
  checkouts do not support it, but a B2B draft order with local delivery preselected does.
* Pickup in store is activated per location with a processing time, optional store transfers (inventory moved from other
  locations when the pickup location lacks stock), transfer exclusions by collection and a "ready for pickup"
  notification. When a store transfer is needed, the checkout shows an estimated pickup time that includes the transfer.
* Pickup points (post offices, relay points, lockers) are early access for eligible stores and carriers. Apps that
  generate pickup point options with Shopify Functions are available only to custom apps on development stores or Plus
  stores.
* Ship and pickup in one order lets Plus and Enterprise merchants offer shipping or pickup per item in one checkout; it
  was in feature preview on 2026-10-27. Checkouts then have several delivery groups and orders can contain fulfillment
  orders with different delivery methods (`SHIPPING` and `PICK_UP`), so apps must not assume one method per order.
* Split shipping happens when a cart cannot ship in one shipment: pre-orders, subscriptions, items from different
  locations, products from different profiles. The buyer sees the number of shipments and a method per shipment; by
  default the lowest total cost is selected. The setting is retired for stores upgraded to shipping options by market,
  where split shipping is always on.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/delivery-methods/local-delivery[Shopify Help Center: Setting up local delivery for online orders]

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/split-shipping[Shopify Help Center: Split shipping in Shopify Checkout]

=== Market-driven shipping (shipping options by market)

Shopify is replacing merchant-owned delivery profiles, location groups and zones with shipping options attached to each
market. A market has options (flat, order-value, weight or carrier-calculated), and inside one option the rate can vary
by product and location conditions. Product conditions are all products or collections (rates are no longer assigned to
single variants, so a group of variants needs a collection), and location conditions pick the fulfillment locations.
When several rates in the same option match a cart, only the highest matching rate is charged instead of the sum, and
when several options match, the option prices are added. The upgrade combines rates with the same name into one option,
turns the former product groupings into unpublished collections with the prefix `[Shipping]`, can create sub-region
markets to keep the former zone coverage, and keeps split shipping on. Staff need the Markets permission to manage it
because existing roles do not receive it automatically.

The timeline (the dates are targets and may shift) is: feature preview from 2026-07-01 (selected when a development
store is created), merchant rollout beginning on 2026-10-01 for stores whose apps are compatible, and all merchants on market-driven shipping by 2027-07-01.
Until then the Help Center page for shipping options by market is labeled early access and only for certain stores, and a
store keeps its profile-based setup until it is upgraded; a merchant can opt out during a window after the upgrade.

For apps the rule is simple. App-owned profiles keep working with `deliveryProfileCreate` and `deliveryProfileUpdate`
and can now use `coversAllItems`. The merchant-owned profile fields and mutations (`deliveryProfile`, `deliveryProfiles`,
`deliveryProfilesCount`, `deliveryProfileLocationGroup`, `deliveryProfileLocationGroups`, `deliveryProfileCreate`,
`deliveryProfileUpdate`, `deliveryProfileRemove`) no longer represent the live merchant configuration on a shop that
uses the new model: reads may return a stale snapshot and writes can succeed without changing anything. Readers should
switch to Contextual Product Feeds (for sales channels) or read `Market.delivery`, writers should become app-profile
integrations or write with `marketCreate` and `marketUpdate`, and the model of a shop is detected with
`ShopFeatures.marketDrivenShipping`. Apps that ship from their own locations (fulfillment networks, print on demand) fit
app-owned profiles; carrier-rate apps for the merchant's own locations need the Markets API because, before the shop is
upgraded, the merchant's rates take precedence at a location that appears in both profiles.

=== Customizing delivery options with a Shopify Function

A Delivery Customization Function changes the options that exist at checkout without changing the stored rates. It can
hide an option, rename it and move it to another position; the target is `cart.delivery-options.transform.run`. The
carrier name is prepended automatically when a carrier-calculated option is renamed (UPS Standard can become UPS Standard
Shipping, but never just Standard Shipping), and reordering must not push a more expensive option above the cheapest one
as the default. Shopify caches delivery options during checkout and may reuse function results. Since 2026-09-15 on API
version 2026-10 a delivery option exposes metafields to the function input, so a delivery option generator and a
customization function can exchange structured data instead of parsing titles. The CLI template is created with
`shopify app generate extension --template delivery_customization --flavor vanilla-js --name pocket-coffee-delivery`;
that command needs a logged-in organization and was not run. The extension below was created from the template files
instead (the schema and structure of `functions-delivery-customization-js` in Shopify's extension templates repository).

The Pocket Coffee rule: show the Express option only to destinations in a configurable list of countries (Spain by
default) and call the Standard option "Standard (Coffee Club)" for customers with the tag Coffee Club. The tag test uses
an aliased `hasAnyTag` field in the input query.

// @ts-check

/**
 * @typedef {import("../generated/api").CartDeliveryOptionsTransformRunInput} CartDeliveryOptionsTransformRunInput
 * @typedef {import("../generated/api").CartDeliveryOptionsTransformRunResult} CartDeliveryOptionsTransformRunResult
 */

/** @type {CartDeliveryOptionsTransformRunResult} */
const NO_CHANGES = { operations: [] };

/**
 * @param {CartDeliveryOptionsTransformRunInput} input
 * @returns {CartDeliveryOptionsTransformRunResult}
 */
export function cartDeliveryOptionsTransformRun(input) {
  const configuration = input?.deliveryCustomization?.metafield?.jsonValue ?? {};
  const expressCountries = configuration.expressCountries ?? ["ES"];
  const isClubMember = input.cart.buyerIdentity?.customer?.isClubMember ?? false;

  const operations = [];
  for (const group of input.cart.deliveryGroups) {
    const country = group.deliveryAddress?.countryCode;
    for (const option of group.deliveryOptions) {
      if (option.title === "Express" && !expressCountries.includes(country)) {
        operations.push({ deliveryOptionHide: { deliveryOptionHandle: option.handle } });
      } else if (option.title === "Standard" && isClubMember) {
        operations.push({
          deliveryOptionRename: {
            deliveryOptionHandle: option.handle,
            title: "Standard (Coffee Club)",
          },
        });
      }
    }
  }
  return operations.length ? { operations } : NO_CHANGES;
}

== Taxes

=== Responsibility and tax services

Tax is the merchant's responsibility. Shopify calculates and, with Shopify Tax and automated filing, can file, but it does
not remit or file taxes otherwise, and the Help Center repeats on every tax page that merchants should confirm rates with
a tax professional. The tax service available depends on the store location:

[cols="3,5",options=header]
|===
|Store location
|Available tax services

|United States, Canada, European Union, United Kingdom
|Shopify Tax, or Manual Tax to set rates yourself. In Canada, the United Kingdom and the European Union Basic Tax is available only for regions in which the store was set up to collect tax before 2026-05-13 and not if the store uses Manual Tax there; in the United States stores cannot enroll in Basic Tax as of 2025-07-14.
|Norway, Switzerland, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore
|Shopify's Basic Tax service.
|Every other country
|Manual Tax.
|Everywhere
|Paid third-party tax apps instead of Shopify's tax settings.
|===

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/taxes[Shopify Help Center: Taxes]

=== Displaying tax: included or added

By default prices are shown without tax and tax is added at checkout: a 100 USD product at 15 percent shows 100 USD and

=== Overrides, exemptions and product tax data

* A single product can be taxed or not with "Charge tax on this product" (Admin API: `ProductVariant.taxable`). For a
  group, an override applies a tax rate to a collection of manually added products (not smart collections and not
  collections populated by an app) or to shipping charges, and the percentage entered is the amount to collect, not the
  amount exempted. Set 0 percent to exempt. An override beats the product category, and an unpublished collection works
  as an override carrier.
* A customer's tax exemption always wins over overrides. Customer exemptions can be set in the profile or with
  `customerAddTaxExemptions` (scope `write_customers`), whose enum covers rules such as
  `EU_REVERSE_CHARGE_EXEMPTION_RULE`; B2B company locations carry tax IDs and exemptions.
* With Shopify Tax, product categories from the Standard Product Taxonomy decide exemptions and reduced rates, which
  reduces the need for overrides; with Manual Tax the overrides do the job.
* "Company VAT number" in the checkout customer information triggers a reverse charge for a valid number on an eligible
  EU or UK cross-border order: VAT is removed and the customer reports it.
* Charge tax on shipping rates is a global setting only for countries where tax on shipping is not automatic; in
  Australia, Canada, the European Union, New Zealand, Norway, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States
  shipping is taxed where applicable regardless of it. When items in an order are not taxable the tax on shipping is
  proportional (by quantity, by price or exempt when everything is exempt, depending on the country), and in the EU and
  UK it follows the proportion of each VAT rate in the order.
* Digital goods sold to EU consumers are charged VAT at the customer's country rate; the Help Center lists OSS, Non-Union
  OSS and IOSS (physical goods only) and asks to collect two pieces of evidence of the buyer's location.
* Duties and import taxes can be charged at checkout when carriers support DDP labels, Shopify Fulfillment Network is not
  used and HS codes are set on products; the setting is managed in Taxes and duties and per market.

=== Tax apps and the Tax Platform

A third-party tax app replaces Shopify's tax service for the store; the two cannot be active together. It needs an
account at the provider and complete location addresses, keeps tax-inclusive pricing, the taxable flag, customer
non-taxable settings and duties collection active, and falls back to the settings in the admin when the app is
unavailable. Imported historical orders are sent to the provider unless they carry the tag `import` or `imported`.
Shopify's Tax Platform for building tax calculation apps is available by invitation only to selected tax software
companies: the `taxAppConfigure` mutation (scope `write_taxes`, restricted to tax calculation apps) marks the app ready,
the `tax_calculation` extension template defines the calculation endpoint, and requests and the `tax_summaries/create`
webhook have received fields over time (merchant business entity since 2026-04-01, customer and company metafields,
cart line properties, global IDs on 2026-10-01). `ProductVariant.taxCode` is deprecated since 2025-10 because the Avalara
AvaTax app it served is discontinued; it applies only to stores that had that app. A change in 2026-10 recalculates
taxes when the shipping address of an order is updated.

== Payments

=== Choosing providers

Customers pay with the methods activated in Settings, Payments, Payment providers. A store can use Shopify Payments, a
third-party provider or both, and add PayPal, Amazon Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay, manual methods and others.
Three facts constrain the choice: Shopify Payments exists only in a list of countries (Spain is one of them), stores
that sell subscription products need Shopify Payments as the primary gateway, and customers in Finland get no payment
method preselected to comply with regulation.

Shopify Payments is the integrated processor. It needs two-step authentication on the account, accepts the major methods
as soon as the store exists, and charges no third-party transaction fee on orders processed through Shopify Payments,
Shop Pay and Shop Pay Installments or on manual methods such as cash on delivery and bank transfer; PayPal is exempt from
the fee when Shopify Payments is the primary provider. It offers test mode, payouts with a schedule, a store currency
setting for display and payout currencies, and "managed payment methods", which activate local methods by region (for
example Bancontact for Belgium or BLIK for Poland) and personalize their order at checkout; that feature requires
Shopify Network Intelligence to be activated in the customer privacy settings.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments[Shopify Help Center: Shopify Payments]

A third-party provider is either direct (the buyer pays on the store) or external (the buyer pays on a page hosted
elsewhere). More than 100 card providers are available, and a provider can be the only card processor or can run
alongside Shopify Payments. Third-party transaction fees apply to every transaction processed through third-party and
alternate gateways, even when Shopify Payments is activated. Using Shopify Payments next to one direct third-party
provider is available on request: the provider remains the primary processor for cards, Shopify Payments handles Shop
Pay and local payment methods that the provider does not offer and can recover failed card payments. Wallets are routed
to the provider that offers them, and the recovery applies only to credit and debit cards (not to wallets, local methods
or recurring subscription charges). Fees there are the third-party fee for the provider, the standard Shopify Payments
rate plus a 1.25 percent premium for Shopify Payments transactions and the same for recovered payments; Shopify Capital
eligibility counts both providers, Shopify Protect covers only Shopify Payments transactions, and multi-entity stores can
use only Shopify Payments as a direct provider. The feature is in early access for Plus stores with a contract.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/using-shopify-payments-with-a-third-party-provider[Shopify Help Center: Using Shopify Payments with a third-party provider]

Manual payment methods (cash on delivery, bank deposit, money order, custom) create unpaid orders that the merchant
marks as paid after receiving the money; there are no transaction fees. Custom manual methods have a name, additional
details and payment instructions; the names Bank Deposit, Cash, Cash on Delivery (COD), custom, External Credit, External
Debit, Gift Card, Money Order and Store Credit are reserved. In the API `orderMarkAsPaid` (scope `write_orders` and the
mark_orders_as_paid permission) performs the same action.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/manual-payments[Shopify Help Center: Manual payment methods]

=== Authorization and capture

A card payment is authorized (checked and held) and then captured (charged) within the provider's authorization period.
The store setting in Settings, Payments, Payment capture chooses when:

* Automatically at checkout (the default): the payment is captured at the sale.
* Automatically when the order is fulfilled: authorized at checkout and captured when the whole order is fulfilled,
  provided the authorization has not expired; a notification email can warn one day before expiry.
* Automatically per fulfillment (Plus only): captured as line items are fulfilled.
* Manually: the order stays Authorized until the merchant captures it, which suits fraud review, accounting rules that
  require capture on fulfillment, regulations that require cash on delivery to be offered, or multiple captures.

Capture settings apply to online store orders only, not to POS. Providers that do not support manual capture capture
automatically, and combining a Shopify capture setting with an app that also captures can conflict. The Admin API reads
the same state in `Order.transactions` (`kind`, `status`, `gateway`, `manuallyCapturable`, `authorizationExpiresAt`) and
captures with `orderCapture`, which takes the order id, the parent authorization transaction id, the amount, the
currency and `finalCapture`.

=== Building a payment gateway with a payments extension

Third-party gateways that integrate with Shopify checkout are payments extensions built on the Payments Platform. They
can be built only by approved Payments Partners (custom payments extensions are limited to eligible Plus merchants), and
a public listing requires meeting the published criteria. The five types are:

* Credit card payments, with 3D Secure and optional UI extensibility for extra fields such as installments.
* Custom credit card payments, where the partner defines the hosted fields.
* Alternative payment extensions (onsite), where partners collect extra fields and complete the payment with or without
  redirecting the buyer.
* Offsite payments, which redirect buyers to an app-hosted page.
* Redeemable payments for third-party gift cards.

The supported operations are charge, refund, authorize, capture and void, and the methods include cards, buy now pay
later and installments, bank transfers, cryptocurrency, cash and ATM and wallets (with limitations). The lifecycle: a
merchant installs the app, configures it, the app marks itself ready through the Payments Apps API, the merchant
activates it and deactivates the legacy gateway to avoid duplicates. The Payments Apps API endpoint is
`+https://{shop_domain}/payments_apps/api/2026-10/graphql.json+`, requires an approved partner and resolves, pends or
rejects payment sessions and rejects or resolves captures, refunds and voids. The scopes `write_payment_gateways` and
`write_payment_sessions` are granted automatically through the extension and are omitted from the first deployment of
`shopify.app.toml`.

build`:

=== Customizing payment methods with a Shopify Function

A Payment Customization Function changes the methods shown in checkout: hide, rename, reorder, set payment terms and, for
B2B, add a review requirement. The target is `cart.payment-methods.transform.run`. The documented limits are: at most 25
active payment customization functions per store; the function input always lists all payment methods, but operations on
restricted methods have no effect; hiding by placement (the Express section versus the payment section) works only on
Plus and elsewhere the whole method is hidden; wallets and the native gift card field cannot be renamed (names that are
logos, such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay), wallets can be removed from the Express section but not reordered;
Point of Sale does not run payment customizations; in Shop Pay they apply only to the native gift card field; payment
terms apply to the single order, exist only on Plus, cannot be set on accelerated checkouts, and a D2C checkout with
payment terms shows only deferred options. In the United States and Canada, stores below Plus can customize only non-card
methods.

The Pocket Coffee rule: hide the cash on delivery method when the cart total is above a limit stored in a metafield
(100 EUR by default). Payment method ids are opaque, so the function matches by name.

/** @type {CartPaymentMethodsTransformRunResult} */

/**

export function cartPaymentMethodsTransformRun(input) {
  const configuration = JSON.parse(input?.paymentCustomization?.metafield?.value ?? "{}");

=== Testing payments without real money

There is no real-money test on a development store: dev stores are always password protected, cannot be transferred and
do not support real transactions, so tests use one of three gateways.

* The Test payment gateway: a payment provider any store can activate after choosing a paid plan, replacing the card
  provider while active (deactivate Shopify Payments by switching to a third-party provider first). It accepts the name on
  card "Test payment gateway", any future expiry, any 3-digit CVV and the card numbers 1 (approved), 2 (declined) and 3
  (gateway failure). Real cards are rejected. It does not work with Shopify POS or subscription products; the test amount
  must exceed the equivalent of 1 USD.
* Shopify Payments test mode (Settings, Payments, Manage, Enable test mode): real cards are not accepted for orders, but
  express wallets such as Apple Pay and Google Pay can still complete with real cards and no charge (check for the test
  banner on the order), local methods such as iDEAL are not available, and POS readers, Shop Pay Installments and PayPal
  Wallet are not covered. Published test card numbers include 4242424242424242 (Visa), 5555555555554444 (Mastercard) and
  378282246310005 (American Express, 4-digit security code); failure cards include 4000000000000002 (declined),
  4000000000009995 (insufficient funds), 4000000000000069 (expired) and 4000000000000259 (dispute). The expiry must be in
  the future and the name needs at least two words. A Shop Pay test card is added by starting the card nickname with
  `test_card`.
* Test mode of a third-party provider, using that provider's own test credentials.

Test orders and simulated transactions do not appear in payouts or reports. Customers cannot place live orders while a
payment provider is in test mode, and shipping labels bought for test orders are charged, so test orders must not be
fulfilled. B2B tests need customer accounts and a test company with a location.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/test-orders/payments-test-mode[Shopify Help Center: Activating a payment gateway in test mode]

== Checkout settings

=== Settings, Checkout

Checkout settings live under Settings, Checkout and take up to 30 seconds to appear in the checkout. The main groups are:

[cols="3,5",options=header]
|===
|Setting
|What it controls

|Customer contact method
|Email (the default on new stores) or "Phone number or email"; a link to track orders with the Shop app; whether customers must sign in to a customer account first. Requiring sign-in hides accelerated checkout buttons in the cart so that nobody bypasses it. Requiring both email and phone is not possible; make the shipping phone required instead.
|Customer information
|Full name (first and last name, or last name only), Company name (not included, optional or required), Company VAT number (not included or optional), Address line 2 and Shipping address phone number (not included, optional, required). They apply to the customer profile and shipping address, not billing, and not to B2B checkouts. Varying fields by country needs a Plus checkout app. Some regional components are fixed by country.
|Marketing opt-in
|Email checkbox on checkout, sign-in or both, with regions where it is preselected (Shopify's recommendation or your choice); SMS opt-in that cannot be preselected; WhatsApp in some regions. Labels can be customized.
|Additional tax fields
|Extra required tax fields shown after the payment details in some countries; passport numbers are accepted for foreign nationals in most cases.
|Tipping
|Up to 3 percentage presets plus a custom amount, hide until chosen, calculated on the subtotal before tax and shipping, limited to 100 percent of the order and 1,000 USD, not available with Shop Pay Installments or with selling plans; offsite gateways may not recognize the tip line.
|Address collection
|Allow different billing and shipping addresses, require them to match (upgraded checkout, recommended only with high fraud; not applied to pickup, local delivery, draft order and B2B checkouts), address autocompletion and shipping address validation.
|Order processing
|A confirmation step (Review order page) that is on by default only where required by law, such as Japan and Europe; manual or automatic fulfillment (manual by default; automatic only for digital goods, gift cards or fulfillment services, and it needs captured payment); automatic archiving.
|Add-to-cart limit
|A maximum per variant in one cart, on by default, recommended from sales history (50 below 250 sales); it exists to avoid revealing stock and does not apply to POS, draft orders, B2B catalogs with quantity rules, B2B customers, untracked inventory and continue-selling variants.
|Checkout rules
|Cart and checkout validation Functions the merchant installs; a rule can block the order or allow it to complete on error.
|Store policies, language
|Policies shown in checkout and the checkout translations.
|===

=== The checkout and accounts editor and its API

Appearance and apps are edited in the checkout and accounts editor (Settings, Checkout, Configurations), separate from the
theme editor. It sets branding for checkout, Thank you and Order status, customer accounts pages and sign-in, installs
apps and switches the layout between one-page checkout (the default) and three-page checkout. Customizations survive a
layout switch, although the header differs and a background image may need to become a color. Configurations can be
active or draft, and checkout and accounts pages can be adapted per market, which requires the Advanced or Plus plan.
Apps that customize the Thank you and Order status pages and all customer accounts pages work on every paid plan,
whereas apps for the information, shipping and payment steps and access to the Checkout Branding API are Plus only. On
the Thank you and Order status pages `checkout.liquid` and additional scripts were sunset on 2025-08-28 and script tags
on those pages ended on 2025-08-28 for Plus and 2026-08-26 for the other plans; `checkout.liquid` is unsupported on the
information, shipping and payment steps; Shopify Scripts stopped executing on 2026-06-30.

The Admin API for branding and configuration is `checkoutAndAccountsConfigurations` and
`checkoutAndAccountsConfigurationUpdate`. It requires `write_checkout_and_accounts_configurations` (or the approval-gated
`write_checkout_settings`), the user permission `manage_checkout_settings`, access to the editor, and for overrides the
Markets permissions. The older `checkoutBrandingUpsert`, `checkoutBranding`, `checkoutProfile` and `checkoutProfiles`
operations are deprecated in the 2026-10 schema in favor of the new ones.

[source,graphql]
----
query CheckoutConfigurations {
  checkoutAndAccountsConfigurations(first: 10) {
    nodes {
      id
      name
      isPublished
    }
  }
}
----

Source: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/queries/checkoutAndAccountsConfigurations[Admin GraphQL API: checkoutAndAccountsConfigurations]

----
mutation BrandCheckout($id: ID!, $configuration: CheckoutAndAccountsConfigurationInput!) {

checkout header with a divider. The changelog also lists a unified branding Checkout and Accounts Configuration API

extensibility page of this subsection.

=== Cart permalinks

A cart permalink builds a checkout from a URL: the store domain, `/cart/`, then one or more `variant_id:quantity` pairs
separated by commas, followed by optional query parameters for line item properties, pre-filled checkout fields, a sales
channel attribution, discounts, tracking, the locale, `payment=shop_pay` for a Shop Pay checkout, and
`storefront=true` to land on the cart page instead of the checkout. The same link creates a new checkout for each
customer. For Pocket Coffee (the domain and variant ids are placeholders):

[source,text]
----
https://your-store.myshopify.com/cart/1001:2,1004:1
https://your-store.myshopify.com/cart/1001:1?payment=shop_pay&discount=CLUB10
https://your-store.myshopify.com/cart/1001:1?storefront=true
----

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/cart-permalink[Shopify Help Center: Cart permalinks]

=== Bot protection and other safeguards

Bot protection (Plus, activated through Plus support) is a fairness control for flash sales: it can protect up to 500
individual products or all products of the Online Store channel (not collections), one event at a time for at most 60
minutes, with a reCAPTCHA or hCaptcha challenge, and an active event cannot be edited, only deactivated. It does not
address payment fraud. Fraud analysis on orders, chargeback monitoring programs and the add-to-cart limit are the other
checkout-side safeguards.

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/bot-protection[Shopify Help Center: Protecting your store from selling to bots]

== Pocket Coffee end to end

Putting the pieces together for the Spanish shop, in the order a merchant would do them (the admin steps come from the
Help Center and were not executed):

. Products: weights on every variant (0.25 kg and 1 kg), `requiresShipping` on, harmonized system code and country of
  origin set, `taxable` on (the `productVariantsBulkUpdate` example above).
. Shipping: a General profile with the Spain zone (Standard 4.90 EUR below 40 EUR, Free shipping from 40 EUR) and the
  European Union zone (9.90 EUR up to 5 kg), or the equivalent options on the Spain and EU markets once the store is
  upgraded; rate names kept identical across profiles where they should be added together, and different where they
  should not.
. Optional Express from the carrier-service callback; remember that from API 2026-10 the carrier rate has to be added to
  a profile or market by the merchant or the app.
. A Delivery Customization Function for the Coffee Club label and Express restriction.
. Taxes: Shopify Tax for the EU, a registration for Spain and any OSS scheme the business uses, tax-inclusive prices,
  product categories on the beans, Coffee Club customers not tax exempt, VAT number field enabled only if B2B customers
  are expected.
. Payments: Shopify Payments (available in Spain) with managed payment methods, a manual cash on delivery method only if
  needed, capture at checkout (or at fulfillment if beans are made to order), and the Payment Customization Function to
  hide cash on delivery above 100 EUR.
. Checkout settings: email as the contact method, last name required, phone number optional, no company field, marketing
  email opt-in preselected only in regions where that is allowed, add-to-cart limit left on, one-page checkout.
. Tests: the Test payment gateway or Shopify Payments test mode on a development store, one order per zone, one order as
  ada@example.test (tag Coffee Club) and one as grace@example.test (guest), a 40 EUR boundary cart, an order above 100 EUR
  to confirm cash on delivery disappears, and a refund.

== What was run and what was not

Ran offline with Shopify CLI 5.0.0 and Node.js 24 on 2026-10-27, in throwaway local projects, with no account and no
login:

* 16 GraphQL documents with their variables (14 against the 2026-10 Admin schema, covering profile, market shipping,
  carrier service, customization, capture, tax exemption and checkout configuration operations, and 2 against the
  2026-10 Storefront schema) validated with the `graphql` library, 16.14.2. A wrong enum value
  and a misspelled field were rejected in negative tests.
* Two JavaScript Shopify Functions built (`shopify app build`) and run with the bundled function runner for four
  inputs; instruction counts and module sizes are quoted above.
* `shopify.app.toml` and the extension descriptor of an offsite payments extension validated by the build, including a
  negative test with a wrongly typed field.
* Three Liquid files and locale keys checked with Theme Check in a copy of the Skeleton theme (no offenses) plus two
  negative checks.
* The carrier-service callback started on 127.0.0.1 and called with three requests and one malformed body.

Not run, because they need a Shopify store or account: creating and reading delivery profiles, market shipping options,
carrier services, customizations, tax exemptions, captures and checkout configurations against a store, so the response
shapes and any business rule errors (`userErrors`) are not shown; installing and activating the Functions on a store;
the Payments Apps API and a real gateway; Shopify Payments, Shopify Tax and Shopify Markets behavior in checkout;
and every admin screen and plan behavior quoted from the Help Center. The Rust variants of the Functions were not
compiled because no Rust toolchain is installed in the environment.

== Pitfalls

* Treating a profile as a rate table: a variant is in exactly one profile, and rates from several profiles are summed
  unless the names match or the store uses shipping options by market.
* Writing to merchant-owned delivery profiles on a shop that moved to market-driven shipping: calls may succeed and
  change nothing. Detect `ShopFeatures.marketDrivenShipping` first.
* Creating a carrier service on API 2026-10 and expecting rates: it is registered but not placed in any profile.
* Weight-based rates with products without weight, which also produces a weight of 0 for backup rates.
* Computing a free-shipping threshold from the base currency in a theme: use the presentment amounts the cart returns.
* Manual Tax together with Basic Tax or Shopify Tax in the EU and UK, which breaks duty collection.
* Tax override percentages entered as the exempt part instead of the collected part, and overrides on smart collections,
  which are not supported.
* Showing tax numbers from the Storefront cart: the tax and duty fields of `CartCost` are deprecated.
* Combining automatic capture with an app that also captures payments.
* Testing with Shopify Payments test mode in a production store, which blocks real card orders while it is on.
* Fulfilling test orders, which buys real shipping labels.
* Relying on payment customizations in POS or in Shop Pay (only the gift card field is customizable there) or hiding
  Express placements on non-Plus stores (the whole method is hidden instead).
* Renaming a carrier-calculated option without its carrier name: the carrier name is always prepended.
* Using the REST CarrierService page as an API reference for new code: it documents the callback wire format that both
  APIs share, but REST is legacy.
