This page compares *48 backend frameworks* on the same metrics: the frameworks documented on this site, plus others that matter because they are popular, because they are growing, or for historical context.
Every number carries a source and a date, and anything that could not be sourced is shown as *n/a* instead of being estimated.
The data snapshot date is *2026-10-27*: all sources were read on that day, and each caption names the edition or measurement date of its data.
Downloads, survey shares and stars change every month, so treat the figures as a dated snapshot.

The raw data (CSV files), the chart and table generators and the scoring rubric are kept in the repository under `scripts/framework-charts/`; `npm run charts:frameworks` regenerates every figure and table on this page.
The view layers of full-stack frameworks (Razor and Blazor, Django templates, Blade, Livewire and Inertia, Thymeleaf) are compared on the xref:web/frameworks-comparison.adoc[Web Frameworks Comparison]; language-level metrics (indices, benchmarks of the languages themselves, salaries) are on the xref:programming-languages/comparative.adoc[Programming Languages Comparative].
Jump to the <<_summary_and_recommendations,Summary and recommendations>> if you only need the conclusions.

== Scope and inclusion criteria

A framework is included when at least one of these holds:

. It has its own section on this site: xref:backend/springboot/index.adoc[Spring Boot], xref:backend/quarkus/index.adoc[Quarkus], Jakarta EE (through the xref:web/jsf/index.adoc[JSF] and xref:web/jsp-struts/index.adoc[JSP and Struts] sections), xref:web/aspnet/core/index.adoc[ASP.NET Core], xref:web/aspnet/mvc/index.adoc[ASP.NET MVC 5 and Web API 2], xref:web/aspnet/web-forms/index.adoc[ASP.NET Web Forms], Express and Fastify (in xref:backend/nodejs/index.adoc[Node.js]), xref:backend/nestjs/index.adoc[NestJS], xref:web/django/index.adoc[Django], xref:backend/fastapi/index.adoc[FastAPI], xref:backend/flask/index.adoc[Flask], xref:backend/tornado/index.adoc[Tornado], xref:backend/pyramid/index.adoc[Pyramid], xref:web/php-laravel/index.adoc[Laravel], xref:backend/axum/index.adoc[axum] and xref:backend/vapor/index.adoc[Vapor].
. It appears in the framework lists of the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025, State of JS 2025 or the JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2023, or ranks in its registry's downloads: Micronaut, Helidon, Ktor, Vert.x, Javalin and Play (JVM); Hono, Koa, AdonisJS and Elysia (Node.js and Bun); Litestar, Sanic, Django REST framework and Django Ninja (Python); Symfony, Slim and CodeIgniter (PHP); Actix Web, Rocket and Loco (Rust); Hummingbird (Swift); Ruby on Rails, Gin, Echo, Fiber, Go `net/http` as the baseline, and Phoenix (Elixir).
. It matters for history: ASP.NET Web Forms, Apache Struts, J2EE and EJB 2, Zend Framework and Laminas, CakePHP.

Frameworks without a section on this site get fewer metrics where data is scarce: the feature matrix, the deployment-target coverage and some concurrency rows are only assessed for the documented ones.
Runtimes (Node.js, Deno, Bun) and content-management systems (WordPress, Drupal) appear in some surveys but are not frameworks and are excluded.
Every framework name links to its section on this site, or to its official website when the site has no section.

== How metrics, scales and scores were assigned

*Metrics.* A value is recorded only when it was read in the source named for it; otherwise it is *n/a* and charts draw a gap or an "n/a" label, never a zero.
Values derived from published data (State of JS ratios from the published answer counts, TechEmpower requests per second from the raw results, js-framework-benchmark factors) use the publisher's own formula, documented in `scripts/framework-charts/data/NOTES.md`.

*Radar scores (0 to 10, higher is better).* Each radar axis is either *computed* (C), a formula over the data, or *editorial* (E), a judgement with a written basis per framework.
Scores are relative to the frameworks on this page: 10 is the best in this comparison, not a theoretical maximum, and an axis without inputs is *n/a*.

* *Popularity (C)*: mean of the survey usage relative to the most used framework, registry downloads on a log scale within one registry, and GitHub stars on a log scale.
* *Growth (C)*: change in survey usage from 2023 to 2025, in npm downloads and Packagist downloads year over year, and in State of JS usage, each scaled between the slowest and fastest grower.
* *Talent pool and jobs (C)*: professional usage in the survey and Hacker News job-posting share, relative to the best framework.
* *Ease of use (E)*: concepts to learn before the first endpoint, configuration needed, conventions, and quality of the official tutorial, supported by the admired and retention scores.
* *Ecosystem and maturity (E)*: age, release cadence, support policy and LTS, governance, extension ecosystem and official integrations.
* *Productivity (E)*: batteries included (the <<_productivity,feature matrix>>), scaffolding and hot reload.
* *Throughput (C)*: TechEmpower round 23 fortunes, `10 × (1 − log10(best / rps) / 2)`; 100 times slower than the best scores 0.
* *Memory and Startup (C)*: from the like-for-like Quarkus comparison only, JIT variant; 10 times more than the best scores 0.

Registry downloads count CI runs, mirrors and caches, not people, and the units of the registries differ (npm counts per tarball, Packagist per install, PyPI without mirrors). They are therefore only compared within one registry, never on one axis.
The full formulas, the rubric and the method of every dataset are in `scripts/framework-charts/data/NOTES.md`.

== Overview and timeline

The oldest frameworks still in wide use date from the late 2000s: xref:web/django/index.adoc[Django], xref:backend/flask/index.adoc[Flask] and Tornado reached PyPI in 2010, Express in 2010 and Ruby on Rails on RubyGems in 2009.
The JVM frameworks built for containers (Quarkus, Micronaut, Helidon) arrived in 2018 and 2019, and the newest frameworks here are Hono (2021), Elysia (2022), Litestar (2.0 in 2023) and Loco (2023).
Registry dates are the first publication on the registry, which for older projects can be later than the first release; the timeline uses them so that every date has a source.

== Popularity

Three independent signals are used: what developers say they use (surveys), what build systems download (registries) and what developers star (GitHub).
In the Stack Overflow survey 2025, the most used backend frameworks are Express (19.9% of respondents), ASP.NET Core (19.7%), FastAPI (14.8%), Spring Boot (14.7%) and Flask (14.4%); classic ASP.NET still has 14.2%.
The highest admired scores belong to the less used Phoenix (79%) and axum (76.4%), followed by ASP.NET Core (61.3%) and FastAPI (55.5%).

== Talent pool and job market

*Ease of finding developers.* The pool is largest for the frameworks with the highest professional usage in the 2025 survey: ASP.NET Core (21.3% of professional respondents), Express (20.3%) and Spring Boot (15.6%), followed by FastAPI and Flask.

*Job opportunities.* In the 2025 Hacker News "Who is hiring?" threads, Ruby on Rails appears in 3.79% of the postings, FastAPI in 2.95%, Django in 2.65% and NestJS in 0.82%; Rails was at 12.44% in 2015 and Django at 5.98%.
These threads lean towards start-ups and remote work, and framework names that are common words (Gin, Echo, Fiber, Play, Rocket, Phoenix on its own) are not counted.

== Ease of use and learning curve

Ease of use is an editorial score (rubric above, basis per framework in the <<_radar_comparisons,radar tables>>).
Micro-frameworks (Flask, Express, Hono, Gin) need the fewest concepts before the first endpoint; convention-driven full-stack frameworks (Ruby on Rails, Laravel, Django) need more concepts but guide every step; frameworks built on dependency injection and modules (Spring Boot, NestJS) and on new runtime models (axum, Actix Web, Phoenix) take longest to learn.
Supporting signals are the admired scores (Phoenix 79%, axum 76.4%, ASP.NET Core 61.3%) and State of JS retention (Hono 95%, Elysia 91.1%, Fastify 86.5%).
The site's own comparisons go deeper for some pairs: xref:backend/quarkus/quarkus-vs-spring-boot.adoc[Quarkus versus Spring Boot], xref:backend/nodejs/web-frameworks-express-and-fastify.adoc[Express and Fastify], xref:backend/axum/migration-and-framework-comparison.adoc[axum and the other Rust frameworks], xref:backend/vapor/ecosystem-and-framework-comparison.adoc[Vapor and other Swift servers] and xref:web/aspnet/choosing-a-framework.adoc[choosing an ASP.NET framework].

== Performance

=== Throughput

TechEmpower's round 23 (Citrine hardware, run on 2025-01-30) is the last published round: the FrameworkBenchmarks repository has been archived.
In the fortunes test (a database query, an HTML template and escaping, the closest to a real endpoint) the best implementations of Helidon (696,144 responses per second), ASP.NET Core (691,656 with Native AOT, 609,967 with Minimal APIs on the JIT), Micronaut (660,238) and Quarkus (431,465) lead, ahead of Fiber (411,214), Spring Boot (243,640) and FastAPI (109,167); Django (31,793) and Laravel (16,493) are at the low end.
These are highly tuned implementations, and real applications spend most of their time in their own code and database, so the differences shrink in practice.
For load testing your own service see xref:backend/jmeter/index.adoc[JMeter] and xref:backend/springboot/performance-testing-jmeter.adoc[performance testing a Spring Boot application].

=== Memory consumption and startup time

No house measurements were made for this page: memory and startup figures come only from published sources and are labelled.
The Quarkus team's comparison (2026-06-02, 4 CPUs, Quarkus 3.35.4 and Spring Boot 4.0.6) measures both frameworks the same way: boot and first response 4,352 ms (Quarkus JIT) against 10,582 ms (Spring Boot JIT), and 574 ms against 2,169 ms as native images; RSS after the first request 271 MiB against 477 MiB (JIT) and 98 MiB against 303 MiB (native).
These are vendor-reported for Quarkus, and published by a competing project for Spring Boot.
The Micronaut and Helidon home pages publish smaller numbers (Micronaut native startup 15 ms and 42 MB) without a method, so they are not comparable with the Quarkus figures.
The ASP.NET Core Native AOT documentation shows its results only as a chart image, so .NET is n/a; for native images and tuning on the JVM see xref:backend/quarkus/native-executables.adoc[Quarkus native executables] and xref:backend/quarkus/performance-and-tuning.adoc[Quarkus performance and tuning].

=== Concurrency model

The concurrency model decides what happens when a handler blocks on I/O or computes for a long time.
Thread-per-request stacks (Spring MVC, Struts) can switch to Java virtual threads (xref:backend/springboot/concurrency-alternatives.adoc[Spring Boot concurrency alternatives]); event-loop stacks (Node.js frameworks, Vert.x, Quarkus's reactive core) must never block the loop (xref:backend/nodejs/concurrency-models.adoc[Node.js concurrency models]); async Python (xref:backend/fastapi/async-and-concurrency.adoc[FastAPI async and concurrency]) and Rust frameworks run async handlers on an executor; PHP runs one request per worker process; Go starts a goroutine per request and Phoenix one BEAM process.

== Summary and recommendations

These recommendations are an *editorial judgement* built on the data above; each cites the metric it relies on. The snapshot date is 2026-10-27, and surveys and downloads change every month, so re-check the numbers before a decision that will last years.

*Safe mainstream choices, by language.*

* *JVM: xref:backend/springboot/index.adoc[Spring Boot].* 72% of Java developers use it (JetBrains 2023), 14.7% of all survey respondents (2025), the most complete feature matrix and support dates per minor version. Choose xref:backend/quarkus/index.adoc[Quarkus] when startup time and memory matter (574 ms and 98 MiB native, vendor-reported).
* *.NET: xref:web/aspnet/core/index.adoc[ASP.NET Core].* 19.7% survey usage, 61.3% admired, top-tier TechEmpower throughput and LTS releases.
* *Python: xref:web/django/index.adoc[Django] for full applications, xref:backend/fastapi/index.adoc[FastAPI] for APIs.* Django has an admin, ORM and LTS releases; FastAPI doubled its survey share between 2023 and 2025 and generates OpenAPI from type hints.
* *PHP: xref:web/php-laravel/index.adoc[Laravel].* 61% of PHP developers (JetBrains 2023), rising Packagist downloads, yearly majors with dated support.
* *Node.js: xref:backend/nestjs/index.adoc[NestJS] for structured services, Fastify for lean ones* (in xref:backend/nodejs/web-frameworks-express-and-fastify.adoc[Node.js]). Express remains the most downloaded but provides the least out of the box.

*Trends to watch.* The growth data support three: FastAPI (survey share 7.42% to 14.8%, 2023 to 2025), Hono (841 million npm downloads in 12 months, 95% State of JS retention) and ASP.NET Core (16.57% to 19.7%).
xref:backend/axum/index.adoc[axum] and Phoenix are niche (2.8% and 2.4% usage) but the most admired frameworks in the 2025 survey.

*Avoid for new projects, keep for legacy maintenance.*

* xref:web/aspnet/web-forms/index.adoc[ASP.NET Web Forms] and xref:web/aspnet/mvc/index.adoc[ASP.NET MVC 5] run on .NET Framework (current cycle 4.8.1 on endoflife.date), not on modern .NET; MVC 5's last NuGet release is 5.3.0 (2023-10-23) and classic ASP.NET's survey usage fell from 26.3% (2019) to 14.2% (2025). See xref:web/aspnet/choosing-a-framework.adoc[choosing an ASP.NET framework].
* xref:web/jsp-struts/index.adoc[Apache Struts] has the fourth-highest advisory count on this page (60) and 1,370 GitHub stars; new Java web applications fit Spring Boot better.
* J2EE and EJB 2 are historical (their successor is Jakarta EE), and Zend Framework continues as Laminas.

*Hiring perspective.* The largest talent pools are ASP.NET Core, Express, Spring Boot, FastAPI and Flask (the highest professional survey usage); Ruby on Rails, FastAPI and Django lead the 2025 Hacker News postings.
Niche but growing choices (Hono, axum, Phoenix, Litestar) are easier to sell to developers than to staff quickly: plan for training.

The strongest conclusion is the least surprising one: for most teams the right framework is the most used one of the language the team already knows, and the metrics above matter mainly at the edges (startup-sensitive serverless work, extreme throughput, hiring in a small market).
