= Grafana Guide: Time Series Panels
:description: Step-by-step guide to Grafana 13.3: extend the time series panel of the dashboard Bookshelf (guides) with a second query, a table legend with calculations, a unit, axis settings, graph styles, thresholds and a field override that puts the latency on its own right axis.
:keywords: Grafana, time series, panel editor, legend, calculations, unit, axis, second Y axis, graph styles, thresholds, field override, Prometheus, PromQL, Bookshelf, step by step, guide

include::partial$ai-disclaimer.adoc[]

Checked on 2026-11-11 against Grafana 13.3.x.

== What you build

You take the panel *Requests per second by status* that the first-dashboard guide created and turn it into a complete time series panel. A second query adds the 95th percentile of the
response time of the Bookshelf service. The legend becomes a table with the mean and the maximum of each series, the axis gets a label, the lines get a fill, a threshold marks 10 requests per second, and an override
moves the latency to its own axis on the right with the unit seconds.

image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-13-result.svg["The finished panel in view mode: lines for the status 200 and 404 on the left axis, the p95 latency on a right axis, a red threshold line at 10 and a table legend with Mean and Max",width=900]

== Before you start

* Start the Grafana profile of the xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/hands-on-lab.adoc[hands-on lab] and wait about a minute:
+
[source,bash]
----
cd lab
docker compose --profile grafana up -d
----
* Open http://localhost:3000, sign in as `admin` with the password `admin`, and click *Skip* when Grafana 13.3 asks you to
  update the default password.
* Do the previous guide, xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/grafana/guide-first-dashboard.adoc[Create a dashboard and the first panel]. It creates the dashboard *Bookshelf (guides)* in the folder *Guides* with the panel that this guide extends.
  The screens of the dashboard in edit mode and of the panel editor are described by the two screen maps of that guide.
* The data source *Prometheus* is provisioned by the lab and is the default data source. The load generator sends traffic to the Bookshelf service in its default mode, which gives both queries something to draw.
* The guide was checked on Grafana Enterprise 13.3.0 (the free image, no licence key). Editing dashboards needs the *Editor* or *Admin* role of the organization; `admin` has it.
* The figures are drawings of the Grafana 13.3 user interface, not screenshots, so small details can differ from your screen.
  A highlighted box with a number marks the control of the step with the same number. The option pane has more categories than the figures draw; they are collapsed where a step does not need them.

== Steps

All the settings of this guide are in the options pane at the right of the panel editor. Its categories for a time series are *Panel options*, *Panel links*, *Repeat options*, *Show / hide rules*, *Panel styles*, *Tooltip*, *Legend*,
*Axis*, *Graph styles*, *Standard options*, *Data links and actions*, *Value mappings*, *Thresholds* and, at the bottom, the button *Add field override*. Click the title of a category to open or close it.
The *Search options* box at the top of the pane finds an option by name.

=== Open the panel in the editor

. In the left navigation, click *Dashboards*, open the folder *Guides* and click *Bookshelf (guides)*.
. Click *Edit* at the top right of the toolbar.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-01-dashboard-view.svg["Dashboard Bookshelf (guides) in view mode with its one panel, Requests per second by status; callout 2 marks the Edit button of the toolbar",width=900]
. Click the panel *Requests per second by status* to select it. The right sidebar shows the *Panel* pane.
. In the *Panel* pane, click *Edit visualization*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-02-select-panel.svg["Dashboard in edit mode: callout 3 marks the panel Requests per second by status, which is selected, and the Panel pane of the right sidebar has its Edit visualization button marked by callout 4",width=900]

=== Add a second query

[start=5]
. At the bottom of the editor, on the tab *Queries*, click *Add query* under query *A*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-03-add-query.svg["Panel editor of the time series panel with query A in Code mode; callout 5 marks the Add query button under the query",width=900]
. In the new query *B*, click *Code* in the *Builder* / *Code* switch.
. Click inside the query editor first, so that it has the focus, then type the query. It draws the 95th percentile of the response time in seconds, and `label_replace` gives the series the name `p95 latency` that the legend and the override use:
+
[source,promql]
----
label_replace(histogram_quantile(0.95, sum by (le) (rate(http_server_requests_seconds_bucket{application="bookshelf"}[1m]))), "series", "p95 latency", "", "")
----
. Click *Run queries*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-04-query-b.svg["Panel editor with a second query B in Code mode: callout 6 marks the Code option of query B, callout 7 marks the editor with the latency query typed over three lines, and callout 8 marks the Run queries button",width=900]

The preview now draws three lines. The latency, about a tenth of a second, lies flat on the zero line because it shares the axis with the requests per second. The next steps fix that.

=== Show the legend as a table

[start=9]
. In the options pane, open *Legend*. For *Mode*, click *Table*. The *Visibility* switch of the legend is on by default.
. Click the field *Values* and choose two calculations, for example *Mean* and *Max*. The legend table gets one column for each of them.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-05-legend.svg["Panel editor with the Time series options pane and its Legend section open: the preview draws three lines on one axis with a list legend, callout 9 marks the Table option of Mode and callout 10 marks the Values field",width=900]

=== Set the unit and the axis

[start=11]
. Open *Standard options*. Click the *Unit* picker, type `requests` in its search box and choose the entry for requests per second. The tooltip and the legend now show the unit.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-06-unit.svg["Panel editor with the Standard options section of the options pane open: callout 11 marks the Unit picker above the Min and Max fields; the preview now shows a table legend with Mean and Max",width=900]
. Open *Axis*. In *Label*, type `Requests per second`.
. In *Soft min*, type `0`. A soft minimum keeps the axis from starting at the lowest value in the data but still lets it grow below zero if the data requires it.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-07-axis.svg["Panel editor with the Axis section of the options pane open: callout 12 marks the Label field holding Requests per second and callout 13 marks the Soft min field holding 0, below the Placement choices Auto, Left, Right and Hidden",width=900]

=== Choose the graph style

[start=14]
. Open *Graph styles*. Keep *Style* on *Lines*. In *Line width*, type `2`.
. In *Fill opacity*, type `20`.
. For *Gradient mode*, click *Opacity*. The area under each line fades towards the bottom.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-08-graph-styles.svg["Panel editor with the Graph styles section of the options pane open: callout 14 marks the Line width field holding 2, callout 15 marks the Fill opacity field holding 20 and callout 16 marks the Opacity choice of Gradient mode",width=900]

=== Add a threshold

[start=17]
. Open *Thresholds*. The list has a base step in green and a second step in red with the value `80`. Replace `80` with `10`, so that the red step starts at 10 requests per second.
. In *Show thresholds*, choose *As lines*. A red line now marks the value 10 in the preview. The list offers other ways to show the threshold, for example as filled regions; choose another entry to compare and come back to *As lines*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-09-thresholds.svg["Panel editor with the Thresholds section of the options pane open: the list has the base step in green and a red step whose value is 10, marked by callout 17, then the Add threshold button and the Show thresholds list, marked by callout 18",width=900]

=== Give the latency its own axis with an override

[start=19]
. Scroll to the bottom of the options pane and click *Add field override*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-10-add-override.svg["Panel editor with the sections of the options pane collapsed; the preview shows a red threshold line at 10, and callout 19 marks the Add field override button at the bottom of the options pane",width=900]
. In the new override, choose which fields it applies to: use the selection by field name and pick `p95 latency`.
. Click *Add override property*, choose *Axis* → *Placement* and set it to *Right*.
. Click *Add override property* again, choose *Standard options* → *Unit* and choose the entry for seconds.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-11-override.svg["Panel editor with an override in the options pane: callout 20 marks the field selection p95 latency, callout 21 marks the Right choice of the property Axis placement and callout 22 marks the Unit property set to seconds; the preview draws the latency on its own right axis",width=900]

=== Save the panel

[start=23]
. Click *Save* in the header of the editor. The 13.3 panel editor has no *Apply* button; *Save* saves the whole dashboard and *Back* returns to it.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-grafana-guide-g6-12-save.svg["Panel editor with the finished panel: three series, the latency on a right axis, a table legend and a threshold line at 10; callout 23 marks the Save button of the editor header",width=900]
. In the *Save dashboard* drawer, optionally type a note in *Message* and click *Save*.
. Click *Back*.
. Click *Exit edit*. If Grafana 13.3.0 shows the dialog *Unsaved changes* ("Do you want to save your changes?"), which it does even right after a save, click *Save*.
. If a second *Save dashboard* drawer opens, click *Save* again. Edit mode closes and the dashboard shows the finished panel.

== Check the result

Open *Dashboards* → *Guides* → *Bookshelf (guides)*. The panel must show a green line for `200`, a yellow line for `404` and a blue line for `p95 latency` that reads against the right axis, a red line at 10 on the left axis and a legend table with the columns *Mean* and *Max*.

To see the right axis react, slow the service down and press *Refresh*; the blue line rises while the left axis does not change:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/slow
----

Force errors to add a line for the status `500` to the requests, and restore normal traffic afterwards:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/failing
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal
----

== Variations

* In *Graph styles*, set *Style* to *Bars* or *Points*, or *Stack series* to *Normal* to pile the status codes on top of each other.
* In *Legend*, set *Placement* to *Right* when the panel is tall and narrow, and use *Series visibility* to hide a series from the legend.
* In *Tooltip*, set *Tooltip mode* to *All* to see every series when you hover the chart.
* On the dashboard, open the panel in *View panel* mode: its sidebar pane offers *Quick toggles* for the legend, the axis scale and stacking, and *Fan-out by series or label* to split a multi-series panel into one chart per series without editing it.

== What can go wrong

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Cause |Fix

|Query *B* shows *No data*
|The text was typed before the editor had the focus, or the lab started less than a few minutes ago and Prometheus has no histogram samples yet.
|Click in the editor, select all with Ctrl+A (Cmd+A on macOS) and type the query again, or wait a few minutes and press *Refresh*.

|The latency line lies flat on the zero line
|It shares the left axis with the request rate, or the override does not apply.
|Check that the override selects the field `p95 latency` and that its *Axis* → *Placement* is *Right*.

|The override lists no field to choose
|Query *B* has not run yet, so Grafana does not know the field names.
|Click *Run queries*, then add the override again.

|The legend table has no *Mean* or *Max* column
|*Values* is empty, or *Mode* is still *List*.
|Set *Mode* to *Table* and choose the calculations in *Values*.

|*Exit edit* keeps showing *Unsaved changes*
|Grafana 13.3.0 raises the dialog even after a save.
|Click *Save*, and *Save* again in the drawer; the dialog does not return once edit mode has closed.
|===

== Clean up

Keep the dashboard: the following guides in this series add panels to it. Send `curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal` if you changed the mode of the load generator. To remove everything the lab stores, stop the
profile with `docker compose --profile grafana down -v`, which also deletes the volumes and therefore the dashboard.
