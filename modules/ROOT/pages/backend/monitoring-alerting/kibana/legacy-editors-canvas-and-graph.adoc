= Kibana: Legacy Editors, Canvas & Graph
:description: The older Kibana visualization tools (aggregation-based editors, TSVB and Timelion), Canvas and Graph: what they were for, where they are still available, how to move each to Lens, and which provided books still teach them.
:keywords: Kibana, legacy editors, aggregation based, TSVB, Time Series Visual Builder, Timelion, Canvas, workpad, Graph, Lens, Convert to Lens, migration

Checked on 2026-11-09 against Kibana 9.5.x.

include::partial$monitoring-alerting-kibana-disclaimer.adoc[]

Old Kibana tutorials, blog posts and dashboards you inherit are full of tools that are no longer the way to build something new:
the "Visualize" editors, the Time Series Visual Builder (TSVB), Timelion, Canvas workpads and the Graph app. They still run in the
Elastic Stack, they are not available on Serverless, and you will meet them in dashboards that were built years ago and must
keep working. This page explains what each of them did, how they differ from
xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/lens.adoc[Lens], how to convert what can be converted, and when to leave something alone. It
also helps to read the books that teach Kibana (see the table at the end) without copying outdated steps. The Grafana parallel is the
removal of the Angular panels (Graph, Singlestat, Worldmap) described in
xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/grafana/visualizations-logs-traces-and-special.adoc[Grafana Logs, Traces and Special Panels], and the
chart choice for new work is in xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/choosing-visualizations.adoc[Choosing Visualizations].

Source: https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize/legacy-editors[Kibana: Legacy editors]. The page calls them
"still available but replaced by better alternatives", lists aggregation-based, TSVB and Timelion, marks them generally available on the
Stack and unavailable on Serverless, and does not give a removal date.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Tool |Status in 9.5 |Move to
|Aggregation-based editors |Stack only; unavailable on Serverless; opens in Lens for most types |Lens
|TSVB |Stack only; unavailable on Serverless; most panel types open in Lens |Lens, ES{vbar}QL visualizations, Vega
|Timelion |Deprecated since 7.10; unavailable on Serverless |Lens formulas, ES{vbar}QL, Vega
|Canvas |Stack only; only on upgraded installations that already have workpads; unavailable on Serverless |Dashboards with Markdown, Image and Links panels
|Graph |Stack only; unavailable on Serverless |Lens, Discover, or the APM service map for service relations
|===

== Finding the legacy editors

Source: https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize/legacy-editors[Kibana: Legacy editors] (create a legacy visualization).

On 9.4 and later go to *Dashboards*, *Visualizations*, *Create visualization*, the *Legacy* tab, and choose *TSVB* or
*Aggregation-based*. On 9.0 to 9.3 the entry point is the *Visualize library* (search for it in the menu bar). Legacy panel types appear in
the dashboard *Add* menu only if the dashboards already contain them: if you have never used them, use Lens, as the landing page of
https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize[Panels and visualizations] advises.

== Aggregation-based editors

Source: https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize/legacy-editors/aggregation-based[Kibana: Aggregation-based visualizations].

*What it is.* The original Kibana panels: area, data table, gauge, goal, heat map, horizontal bar, vertical bar, line, metric, pie and
tag cloud. Each is a form that stacks bucket and metric aggregations (the vocabulary of
xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/metric-types-and-aggregation.adoc[Metric Types and Aggregation]): choose a metric, then a bucket for
the X axis and up to three levels of split series or split charts. That is more nesting than Lens or TSVB allow, and the editor accepts non-time
series data and a saved Discover session as its input; its limits are few styling options, no maths between aggregations, no
multiple indices, and a Date Histogram on the automatic interval requires the data view's primary time field. A worked example on
the page is a vertical bar of Date Histogram split by a `terms` bucket on a source field, which shows the top traffic sources over time.

*Moving to Lens.* The area, data table, gauge, goal, heat map, horizontal bar, line, metric, pie and vertical bar types can be
opened in Lens with *Edit visualization in Lens* in the toolbar. To convert a panel already on a dashboard, enter edit mode, open the
panel menu and choose *Convert to Lens* (it appears only when Kibana can convert the configuration, and needs at least read access to
the Visualize library feature), then *Save and return* to replace the panel. Compare the result with the original before saving: a
conversion that cannot represent a setting does not offer itself.

== TSVB

Source: https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize/legacy-editors/tsvb[Kibana: TSVB].

*What it is.* The Time Series Visual Builder combines many aggregations in a time series chart, annotates the series with events from an
Elasticsearch index, shows several data views in one visualization and applies functions and maths. Its panel types are Time Series,
Metric, Top N, Gauge, Table and Markdown (Handlebars syntax with custom CSS). Each series behaves like a separate aggregation, with
functions such as Filter Ratio (the error rate as one document set divided by another), Counter Rate, Positive Only, Series
Agg and Math, and group-by options Filters and Terms. It is exactly the tool that people used to chart Bookshelf's error ratio
before Lens had formulas.

*Limits that explain why it was replaced.* No maths across series (the page recommends Timelion or Vega for differences between
series), month-over-month changes only partly supported, no event durations computed from start and end times, derivatives that
return nothing when the interval is too small, the last bucket dropped by default, and a y-axis that includes zero unless the fill is
changed. Using an Elasticsearch index string instead of a data view is deprecated and will be removed; an administrator can still
enable it with the `metrics:allowStringIndices` advanced setting.

*Moving to Lens.* Time Series, Metric, Top N, Gauge and Table panels open in Lens through *Edit visualization in Lens* and convert
with *Convert to Lens*; Markdown is not listed as convertible and becomes a Markdown text panel (see
xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/maps-vega-and-other-panels.adoc[Maps, Vega & Other Panels]). By purpose the old features map as
follows:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|TSVB feature |Replacement
|Filter Ratio |A Lens formula such as `+count(kql='...') / count()+`
|Counter Rate, derivative |The counter-rate and difference functions in the Lens formula reference, or `RATE` in an ES{vbar}QL `TS` query
|Annotations from an index |An annotation layer with a custom query
|Top N |A horizontal bar with a rank limit
|Markdown panel |A Markdown text panel
|Math across series |A Lens formula, an ES{vbar}QL `EVAL`, or Vega
|===

The `TS` command and `RATE` are covered in
xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/querying-with-kql-esql-and-promql.adoc[Querying with KQL, ES|QL & PromQL]; check the Lens formula reference icon for
the exact function names of your version.

== Timelion

Source: https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize/legacy-editors/timelion[Kibana: Timelion].

Timelion is an expression language for time series: functions start with a dot and chain, for example
`+.es(index=logs-*, timefield='@timestamp', metric='max:bytes').derivative().divide(1024).label('KB per second')+`, with
`.es` fetching data (counting documents by default), series separated by commas, and functions for offsets, styling, multiple y-axes,
conditions (`.if()`) and moving averages. It is marked deprecated since 7.10 and unavailable on Serverless. A Timelion panel is created from
*Create visualization*, *Legacy*, *Aggregation-based*, *Timelion* (9.4 and later) and has to be saved to the library before it can be
added to a dashboard from the library. Existing expressions are the hard case to migrate, because there is no converter: rewrite each
one as a Lens formula when it is a ratio or a shift, as an ES|QL query when it is a calculation over several fields, or as Vega
when it needs several series with maths between them.

== Canvas

Source: https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize/canvas[Kibana: Canvas].

*What it is.* Canvas presents live Elasticsearch data in a multi-page *workpad*: backgrounds, borders, fonts, images and elements (charts,
progress monitors, text, images) that you can also save as reusable elements. An element takes its data from Elasticsearch SQL,
from Elasticsearch documents (raw values, suited to small data sets) or from Timelion, and a workpad can embed panels from the Visualize
library. A workpad is created blank, from a template, from an imported JSON file or from a sample data set; it can be shared through
the *Share* button (see the reporting documentation) and exported. The page states one important limit: Canvas is only available for
upgraded installations with existing workpads, and it is unavailable on Serverless.

*What to do.* Leave a working workpad alone, export its JSON as a backup, and rebuild it as a dashboard when it is touched:
Markdown, Image and Links panels cover text, logos and navigation, Lens covers the charts, and the Reporting features in
xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/sharing-reporting-and-saved-objects.adoc[Sharing, Reporting & Saved Objects] replace workpad
PDFs. For a status-board look on a new installation, the custom panel (Serverless preview, Planned on the Stack) is the intended
direction. Grafana's Canvas panel is a different thing: it is a panel on a dashboard, not a separate workpad app.

== Graph

Source: https://www.elastic.co/docs/explore-analyze/visualize/graph[Kibana: Graph].

Graph explores how terms in an index relate to each other. Vertices are indexed terms; a connection summarizes the documents that
contain both terms, computed on the fly with aggregations and ranked by relevance so that meaningful relations stand out from
noise. No extra data is stored. The documented uses are fraud and attack pattern discovery, recommendations and finding people with overlapping expertise;
it is generally available on the Stack and unavailable on Serverless. The workflow is to choose a data source, adjust the
fields (by default each search or expand adds the five most relevant terms per field, set with *Terms per hop*), enter a query and expand or link vertices,
with blocking of noise terms and drilldown URLs as options. The same engine is exposed as the graph exploration API.

For monitoring it is a niche tool. Relations between services are answered better by the service map of APM (see
xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/apm-and-synthetics.adoc[APM & Synthetics]), and relations between log fields by Discover and the
log pattern analysis in xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/data-views-and-discover.adoc[Data Views & Discover]. Graph can help to see which
exception classes, routes and hosts co-occur in the Bookshelf error logs, but it needs a data source with terms worth relating.
A graph query can be expensive on large indices; the page notes sampling controls and timeouts that limit the load on the cluster.

== Verifying in the lab

. In a space of the lab Kibana, create an aggregation-based vertical bar (Date Histogram on `@timestamp`, split by a `terms`
  bucket) on the Bookshelf logs, add it to a dashboard, then use *Convert to Lens* in edit mode and compare the two.
. Open TSVB, build a Filter Ratio series for the 5xx share and reproduce it as the Lens formula of
  xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/lens.adoc[Lens]; note what the editor needed that the formula did not.
. Check which legacy panel types the dashboard *Add* menu offers (none, on a fresh install) and which dashboards of an imported
  set contain them.

== In the books

Four of the provided books teach these editors. Their steps follow old interfaces, so use them to understand the ideas and
the current page for the clicks.

|Melachi, _Elastic from A to Z_, section 3.7 |Step-by-step creation of visualizations, including a TSVB one, and a dashboard from them |Creates index patterns and visualizations by hand; today use data views, Lens and the managed integration dashboards
