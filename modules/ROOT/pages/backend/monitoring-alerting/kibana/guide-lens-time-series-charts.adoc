= Kibana Guide: Time Series Charts in Lens
:description: Step-by-step guide to Kibana 9.5 Lens time series charts: line, area and bar over time, the date histogram interval, a breakdown with top values and Other, a second layer with a time shift, reference lines, an annotation, percentiles of the latency, and formulas with a value format, on the Bookshelf logs and traces.
:keywords: Kibana, Lens, time series, line chart, area chart, date histogram, breakdown, top values, time shift, reference line, annotation, percentile, formula, value format, Bookshelf, step by step, guide

include::partial$ai-disclaimer.adoc[]

Checked on 2026-11-11 against Kibana 9.5.x.

== What you build

You add three Lens panels to the dashboard *Bookshelf (guides)*. The first one draws the Bookshelf requests per minute as lines, one for each
URI with the rest grouped as *Other*, next to a copy of the total shifted in time, a reference line and an annotation. The second one draws the 95th
percentile of the latency from the traces. The third one draws the error ratio, computed by a formula and shown as a percentage.

image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-15-result.svg["Finished Kibana dashboard Bookshelf (guides) in view mode with four panels: requests per minute by URI as lines with a shifted copy, the 95th percentile of the latency, the error ratio and the bar chart of the first guide",width=900]

== Before you start

* Start the Kibana profile of the xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/hands-on-lab.adoc[hands-on lab] and wait about a minute:
+
[source,bash]
----
cd lab
docker compose --profile kibana up -d
----
* Open http://localhost:5601 and sign in as `elastic` with the password `lab-elastic-password`. When Kibana opens its full-screen
  welcome page, click *Explore on my own*.
* Do the previous guide, xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-first-dashboard.adoc[Create a dashboard and the first Lens panel]. It creates the dashboard
  *Bookshelf (guides)* and the data view *Bookshelf logs (guides)* (index pattern `logs-generic.otel-default`, time field `@timestamp`) comes from
  xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-data-views-and-file-upload.adoc[Upload a file and create a data view].
* The percentiles need a second data view for the traces. In *Stack Management*, open *Data Views*, click *Create data view*, type the *Name* `Bookshelf traces (guides)`,
  the *Index pattern* `traces-generic.otel-default` and the *Timestamp field* `@timestamp`, and click *Save data view to Kibana*.
* The load generator must run in its default mode, which feeds both the logs and the traces. Lens and dashboards need no licence beyond the basic one; the lab runs a trial licence anyway.
* The guide was checked on Elasticsearch and Kibana 9.5.6. The figures are drawings of the Kibana 9.5 user interface, not screenshots, so small details can differ from your screen,
  and the numbers in the charts are only illustrations. A highlighted box with a number marks the control of the step with the same number.
  The dimension editors, the chart type menu, the *Add layer* menu and their settings were not all opened one by one in the lab for this guide; the figures show them in general form and the steps name
  the controls without describing their exact layout.

== Steps

The Lens editor is the same as in the first guide: the field list at the left, the workspace in the middle and the configuration panel with the layers at the right. The map of
the editor is in xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-first-dashboard.adoc[Create a dashboard and the first Lens panel]; the figures below show the parts that this guide uses.

=== Open Lens from the dashboard

. Open the primary navigation with the menu button, click *Dashboards* under *Analytics*, and click *Bookshelf (guides)*.
. Click *Edit* in the second bar.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-01-dashboard-view.svg["Dashboard Bookshelf (guides) of Kibana 9.5 in view mode with the bar chart panel of the first guide; the Edit button of the second bar is marked by callout 2",width=900]
. Click *Add* in the second bar.
. In the *Add to dashboard* flyout, click *Visualization*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-02-add-visualization.svg["Bookshelf (guides) in edit mode with the Add to dashboard flyout open: the Add button of the second bar is marked by callout 3 and the entry Visualization of the flyout by callout 4",width=900]

=== Draw lines, areas and bars over time

[start=5]
. Lens opens with an empty workspace. Check that the *Data view* picker shows *Bookshelf logs (guides)*, then click the chart type button of the layer, which shows *Bar*, and choose *Line*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-03-chart-type.svg["Empty Lens editor with the chart type button of the layer open as a menu listing Bar, Area and Line; the entry Line is marked by callout 5",width=900]
. Drag the field `@timestamp` from the field list onto the dashed *Horizontal axis* button. Lens fills the axis with a date histogram.
. Drag the entry *Records*, above *Available fields*, onto the dashed *Vertical axis* button. Lens shows it as *Count of records*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-04-time-axis.svg["Lens editor with a Line layer and empty axes; the field @timestamp of the list is dragged onto the Horizontal axis (callout 6) and the entry Records onto the Vertical axis (callout 7)",width=900]
. The workspace draws the count of records over time as one line. Click the chart type button again and choose *Area*. The same data is now filled below the line.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-05-line-chart.svg["Lens editor with a line chart of Count of records over time, one line; the chart type button of the layer, showing Line, is marked by callout 8",width=900]
. Click the chart type button and choose *Bar*. Compare the three shapes: a line follows a value, an area stresses the volume, bars show each interval as a separate amount.
. Click the chart type button and choose *Line* again. The rest of the guide uses lines.

=== Set the date histogram interval

[start=11]
. Click the *Horizontal axis* entry `@timestamp` to open its editor. Its function is the date histogram.
. In the editor, set the interval to `1 minute`. A short interval draws more points and a noisier line, a long interval smooths the line but hides short peaks; with *Last 15 minutes* in the time picker, one minute is a good size.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-06-interval.svg["Dimension editor of the Horizontal axis in Lens showing the function Date histogram on the field @timestamp, with the Interval control set to 1 minute and marked by callout 12",width=900]
. Close the editor.

=== Break the line down by URI

[start=14]
. Drag the field `attributes.uri` onto the dashed *Breakdown* button.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-07-breakdown.svg["Lens editor with the count of records over time as one line; the field attributes.uri of the list is dragged onto the dashed Breakdown button of the layer, which is marked by callout 14",width=900]
. Click the new *Breakdown* entry to open its editor. The function is *Top values*: one line for each of the most frequent values of the field.
. Set the number of values to `2`, so that only the two busiest URIs get a line.
. Turn on the switch for the group *Other*, so that the requests of all the remaining URIs are added together as one more line called *Other*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-08-top-values-other.svg["Dimension editor of the Breakdown in Lens with Top values on attributes.uri; the Values control set to 2 is marked by callout 16 and the Other switch, turned on, by callout 17; the chart behind shows three lines",width=900]
. Close the editor.

=== Add a second layer, reference lines and an annotation

A layer is one set of axes of its own inside the same chart. Reference lines and annotations are layers too.

[start=19]
. Click *Add layer* at the top of the configuration panel.
. Choose the entry for a data layer.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-09-add-layer.svg["Lens editor with the Add layer button of the configuration panel open as a menu listing Layer, Reference line and Annotations; the button is marked by callout 19 and the entry Layer by callout 20",width=900]
. In the new layer, drag `@timestamp` onto its dashed *Horizontal axis* button.
. Drag *Records* onto its dashed *Vertical axis* button.
. Click the new *Vertical axis* entry, *Count of records*, to open its editor.
. In the editor, type `10m` in the *Time shift* control. The layer now draws the total number of requests as it was ten minutes earlier, so you can compare it with the present lines. In a lab that has run for weeks, `1w` compares with the same time a week earlier.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-10-time-shift.svg["Dimension editor of the Vertical axis of the second layer in Lens with the function Count on Records; the Time shift control, typed 10m, is marked by callout 24; the chart behind shows the lines of the first layer and a second line that is shifted in time",width=900]
. Close the editor, click *Add layer* again and choose the entry for a reference line.
. In the editor of the reference line, select the *Static value* tab and type `500`, the number of requests per minute that you do not want to exceed.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-11-reference-line.svg["Reference line editor in Lens with the tab Static value selected and the value 500 typed in the Value control, which is marked by callout 26; the tabs Quick functions and Formula are next to it",width=900]
. To draw the average instead of a fixed number, close the editor, add another reference line, open its *Formula* tab and type:
+
[source,text]
----
overall_average(count())
----
. Click *Add layer* once more and choose the entry for annotations.
. In the editor of the annotation, type `failing mode on` in the *Label* control.
. Set the *Time* control to the moment the annotation marks, for example the minute when you will switch the load generator to its failing mode in the check below.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-12-annotation.svg["Annotation editor in Lens with the Label control typed failing mode on, marked by callout 29, and the Time control with a date and time, marked by callout 30",width=900]
. Click *Save and return* in the second bar.

=== Draw percentiles of the latency

The logs have no duration, but the traces do, so this panel uses the second data view.

[start=32]
. Back on the dashboard, click *Add* and then *Visualization*.
. In Lens, choose the chart type *Line*.
. Open the *Data view* picker and choose *Bookshelf traces (guides)*.
. Drag `@timestamp` onto the dashed *Horizontal axis* button.
. Drag the field `attributes.transaction.duration.us`, the duration of the transactions in microseconds, onto the dashed *Vertical axis* button.
. Click the new *Vertical axis* entry and choose the function *Percentile* for the field.
. Type `95` as the percentile. Type `p95 of duration.us` in the name control if you want the short label of the figure. The line shows the duration below which 95 percent of the transactions finish. Add the 50th percentile and the 99th in the same way if you want to compare the typical and the worst cases.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-13-percentile.svg["Lens editor on the data view Bookshelf traces (guides) with the dimension editor of the Vertical axis: the function Percentile is marked by callout 37 and the percentile value 95 by callout 38",width=900]
. Close the editor and click *Save and return*.

=== Compute the error ratio with a formula

[start=40]
. Click *Add* and then *Visualization* once more.
. In Lens, choose the chart type *Line* and open the *Data view* picker to choose *Bookshelf logs (guides)* again.
. Drag `@timestamp` onto the dashed *Horizontal axis* button.
. Click the dashed *Vertical axis* button and open the *Formula* tab of its editor.
. Type the formula that divides the number of log records with the severity `ERROR` by the number of all records:
+
[source,text]
----
count(kql='severity_text : "ERROR"') / count()
----
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k6-14-formula.svg["Dimension editor of a new Lens chart with the tab Formula selected: the formula that divides the ERROR records by all records is typed in the editor (callout 44) and the Value format control is set to Percent (callout 45)",width=900]
. Set the *Value format* control to `Percent`, so that the axis and the tooltip show `5%` instead of `0.05`.
. Type `Error ratio` in the name control of the editor, so that the chart shows that label instead of the formula.
. For the week-over-week change, a second *Vertical axis* entry with this formula and the same value format draws how much the number of requests grew compared with the same time a week earlier. In the lab, use `10m` instead of `1w`, because the lab has no week of data:
+
[source,text]
----
count() / count(shift='1w') - 1
----
. Close the editor and click *Save and return*.
. Back on the dashboard, click *Save* in the second bar.
. Click *Exit edit*.

== Check the result

Open *Dashboards* and then *Bookshelf (guides)*. It must show the lines of the requests per minute with the shifted copy and the reference line, the percentile of the latency, and the error ratio, which is
close to `0%` in the normal mode.

To see the error ratio and the annotation react, force errors and press *Refresh*:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/failing
----

Within about a minute the error ratio rises and the lines of the URIs change. Restore normal traffic afterwards:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal
----

== Variations

* Replace the percentile of the traces by the average or the maximum of the same field and compare the shapes.
* Break the first panel down by `attributes.status` instead of `attributes.uri` to see the share of the `404` responses over time.

== What can go wrong

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Cause |Fix

|The workspace says that no results were found
|The time picker covers *Last 15 minutes* and the load generator is stopped, or the lab started a moment ago.
|Widen the time picker to *Last 1 hour*, check the mode with `curl localhost:8090/`, and send `curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal` if it is not `normal`.

|The data view *Bookshelf traces (guides)* is not in the picker
|The data view was not created.
|Create it as described in *Before you start*, then reopen the editor.

|The shifted line is empty
|The time shift is longer than the data that Elasticsearch holds, or the time picker does not cover it.
|Use a shift shorter than the age of the lab data, such as `10m`, and a time picker of at least *Last 1 hour*.

|The formula shows a red error
|A function name or a quote is mistyped, or the formula mixes a field of the logs with a data view of the traces.
|Retype the formula with the quotes as shown, and check that the data view of the layer is *Bookshelf logs (guides)*.

|The error ratio stays at `0%` in the normal mode
|There are no log records with the severity `ERROR` in the time range.
|Switch the load generator to its failing mode as in *Check the result*.
|===

== Clean up

Keep the dashboard and the panels: the following guides in this series extend it. The data view *Bookshelf traces (guides)* can stay too; delete it in *Stack Management* under *Data Views* when you no longer need it.
Stopping the profile with `docker compose --profile kibana down -v` deletes the volumes and therefore all of them.
