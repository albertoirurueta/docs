= Kibana Guide: Proportion and Distribution Charts
:description: Step-by-step guide to Kibana 9.5: build a donut, a treemap, a waffle, a mosaic, a tag cloud and a heat map in Lens from the Bookshelf log records, add the donut and the heat map to the dashboard Bookshelf (guides), and learn when each of these charts is the wrong choice.
:keywords: Kibana, Lens, pie, donut, treemap, waffle, mosaic, heat map, tag cloud, Slice by, partition chart, proportion, distribution, Bookshelf, step by step, guide

include::partial$ai-disclaimer.adoc[]

Checked on 2026-11-11 against Kibana 9.5.x.

== What you build

You add two panels to the dashboard *Bookshelf (guides)*: a donut that shows what share of the Bookshelf log records each URI has, and a heat map that shows how busy each URI is over time.
On the way you draw the same data as a pie, a treemap, a waffle, a mosaic and a tag cloud in the Lens editor, so that you see what each chart is good at and when it is the wrong choice.

image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-14-result.svg["Finished Kibana dashboard Bookshelf (guides) in view mode with a donut of the log records by attributes.uri and a heat map of the requests per attributes.uri over time",width=900]

== Before you start

* Start the Kibana profile of the xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/hands-on-lab.adoc[hands-on lab] and wait about a minute:
+
[source,bash]
----
cd lab
docker compose --profile kibana up -d
----
* Open http://localhost:5601 and sign in as `elastic` with the password `lab-elastic-password`. When Kibana opens its full-screen
  welcome page, click *Explore on my own*.
* Do the guide xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-first-dashboard.adoc[Create a dashboard and the first Lens panel], which creates the dashboard *Bookshelf (guides)* and shows
  the maps of the Lens editor and of a dashboard in edit mode. Do the guides on time series charts and on metric, gauge and table too if you want the dashboard to carry their panels; this guide does not need them.
  It needs the data view *Bookshelf logs (guides)*, with the index pattern `logs-generic.otel-default` and the time field `@timestamp`.
* The load generator sends traffic to the Bookshelf service in its default mode, which fills the log data stream. Lens needs no licence beyond the basic one; the lab runs a trial licence anyway.
* The guide was checked on Elasticsearch and Kibana 9.5.6. The figures are drawings of the Kibana 9.5 user interface, not screenshots, so small details can differ from your screen;
  the chart type list and the names of the drop zones of the partition charts and of the heat map are drawn from the Elastic documentation. A highlighted box with a number marks the control of the step with the same number.

== Steps

The guide works in the Lens editor and on a dashboard in edit mode. The screen maps of both are in the first dashboard guide; come back to them when a control is not where a figure shows it.
Whenever Lens leaves a dimension empty after you change the chart type, drag the field onto the dashed button again: the types keep what they can and ask for the rest.

=== Open the dashboard in edit mode

. Open *Dashboards* in the primary navigation and click *Bookshelf (guides)*.
. Click *Edit* in the second bar.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-01-open-dashboard.svg["Kibana dashboard Bookshelf (guides) in view mode with its bar chart panel; the Edit button of the second bar is marked by callout 2",width=900]
. Click *Add* in the second bar. The *Add to dashboard* flyout opens.
. In the flyout, click *Visualization* ("Build charts, metrics, and tables with a point-and-click editor."). *Visualization (query)* is the ES|QL editor of the next guide.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-02-add-visualization.svg["Dashboard in edit mode with the Add to dashboard flyout open: the Add button of the second bar is marked by callout 3 and the Visualization entry of the flyout by callout 4",width=900]

=== Build a pie and a donut

[start=5]
. Lens opens. Check that the *Data view* picker shows *Bookshelf logs (guides)*. Open the chart type list with the button of the layer card, which shows *Bar*, and choose *Pie*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-03-chart-type-list.svg["Lens editor with an empty workspace and the chart type list open under the Bar button of the layer card; the entry Pie is marked by callout 5",width=900]
. In *Search field names*, type `uri`. Drag the field `attributes.uri` onto the dashed *Slice by* button, so that each URI becomes one slice.
. Drag the entry *Records*, above *Available fields*, onto the dashed *Metric* button. Lens shows it as *Count of records* and draws the pie.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-04-pie-dimensions.svg["Lens editor with the chart type Pie, the field list narrowed to attributes.uri and Records; the field is dragged onto the dashed Slice by button (callout 6) and Records onto the dashed Metric button (callout 7)",width=900]
. Open the chart type list again and choose *Donut*. The slices keep their data and the centre of the chart opens. Pie and donut show the same numbers; the donut leaves room for a total in its hole and is easier to read next to other panels.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-05-donut.svg["Lens editor with a pie chart of the log records by attributes.uri and the chart type list open; the entry Donut is marked by callout 8",width=900]
. Click *Save and return*. The dashboard is back in edit mode with the donut as a new panel.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-06-save-the-donut.svg["Lens editor with a donut chart of the log records by attributes.uri; the Save and return button of the second bar is marked by callout 9",width=900]

=== Try a treemap, a waffle, a mosaic and a tag cloud

[start=10]
. Click *Add* and then *Visualization* again, as in steps 3 and 4. A new Lens editor opens with an empty workspace.
. Open the chart type list and choose *Treemap*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-07-treemap-type.svg["A new Lens editor with an empty workspace and the chart type list open; the entry Treemap is marked by callout 11",width=900]
. Drag the field `attributes.uri` onto the dashed *Slice by* button.
. Drag the field `attributes.status` onto the second dashed *Slice by* button, which appears once the first one is filled. Each URI tile is now split by status, so a small tile for `404` sits inside the tile of every URI.
. Drag *Records* onto the dashed *Metric* button. The treemap draws one tile per URI, sized by the count, and splits it by status.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-08-treemap-dimensions.svg["Lens editor with the chart type Treemap: attributes.uri is in the first Slice by button (callout 12), attributes.status is dragged onto the second dashed Slice by button (callout 13) and Records onto the dashed Metric button (callout 14)",width=900]
. Open the chart type list and choose *Waffle*. A waffle draws 100 squares and colours as many as each value's percentage, which makes a share such as 12 per cent of status `404` easy to count.
. Open the list again and choose *Mosaic*. A mosaic draws one column per value of the first dimension, as wide as its share, and stacks the second dimension inside it, so it shows two proportions at once.
. Open the list again and choose *Tag cloud*. A tag cloud writes each value of the first dimension as a word sized by the metric. It is decorative: the sizes cannot be read as numbers.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-09-partition-types.svg["Lens editor with a treemap of attributes.uri and attributes.status and the chart type list open; the entries Waffle, Mosaic and Tag cloud are marked by callouts 15, 16 and 17, one for each time the list is opened",width=900]

=== Build a heat map and save it

[start=18]
. Open the chart type list and choose *Heat map*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-10-heat-map-type.svg["Lens editor with a tag cloud of the attributes.uri values and the chart type list open; the entry Heat map is marked by callout 18",width=900]
. Drag the field `@timestamp` onto the dashed *Horizontal axis* button, so that time runs from left to right.
. Drag the field `attributes.uri` onto the dashed *Vertical axis* button, so that each URI is one row. The cell value is the count of records; if the cell value button is empty, drag *Records* onto it.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-11-heat-map-dimensions.svg["Lens editor with the chart type Heat map: the field @timestamp is dragged onto the dashed Horizontal axis button (callout 19) and attributes.uri onto the dashed Vertical axis button (callout 20); the Cell value button holds Count of records",width=900]
. Each cell is now one time bucket of one URI, and darker cells have more records. Click *Save and return*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-12-save-the-heat-map.svg["Lens editor with a heat map of the requests per attributes.uri over time; the Save and return button of the second bar is marked by callout 21",width=900]

=== Save the dashboard

[start=22]
. The dashboard shows the donut and the heat map in edit mode (the figure leaves out the bar chart of the first guide). Click *Save* in the second bar.
. Click *Exit edit* to return to view mode.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k8-13-save-the-dashboard.svg["Kibana dashboard in edit mode with the donut panel and the heat map panel; the Save button of the second bar is marked by callout 22 and the Exit edit button by callout 23",width=900]

== Check the result

Open *Dashboards* and then *Bookshelf (guides)*. The dashboard must show the donut with one slice per URI and the heat map with one row per URI and one column per time bucket. The shares in the figures are examples; the numbers on your screen depend on how long the lab has been running.

Change the traffic and press *Refresh* to see the panels react:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/failing
----

Within about a minute the counts per URI move, because the load generator writes error lines in this mode. Restore normal traffic afterwards:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal
----

== Variations

* Break the donut down by `attributes.status` instead of `attributes.uri` to see the share of `200` and `404`, and compare it with the waffle.
* Add `severity_text` as a second slice dimension of the treemap to see the share of `INFO` and `ERROR` lines per URI.
* Change the heat map to `attributes.status` on the vertical axis to see when the `404` lines cluster in time.

When each chart is the wrong choice:

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Chart |Wrong when |Use instead
|Pie and donut |There are more than about five slices, the slices are close in size, or you want to follow the values over time. |A bar chart sorted by size, or a table.
|Treemap |You need exact values, or the tiles are very unequal: the small ones become unreadable. |A table with a bar next to the number, or a bar chart.
|Waffle |There are many categories, or the shares are tiny: a value below 1 per cent has no square. |A metric with a percentage, or a bar chart.
|Mosaic |Readers are not used to it, or one of the two dimensions has many values. |Two donuts, or a stacked bar chart.
|Heat map |You need the exact number of a cell, or the colour range is not fixed, so that two heat maps cannot be compared. |A table, or a line chart with a few series.
|Tag cloud |The values must be compared or ranked. |A table sorted by the count.
|===

== What can go wrong

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Cause |Fix
|The chart type list does not show *Waffle* or *Tag cloud* in the place of the figure
|The list is grouped or ordered differently in your Kibana version.
|Look for the name in the whole list; the names are the ones of the Elastic documentation.

|The chart is empty after you change the chart type
|Lens could not carry a dimension over to the new type.
|Drag the field onto the dashed button of the new type again.

|A slice named *Other* appears
|Lens shows the top values of the field only and folds the rest into *Other*.
|Open the dimension and raise the number of values, or accept the grouping.

|The heat map has one single colour
|All cells have nearly the same count, or the time range is too short to make buckets.
|Widen the time picker, for example to *Last 1 hour*.

|The workspace says that no results were found
|The time picker covers *Last 15 minutes* and the load generator or the Bookshelf service is stopped.
|Check the mode with `curl localhost:8090/`, send `curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal` and press *Refresh*.
|===
