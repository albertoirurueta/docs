:description: Step-by-step guide to Kibana 9.5: read the Alerts table and the alert details, acknowledge, mark as untracked and snooze alerts, snooze and disable a rule, create a maintenance window, open a case from an alert, add a comment and a visualization, and close the case.

Checked on 2026-11-11 against Kibana 9.5.x.

== What you build

You work on the alerts the Bookshelf rules produce. You read an alert in the Observability *Alerts* page, acknowledge one alert, mark another as untracked and snooze a third, then
snooze and disable a rule. You schedule a maintenance window that suppresses the notifications of one rule, and you open a case from an alert, add a comment and a
visualization to it and close it. The result is a closed case named `Guide error spike` that holds the alert and its comment.

image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-10-result.svg["Case page Guide error spike in Kibana 9.5, drawn generically, with the status Closed, the alert attached from the Alerts table and the comment added to the activity feed",width=900]

== Before you start

* Start the Kibana profile of the xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/hands-on-lab.adoc[hands-on lab] and wait about a minute:
+
[source,bash]
----
cd lab
docker compose --profile kibana up -d
----
* Open http://localhost:5601 and sign in as `elastic` with the password `lab-elastic-password`. When Kibana opens its full-screen
  welcome page, click *Explore on my own*.
* Do the previous guide, xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-observability-rules-and-slos.adoc[Observability rules and SLOs]. This guide uses its rule `Guide Bookshelf error ratio`,
  which keeps one alert for each URI. The maintenance window uses the rule `Guide Bookshelf error logs` and its webhook connector from
  xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-create-a-rule.adoc[Create a rule with a connector].
* Alerts need failing traffic. Force errors and wait two or three minutes, until the *Alerts* page lists at least two active alerts:
+
[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/failing
----
* Licence: alert actions and cases need the basic features of the Observability solution. Maintenance windows need a Platinum licence, which the trial licence of the lab includes.
* Not run end to end: the rules list controls, the case page after the case is created, the visualization control of a case and the way a case is closed were not opened in the lab. The alert menu, the alert snooze panel, the *Maintenance Windows* pages and the *Create case* form were replayed.
  The steps name the control and leave the exact layout to your screen. The labels of the Alerts table and of the alert details come from the lab.
* The figures are drawings of the Kibana 9.5 user interface, not screenshots, so small details can differ from your screen.
  A highlighted box with a number marks the control of the step with the same number.

== Steps

Three screens are used: the Observability *Alerts* page, the rules list of Stack Management and the *Maintenance Windows* page. Kibana 9.5 has no *Mute* action; the actions of an alert are
*Acknowledge*, *Mark as untracked* and *Snooze*. The page xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/alert-lifecycle-snooze-and-maintenance-windows.adoc[Alert Lifecycle, Snooze & Maintenance Windows]
explains how they differ.

=== Read the Alerts table

. Click the menu button (*Toggle primary navigation*) at the left of the second bar and, under *Observability*, click *Alerts*. The page shows the counters *Rule count*, *Disabled*, *Snoozed* and *Errors* at the top right, and a table with the alerts of the last 24 hours.
. In the first row, click the *Alert details* icon (the expand icon) under *Actions*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-01-alerts-table.svg["Observability Alerts page of Kibana 9.5 with the counters Rule count, Disabled, Snoozed and Errors, the filters, and a table of three Active alerts; the menu button of the second bar is marked by callout 1 and the Alert details icon of the first row by callout 2",width=900]
. In the flyout, read the *Reason* and the *Document Summary* table: *Status*, *Triggered*, *Duration*, *Observed value*, *Threshold*, *Rule name*, *Rule type* and *Cases*.
. Click the tab *Metadata* to see the fields of the alert document.
. Close the flyout with the close button at its top right.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-02-alert-details.svg["Alert details flyout of Kibana 9.5 over the Alerts table: the Reason text and the Document Summary table with Status, Triggered, Duration, Observed value, Threshold, Rule name, Rule type and Cases are marked by callout 3, the Metadata tab by callout 4 and the close button by callout 5",width=900]

=== Acknowledge and untrack alerts

[start=6]
. In the first row, click *More actions* (the three dots under *Actions*) and then *Acknowledge*. The menu lists *Add to existing case*, *Add to new case*, *View rule details*, *View alert details*, *Acknowledge*, *Mark as untracked*, *Snooze* and *Edit tags*. The alert stays active and gets an acknowledged badge, which tells the team that someone is looking at it.
. In the second row, click *More actions* and then *Mark as untracked*. Kibana stops tracking that alert: its actions stop, its status never changes again, and the choice cannot be reverted for that alert.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-03-acknowledge-menu.svg["Alerts table of Kibana 9.5 with the More actions menu of the first row open: Add to existing case, Add to new case, Acknowledge, Mark as untracked, Snooze and Edit tags; Acknowledge is marked by callout 6 and Mark as untracked by callout 7",width=900]

=== Snooze and disable a rule

[start=8]
. In the third row, click *More actions* and then *Snooze*. Snoozing one alert keeps the rule and its other alerts running.
. In the panel *Snooze*, which has the tabs *Quick Snooze* and *Condition based*, answer "How long should this alert be snoozed?" with *1h* (the other choices are *Indefinitely*, *8h*, *24h* and *Custom*) and click *Snooze alert*.
. Click *Manage Rules* at the top right of the *Alerts* page.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-04-snooze-menu.svg["Alerts table of Kibana 9.5 with the More actions menu of the third row open; the entry Snooze is marked by callout 8 and the Manage Rules link at the top right by callout 10",width=900]
. In the rules list, find `Guide Bookshelf error ratio` and use its snooze control to snooze the rule for a short period. A snoozed rule keeps running and creating alerts, but they send no actions.
. Turn the *Enabled* switch of the rule off to disable it. A disabled rule stops running, and the *Disabled* counter of the *Alerts* page goes up. Turn it on again when you finish the guide.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-05-rules-list.svg["Rules page of Stack Management in Kibana 9.5, drawn generically, with one rule row Guide Bookshelf error ratio; its snooze control is marked by callout 11 and its Enabled switch by callout 12",width=900]

=== Create a maintenance window

A maintenance window suppresses the notifications of the alerts it matches for a period. The alerts are still created. The window below is aimed at the rule `Guide Bookshelf error logs`, the rule with the webhook,
so that you can see the effect on the webhook receiver.

[start=13]
. Open *Stack Management* from the menu and, in the left menu under *Alerts and Insights*, click *Maintenance Windows*.
. Click *Create a maintenance window* (on a page that already lists windows the button is *Create window*). The page *Create maintenance window* has the sections *Name*, *Timeframe* and *Filters*.
. In *Name*, type `Guide deploy window`.
. In *Timeframe*, the *Schedule* starts now and ends in 30 minutes; move the end 30 minutes later so that it lasts one hour, check the *Time zone*, and leave *Repeat* off.
. In *Filters*, turn on *Filter alerts* and type the KQL filter below.
+
[source,text]
----
kibana.alert.rule.name : "Guide Bookshelf error logs"
----
. Click *Create maintenance window*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-06-maintenance-window.svg["Create maintenance window page of Kibana 9.5 with the sections Name, Timeframe and Filters: the name Guide deploy window is marked by callout 15, the Schedule and Time zone by callout 16, the Filter alerts query on the rule name by callout 17 and the Create maintenance window button by callout 18",width=900]

=== Open a case from an alert

[start=19]
. Go back to the *Alerts* page. In a row, click *More actions* and then *Add to new case*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-07-add-to-new-case-menu.svg["Alerts table of Kibana 9.5 with the More actions menu of the first row open; the entry Add to new case is marked by callout 19",width=900]
. In the form that opens, which has the fields of the *Create case* page, type the *Name* `Guide error spike`.
. In *Tags*, type `guide`.
. In *Description*, type `The error ratio of the Bookshelf logs is above 0.3 for every URI.` and keep the *Severity* *Low*.
. Click *Create case*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-08-create-case.svg["Create case form of Kibana 9.5 drawn from the Create case page: the Name Guide error spike is marked by callout 20, the tag guide by callout 21, the Description by callout 22 and the Create case button by callout 23; Severity keeps its default Low",width=900]

=== Add a comment and a visualization, and close the case

The case page was not opened in the lab, so the figure draws it generically.

[start=24]
. Open the case if Kibana did not open it. On the tab *Activity*, type this comment in the comment box and click the button that adds it.
+
[source,text]
----
Error ratio above 0.3 on all URIs since the failing mode started.
----
. Add a visualization to the case with its *Visualization* control, for example a Lens bar chart of the count of records by `severity_text`. Use an absolute time range, so that the chart keeps showing the incident later.
. Use the status control of the case, which shows *Open*, to close the case.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k15-09-case-comment-and-status.svg["Case page Guide error spike in Kibana 9.5, drawn generically, with the Activity tab: the comment box holding the text is marked by callout 24, the Visualization control of its toolbar by callout 25 and the status control, showing Open, by callout 26",width=900]

== Check the result

* On the *Alerts* page, the first alert carries the acknowledged badge, the second alert is untracked and no longer changes, and the *Snoozed* and *Disabled* counters at the top right count your snoozed and disabled rules.
* Open the details of the alert you attached to the case: the row *Cases* of the *Document Summary* names `Guide error spike`.
* In *Cases*, the case `Guide error spike` is listed as closed, with the alert under its attachments and your comment in its activity feed.
* To see the maintenance window work, read the webhook receiver before and after a failing run. During the window the rule `Guide Bookshelf error logs` still turns active, but no new delivery should arrive:
+
[source,bash]
----
curl -s localhost:8081
----
+
Without the window the same rule sends a delivery about three seconds after it turns active. This effect was not run end to end in the lab.

Restore normal traffic afterwards:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal
----

== Variations

* Add the filter `kibana.alert.rule.name : "Guide*"` to the maintenance window to cover every rule of these guides, or turn on *Repeat* for a weekly window.
* Use *Add to existing case* instead of *Add to new case* to collect several alerts in one case.
* Snooze the alert until its severity changes instead of for a period when the rule has a warning threshold; the reference page on the alert lifecycle describes the conditions.

== What can go wrong

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Cause |Fix

|The *Alerts* table is empty
|The time picker covers *Last 24 hours* but the lab is in normal mode and the rules have not fired, or the *Status* filter is `active` and every alert recovered.
|Send `curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/failing`, wait two or three minutes and press *Refresh*; open the *Status* filter to see recovered alerts.

|*Mark as untracked* cannot be undone
|An untracked alert is closed for good: its status never changes again.
|Use *Acknowledge* or *Snooze* when you only want to calm an alert.

|The webhook still receives a delivery during the maintenance window
|The alert started before the window, or the filter does not match the rule name, or the window has not started.
|Check the schedule and the time zone, and compare the filter with the *Rule name* in the *Document Summary* of the alert.

|The window page is not found in Stack Management
|The licence is not Platinum or trial.
|Check the licence with `curl -s -u elastic:lab-elastic-password http://localhost:9200/_license`; the lab starts a trial licence.

|The case cannot be created
|The *Name* or *Description* is empty.
|Fill both fields; they are required.
|===

== Clean up

Turn the rule `Guide Bookshelf error ratio` on again and remove its snooze, so that the following guides see its alerts. Delete the maintenance window `Guide deploy window` on the *Maintenance Windows* page when it is no longer needed. Keep the case if you
want to look at it again, or delete it in *Cases*. Stopping the profile with `docker compose --profile kibana down -v` deletes everything the lab stores.
