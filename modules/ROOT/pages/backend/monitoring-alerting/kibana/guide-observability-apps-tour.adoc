== What you build

You walk through the Observability apps with the data of the lab: the *Service inventory* of APM, the page of the `bookshelf` service, one of its transactions with the waterfall of a sample trace, and the log records of that trace in Discover.
Nothing is created; the result is a route you can repeat for any service: inventory, service, transaction, trace, logs. The guide ends with the *Hosts* page, which stays empty in the lab, and says why.

image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-10-result.svg["Summary of the tour in four cards: the Service inventory with the bookshelf service, the Transactions of the service, the trace waterfall of GET /api/search and Discover filtered by the trace id",width=900]

== Before you start

* Start the Kibana profile of the xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/hands-on-lab.adoc[hands-on lab] and wait about a minute:
+
[source,bash]
----
cd lab
docker compose --profile kibana up -d
----
* Open http://localhost:5601 and sign in as `elastic` with the password `lab-elastic-password`. When Kibana opens its full-screen
  welcome page, click *Explore on my own*.
* Do the previous guide, xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-anomaly-detection-and-aiops.adoc[Anomaly detection and AIOps]. The guide that creates the data view, xref:backend/monitoring-alerting/kibana/guide-data-views-and-file-upload.adoc[Upload a file and create a data view], is needed too: the last steps use the data view *Bookshelf logs (guides)*.
* Wait about two minutes after the start, with the load generator in its default mode, so that APM has transactions to show.
* The lab sends logs and traces to Elasticsearch, and APM builds its views from the traces. It sends no system metrics, so *Infrastructure* and *Hosts* have no data; the last step shows the empty page and does not try to fill it.
* APM and the Observability pages need no licence beyond the basic one; the lab runs a trial licence anyway.
* The guide was checked on Elasticsearch and Kibana 9.5.6. The figures are drawings of the Kibana 9.5 user interface, not screenshots, so small details can differ from your screen.
  The trace waterfall, the *Logs* tab, the *Service map* and the Discover document list are drawn generically; look for a control by its name when it is not where a figure shows it.
  A highlighted box with a number marks the control of the step with the same number.

== Steps

The guide works in three apps of the primary navigation: *Applications* (APM), *Discover* and *Infrastructure*. The numbers you see, such as 92 ms, 264.0 tpm and 0.9 %, change with the load; the structure of the pages does not. Kibana 9.5 still shows a banner on the Observability pages that offers the Observability navigation; ignore it, this guide uses the classic navigation of the default space.

=== Open the service inventory

. Click the menu button (*Toggle primary navigation*) at the left of the second bar.
. Under *Observability*, click *Applications*. If the entry opens a submenu, click *Service inventory* in it.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-01-primary-navigation.svg["Kibana 9.5 with the primary navigation open and the Observability section listing Overview, Alerts, SLOs, Cases, Logs, Infrastructure, Applications, Synthetics and User Experience; callout 1 marks the menu button of the second bar and callout 2 marks Applications",width=900]
. On the tab *Inventory*, find the row `bookshelf` in the environment `lab`. The columns are *Name*, *Environment*, *Latency (avg.)*, *Throughput*, *Failed transaction rate* and *Actions*. The tabs *Service map* and *Service groups* sit next to *Inventory*; the filters *Environment* and *Comparison* narrow the table and compare it with the day or week before.
. Click *bookshelf* in the *Name* column.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-02-service-inventory.svg["Service inventory of APM in Kibana 9.5 with the tabs Inventory, Service map and Service groups, the Environment and Comparison filters, the anomaly detection banner and a table with one row, bookshelf in the environment lab; callout 3 marks the row and callout 4 marks the name bookshelf",width=900]

=== Follow one transaction

[start=5]
. The service page opens with the header *bookshelf* and the tabs *Overview*, *Transactions*, *Dependencies*, *Errors*, *Metrics*, *Infrastructure*, *Service map*, *Logs*, *Alerts* and *Dashboards*. Click the tab *Transactions*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-03-service-overview.svg["Service page of bookshelf in Kibana 9.5 on its Overview tab with the tabs Overview, Transactions, Dependencies, Errors, Metrics, Infrastructure, Service map, Logs, Alerts and Dashboards and three charts for latency, throughput and failed transaction rate; the Transactions tab is marked by callout 5",width=900]
. Check that *Transaction type* is `request`, then click `GET /api/search` in the table of transactions. The table lists `GET /api/loans`, `GET /api/search`, `GET /api/books` and `+GET /api/books/{id}+`, each with *Latency (avg.)*, *Throughput*, *Failed transaction rate* and *Impact*.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-04-transactions.svg["Transactions tab of the bookshelf service in Kibana 9.5 with the transaction type request, charts for latency, throughput and failed transaction rate, and a table of four transactions, GET /api/loans, GET /api/search, GET /api/books and GET /api/books/\{id}; the row GET /api/search is marked by callout 6",width=900]
. On the page of the transaction, scroll to the waterfall of the trace sample. Each bar is a span of the request; its width is its duration on the time axis. A request that the service handled by itself shows as one bar.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-05-trace-waterfall.svg["Page of the transaction GET /api/search in Kibana 9.5 with a latency distribution and, in the Trace sample panel, a waterfall whose bar for the transaction GET /api/search spans the whole time axis from 0 to 92 ms; the waterfall is marked by callout 7",width=900]

=== From the trace to its logs

[start=8]
. Go back to the service page (use the breadcrumb *bookshelf*) and click the tab *Logs*. The tab lists the log records of the service, such as `Served /api/search`.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-06-service-logs.svg["Logs tab of the bookshelf service in Kibana 9.5 listing four log lines with a timestamp and a message such as Served /api/search; the Logs tab is marked by callout 8",width=900]
. To follow one request through all its records, open *Discover* from the primary navigation (*Analytics* → *Discover*) and check that the data view is *Bookshelf logs (guides)*.
. Expand one record of the document table and copy the value of the field `trace_id`.
. In the query bar, type `+trace_id : "PASTE-THE-ID"+`, with the copied value in place of `PASTE-THE-ID`, and click *Refresh*. Every record of that request, from the same trace, remains.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-07-discover-trace.svg["Discover of Kibana 9.5 with the data view Bookshelf logs (guides) marked by callout 9, the query bar holding trace_id with a trace id and marked by callout 11, and a table of two log documents with the columns @timestamp, body.text, severity_text and trace_id, the first row marked by callout 10",width=900]

=== Look at the other views

[start=12]
. Go back to the service page and click the tab *Service map*. The map draws the service and, when the traces show them, the services and databases it calls.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-08-service-map.svg["Service map tab of the bookshelf service in Kibana 9.5 with a single node named bookshelf in the middle of the map; the Service map tab is marked by callout 12",width=900]
. Open *Infrastructure* from the primary navigation and choose *Hosts*. The page shows the message "Detect and resolve problems with your hosts" and the button *Add data*, and nothing else. The lab collects no host or system metrics, so *Hosts* and the *Infrastructure inventory* have nothing to draw; with a collector that sends them, this page lists hosts with their CPU, memory and disk. Do not click *Add data* in the lab.
+
image::monitoring-alerting-kibana-guide-k17-09-hosts-empty.svg["Infrastructure Hosts page of Kibana 9.5 in the lab: it shows only the message Detect and resolve problems with your hosts and the button Add data, because the lab sends no host metrics; the message is marked by callout 13",width=900]

== Check the result

You went from the *Service inventory* to the transactions of `bookshelf`, to the waterfall of one trace, and to the log records of the same trace in Discover. To see the failures show up in the same pages, force errors and reload the *Transactions* tab with a time range of *Last 15 minutes*:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/failing
----

Within about two minutes the *Failed transaction rate* of the transactions rises well above 1 %, and the failed requests have log records with `severity_text` `ERROR` in Discover. Restore normal traffic afterwards:

[source,bash]
----
curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal
----

== Variations

* In the *Service inventory*, turn on the anomaly detection that the banner offers once you have done the previous guide; it adds an anomaly score to each service.
* Open the other transactions of the table the same way and compare their waterfalls and latency.

== What can go wrong

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Cause |Fix

|The *Service inventory* is empty
|The lab started a moment ago, or the load generator is stopped, so APM has no transactions in the time range.
|Wait two minutes, widen the time picker to *Last 1 hour*, check the mode with `curl localhost:8090/`, and send `curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal` if it is not `normal`.

|The page of a transaction shows no trace sample
|The time range holds no sample of that transaction.
|Widen the time range and press *Refresh*.

|The query `+trace_id : "..."+` returns no documents in Discover
|The data view is not *Bookshelf logs (guides)*, the id was copied with extra characters, or the time range does not cover the record.
|Check the data view, paste the id between the quotes only, and widen the time range.

|*Hosts* shows only *Add data*
|The lab sends no host metrics.
|This is expected here; the page fills once a collector sends system metrics, as the reference page on infrastructure monitoring explains.
|===

== Clean up

The guide creates nothing; there is nothing to remove. If you changed the load generator, restore it with `curl -X POST localhost:8090/mode/normal`.
