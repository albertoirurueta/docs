A cron expression is a compact string that describes a recurring schedule. Quartz uses it in the `CronTrigger` (see
xref:backend/scheduling/triggers-and-schedules.adoc[Triggers and Schedules] for the other trigger types), Spring uses a
close relative of it in `@Scheduled(cron = ...)` and in `org.springframework.scheduling.support.CronExpression`, and Unix
`cron` and the Kubernetes `CronJob` use a third, shorter dialect. The three dialects look alike and are *not* the same:
copying an expression from one to another is the most common source of a job that runs on the wrong day, every second, or
never. This page defines the Quartz grammar, shows how to validate and preview an expression with the `CronExpression` API,
explains time zones and daylight-saving transitions, and translates between the dialects. For where a cron schedule fits
among the other options, see
xref:backend/scheduling/standard-scheduling-vs-quartz.adoc[Standard Scheduling vs Quartz]; for what happens when a fire
time is missed, see xref:backend/scheduling/misfires.adoc[Misfires].

It was written against Quartz 2.5.2, Spring Boot 4.1.1, Spring Framework 7.0.9 and Java 21 and verified on 5 October 2026:
every expression below was parsed and evaluated with `org.quartz.CronExpression.getNextValidTimeAfter` (Quartz 2.5.2) and,
for the Spring column, with `org.springframework.scheduling.support.CronExpression.next` (Spring Framework 7.0.9) in a
scratch project, and the fire times quoted are the measured ones. The Unix and Kubernetes columns are taken from the
`crontab(5)` manual page and the Kubernetes documentation and were *not* executed. The Quartz cron grammar is part of Quartz
itself, so it is identical on Spring Boot 3.5.16 (which also manages Quartz 2.5.2); the Spring column was measured on Spring
Framework 7.0.9 only, and Spring Boot 3.5 uses Spring Framework 6.2.19.

In the running *Bookshelf* scenario, three of the five jobs are cron-driven: the nightly overdue-loan reminders, the weekly
statistics report and the monthly retention cleanup. The cache refresh is a fixed-rate job and the per-member reminders are
one-shot triggers, so neither needs cron.

== The grammar

A Quartz expression has six required fields and one optional field, separated by white space. The order is fixed, and
unlike Unix cron it starts with *seconds*.

.The fields of a Quartz cron expression (verified against CronExpression 2.5.2)
[cols="1,2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Position |Field |Allowed values |Special characters

|1 |Seconds |`0`-`59` |`,` `-` `*` `/`
|2 |Minutes |`0`-`59` |`,` `-` `*` `/`
|3 |Hours |`0`-`23` |`,` `-` `*` `/`
|4 |Day-of-month |`1`-`31` |`,` `-` `*` `?` `/` `L` `W`
|5 |Month |`1`-`12` or `JAN`-`DEC` |`,` `-` `*` `/`
|6 |Day-of-week |`1`-`7` or `SUN`-`SAT`, where `1` is Sunday |`,` `-` `*` `?` `/` `L` `#`
|7 |Year (optional) |a year, or omitted |`,` `-` `*` `/`
|===

Names and special characters are case-insensitive: `0 0 12 ? * mon-fri` is accepted, and `toString()` returns it in upper
case. Out-of-range values are rejected when the expression is parsed, with a `java.text.ParseException`: hour `24`
("Hour values must be between 0 and 23"), second or minute `60` ("Minute and Second values must be between 0 and 59") and
month `13` ("Month values must be between 1 and 12") all fail.

The Javadoc of `CronExpression` in 2.5.2 still says that the month field is `0-11`. The parser disagrees: month `0` is
rejected ("Month values must be between 1 and 12") and month `12` is accepted and means December. Trust the parser, and
prefer the `JAN`-`DEC` names, which cannot be misread.

== Special characters

.What each special character means in Quartz
[cols="1,2,5,4",options="header"]
|===
|Character |Fields |Meaning |Example and measured result

|`*`
|all
|Every value of the field.
|`0 * * * * ?` fires every minute (12:01:00, 12:02:00, ...).

|`?`
|day-of-month, day-of-week
|"No specific value": used in whichever of the two day fields you are *not* restricting.
|`0 0 2 ? * MON-FRI`: day-of-month is unrestricted, day-of-week is restricted.

|`-`
|all
|An inclusive range.
|`0 0 9-17 ? * MON-FRI` fires hourly from 09:00 to 17:00 on weekdays.

|`,`
|all
|A list of values or ranges.
|`0 0 8,12,18 * * ?` fires at 08:00, 12:00 and 18:00.

|`/`
|all
|An increment: `a/n` means a, a+n, a+2n, ... up to the maximum of the field. It does not wrap around.
|`0 5/15 * * * ?` fires at minutes 5, 20, 35 and 50. `7/6` in the month field means July only.

|`L`
|day-of-month, day-of-week
|"Last". In day-of-month: the last day of the month; `L-3` is the third-to-last day. In day-of-week alone: `7` (Saturday). After a weekday: the last such weekday of the month.
|`0 0 12 L * ?` fires on 2026-10-31, 2026-11-30, 2026-12-31. `0 0 12 ? * 6L` fires on the last Friday: 2026-10-30. `0 0 0 ? * L` fires on Saturdays.

|`W`
|day-of-month
|"Nearest weekday" to the given day. It never crosses a month boundary.
|`0 0 12 15W * ?` fires on 2026-10-15 (a Thursday), then on Monday 2026-11-16, because 15 November is a Sunday. `1W` in August 2026 fires on Monday the 3rd, because the 1st is a Saturday.

|`LW`
|day-of-month
|The last weekday (Monday to Friday) of the month.
|`0 0 12 LW * ?` fires on Friday 2026-10-30, Monday 2026-11-30, Thursday 2026-12-31.

|`#`
|day-of-week
|"The nth such weekday of the month": `6#3` is the third Friday. Only one such expression is allowed in the field.
|`0 0 12 ? * 6#3` fires on 2026-10-16, 2026-11-20, 2026-12-18. `#6` is rejected ("A numeric value between 1 and 5 must follow the '#' option") and `3#1,5#2` is rejected ("Support for specifying multiple "nth" days is not implemented.").
|===

Two practical points about `L` and the others. First, the combinations the parser does *not* implement are rejected rather
than guessed: `6L,2` (an `L` weekday plus another weekday) fails with "Support for specifying 'L' with other days of the
week is not implemented". Second, the parser is more permissive than the documentation: `L,15` in day-of-month is accepted
and works (it fires on the 15th and on the last day), but `W` combined with a range, such as `10-20W`, is accepted and
produces consecutive days including weekends, which is not "nearest weekday". The Javadoc says not to combine `L` or `W`
with lists or ranges; follow it.

=== The 2.5.1 change to `L`

In the 2.5.0 release candidates a refactoring broke `L` and `L-n` in months with fewer than 31 days, so that "last day of
the month" schedules never fired in short months. The fix is pull request #1284, "Fix regression: 'L' last day of month
specifier with short months won't be triggered", in the Quartz 2.5.1 milestone. On 2.5.2 the behaviour is correct, and it
was measured across a leap-year boundary:

.`L`, `L-1` and `LW` in short months, Quartz 2.5.2 (measured from 2027-01-31 13:00, Europe/Madrid)
[cols="2,6",options="header"]
|===
|Expression |Fire times

|`0 0 12 L * ?` |2027-02-28, 2027-03-31, 2027-04-30, 2027-05-31, and later 2028-02-29
|`0 0 12 L-1 * ?` |2027-02-27, 2027-03-30, 2027-04-29, 2027-05-30
|`0 0 12 LW * ?` |Friday 2027-02-26, Wednesday 2027-03-31, Friday 2027-04-30, Monday 2027-05-31
|===

The same expressions on Quartz 2.4.1 give the same dates. The 2.5.0 and 2.5.1 artifacts were not run for this page, so the
broken behaviour itself is quoted from the pull request, not reproduced. The practical advice is simple: on Spring Boot 4.1
and 3.5.16 the starter manages Quartz 2.5.2, so you are not affected; if you pin Quartz yourself, do not stay on 2.5.0.

Source: https://github.com/quartz-scheduler/quartz/pull/1284[Quartz pull request #1284 -- Fix regression: "L" last day of month specifier with short months], https://github.com/quartz-scheduler/quartz/releases/tag/v2.5.1[Quartz release v2.5.1]

== The `?` rule

Quartz cannot combine a day-of-month restriction with a day-of-week restriction (Unix cron would run the job when *either*
matches). Instead, exactly one of the two fields must be `?`, which means "I do not care about this field".

.The ? rule: valid and invalid combinations (Quartz 2.5.2, measured)
[cols="3,2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Expression |Valid? |Result

|`0 0 2 * * ?` |yes |Every day at 02:00.
|`0 0 2 ? * MON-FRI` |yes |Weekdays at 02:00.
|`0 30 3 1 * ?` |yes |The 1st of every month at 03:30.
|`0 0 2 * * *` |no |"Support for specifying both a day-of-week AND a day-of-month parameter is not implemented."
|`0 0 12 13 * FRI` |no |Same message: "Friday the 13th" cannot be written in one Quartz expression.
|`0 0 2 ? * ?` |no |"'?' can only be specified for Day-of-Month -OR- Day-of-Week."
|===

If you need "Friday the 13th", use a `CronTrigger` on the 13th (`0 0 12 13 * ?`) and let the job check the day of the week,
or use a `Calendar` (see xref:backend/scheduling/calendars.adoc[Calendars]).

== The `CronExpression` API

`org.quartz.CronExpression` is a standalone parser and evaluator. You can use it without a scheduler
to validate user-supplied schedules, to preview fire times in a UI and to test trigger definitions. The members used in
this page, all checked against the 2.5.2 source and run:

.The CronExpression API
[cols="4,6",options="header"]
|===
|Member |Behaviour (measured)

|`new CronExpression(String)`
|Parses the expression, throws `java.text.ParseException` (checked) if it is invalid and `IllegalArgumentException` if it is `null`.

|`static boolean isValidExpression(String)`
|`true` if the expression parses. `0 2 * * *` (five fields), `@daily` and `0 0 24 * * ?` all return `false`.

|`static void validateExpression(String)`
|Throws the `ParseException` instead of returning `false`; the message says what is wrong.

|`Date getNextValidTimeAfter(Date)`
|The first fire time strictly after the argument, in the expression's time zone, or `null` if there is none (`0 0 2 31 2 ?` never fires).

|`Date getNextInvalidTimeAfter(Date)`
|The first second after the argument that does *not* match; from Monday 02:00:00 with `0 0 2 ? * MON-FRI` it returns 02:00:01.

|`Date getTimeBefore(Date)`
|The fire time before the argument. Added in 2.5.1 (pull request #1350); from Monday 12:00 it returns Monday 02:00.

|`boolean isSatisfiedBy(Date)`
|`true` only if the date matches at second granularity: Monday 02:00:00 yes, Monday 02:00:30 no.

|`void setTimeZone(TimeZone)` / `TimeZone getTimeZone()`
|The zone in which the fields are read. If you never set it, `getTimeZone()` returns the JVM default zone (measured with `-Duser.timezone=Asia/Tokyo`).

|`Date getFinalFireTime()`
|Not implemented: the source says "NOT YET IMPLEMENTED" and it always returns `null`, even for `0 0 0 1 1 ? 2030`.

|`String getExpressionSummary()`
|A multi-line dump of the parsed sets (`daysOfWeek: 2,3,4,5,6` for `MON-FRI`), useful when debugging numbering.
|===

The two helpers below are the ones worth keeping in a project: one that fails fast with a readable message, and one that
previews the next fire times in an explicit zone.

.Validating an expression and previewing its next fire times
[source,java]
----
import org.quartz.CronExpression;

import java.text.ParseException;
import java.time.ZoneId;
import java.time.ZonedDateTime;
import java.util.ArrayList;
import java.util.Date;
import java.util.List;
import java.util.TimeZone;

public final class CronSupport {

    private CronSupport() { }

    /** Fails fast with the parser's message, e.g. when a schedule comes from configuration. */
    public static void requireValidCron(String expression) {
        try {
            CronExpression.validateExpression(expression);
        } catch (ParseException e) {
            throw new IllegalArgumentException(
                    "Invalid Quartz cron '" + expression + "': " + e.getMessage(), e);
        }
    }

    /** The next {@code count} fire times after {@code after}, read in {@code zone}. */
    public static List<ZonedDateTime> nextFireTimes(String expression, ZoneId zone,
                                                    ZonedDateTime after, int count) throws ParseException {
        CronExpression cron = new CronExpression(expression);
        cron.setTimeZone(TimeZone.getTimeZone(zone));
        List<ZonedDateTime> result = new ArrayList<>();
        Date next = Date.from(after.toInstant());
        for (int i = 0; i < count; i++) {
            next = cron.getNextValidTimeAfter(next);
            if (next == null) {
                break; // the expression never fires again
            }
            result.add(next.toInstant().atZone(zone));
        }
        return result;
    }
}
----
Source: https://www.quartz-scheduler.org/api/2.5.x/org/quartz/CronExpression.html[Quartz 2.5.x API: CronExpression]

Running these on Friday 2026-10-09 12:00 in Europe/Madrid gives exactly what the expression promises:

=== Using an expression in a trigger

`CronScheduleBuilder.cronSchedule(String)` validates the expression and throws a `RuntimeException` ("CronExpression '...'
is invalid.", with the `ParseException` as the cause) if it is wrong. Without `inTimeZone`, the trigger uses the JVM default
zone (measured); in a service that moves between hosts or containers, always set the zone.

.A cron trigger for the Bookshelf overdue-loan reminders
[source,java]
----
import org.quartz.CronScheduleBuilder;
import org.quartz.CronTrigger;
import org.quartz.TriggerBuilder;

import java.util.TimeZone;

class BookshelfTriggers {

    static CronTrigger overdueTrigger() {
        return TriggerBuilder.newTrigger()
                .withIdentity("overdueLoanRemindersTrigger", "bookshelf")
                .forJob("overdueLoanReminders", "bookshelf")
                .withSchedule(CronScheduleBuilder.cronSchedule("0 0 2 ? * MON-FRI")
                        .inTimeZone(TimeZone.getTimeZone("Europe/Madrid"))
                        .withMisfireHandlingInstructionFireAndProceed())
                .build();
    }
}

// overdueTrigger().getCronExpression()  -> 0 0 2 ? * MON-FRI
// overdueTrigger().getTimeZone().getID() -> Europe/Madrid
// first fire after Fri 2026-10-09 12:00 -> 2026-10-12T02:00+02:00[Europe/Madrid]
----
Source: https://www.quartz-scheduler.org/documentation/quartz-2.5.x/tutorials/tutorial-lesson-06.html[Quartz Tutorial -- Lesson 6: CronTrigger]

The builder also has shortcuts that generate the expression for you. Their output, measured, shows how they number days:
`dailyAtHourAndMinute(2, 0)` produces `0 0 2 ? * *`, `weeklyOnDayAndHourAndMinute(DateBuilder.MONDAY, 6, 0)` produces
`0 0 6 ? * 2` (`DateBuilder.MONDAY` is `2`), `monthlyOnDayAndHourAndMinute(1, 3, 30)` produces `0 30 3 1 * ?` and
`atHourAndMinuteOnGivenDaysOfWeek(2, 0, DateBuilder.MONDAY, DateBuilder.FRIDAY)` produces `0 0 2 ? * 2,6`. Use the
`DateBuilder` constants rather than raw numbers, and the page xref:backend/scheduling/triggers-and-schedules.adoc[Triggers and Schedules] for the rest of the builders.

=== The Spring side

Spring has its own parser. `@Scheduled(cron = ...)` takes a Spring expression and an optional `zone`; the default `zone` is
the empty string, meaning the server's local zone. The special value `-` (the constant `Scheduled.CRON_DISABLED`) turns
the task off, which is handy with a property placeholder.

.The same schedule with @Scheduled and with Spring's CronExpression
[source,java]
----
import org.springframework.scheduling.annotation.Scheduled;
import org.springframework.scheduling.support.CronExpression;

import java.time.ZonedDateTime;

class Reminders {

    @Scheduled(cron = "0 0 2 * * MON-FRI", zone = "Europe/Madrid")
    void overdueLoanReminders() { /* ... */ }

    // an empty or "-" value disables the task, e.g. in a profile that must not run it
    @Scheduled(cron = "${bookshelf.statistics.cron:-}", zone = "Europe/Madrid")
    void weeklyStatistics() { /* ... */ }
}

class SpringCron {

    static ZonedDateTime nextOverdueReminder(ZonedDateTime after) {
        return CronExpression.parse("0 0 2 * * MON-FRI").next(after);
    }
}

// nextOverdueReminder(2026-10-09T12:00 Europe/Madrid) -> 2026-10-12T02:00+02:00[Europe/Madrid]   (measured)
----
Source: https://docs.spring.io/spring-framework/reference/integration/scheduling.html[Spring Framework: Task Execution and Scheduling], https://docs.spring.io/spring-framework/docs/7.0.x/javadoc-api/org/springframework/scheduling/support/CronExpression.html[Spring Framework API: CronExpression]

The annotation compiles and its `cron()` and `zone()` values read back as written; the `-` constant is checked. The
placeholder form was not executed in an application context on this page.

== Time zones and daylight saving

A cron expression has no time zone of its own: the zone belongs to the trigger (Quartz `setTimeZone` /
`inTimeZone`) or to the annotation (`zone`). The same expression therefore fires at different instants in different zones.

.`0 0 2 * * ?` evaluated in four zones, next fire after 2026-10-05T10:00Z (measured)
[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Zone |Next fire (UTC)

|`UTC` |2026-10-06T02:00:00Z
|`Europe/Madrid` |2026-10-06T00:00:00Z
|`America/New_York` |2026-10-06T06:00:00Z
|`Asia/Kolkata` |2026-10-05T20:30:00Z
|===

Daylight-saving transitions create two special days. In Europe/Madrid in 2026 the clocks went forward on 29 March
(02:00 became 03:00, so 02:00-02:59 does not exist) and back on 25 October (03:00 became 02:00, so 02:00-02:59 happens
twice). The measured behaviour of both engines:

.Daylight-saving behaviour, Europe/Madrid 2026 (measured; first fire times listed)
[cols="3,4,4",options="header"]
|===
|Expression |Quartz 2.5.2 |Spring Framework 7.0.9

|`0 30 2 * * ?` on the gap day (29 March)
|Skipped: 28 March 02:30, then 30 March 02:30.
|Skipped: same as Quartz.

|`0 30 3 * * ?` on the gap day
|Fires 29 March 03:30+02:00.
|Same.

|`0 0 2,3 * * ?` on the gap day
|Fires only 03:00+02:00.
|Same.

|`0 30 2 * * ?` on the repeated day (25 October)
|Fires *once*, 02:30+01:00 (the second occurrence).
|Fires *twice*: 02:30+02:00 and 02:30+01:00.

|`0 0 2,3 * * ?` on the repeated day
|02:00+01:00 and 03:00+01:00.
|02:00+02:00, 02:00+01:00 and 03:00+01:00.

|`0 0/30 * * * ?`, `0 0 * * * ?`
|Unaffected on both days.
|Unaffected.
|===

The consequences are practical. A nightly job at 02:30 in a DST zone silently skips one night a year, whereas `0 30 3 * * ?`
fired on both transition days in the measurements above. A job that must run exactly once per day should be scheduled
outside the 02:00-02:59 window of the zone, or in `UTC`. A job that is
not safe to run twice must be idempotent if it runs under Spring's cron, because on the repeated day Spring fires it twice
(under Quartz it fires once). An unreleased change on the Quartz `main` branch touches the fall-back handling in
`CronExpression`, and it is not in 2.5.2, so the table above is what you get today.

Interval-style expressions (`0 0/30 * * * ?`) follow wall-clock fields, not elapsed time. If you need "every 90 minutes of
elapsed time" regardless of the clock, cron is the wrong tool; see the next sections and
xref:backend/scheduling/triggers-and-schedules.adoc[Triggers and Schedules].

Source: https://www.quartz-scheduler.org/documentation/quartz-2.5.x/tutorials/crontrigger.html[Quartz Cron Trigger Tutorial]

To translate by hand, shift the day-of-week and fix the day fields. The Bookshelf schedules, in all four dialects:

[cols="3,3,3,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Job |Quartz |Spring |Unix or Kubernetes |Notes

|Overdue-loan reminders, weekdays 02:00
|`0 0 2 ? * MON-FRI`
|`0 0 2 * * MON-FRI`
|`0 2 * * 1-5`
|Kubernetes also needs `timeZone: Europe/Madrid`.

|Weekly statistics, Monday 06:00
|`0 0 6 ? * MON`
|`0 0 6 * * MON`
|`0 6 * * 1`
|As a number, Monday is `2` in Quartz but `1` in Spring and Unix.

|Retention cleanup, last weekday 03:00
|`0 0 3 LW * ?`
|`0 0 3 LW * *`
|not expressible
|Unix and Kubernetes have no `LW`; run daily and let the job check.
|===

Use `MON`-`SUN` names whenever the expression has to survive a copy between dialects: a name means the same weekday in
all three, a number does not.

== A cookbook of annotated expressions

Every row was run: the last column is the first fire time after *Monday 2026-10-05 12:00 in Europe/Madrid* (so "the next
one" is always in the future), computed with `CronExpression.getNextValidTimeAfter` for the Quartz column and
`CronExpression.next` for the Spring column. A dash in the Spring column means the Spring parser rejects that form.

Expressions 11, 13 and 20 are the three Bookshelf cron jobs. They run from the Spring Boot
`spring-boot-starter-quartz` setup shown in
xref:backend/scheduling/getting-started-with-spring-boot.adoc[Getting Started with Spring Boot]; a holiday calendar for the
reminders is added in xref:backend/scheduling/calendars.adoc[Calendars].

Two cookbook recipes from the Quartz documentation cannot be written as cron at all: "every 90 minutes" (increments of 60
or more are rejected, and an hourly pattern is not 90-minute spacing) and "every other week" (cron has no concept of weeks
since a start date). Both are `SimpleTrigger` or `CalendarIntervalTrigger` schedules.

== Testing an expression

Put every production expression under a unit test that states the fire times you expect. It costs a few lines, it needs no
scheduler and it catches the Sunday-numbering and `?` mistakes before deployment.

.A JUnit test for the Bookshelf schedules
[source,java]
----
import org.junit.jupiter.api.Test;

import java.time.ZoneId;
import java.time.ZonedDateTime;
import java.util.List;

import static org.junit.jupiter.api.Assertions.assertEquals;

class BookshelfCronTest {

    private static final ZoneId MADRID = ZoneId.of("Europe/Madrid");

    @Test
    void overdueRemindersRunOnBusinessDays() throws Exception {
        ZonedDateTime friday = ZonedDateTime.of(2026, 10, 9, 12, 0, 0, 0, MADRID);
        List<ZonedDateTime> times =
                CronSupport.nextFireTimes("0 0 2 ? * MON-FRI", MADRID, friday, 3);
        assertEquals(List.of(
                ZonedDateTime.of(2026, 10, 12, 2, 0, 0, 0, MADRID),
                ZonedDateTime.of(2026, 10, 13, 2, 0, 0, 0, MADRID),
                ZonedDateTime.of(2026, 10, 14, 2, 0, 0, 0, MADRID)), times);
    }

    @Test
    void aOneOffYearExpressionStopsFiring() throws Exception {
        ZonedDateTime friday = ZonedDateTime.of(2026, 10, 9, 12, 0, 0, 0, MADRID);
        assertEquals(1, CronSupport.nextFireTimes("0 0 0 1 1 ? 2030", MADRID, friday, 5).size());
    }
}
----
Source: https://www.quartz-scheduler.org/api/2.5.x/org/quartz/CronExpression.html[Quartz 2.5.x API: CronExpression]

Both tests passed on the baseline. The testing page, xref:backend/scheduling/testing.adoc[Testing Scheduled Jobs], builds on
this with `TriggerUtils.computeFireTimes` and integration tests with a running scheduler.

== Pitfalls

.Cron pitfalls and what actually happens (all measured on Quartz 2.5.2 and Spring Framework 7.0.9)
[cols="3,6,5",options="header"]
|===
|Mistake |What happens |What to do

|`0 * * * * ?` when you meant "hourly"
|Fires every minute (12:01:00, 12:02:00, ...). `* * * * * ?` fires every second.
|Always set the seconds and minutes: `0 0 * * * ?` is hourly.

|Both day fields set (`0 0 2 * * *`)
|Quartz rejects it: "Support for specifying both a day-of-week AND a day-of-month parameter is not implemented."
|Put `?` in one of the two day fields.

|`?` in both day fields
|Quartz rejects it; Spring accepts it (`0 0 2 ? * ?` is daily at 02:00).
|Put `?` in exactly one of the two day fields and the restriction (or `*`) in the other.

|Copying a weekday number between dialects
|Quartz `1` is Sunday, Spring `1` is Monday: `0 0 0 ? * 1` fires on Sundays in Quartz, `0 0 0 * * 1` on Mondays in Spring. `1/2` in the day-of-week field gives Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday in Quartz and Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Sunday in Spring.
|Use `MON`-`SUN` names. Quartz rejects `0` ("Day-of-Week values must be between 1 and 7"), Spring accepts it.

|Pasting a Unix 5-field expression
|Quartz fails ("Unexpected end of expression.") and Spring fails ("must consist of 6 fields (found 5 ..."), both at startup.
|Add the seconds field in front and, for Quartz, a `?`.

|Using a macro such as `@daily` in Quartz
|"Unexpected character: @".
|Use `0 0 0 * * ?`. Macros work in Spring and Kubernetes only.

|`0 0/90 * * * ?` for "every 90 minutes"
|Quartz rejects it ("Increment >= 60 : 90"); Spring accepts it and fires every hour at minute 0, which is not what was meant.
|Use a `SimpleTrigger` or `CalendarIntervalTrigger` for intervals that do not divide the field.

|Expecting `/` to wrap around
|`7/6` in the month field means July only. `1/2` in day-of-month restarts every month: 31 October, then 1 November.
|Use a list, or a different trigger type.

|A date that does not exist (`0 0 2 31 2 ?`, or `5#6` in Spring)
|The expression is accepted but `getNextValidTimeAfter` returns `null`: the trigger never fires and nothing is logged as an error. Quartz rejects `#6` at parse time.
|Preview with `nextFireTimes` and assert on it.

|02:00-02:59 local time in a DST zone
|The job is skipped once a year on the spring-forward day, and fires twice on the fall-back day under Spring.
|Move the job out of the transition window, use `UTC`, or make it idempotent.

|No explicit time zone
|The trigger uses the JVM default zone, so the same code fires at a different instant in a container with another `TZ`.
|Always call `inTimeZone` (Quartz) or set `zone` (Spring).

|`L` or `W` with lists and ranges
|Accepted but not meaningful: `10-20W` fires on consecutive days including weekends. `6L,2` is rejected.
|Use one `L` or `W` value per field, as the Javadoc says.

|Friday the 13th (day-of-month and weekday)
|Quartz cannot express it; Spring requires both to match; Unix cron runs on either.
|In Quartz, schedule the 13th and check the weekday in the job.

|`W` on the 31st at the end of a month
|Measured difference: in January 2027 (the 31st is a Sunday) Quartz `31W` fires on Friday 29 January, while Spring skipped January and fired next on 31 March.
|Prefer `LW` for "last weekday".

|Expecting `getFinalFireTime()` to work
|Always `null`: it is not implemented.
|Iterate with `getNextValidTimeAfter` instead.

|Trusting the Javadoc month range
|`0`-`11` is documented, `1`-`12` is what the parser accepts.
|Use `JAN`-`DEC`.
|===

== What changed recently

* Quartz 2.5.1 fixed `L` and `L-n` in months shorter than 31 days (pull request #1284) and added `CronExpression.getTimeBefore`
  (pull request #1350). In 2.5.2, `L 30 * * * ?` (an `L` in the seconds field) is rejected at parse time
  ("'L' not expected in seconds, minutes or hours fields."); the issue that asked for this, #1283, is in the 2.5.1 milestone.
* Quartz 2.5.0 moved to the Jakarta namespace and JDK 11; the cron grammar did not change.
* Spring Boot 3.5.16 and 4.1.1 both manage Quartz 2.5.2, so the Quartz grammar above applies to both. Only the Spring
  Framework version differs (6.2.19 against 7.0.9); the Spring column was measured on 7.0.9.
* An unreleased change on the Quartz `main` branch alters `CronExpression` handling of the DST fall-back hour. It is not in
  2.5.2 and nothing on this page depends on it.
