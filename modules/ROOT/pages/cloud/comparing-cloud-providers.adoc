= Comparing Cloud Providers
:description: A sourced, dated comparison of AWS, Azure and Google Cloud on popularity, ecosystem, learning curve, pricing, availability regions (including China, sovereign and government clouds, and per-region service and AI model availability) and other metrics, with time-series and radar charts, a worked cost example, a recommendation matrix by workload type and region, and a decision flowchart.
:keywords: cloud comparison, AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, cloud market share, cloud pricing comparison, cloud regions, availability zones, AWS China, Azure 21Vianet, sovereign cloud, GovCloud, learning curve, cloud decision flowchart, cloud recommendation matrix, Synergy Research, Stack Overflow survey, Flexera, radar chart

include::partial$cloud-disclaimer.adoc[]

This page compares the three public cloud providers documented in this section, xref:cloud/aws/index.adoc[AWS],
xref:cloud/azure/index.adoc[Azure] and xref:cloud/google-cloud/index.adoc[Google Cloud], on popularity, ecosystem,
learning curve, pricing, availability regions and a few other metrics. It ends with a recommendation by workload type
and target region, plus a decision flowchart. All figures are a snapshot *as of 2026-10-26*: each number is linked to
a source listed in the <<Bibliography>>, and the data files behind the tables and figures are kept in the repository
under scripts/cloud-comparison-charts/data.

The scope is deliberately limited to the three providers above. Other providers, such as Oracle Cloud Infrastructure,
Alibaba Cloud and IBM Cloud, exist and matter in some markets, but they are mentioned for context only and are not
scored. The Gartner positioning in <<Analyst positioning>> notes, for example, that Alibaba Cloud was also named a
Leader in 2026.

== Methodology

=== Sources, dates and missing data

Every row of every data file has a source id and a retrieval date (2026-10-26, the snapshot date). Primary and
official sources are preferred: the providers' earnings releases, official pricing pages and price-list files,
official region and service-availability pages, SLA documents, and recognised analyst and survey publications. Source
ids appear next to each table or figure as links into the <<Bibliography>>, which lists every source with its
publication or access date. Blogs and unsourced aggregators are not used for numbers.

A value for which no reliable public source was found is shown as n/a (not available). It is never plotted as zero and
never estimated. Where a figure had to come from a secondary source because the primary page could not be read, it is
labelled as secondary in the text:

* JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2025 is taken from an AWS Insider report, because the JetBrains page
  returned an access error.
* Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026 usage, admired and desired figures are taken from a Virtualization Review
  article dated 2026-10-06, because the survey page text had no per-provider figures.
* Canalys (Omdia) quarterly shares come from search-engine excerpts of the publisher pages, which returned an
  access error when fetched, so some provider shares are missing.
* Gartner Magic Quadrant positioning for 2020 to 2022 comes from press coverage, and the 2025 and 2026 positioning
  of AWS and Google Cloud comes from the providers' own statements. The Gartner reports themselves are not public.

=== Caveats that apply to the whole page

* Synergy Research Group and Canalys (Omdia) market-share series are different series with different scopes and
  methods and must not be spliced together.
* Synergy changed its treatment of Microsoft from the third quarter of 2024: some Azure revenue was reclassified
  as software as a service, which lowered Microsoft's share from that release onward. The share series has a break
  at that point.
* Revenue segments are not like for like: AWS reports segment net sales, Google Cloud reports a segment that
  includes Google Workspace, Microsoft reports Intelligent Cloud, which is broader than Azure, and discloses Azure
  only as a growth rate.
* Developer surveys measure different populations and wordings, so their percentages are not comparable across
  surveys.
* A region or an availability zone is not defined identically by the three providers, and the Azure count used
  here is the number of regions listed on the Microsoft regions page, which may include regions that are not open
  to every customer.
* Prices are list prices on the retrieval date, on demand, in US dollars, without taxes, discounts, free
  allowances or support plans. They are not a quote.

=== Criteria and score derivation

The radar chart in <<Scorecard>> uses six axes scored from 0 to 10, where 10 means best of the three providers on that
axis (not a theoretical maximum). Four axes are computed from the data files by a script, and two are editorial. The
rule for each axis is:

[cols="18,12,70",options="header"]
|===
| Axis | Kind | Rule
| Popularity | Computed | 10 times the mean of (Synergy 2026-Q2 share divided by the largest share) and (Stack Overflow 2026 usage divided by the largest usage)
| Ecosystem breadth | Computed | 10 times the mean of (service categories with a service, out of the 31 mapped) and (official SDK languages divided by the largest count)
| Ease of use | Editorial | Judgement from trial credit and length, Stack Overflow admired score, size of the first-party learning catalogue and certification path size
| Price competitiveness | Computed | 10 times the mean over the four reference architectures of (cheapest monthly list-price total divided by own total)
| Regional coverage | Computed | 10 times the mean of (generally available public regions divided by the most), (their availability zones divided by the most) and (sovereign categories available out of China, government and sovereign, divided by 3). Region and zone counts come from the footprint data on the same basis for all three providers: China, government and sovereign partitions are left out of the counts, because they are scored by the third term
| AI offering | Editorial, low confidence | Judgement from managed ML platform, foundation-model platform, prebuilt AI services and AI certifications; no model-quality or market data was fetched
|===

The scores, the evidence behind each one and the computed-versus-editorial split are given in <<Scorecard>>. The data
files and their refresh procedure are documented in the repository (scripts/cloud-comparison-charts/data/NOTES.md),
and the figures are regenerated from them with `npm run charts:cloud-comparison`.

=== Integration strengths

The sources used measure breadth and tooling, not integration quality, so the strengths below are limited to what they
show. Claims about, for example, Microsoft 365 or .NET integration are not quantified by any dataset here and are not
scored.

* AWS: the widest mapped catalogue (31 of 31 categories), the most official SDK languages (11), the most
  downloaded Terraform provider (about 8.1 billion cumulative downloads against 2.1 billion for Azure and 2.7
  billion for Google Cloud), and AWS Ground Station, for which Microsoft states that Azure has no managed
  equivalent and which the Google comparison page does not list. Sources:
  <<src-svc-ms-aws-index,svc-ms-aws-index>>, <<src-sdkref-aws,sdkref-aws>>,
  <<src-terraform-registry,terraform-registry>>.
* Azure: Microsoft Entra ID is the mapped identity service, Azure DevOps and GitHub Actions are the mapped CI/CD
  services, and Azure Arc and Azure Local cover hybrid and edge, a category for which the Google comparison page
  lists no equivalent. Sources: <<src-svc-ms-aws-index,svc-ms-aws-index>>, <<src-svc-ms-compute,svc-ms-compute>>.
* Google Cloud: BigQuery is the mapped data warehouse, Google Kubernetes Engine the mapped managed Kubernetes
  service, and Infrastructure Manager (Terraform-based) and Config Connector the native infrastructure as code.
  Sources: <<src-svc-gcp-comparison,svc-gcp-comparison>>, <<src-svc-gcp-infra-manager,svc-gcp-infra-manager>>.

The provider guides on this site cover each ecosystem in depth. Landing pages: xref:cloud/aws/index.adoc[AWS],
xref:cloud/azure/index.adoc[Azure] and xref:cloud/google-cloud/index.adoc[Google Cloud]. Compute decision guides:

== Learning curve

Ease of use cannot be measured directly, so this section lists the objective signals that were found and then states
the editorial score and its basis. The signals are free tier and trial credit, certification and learning-path counts,
and the Stack Overflow admired and desired scores (the share of developers who used a technology and want to keep
using it, and the share who want to use it).

[cols="34,22,22,22",options="header"]
|===
| Criterion | AWS | Azure | Google Cloud
| Trial credit | USD 200 over 180 days | USD 200 over 30 days | USD 300 over 90 days
| Always-free products | 30{plus} services | 65{plus} services | 29 products
| Certifications | 11 | 20 | 13
| AI-named certifications | 3 | 5 | 2
| Vendor learning paths | n/a | 283 | n/a
| Vendor learning modules | n/a | 1,161 | n/a
| Stack Overflow 2026: used (secondary source) | 41.8% | 25.1% | 24.5%
| Stack Overflow 2026: admired (secondary source) | 45.8% | 43.2% | 42.6%
| Stack Overflow 2026: desired (secondary source) | 24.1% | 13.7% | 13.9%
|===

Always-free counts are not like for like: Google Cloud's 29 is a count of the rows in its usage-limit table, AWS says
more than 30 services and Azure more than 65. The AWS certification count is a lower bound because the professional
tier rendered only partly, and Azure counts role-based and specialty titles containing the word Azure, excluding three
marked Legacy. AWS and Google Cloud do not publish a learning-catalogue size, so those cells are n/a, while Microsoft
Learn exposes a catalogue API (learning paths and modules tagged Azure). The Stack Overflow 2026 usage, admired and
desired figures come from a Virtualization Review article (secondary source), and the 2024 and 2025 admired and
desired figures for the three providers were not found. Sources: <<src-aws-free,aws-free>>,
<<src-azure-free,azure-free>>, <<src-gcp-free,gcp-free>>, <<src-gcp-free-features,gcp-free-features>>,
<<src-aws-cert,aws-cert>>, <<src-ms-learn-catalog,ms-learn-catalog>>, <<src-gcp-cert,gcp-cert>>,
<<src-so-2026-vr,so-2026-vr>>.

Two of the criteria in the list above, console and IAM complexity and the time to a first reference deployment, have
no objective public measurement, and none was invented. The best way to judge them is to follow each provider's
getting-started guide on this site, which walks through a hands-on first deployment:
xref:cloud/aws/getting-started.adoc[AWS], xref:cloud/azure/getting-started.adoc[Azure] and
xref:cloud/google-cloud/getting-started.adoc[Google Cloud]. The identity pages
(xref:cloud/aws/identity-and-access.adoc[AWS], xref:cloud/azure/identity-and-access.adoc[Azure],
xref:cloud/google-cloud/identity-and-access.adoc[Google Cloud]) show how much IAM surface each provider asks a new
team to learn.

[cols="14,43,43",options="header"]
|===
| Provider | Strengths shown by the data | Pain points shown by the data
| AWS | Highest Stack Overflow usage (41.8%), admired (45.8%) and desired (24.1%) scores; a six-month free plan with up to USD 200 of credit; the most community Q&A volume implied by its usage | The widest service surface (31 mapped categories) means the most to learn; only 11 certifications counted (lower bound); no public learning-catalogue size
| Azure | The largest first-party learning catalogue found (283 learning paths and 1,161 modules on Microsoft Learn); 20 Azure certifications, 5 of them AI-named; more than 65 always-free services | The shortest trial window (USD 200 over 30 days); admired score 43.2% is below AWS
| Google Cloud | The longest and largest trial (USD 300 over 90 days); the leanest certification set (13), which keeps the learning path short | The lowest admired score of the three (42.6%) and the fewest AI-named certifications (2); no public learning-content counts, so its catalogue could not be compared
|===

The editorial ease-of-use scores that follow from this evidence are 7 for AWS, 7 for Azure and 6.5 for Google Cloud on
the 0 to 10 scale. The differences are small, and the score is a judgement, not a measurement.

=== China

Mainland China is a separate market with its own legal and operating model, and the three providers differ sharply
there:

[cols="10,22,20,20,16,12",options="header"]
|===
| Provider | Offering | Operator | Scope | Regions | Status
| AWS | AWS China (Beijing) Region | Beijing Sinnet Technology Co., Ltd. (Sinnet) | China only; separate partition and account | cn-north-1 | available (launched 2016-09)
| AWS | AWS China (Ningxia) Region | Ningxia Western Cloud Data Technology Co., Ltd. (NWCD) | China only; separate partition and account | cn-northwest-1 | available (launched 2017-12)
| Azure | Microsoft Azure operated by 21Vianet | Shanghai Blue Cloud Technology Co., Ltd. (21Vianet) | China only; physically separated instance; separate agreement (OSPA) | China East; China East 2; China East 3; China North; China North 2; China North 3 | available
| Google Cloud | Google Cloud in mainland China | none | No region in mainland China | none | none
|===

* Accounts: an AWS (China) account reaches only the Beijing and Ningxia regions, and accounts are not shared with
  the global partition. Azure operated by 21Vianet is a physically separated instance run under a separate
  agreement (OSPA). Google Cloud has no mainland China offering, so a customer there cannot be served from a
  Google Cloud region in the country.
* ICP licensing: a public website served from mainland China needs an Internet Content Provider (ICP) filing
  with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) if it is non-commercial, or a value-added
  telecommunications licence (the ICP licence) if it is commercial, on either provider. The customer obtains it and
  pays any government fees; the operators help with the paperwork. AWS says Sinnet (Beijing) and NWCD (Ningxia)
  support ICP requests at no extra charge, and that you may have to show the filing or licence before you host
  public content. On Amazon S3 in the China regions, anonymous GET requests and pre-signed URLs work only when the
  account has a valid ICP licence, so a public S3 static website needs one too. Microsoft says a domain without an
  ICP filing is not allowed online, and the filed domain must resolve to a filed IP address. A public security
  (PSB) filing is also due within 30 days of launch, or 21Vianet may suspend access. Both providers also require a
  legal entity in China (Azure) or a separate China account (AWS), so plan the filing before the launch date. Google
  Cloud has no mainland China region, so it has no ICP path of its own.
* Service parity: Microsoft describes a feature parity gap with global Azure that is narrowing. No parity
  measurement was found for AWS China, so none is given.
* Latency: the nearest Google Cloud regions to mainland China are Hong Kong (asia-east2) and Taiwan (asia-east1),
  as derived from the list of 43 regions. No latency measurements were gathered for this page.

== Summary: which provider to choose

The recommendation below is drawn only from the metrics in the previous sections. Each cell is the fit of a provider
for a workload (best fit, good fit or acceptable) with a relative index in brackets, where the best provider on the
metric is 100. Fit is best at an index of 95 or more, good from 80 and acceptable below. The matrix is computed from
the data files, so it matches the figure and can be regenerated.

=== Enterprise and Microsoft-centric workloads (editorial)

This row is not part of the computed matrix and has no index. It is an editorial reading of two sourced facts from
earlier sections, and integration quality itself (Microsoft 365, .NET tooling) is still not measured:

* Licence cost: Azure Hybrid Benefit is the only licence-reuse discount in the discount data, at up to 80% for Windows
  Server VMs and up to 85% for SQL Server (see <<Discount models>>). The discount data has no equivalent entry for
  AWS or Google Cloud, so Azure is the only one of the three for which this page quantifies a saving when existing
  Windows Server and SQL Server licences with Software Assurance are moved to the cloud.
* Identity: Microsoft Entra ID is Azure's mapped identity service (see <<Integration strengths>>), so an organisation
  that already runs Entra ID for Microsoft 365 starts with its identity provider in place.

=== What the metrics say, workload by workload

* Static website: Azure and AWS are best fit and within 4 percent on list price (USD 46.59 and 48.20 per month for
  the reference site), while Google Cloud is acceptable at USD 63.05, mainly because of its egress price. How-to
  guides: xref:cloud/aws/s3-security-and-static-hosting.adoc[AWS static hosting],
  xref:cloud/azure/blob-storage.adoc[Azure Blob Storage] and
  xref:cloud/google-cloud/cloud-storage-security-and-static-sites.adoc[Google Cloud static sites].
* Simple dynamic site: Azure (USD 144.95) and AWS (USD 147.08) are best fit. Google Cloud (USD 186.13) is
  acceptable, partly because its PostgreSQL shape is a dedicated-core instance. Compute guides:
  xref:cloud/aws/choosing-a-compute-service.adoc[AWS], xref:cloud/azure/choosing-a-compute-service.adoc[Azure] and
  xref:cloud/google-cloud/choosing-a-compute-service.adoc[Google Cloud].
* Microservices: Azure (USD 435.54) and AWS (USD 447.30) are best fit, Google Cloud (USD 507.43) is a good fit.
  All three charge the same USD 0.10 per hour for the managed Kubernetes control plane. Guides:
  xref:cloud/aws/eks-microservices.adoc[AWS], xref:cloud/azure/aks-microservices.adoc[Azure] and
  xref:cloud/google-cloud/gke-microservices.adoc[Google Cloud].
* Services using AI: the LLM price is identical on all three, so cost does not separate them (USD 991.06 on Azure,
  995.48 on AWS and 1,002.00 on Google Cloud). Availability does: AWS offers the checked models in 17 of 18
  combinations, against 12 of 18 for Azure and Google Cloud, so AWS is best fit and the other two are good fit.
  The result is specific to the models and regions checked. The AI offering radar score (editorial, low
  confidence) rates Azure highest. AI guides: xref:cloud/aws/ai-data-and-analytics-overview.adoc[AWS],
  xref:cloud/azure/ai-data-and-iot-overview.adoc[Azure] (with xref:cloud/azure/foundry-tools/index.adoc[Foundry
  Tools]) and xref:cloud/google-cloud/ai-data-and-analytics-overview.adoc[Google Cloud].
