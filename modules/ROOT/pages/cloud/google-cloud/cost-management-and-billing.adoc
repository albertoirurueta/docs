== Billing accounts and projects

Google Cloud separates *who pays* from *who owns resources*:

Cloud Billing account:: A resource that holds the payment profile (a card or bank account for self-serve accounts, or invoice terms for invoiced accounts) and receives the usage charges of every project linked to it. It has an ID of the form `000000-000000-000000` and exists independently of the resource hierarchy, although an organization can own it.
Project:: The container of resources, APIs, IAM bindings and quotas. A project is linked to *at most one* billing account at a time, and a project with no billing account cannot use paid services (its resources are shut down when the link is removed).
Payments profile:: The Google-wide record of the legal entity, address and payment instruments. One payments profile can back several billing accounts. It is administered in the Google payments center, not in the Cloud console.

One billing account can pay for any number of projects, in different folders or even different organizations, and
every charge on the invoice is attributed to the project that incurred it. This is why projects are the natural unit
for cost reporting (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/resource-hierarchy-and-projects.adoc[]). Billing accounts come in two
kinds: *self-serve* accounts, paid online by card or direct debit, charged automatically, and the only kind that
can start the free trial; and *invoiced* accounts, paid by invoice or wire after an agreement with Google or a
reseller, typically with a credit limit and monthly invoicing. Resellers and large customers can additionally create
*sub-accounts* beneath a master billing account to separate customers or departments; each sub-account has its own
projects, budgets and reports, and the parent sees the roll-up.

=== IAM roles on billing

Access to billing is granted with IAM roles on the *billing account* (or on the organization, for creation rights),
separately from the roles on projects. A project owner can therefore deploy resources without being able to see or
change how they are paid for.

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Role | What it allows

| Billing Account Creator (`roles/billing.creator`)
| Create new self-serve billing accounts. Granted on the organization.

| Billing Account Administrator (`roles/billing.admin`)
| Full control of one billing account: link and unlink projects, manage budgets, set IAM on the account, view costs.

| Billing Account User (`roles/billing.user`)
| Link projects to the billing account. Does not allow viewing costs or changing the account.

| Billing Account Costs Manager (`roles/billing.costsManager`)
| Manage budgets and cost-management settings such as exports and commitments, and view costs, without linking projects or editing IAM.

| Billing Account Viewer (`roles/billing.viewer`)
| View cost information, transactions and budgets, read-only.

| Project Billing Manager (`roles/billing.projectManager`)
| On a project: link or unlink *that project* to a billing account the person is allowed to use. Combine with Billing Account User on the account.
|===

Linking a project needs two permissions together: permission to associate resources with the billing account
(`roles/billing.user` on the account) and permission to change the project's billing assignment (the Project
Owner or Project Billing Manager role on the project). Following least privilege, give finance people
`roles/billing.viewer` or `roles/billing.costsManager` through a Google group, give platform teams
`roles/billing.user`, and keep `roles/billing.admin` to two or three break-glass administrators. The full role
descriptions are in the official access-control page.

=== In the Google Cloud console

. Open the navigation menu, then *Billing*. If you manage several billing accounts, choose *Go to linked billing account* or the account selector at the top.
. Open *Billing account management* (or *Account management*) to see the account ID, the linked payments profile, the status and the list of *Projects linked to this billing account*.
. To link or move a project, choose *Actions* next to the project, then *Change billing*, pick the target billing account and confirm.
. To review who has access, open *Account management*, select the account and use the *Permissions* panel to grant a role such as *Billing Account Viewer* to a group.

=== With the gcloud CLI

[source,bash]
----
# Listing and describing billing accounts needs at least the Billing Account Viewer role
gcloud billing accounts list --filter="open=true" \
  --format="table(name,displayName,open,masterBillingAccount)"
gcloud billing accounts describe 000000-000000-000000

# Which billing account pays for this project?
PROJECT_ID=$(gcloud config get-value project)
gcloud billing projects describe "$PROJECT_ID"

# Link, move and unlink a project
gcloud billing projects link "$PROJECT_ID" --billing-account=000000-000000-000000
gcloud billing projects unlink "$PROJECT_ID"

# Projects paid by one account
gcloud billing projects list --billing-account=000000-000000-000000

# Give the finance group read-only access to costs, and the platform group the right to link projects
gcloud billing accounts add-iam-policy-binding 000000-000000-000000 \
  --member="group:finance@example.com" --role="roles/billing.viewer"
gcloud billing accounts add-iam-policy-binding 000000-000000-000000 \
  --member="group:platform@example.com" --role="roles/billing.user"
gcloud billing accounts get-iam-policy 000000-000000-000000
----

Unlinking is destructive: the project's paid resources stop and may be deleted after a grace period, so never script
`unlink` without the safeguards discussed in <<_reacting_to_a_budget_programmatically,Reacting to a budget programmatically>>.

== Per-second billing and what else is metered

Most of the compute and database products that bill by time meter it *per second*, which is why starting a large VM
for a ten-minute test costs about ten minutes and not an hour. The details differ by product, so check the unit of
each:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Product | How usage is metered

| Compute Engine VMs and GKE nodes
| Per second, with a one-minute minimum per VM start. Stopped VMs stop paying for vCPU and memory but keep paying for their disks and reserved IP addresses.

| Cloud Run and Cloud Run functions
| vCPU-seconds and GiB-seconds, rounded up to 100 milliseconds, plus a per-request fee in request-based billing; instance-based billing meters the whole instance lifetime. Minimum instances bill at an idle rate.

| GKE Autopilot
| Per second for the vCPU, memory and ephemeral storage requested by running Pods, plus a cluster management fee.

| Cloud SQL, AlloyDB, Memorystore
| Per second or per hour of instance time for the provisioned shape, plus storage, backups and network, whether or not queries are running.

| Cloud Storage
| GiB-months of data stored by class (including soft-deleted bytes), operations per class, retrieval and early-deletion fees, and network egress.

| BigQuery
| Either bytes processed per query (on-demand) or slot-hours (capacity editions), plus storage billed in logical or physical bytes.

| Network
| GiB of traffic leaving a region, zone or Google Cloud, and hourly fees for forwarding rules, NAT gateways and IP addresses.
|===

Per-second metering rewards short-lived infrastructure (CI runners, batch jobs, preview environments) and punishes
forgotten always-on components, which is the theme of the later <<_hidden_cost_drivers,Hidden cost drivers>> section. The exact minimums
and rounding for each product are on its own pricing page; the individual service pages in this section state them in
their *Pricing* paragraph.

Reference: https://docs.cloud.google.com/compute/all-pricing[Compute Engine pricing],
https://cloud.google.com/run/pricing[Cloud Run pricing],
https://cloud.google.com/pricing[Google Cloud pricing hub]

== Budgets and alerts

A *budget* tracks the spend of a billing account (or a scope inside it) against an amount and sends notifications
when thresholds are crossed. **A budget alerts; it does not cap spending.** Services keep running and charges keep
accruing after the last threshold, and the data behind the alerts lags real usage (usage and costs are reported with
a delay of up to a day or more for some services), so the final bill can exceed the budget. Treat budgets as an early
warning system and combine them with quotas, `max-instances` limits and a programmatic reaction when a hard limit is
required.

A budget is defined by:

Scope:: Which charges count: the whole billing account, selected projects, selected services, selected labels or a combination. Savings and credits can be included or excluded.
Time range:: A calendar month, quarter or year, or a custom date range.
Amount:: A *specified amount* in the billing currency, or *the last period's spend* so the budget follows the previous month.
Thresholds:: Percentages of the amount (for example 50, 90 and 100), each on *actual* spend or *forecasted* spend. A forecast threshold fires when Google predicts you will exceed the amount before the period ends.
Notifications:: E-mail to billing administrators and users (on by default through IAM), Cloud Monitoring notification channels such as Slack or PagerDuty, and optionally a Pub/Sub topic for programmatic reactions.

Spend-cap budgets are a newer, Preview-stage capability that can stop supported spend when a limit is reached. They
are not generally available and have service coverage restrictions, so read the budget documentation before
depending on them, and do not treat Preview behaviour as a production safety net.

=== In the Google Cloud console

. Open *Billing*, select the billing account, then *Budgets & alerts*, and choose *Create budget*.
. Name it `bookshelf-monthly`. Under *Scope*, choose the time range *Monthly* and select only the Bookshelf projects (and optionally a label filter such as `app:bookshelf`). Choose *Next*.
. Under *Amount*, choose *Specified amount* and enter the target, for example 200 in your billing currency, or choose *Last month's spend*.
. Under *Actions*, set threshold rules for 50%, 90% and 100% of *Actual* spend and add 100% of *Forecasted* spend.
. Under *Manage notifications*, keep *Email alerts to billing admins and users*, optionally add a Cloud Monitoring channel and *Connect a Pub/Sub topic to this budget*, then choose *Finish*.

=== With the gcloud CLI

[source,bash]
----
gcloud services enable billingbudgets.googleapis.com cloudbilling.googleapis.com

PROJECT_ID=$(gcloud config get-value project)
PROJECT_NUMBER=$(gcloud projects describe "$PROJECT_ID" --format="value(projectNumber)")

gcloud billing budgets create \
  --billing-account=000000-000000-000000 \
  --display-name="bookshelf-monthly" \
  --budget-amount=200USD \
  --calendar-period=month \
  --filter-projects="projects/$PROJECT_NUMBER" \
  --threshold-rule=percent=0.5 \
  --threshold-rule=percent=0.9 \
  --threshold-rule=percent=1.0 \
  --threshold-rule=percent=1.0,basis=forecasted-spend

gcloud billing budgets list --billing-account=000000-000000-000000 \
  --format="table(name,displayName,amount.specifiedAmount.units)"
----

Run `gcloud billing budgets create --help` for the complete flag list, including the label and service filters, the
credit-treatment option and the Pub/Sub and notification-channel flags. A budget can also be declared in Terraform,
which fits the practice of keeping billing guardrails in version control:

[source,hcl]
----
resource "google_billing_budget" "bookshelf" {
  billing_account = "000000-000000-000000"
  display_name    = "bookshelf-monthly"

  budget_filter {
    projects = ["projects/<project-number>"]
  }

  amount {
    specified_amount {
      currency_code = "USD"
      units         = "200"
    }
  }

  threshold_rules { threshold_percent = 0.5 }
  threshold_rules { threshold_percent = 0.9 }
  threshold_rules { threshold_percent = 1.0 }
  threshold_rules {
    threshold_percent = 1.0
    spend_basis       = "FORECASTED_SPEND"
  }
}
----

=== Reacting to a budget programmatically

Budgets can publish a JSON message to a Pub/Sub topic every time spend is evaluated, so automation can react: post
to a chat channel, scale a service to zero, or, as a last resort, *disable billing* on a project. Each message carries the
budget display name, the cost so far, the budget amount, the currency, the period start and, when a threshold has just
been crossed, `alertThresholdExceeded`. The wiring is a topic, a budget that publishes to it and a subscriber, here a
Cloud Run function (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/cloud-run-functions-and-events.adoc[]):

[source,bash]
----
gcloud pubsub topics create budget-alerts

# Update the budget to publish to the topic
BUDGET_ID=$(gcloud billing budgets list --billing-account=000000-000000-000000 \
  --filter="displayName=bookshelf-monthly" --format="value(name.basename())")
gcloud billing budgets update "$BUDGET_ID" \
  --billing-account=000000-000000-000000 \
  --notifications-rule-pubsub-topic="projects/$PROJECT_ID/topics/budget-alerts"
----

The function receives the message through an Eventarc Pub/Sub trigger. A cautious handler compares `costAmount` with
`budgetAmount`, and only when spend is at or above the limit calls the Cloud Billing API to remove the billing link
of one named, non-production project. With the Java client library
(`com.google.cloud:google-cloud-billing` from the `libraries-bom`) and Application Default Credentials:

[source,java]
----
package com.example.bookshelf.cost;

import com.google.cloud.billing.v1.CloudBillingClient;
import com.google.cloud.billing.v1.ProjectBillingInfo;
import com.google.cloud.billing.v1.UpdateProjectBillingInfoRequest;
import java.io.IOException;

public class BillingKillSwitch {

    private static final String PROJECT = "projects/bookshelf-sandbox-id";

    public static void disableBilling() throws IOException {
        try (CloudBillingClient client = CloudBillingClient.create()) {
            // An empty billing account name unlinks the project from billing
            ProjectBillingInfo info = ProjectBillingInfo.newBuilder().setBillingAccountName("").build();
            client.updateProjectBillingInfo(UpdateProjectBillingInfoRequest.newBuilder()
                    .setName(PROJECT)
                    .setProjectBillingInfo(info)
                    .build());
        }
    }
}
----

Reference: https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/notify[Programmatic budget notifications],
https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/disable-billing-with-notifications[Disable billing usage with notifications],
https://docs.cloud.google.com/java/docs/reference/google-cloud-billing/latest/overview[Cloud Billing client library for Java]

The caveats make this a blunt instrument, which is why it is shown for a sandbox project and never for production:

* *Latency.* The notification arrives after the cost data does, so spend that already happened (and some that is still in flight) is not undone; the bill can still pass the limit.
* *It stops everything.* Unlinking billing shuts down paid resources in the project, which may delete data and cannot be undone cleanly. Some resources keep incurring charges until deleted, and re-enabling does not restore deleted data.
* *Permissions.* The function's service account needs `roles/billing.projectManager` on the project and `roles/billing.user` on the account, which is a powerful combination; give it to a dedicated service account used by nothing else.
* *Scope.* A budget covering the whole billing account would disable the wrong project; scope each kill-switch budget to one project.

For production, prefer gentler reactions: alert a human, lower `max-instances` on Cloud Run services, scale a node
pool down, and rely on quotas as a hard ceiling.

== Estimating cost before you deploy

Estimating beats surprises. Three tools cover the planning phase:

Pricing calculator:: A web tool where you add products, choose regions and sizes and get a monthly estimate, with sections for Compute Engine, Cloud Run, Cloud Storage, Cloud SQL and most other services. Estimates can be saved and shared by link or exported to CSV, and the result reflects list prices, so apply your negotiated discount and commitments separately.
Cloud Billing Catalog API:: The machine-readable list of every service and SKU with its price tiers, useful to build cost checks into scripts and pull requests.
Product pricing pages:: Each service has a pricing page with examples, the free tier and the SKU units. They are the authoritative source for rounding and minimums.

=== In the Google Cloud console

. Open the pricing calculator in a browser (it does not need an account) and choose *Add to estimate*.
. Choose *Cloud Run*, set the region (for example `us-central1`), the billing mode, CPU, memory, requests per month and average request time, then add *Cloud SQL*, *Cloud Storage* and *Cloud Load Balancing* for the Bookshelf shape.
. Review the *Estimated monthly cost* and the per-service breakdown, choose *Share* or *Save estimate* and keep the link with the architecture documentation.
. In the console, the *Billing > Reports* and *Cost table* pages then show what actually accrued, to compare with the estimate.

=== With the gcloud CLI

There is no `gcloud` command for the calculator, but the Catalog API is a plain REST endpoint that accepts an access
token:

[source,bash]
----
gcloud services enable cloudbilling.googleapis.com

TOKEN=$(gcloud auth print-access-token)

# Find the service ID of Cloud Run in the catalog
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  "https://cloudbilling.googleapis.com/v1/services?pageSize=500" \
  | jq -r '.services[] | select(.displayName=="Cloud Run") | .name'

# List the SKUs and unit prices of that service (replace SERVICE_ID with the value printed above)
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  "https://cloudbilling.googleapis.com/v1/services/SERVICE_ID/skus?currencyCode=USD&pageSize=20" \
  | jq '.skus[] | {description, category: .category.resourceFamily, regions: .serviceRegions}'
----

For bulk analysis, enable the *pricing export* to BigQuery described in <<_exporting_billing_data_to_bigquery,Exporting billing data to BigQuery>>
instead of paging through the API. The Terraform and gcloud workflows for building the infrastructure being estimated
are in xref:cloud/google-cloud/infrastructure-as-code.adoc[].

== Paying less for steady usage

Pricing is pay as you go with no minimum, and there are four ways to reduce the rate for predictable or flexible
workloads.

=== Sustained use discounts

*Sustained use discounts* (SUDs) are automatic: certain VM families get a discount that grows as they run for a larger
share of the month, with nothing to buy or configure. They apply to selected older general-purpose and compute- or
memory-optimized machine series, not to the newest series such as E2, which already have a lower list price
and no automatic discount. Because the applicable series change, check the official table before assuming a discount.
Sustained use discounts do not combine with committed use discounts on the same resources, which take precedence.

=== Committed use discounts

*Committed use discounts* (CUDs) trade a one- or three-year commitment for a lower rate. There are two families:

Resource-based commitments:: You commit to an amount of vCPU, memory, GPUs or local SSD in a *region* (and, for Compute Engine, a machine family). The commitment is a Compute Engine resource that is applied automatically to matching usage in any project of the billing account unless sharing is restricted. You pay for the committed resources every hour whether or not you use them, so commit to the steady baseline, not the peak.
Spend-based (flexible) commitments:: You commit to an hourly dollar amount of eligible spend, and receive a discount on that usage as credits. Flexible commitments for compute cover several machine families and are not tied to one region, and spend-based commitments also exist for databases and analytics such as Cloud SQL, Memorystore, Spanner, AlloyDB and BigQuery capacity. Cloud Run and GKE Autopilot have commitment options of their own.

Commitments generally cannot be cancelled, so size them from the rightsizing recommendations and several weeks of
steady usage. Coverage and the exact products, terms and discount percentages change between releases of the
programme, so read the CUD overview and the per-product pages rather than relying on remembered numbers.

=== Spot VMs and Spot Pods

*Spot VMs* use spare Compute Engine capacity at a deep discount and can be reclaimed (preempted) with a short
warning, with no maximum runtime. They suit stateless, restartable work: CI runners, batch jobs, rendering and
the interruptible node pools of GKE (Spot node pools, or Spot Pods on Autopilot). They are a poor fit for the
`orders-api` database or anything that cannot tolerate eviction. Spot pricing is dynamic, so check the Spot pricing
page rather than hard-coding an assumption. See xref:cloud/google-cloud/compute-engine.adoc[] and
xref:cloud/google-cloud/gke-clusters.adoc[].

=== Cheaper by design

Several of the largest savings come from architecture, not discounts: scale to zero with Cloud Run, choose Cloud
Storage classes and lifecycle rules for cold data (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/cloud-storage.adoc[]), keep traffic
inside a region, cache at the edge with Cloud CDN (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/cloud-cdn-and-media-cdn.adoc[]) and
turn off dev environments outside working hours.

=== In the Google Cloud console

. Open *Billing*, select the billing account, then *Commitments*.
. Choose *Purchase commitment* and pick the commitment type (for example *Compute Engine flexible commitment* or *Compute Engine resource-based*), the term (one or three years) and, for resource-based commitments, the region, machine family and amounts.
. Review the price breakdown and the warning that the commitment cannot be cancelled, then confirm.
. Use the *Committed use discounts analysis* or *Commitments* views and the commitment recommendations in the FinOps hub (see below) to check coverage and utilization.

=== With the gcloud CLI

[source,bash]
----
gcloud services enable compute.googleapis.com recommender.googleapis.com

REGION=us-central1

# List existing commitments and inspect one
gcloud compute commitments list --format="table(name,region.basename(),plan,status,endTimestamp)"
gcloud compute commitments describe COMMITMENT_NAME --region="$REGION"

# Purchase a one-year resource-based commitment for 4 vCPU and 16 GB of E2 capacity
# (this is a binding purchase: run it only after checking the price and the recommendation)
gcloud compute commitments create bookshelf-baseline \
  --region="$REGION" \
  --plan=12-month \
  --type=GENERAL_PURPOSE_E2 \
  --resources=vcpu=4,memory=16GB

# Commitment recommendations produced by Active Assist
gcloud recommender recommendations list \
  --project="$(gcloud config get-value project)" \
  --location="$REGION" \
  --recommender=google.compute.commitment.UsageCommitmentRecommender
----

Flexible (spend-based) commitments are purchased in the console or through the Cloud Commerce Consumer Procurement
API rather than with a dedicated `gcloud` command, so use the console for them.

== Labels for cost allocation

*Labels* are user-defined key-value pairs attached to resources. They are the standard way to slice a bill by
application, environment, team or cost centre, because labels set on a resource (and on a project) are copied into
the billing export and can be used as budget and report filters. A resource may carry up to 64 labels. Keys and values
are lowercase letters, digits, underscores and hyphens, keys start with a letter and both are limited to 63 characters.
Not every service supports labels, and labels on a project are applied to the usage that project generates, while
resource labels apply to that resource's own SKUs.

Labels are different from *tags* (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/resource-hierarchy-and-projects.adoc[]): tags are
managed resources used for conditional IAM and organization policies, while labels are free-form metadata for
reporting. For chargeback, agree on a small mandatory set, for example:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Label key | Meaning and example value

| `app`
| The application or product: `bookshelf`.

| `component`
| The deployable unit: `catalog-api`, `orders-api`, `notifier`, `web`.

| `env`
| The life-cycle stage: `dev`, `stg`, `prod`.

| `cost_center`
| The finance code charged back: `cc1042`.

| `owner`
| The team that answers for the resource: `team-books`.
|===

Enforce the set with infrastructure as code and, in an organization, with an organization policy or a CI check on
Terraform plans, since labels that are optional are quickly forgotten. Remember that labels apply from the moment they
are added; they are not retroactive in billing data.

=== In the Google Cloud console

. Open the resource in its console page, for example *Cloud Run > catalog-api*, and choose *Edit and deploy new revision* or the *Labels* tab.
. Add the label pairs `app`, `component`, `env` and `owner`, then save or deploy.
. For a project, open *IAM & Admin > Settings* (or *Manage resources*), choose the project and *Labels*, and add `app:bookshelf` and `env:prod`.
. In *Billing > Reports*, use *Filters > Labels* or *Group by > Label* to see the cost per label value.

=== With the gcloud CLI

[source,bash]
----
PROJECT_ID=$(gcloud config get-value project)
REGION=us-central1
BUCKET=bookshelf-uploads-$PROJECT_ID

# Project-level labels
gcloud projects update "$PROJECT_ID" --update-labels=app=bookshelf,env=prod,cost_center=cc1042

# Cloud Run service
gcloud run services update catalog-api --region="$REGION" \
  --update-labels=app=bookshelf,component=catalog-api,env=prod,owner=team-books

# Cloud Storage bucket
gcloud storage buckets update "gs://$BUCKET" \
  --update-labels=app=bookshelf,component=uploads,env=prod

# Compute Engine VM, Cloud SQL instance
gcloud compute instances add-labels bookshelf-vm --zone="${REGION}-a" --labels=app=bookshelf,env=prod
gcloud sql instances patch bookshelf-catalog --update-labels=app=bookshelf,component=catalog-db,env=prod

# Find resources by label across services
gcloud asset search-all-resources --scope="projects/$PROJECT_ID" --query="labels.app=bookshelf" \
  --format="table(assetType,name)"
----

== Exporting billing data to BigQuery

The Cloud console reports are good for browsing, but chargeback, anomaly hunting and dashboards need the raw line
items. *Cloud Billing export* writes them to a BigQuery dataset you own, where you query them with SQL and visualize
them in Looker Studio. Several exports exist, each enabled separately per billing account:

Standard usage cost:: Daily-granularity cost data per SKU, project, location and label, in a table named `gcp_billing_export_v1_` followed by the billing account ID with underscores. Enough for most chargeback.
Detailed usage cost:: Everything in the standard export plus *resource-level* fields (such as the specific VM, bucket or Cloud Run revision), in `gcp_billing_export_resource_v1_` followed by the account ID. Larger and more granular, so it costs more to store and query.
Pricing:: The list prices of SKUs, in `cloud_pricing_export`, for joining usage with price tiers.
Commitment (CUD) analysis:: Data to evaluate commitment coverage, utilization and savings. Its availability and shape are newer, so check the export documentation.

Exports begin when you enable them and are not generally back-filled for earlier months, so enable them on day one,
before you need them. The dataset's location is fixed at creation, so choose a multi-region such as `US` or `EU`
that matches your reporting needs and data-residency rules. Enabling an export needs Billing Account Administrator (or
Costs Manager) on the account and BigQuery permissions on the destination project.

=== In the Google Cloud console

. Create the destination dataset first: open *BigQuery*, choose the project, *Create dataset*, name it `billing_export`, pick the location (for example `US`) and *Create dataset*.
. Open *Billing*, select the billing account, then *Billing export*.
. On the *Standard usage cost* tab, choose *Edit settings*, select the project and the `billing_export` dataset and choose *Save*. Repeat on the *Detailed usage cost* and *Pricing* tabs as required.
. Wait a few hours for the first rows, then open *BigQuery* and check for the new tables.

=== With the gcloud CLI

Turning the export on is a console-only step. Everything around it, the dataset and the analysis, is scriptable with the
`bq` tool:

[source,bash]
----
gcloud services enable bigquery.googleapis.com

PROJECT_ID=$(gcloud config get-value project)

# Destination dataset, in a multi-region (the location cannot be changed later)
bq --location=US mk --dataset \
  --description="Cloud Billing export" \
  "$PROJECT_ID:billing_export"

# Grant the finance group read access to the dataset
bq show --format=prettyjson "$PROJECT_ID:billing_export" > dataset.json
# (edit the access array to add {"role":"READER","groupByEmail":"finance@example.com"}, then)
bq update --source dataset.json "$PROJECT_ID:billing_export"

# Cost for the last 30 days by service and by the app label
cat > cost-by-service.sql <<'SQL'
SELECT
  service.description AS service,
  (SELECT value FROM UNNEST(labels) WHERE key = 'app') AS app,
  ROUND(SUM(cost) + SUM(IFNULL((SELECT SUM(c.amount) FROM UNNEST(credits) c), 0)), 2) AS net_cost,
  currency
FROM `my-project-id.billing_export.gcp_billing_export_v1_000000_000000_000000`
WHERE usage_start_time >= TIMESTAMP_SUB(CURRENT_TIMESTAMP(), INTERVAL 30 DAY)
GROUP BY service, app, currency
ORDER BY net_cost DESC
LIMIT 20;
SQL

bq query --use_legacy_sql=false < cost-by-service.sql
----

The query adds the (negative) credit amounts to the gross cost to give the net cost, and filters on
`usage_start_time` because the table is partitioned by it, which keeps the bytes scanned (and therefore the query
cost, see xref:cloud/google-cloud/ai-data-and-analytics-overview.adoc[]) low. Labels are an array of key-value
records, hence the sub-select. Add a dry run with `bq query --dry_run` before running broad queries over the
detailed export.

Reference: https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/export-data-bigquery[Export Cloud Billing data to BigQuery],
https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/export-data-bigquery-tables[Understand the Cloud Billing data tables in BigQuery],
https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/bq-examples[Example queries for Cloud Billing data export],
https://docs.cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/reference/bq-cli-reference[`bq` command-line reference]

== Reports, anomalies and the FinOps hub

Before data reaches BigQuery, the console already offers a lot:

Reports:: *Billing > Reports* charts cost over time with filters (projects, services, SKUs, labels, locations) and grouping, with options to include credits and discounts. It is the quickest answer to what changed this week.
Cost table and cost breakdown:: Tabular views of cost, discounts and credits down to the SKU, and a waterfall from usage cost to the invoice total.
Cost anomaly detection:: Automatic detection of spend that deviates from the historic pattern, shown in the console and available as alerts, at no extra charge.
FinOps hub:: A single console page that gathers optimization opportunities: idle resources, rightsizing, commitment recommendations, an estimate of monthly savings and the utilization of existing commitments.
Active Assist recommenders:: The machine-generated recommendations behind the hub, available through the Recommender API: idle VMs, disks and IP addresses, VM rightsizing, unattended projects and commitment sizing.
Cost and Carbon footprint:: Reports that allocate emissions per project alongside the cost, useful for sustainability reporting.

=== In the Google Cloud console

. Open *Billing > Reports*, set the time range, choose *Group by > Service* and *Filters > Projects* for `bookshelf-prod`, and look for unexpected jumps.
. Open *Billing > Cost table* for a downloadable breakdown, and *Anomalies* (when available) to review detected deviations.
. Open *Billing > FinOps hub* (or search the console for *FinOps*), and review *Recommendations*; each links to the resource and to the action that realizes the saving.
. Under *Notifications*, subscribe the finance group to anomaly alerts.

=== With the gcloud CLI

[source,bash]
----
gcloud services enable recommender.googleapis.com

PROJECT_ID=$(gcloud config get-value project)

# Idle VMs (zonal), idle external IP addresses (regional) and idle persistent disks (zonal)
gcloud recommender recommendations list --project="$PROJECT_ID" --location=us-central1-a \
  --recommender=google.compute.instance.IdleResourceRecommender \
  --format="table(name.basename(),description,primaryImpact.costProjection.cost.units)"
gcloud recommender recommendations list --project="$PROJECT_ID" --location=us-central1 \
  --recommender=google.compute.address.IdleResourceRecommender
gcloud recommender recommendations list --project="$PROJECT_ID" --location=us-central1-a \
  --recommender=google.compute.disk.IdleResourceRecommender

# Rightsizing of VM machine types
gcloud recommender recommendations list --project="$PROJECT_ID" --location=us-central1-a \
  --recommender=google.compute.instance.MachineTypeRecommender
----

Recommendations are advisory: review each one, mark it as claimed, succeeded or dismissed with
`gcloud recommender recommendations mark-claimed|mark-succeeded|mark-dismissed`, and always check the resource's
dependencies before deleting an "idle" one, since a rarely used resource may still be required.

https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/cost-anomaly-detection[Cost anomaly detection],

== Free trial and Always Free

Google Cloud has two separate free programs; the summary lives in xref:cloud/google-cloud/getting-started.adoc[] and this
section adds the details that matter for cost control.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Program | Details

| Free trial
| New customers get a credit of about $300 valid for 90 days, applied before any charge, and it needs a self-serve billing account with a payment method for identity verification. The trial ends when the credit is used up or 90 days pass. Nothing is charged automatically: the account is paused until you activate a full paid account, and resources are deleted some days after the trial ends if you do not upgrade. During the trial some products are unavailable or limited: no GPUs, no Windows Server images, no Marketplace purchases, no quota increases and restrictions on other products. Each person gets the trial once.

| Always Free
| Monthly usage allowances for selected products that do not expire and are not limited to the trial. They include one small `e2-micro` VM in selected US regions, a few GiB of Cloud Storage in selected regions, free monthly Cloud Run requests and compute time, a monthly allowance of BigQuery queries and storage, Pub/Sub, Secret Manager, Firestore and Cloud Build allowances, and no cluster management fee for one GKE Autopilot or zonal cluster per billing account.
|===

Two traps are worth knowing. First, allowances are per billing account (not per project), so creating more projects does
not multiply them. Second, the allowances are measured in specific units and regions; use a different region, a larger
shape or a feature outside the free SKU and the usage is billed at the normal price. Inside the trial, trial credits
are consumed before your card is charged, and credits show up as negative lines in the billing export (which is why
the earlier query sums the `credits` array). Some products also have their own free trials, for example a time-limited
free instance of Cloud SQL, AlloyDB or Spanner, documented on their pages; see
xref:cloud/google-cloud/managed-databases-overview.adoc[]. Credits from programs for startups or education are
separate from the free trial.

Reference: https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features[Free cloud features and trial offer],
https://cloud.google.com/free[Google Cloud Free Program]

== Hidden cost drivers

Most surprises come not from the big compute line but from components that bill by the hour whether or not traffic
flows, or from storage and logs that grow silently. This qualitative table lists the ones that surprise newcomers
most; look up current rates on the linked pricing pages.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Driver | Why it costs money | What to do

| External IPv4 addresses
| In-use and reserved external IPv4 addresses are billed by the hour, and an address that is reserved but unattached is billed too.
| Prefer internal addressing, IAP and Private Google Access; release unused reservations (the idle-address recommender finds them).

| Cloud NAT gateways
| An hourly fee per gateway-and-VM plus data processing for every GiB through the gateway.
| One gateway per region, Private Google Access for Google APIs so that traffic skips the NAT.

| Load balancer forwarding rules
| Each forwarding rule is billed per hour plus data processed, even with no traffic.
| Share one load balancer between services with a URL map; delete test load balancers.

| Cloud Run minimum instances
| Instances kept warm are billed at an idle rate for the whole month.
| Use `min-instances` of 0 outside production; set `max-instances` to cap runaway scaling.

| Soft-deleted bytes in Cloud Storage
| Soft delete is on by default with a retention window, and soft-deleted bytes are billed at the storage rate until the window ends.
| Lower the window or disable it on throwaway buckets (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/cloud-storage.adoc[]).

| Log ingestion and retention
| Cloud Logging charges for ingestion beyond a monthly free allocation per project, plus retention beyond the default period.
| Exclude noisy logs, route to cheaper sinks, and keep verbose debug logging off in production (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/monitoring-and-observability.adoc[]).

| GKE cluster management fee
| Clusters bill an hourly management fee beyond the free cluster per account, in addition to nodes or Pods.
| Consolidate clusters, use namespaces, delete idle test clusters.

| Idle databases
| Cloud SQL, AlloyDB and Memorystore bill for the provisioned shape, storage and backups 24 hours a day.
| Stop or delete dev databases outside working hours and use scheduled start-stop.

| Extended support database fees
| Database versions past community end of life move into paid extended support with an extra per-vCPU charge.
| Upgrade engine versions on schedule (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/cloud-sql.adoc[]).

| Network egress
| Traffic leaving a zone, a region or Google Cloud is charged; inbound traffic is generally free.
| Keep chatty services in the same region and zone, use Cloud CDN, and compress responses.

| Unattached disks and old snapshots
| Persistent disks and snapshots bill per GiB-month after the VM is gone.
| Delete leftovers; use snapshot schedules with a retention period.

| Artifact Registry storage
| Old container images accumulate and bill per GiB-month.
| Add cleanup policies (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/container-registry-and-builds.adoc[]).
|===

Reference: https://cloud.google.com/vpc/network-pricing[Network pricing],
https://cloud.google.com/nat/pricing[Cloud NAT pricing],
https://cloud.google.com/vpc/pricing[VPC and external IP pricing],
https://cloud.google.com/stackdriver/pricing[Cloud Logging pricing]

== Where the Bookshelf money goes

The shape of the *Bookshelf* bill is more instructive than any single number. The table ranks the cost drivers
qualitatively for a small production deployment, from usually largest to usually smallest, and says what controls each.

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Component | Typical share of the bill | Main levers

| Cloud SQL `bookshelf-catalog` (and AlloyDB if used)
| Largest, steady
| Right-size the tier, use a shared-core tier in dev, enable committed use discounts after a stable baseline, stop dev instances out of hours.

| Global external load balancer and Cloud CDN
| Medium, steady
| One shared load balancer; cache static assets so fewer bytes reach the origin; remove test forwarding rules.

| Cloud Run `catalog-api` and `orders-api`
| Medium, scales with traffic
| Request-based billing, a sensible CPU and memory, `max-instances`, and a small `min-instances` only for the user-facing path.

| GKE Autopilot (if used instead of Cloud Run)
| Medium to large, steady
| Right-size resource requests, Spot Pods for batch, one cluster, flexible CUDs.

| Cloud NAT and external IPs
| Small, but constant
| Only for VMs that need outbound internet; reuse one gateway.

| Cloud Storage `bookshelf-web` and `bookshelf-uploads`
| Small, grows over time
| Lifecycle rules, storage class by access pattern, short soft-delete window in dev.

| Firestore (`orders`) and Memorystore
| Small to medium
| Index discipline, TTL policies, a right-sized cache node.

| Cloud Logging, Monitoring, Trace
| Small until it is not
| Log exclusions, sampling, retention matching your needs.

| Pub/Sub, Secret Manager, Cloud Build, Artifact Registry
| Negligible to small
| Inside the free allowances at small scale; cleanup policies for images.

| Network egress
| Variable
| Same-region services, CDN, avoiding cross-region database calls.
|===

Label every component with `app=bookshelf`, `component=...` and `env=...`, export billing to BigQuery on day one and
create a monthly budget per environment, and the cost of each box in the diagram above becomes a single `GROUP BY`.

== Quotas and limits

Billing and budget resources have their own limits, which are rarely reached but should be checked when you
automate at scale:

* Each project is linked to at most one billing account, and each billing account can have a limited number of projects linked by default; the quota is raised on request (and a new trial account has a low initial limit).
* Budgets per billing account and thresholds per budget are capped; the budget documentation lists the current maximums.
* A billing account can have a limited number of sub-accounts and a limited number of active commitments per region.
* Service quotas (see xref:cloud/google-cloud/resource-hierarchy-and-projects.adoc[]) are the only hard ceiling on consumption, so lowering a quota such as the number of CPUs in a region or the number of Cloud Run instances is a legitimate cost control.

Reference: https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/quotas[Cloud Billing quotas and limits]

== Clean up

Cost tooling costs almost nothing, but remove what you created for experiments, in this order:

[source,bash]
----
PROJECT_ID=$(gcloud config get-value project)

# Budgets
BUDGET_ID=$(gcloud billing budgets list --billing-account=000000-000000-000000 \
  --filter="displayName=bookshelf-monthly" --format="value(name.basename())")
gcloud billing budgets delete "$BUDGET_ID" --billing-account=000000-000000-000000

# Budget notification topic (and any function subscribed to it)
gcloud pubsub topics delete budget-alerts

# Billing export dataset (disable the export in Billing > Billing export first)
bq rm -r -f -d "$PROJECT_ID:billing_export"

# Labels added for the experiments
gcloud run services update catalog-api --region=us-central1 --remove-labels=cost_center
gcloud projects update "$PROJECT_ID" --remove-labels=cost_center

# IAM bindings on the billing account
gcloud billing accounts remove-iam-policy-binding 000000-000000-000000 \
  --member="group:finance@example.com" --role="roles/billing.viewer"
----

Disable the BigQuery export in the console before dropping the dataset, delete saved estimates in the pricing calculator
that you no longer need, and remove any service account used by a billing kill-switch function. A *commitment cannot
be cancelled or deleted* once purchased: check its term and price before buying, and note that it keeps billing even
if the project is deleted. Finally, the cleanest way to stop all charges from an experiment is to delete the throwaway
project (`gcloud projects delete my-project-id`), which stops its billing immediately and schedules the resources for
removal after the standard 30-day recovery window.

Reference: https://docs.cloud.google.com/sdk/gcloud/reference/billing/budgets/delete[`gcloud billing budgets delete`],
https://docs.cloud.google.com/resource-manager/docs/creating-managing-projects#shutting_down_projects[Shutting down projects]
