This page describes how articles are written and managed in the Joomla administrator. It was written against *Joomla 6.2.x*
(released on 13 October 2026; PHP 8.3 or later) and its facts were checked on 2026-10-30 against the Joomla! User Guide
(https://guide.joomla.org/), the Joomla 6.2 release announcement and the 6.2 source tree. The screens are the same in the
5.4 line, which only receives security fixes; the differences introduced by 6.2 (the editor, the link picker, the Read More
button and the image CSS class fields) are called out where they apply.

An *article* is the basic unit of content in Joomla. It belongs to exactly one category, has an access level and an
optional language, and is shown on the site through a menu item (a single article, a category blog or list, the featured
articles view, the archive) or through a module. The component behind articles is `com_content`. Articles are used by
four kinds of people: the *author* or *editor* who writes them, the *site builder* who decides how they are laid out, the
*administrator* who controls who may publish, and the *developer* who reads or writes them through the Web Services API.

The running example is the *Tallpine Hiking Club*, a fictitious mountain club. Its volunteers write trail reports in the
nested categories *Trails › Alpine* and *Trails › Valley*, and board announcements in *News*. Categories, tags, custom
fields and menu items have their own pages: see xref:cms/joomla/categories-and-tags.adoc[Categories and Tags],
xref:cms/joomla/custom-fields.adoc[Custom Fields], xref:cms/joomla/menus-and-menu-items.adoc[Menus and Menu Items] and
xref:cms/joomla/media-manager.adoc[Media Manager]. Access levels and permissions are in
xref:cms/joomla/users-groups-access-levels-and-acl.adoc[Users, Groups, Access Levels and ACL], and approval flows in
xref:cms/joomla/workflows.adoc[Workflows].

== The Articles list

*Content → Articles* opens the list of all articles. It is the working screen for editors: it shows the status icon, title,
alias, category, access level, author, language (on multilingual sites), creation date, hit count and ID of each article.
A yellow star in the *Featured* column marks a featured article, and a padlock next to a title marks an article that is
checked out by somebody. The same list is reached from the Content dashboard, and the article form is opened by clicking a
title or the *New* button.

=== Filters and the Actions menu

The filter bar narrows the list as soon as a filter changes, and Joomla remembers the filters for the rest of the session.
The documented filters are:

* *Featured*: featured, unfeatured, or both.
* *Status*: only Published and Unpublished articles are shown by default. Trashed and Archived articles appear only when the
  status filter is set to the matching value or to *All*. This is why a freshly archived article seems to have vanished.
* *Category*: includes subcategories unless *Max Levels* limits the depth, and several categories from different trees can
  be selected together.
* *Access*: Public, Guest, Registered, Special or Super Users, or any custom access level.
* *Author*: none, *Created by me*, or a named author.
* *Language* (multilingual sites only) and *Tag* (several tags can be chosen).

The *Actions* button in the toolbar applies an operation to every ticked row: publish, unpublish, feature, unfeature,
archive, check in, trash and batch. The *Batch* dialog changes the language, access level, tag or workflow stage of many
articles at once and copies or moves them to another category; it is the quickest way to move the Tallpine winter reports
from *News* into *Trails › Alpine*. Trashed articles are only removed from the database by the *Delete* button, which appears when the
list is filtered to Trashed, after a confirmation, which is why trashing is the safe default for
unwanted content.

directly; see xref:cms/joomla/workflows.adoc[Workflows].

== Creating an article

*Content → Articles → New* opens the *Articles: New* form. The form has these tabs: *Content*, *Images and Links*,
*Options*, *Publishing*, *Metadata* and *Permissions* (the last only for users who may change permissions; the tab names
and the Associations and Workflow tabs depend on the installed features). The toolbar of the form offers:

* *Save*, which saves and keeps the form open. Saving often is recommended, because a session timeout loses unsaved text.
* *Save & Close*, which saves, releases the check-out lock and returns to the list. Its drop-down holds *Save & New*,
  *Save to Menu* (saves and opens *Menus: New Item* with the article pre-selected) and *Save as Copy* (creates a copy whose
  title and alias get a number appended, for example `-2`, and leaves the copy open for editing).
* *Versions*, *Preview*, *Accessibility Check*, *Permissions* and *Help*, described in the sections below.
* *Close*, which discards unsaved changes and releases the lock.

=== The fields of the Content tab

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Field |Behaviour

|Title
|Required and the only field that must contain text. Titles do not have to be unique, but a unique title makes the list
easier to search.

|Alias
|Normally left blank. Joomla generates it from the title (lower case, non-alphanumeric characters turned into dashes). The
alias forms the last segment of the SEF URL, so changing the alias of a published article breaks old links unless a
redirect is created (see xref:cms/joomla/seo-sef-urls-redirects-and-schema-org.adoc[SEO, SEF URLs, Redirects and Schema.org]).
Aliases must be unique within a category: saving a duplicate fails with an error until the alias is changed.

|Category
|Required, defaulting to *Uncategorised*. The drop-down is searchable by typing part of the name.

|Status
|Published (the default), Unpublished, Archived or Trashed.

|Featured
|A yes/no switch. A featured article appears in the *Featured Articles* view (see below).

|Access
|The access level allowed to see the article: Public, Guest, Registered, Special, Super Users or a custom level such as the
Tallpine *Trail Warden* level.

|Language
|*All* or one of the installed content languages. It matters on multilingual sites only.

|Tags
|Existing tags are picked from a list, and a new tag is created by typing it and pressing Enter. To avoid near-duplicates
(`family-friendly`, `Family friendly`), create tags in the Tags component first.

|Version Note
|A free-text comment stored with the version created by this save. It is shown in the Versions dialog and cleared after
saving.

|Note
|An internal note shown under the title in the Articles list, never on the site.
|===

Below these fields sits the editor, and the custom fields assigned to the article's category (or to all categories) are
shown on their own tabs or in the Content tab, depending on the field group. Custom fields are stored separately from the
article text, so they are not part of the saved versions (see below).

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-content[Joomla User Guide: Article: Edit - Content]

== The editor

Joomla ships two editors in the core: *Editor - TinyMCE* (the default, a visual editor) and *Editor - CodeMirror* (a plain
code editor), and a third option *Editor - None* (a plain textarea). The editor used for a user is chosen in *Users →
Manage → (user) → Basic Settings → Editor*, and the site-wide default in *System → Global Configuration → Site → Default
Editor*. TinyMCE is the plugin `plg_editors_tinymce`; the 6.2 source tree depends on TinyMCE 8 (the `tinymce` dependency of
the 6.2 development branch is `+^8.9.0+`). The release announcement for 6.2 mentions only that TinyMCE itself was updated.

=== What 6.2 changed

According to the Joomla 6.2 release announcement, the editor received these changes:

* *Media Manager integration*: the image and media picker of the editor opens Joomla's own Media Manager, so the same
  folders, the same upload limits and the same image management apply as everywhere else in the administrator (see
  xref:cms/joomla/media-manager.adoc[Media Manager]).
* *A unified link picker*: one modal handles link insertion for the Article, Contact and Menu buttons, where previously each
  button opened its own modal.
* *A Read More button* in the TinyMCE toolbar that inserts the article break.
* *Text filtering*: TinyMCE uses Joomla's text filter configuration by default, instead of its own valid-elements defaults.
* Corrections to untranslated toolbar-builder buttons.

The text-filter point deserves care, because it is the commonest cause of "my markup disappears". Joomla filters the HTML of
an article on save according to the *Text Filters* tab of the Global Configuration, per user group: the *Guest*, *Public*
and *Registered* groups default to *No HTML*, other groups to the *Default Forbidden List*, and Super Users are not
filtered. The TinyMCE plugin has its own *Prohibited Elements*, *Valid Elements* and *Extended Valid Elements* settings
and a *Use Joomla Text Filter* switch; turning the switch on hides those three fields and makes the global filters
decisive. Elements such as `script`, `applet` and `iframe` are removed from the editor's default list whatever other
settings say, so an embedded map or video may need the plugin settings or the dedicated embed mechanism rather than raw
markup. A subtle trap: content written with *Editor - None*, then re-saved in TinyMCE, loses filtered tags without any
warning.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/editors/editors-html-filters[Joomla User Guide: HTML Filters]

=== Toolbars and toolsets

TinyMCE has three toolsets, assigned to user groups on the plugin's edit page. Toolset 0 is the most extensive (by default
Administrator, Editor and Super Users), toolset 1 is the middle one (Manager and Registered) and toolset 2 is minimal
(Public, for public submission forms). Two toolbars are visible by default and an ellipsis at the end of the second one
reveals more buttons. *Toggle Editor* switches between the visual view and the underlying HTML.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/editors/editors-tinymce-plugin[Joomla User Guide: TinyMCE Plugin]

=== Inserting content

The *Insert* menu adds an external link, image or media item (the editor asks for a URL). The *CMS Content* menu inserts
items from inside the site:

* *Article*: a link to another article, chosen from a list instead of typed, so it survives an alias change.
* *Contact*: a link to a contact.
* *Field*: a placeholder for a custom field value.
* *Media*: a file from the Media Manager.
* *Menu*: a link to a menu item.
* *Page Break*: splits a long article into pages, with a page title and a table-of-contents alias.
* *Read More*: inserts the separator between intro text and full text at the cursor, best placed at a paragraph break.

Linking to an article through the picker, instead of pasting its URL, lets Joomla build the correct SEF URL for the active
menu structure and language, which is why trail reports linked from news items stay valid when a menu is renamed.

== Intro text and full text

Joomla stores an article's body in two database columns, `introtext` and `fulltext`, of the table `+#__content+`. The editor
shows them as one document. The *Read More* separator is the line between them; in the editor's HTML view it is a single
element:

[source,html]
----
<p>The Tallpine summer programme starts on 1 June with the Lakeside Loop.</p>
<hr id="system-readmore">
<p>Meet at the Valley station at 08:30. Bring water, a rain jacket and the signed
waiver. Dogs are welcome on the first section only.</p>
----

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-content[Joomla User Guide: Article: Edit - Content]

When the article is saved, Joomla splits the text at the `system-readmore` rule: everything above it becomes the intro text
and everything below the full text. If there is no separator, the whole body is intro text. This has several consequences:

* *Blog, featured and archive views* show the intro text and a *Read more* button that leads to the single article, whose
  title is part of the button label unless the *Read More with Title* option is switched off. The number of characters shown
  in list-style layouts is controlled by *Read More Limit (characters)*, which defaults to 100.
* *The single article view* shows intro text followed by full text. The article option *Intro Text* can hide the intro
  text on the single article page, which is useful when the intro is a teaser repeated from the body.
* *Partial access restriction*: text without a separator counts as intro text and is visible to everyone, so a members-only
  article meant to give guests a free preview needs a break after the first paragraph, with the article's access set to
  Registered. The Read more link of a restricted article then asks guests to log in or register.

The wording of the button can be changed for one article in the *Read More Text* field of the Options tab. To change it for
the whole site, add a language override for the key `JGLOBAL_READ_MORE_TITLE`, keeping the `%s` placeholder that receives
the article title (language overrides are described in
xref:cms/joomla/language-files-and-internationalisation.adoc[Language Files and Internationalisation]):

[source,ini]
----
; administrator/language/overrides/en-GB.override.ini (created by System > Language Overrides)
JGLOBAL_READ_MORE_TITLE="Full trail report: %s"
----

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-read-more[Joomla User Guide: Article: Edit - Read More]

== The Images and Links tab

This tab sets an *intro image*, a *full article image* and up to three links in predefined places. It matters most when
the article is shown in a category blog, where the intro image appears above the title. Administrators may hide the tab
from some editors in the Articles options.

* *Intro Image*: optional; used in blog and list layouts. Without it, no image is shown.
* *Full Article Image*: shown at the top of the full article; it has the same fields as the intro image.
* For each image: *Image Description (Alt Text)* for screen readers, a *Decorative Image* checkbox (when ticked, an empty
  `alt` attribute is written, which tells assistive technology to skip the image), *Image Class* (defaults to `left`, which
  floats the image; float classes have no effect on grid or flex items) and *Image Caption*. A caption should add context,
  not repeat the alt text, or screen readers announce the same words twice. An image with no description and without the
  decorative checkbox can fail accessibility tests.
* Joomla 6.2 adds separate CSS class fields for the image and for its surrounding figure element, in both intro and full
  article images, so a template can style the wrapper without a layout override.
* *Link A*, *Link B* and *Link C*: each has a URL (the full URL, even for the same site), a link text and a *URL Target
  Window*: use global (open in the parent window), parent window, new window, popup or modal. The parent window is the
  preferred choice, because the Back button still works and mobile users are not confused by a hidden new window. The
  position of the links relative to the text is set by *Position of the Links* on the Options tab.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-images-and-links[Joomla User Guide: Article: Edit - Images and Links]

Images chosen here come from the Media Manager, so the folder, the allowed file types and the maximum upload size are
configured there, not in the article. An image inserted in the body with the editor, in contrast, is plain HTML in the
article text and is not tracked by the article form: deleting the file later leaves a broken image.

== The Publishing tab

The Publishing tab controls when an article is visible and who is credited with it.

* *Start Publishing*: set automatically when the article is first saved; change it to a future date and time to hold the
  article back (an embargo).
* *Finish Publishing*: blank by default. A date in the future makes the article disappear from the site at that moment; the
  list then shows "Published, but has Expired". Clearing the field republishes the article.
* *Start Featured* and *End Featured*: shown only for featured articles, they define the period in which the article
  appears in the Featured Articles view. They are not saved if the article is not featured, and an article whose featured
  period ended stays published and visible elsewhere ("Featured, but has Expired").
* *Created Date*: set on first save and editable, which allows articles to be sorted into a different creation order.
* *Created By* and *Created by Alias*: the first is picked with the user dialog and defaults to the author; the alias
  replaces the username in the article information block. Use *Preview* to check the result.
* Informational fields: *Modified Date*, *Modified By*, *Revision* and *Hits* are read-only, as is the article *ID*.
* *Meta Description*: documented as part of the Publishing panel; when empty, Joomla falls back to the single article menu
  item's description and then to the site description.

All dates are entered in the user's time zone and stored in UTC, so a Tallpine editor in Zurich who schedules a post for
08:00 sees 08:00 even though the database holds 06:00 or 07:00 depending on daylight saving time. Publishing and
expiring happen when a visitor requests the page (there is no cron job for it), so a page cached by a caching plugin or a
reverse proxy may show the old state until the cache expires; see
xref:cms/joomla/caching-and-performance.adoc[Caching and Performance].

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-publishing[Joomla User Guide: Article: Edit - Publishing]

=== Access, language and metadata

The *Access* level hides an article from visitors who do not belong to it, both in the article itself and in lists. The
categories must be public for their menu items to appear to anonymous visitors. The *Unauthorised Links* option decides
whether links to registered-only articles are shown to guests; it can be set globally, or per menu item for category blogs
and the featured articles view. Access levels are explained in
xref:cms/joomla/users-groups-access-levels-and-acl.adoc[Users, Groups, Access Levels and ACL].

*Language* assigns the article to one content language, or to *All*. On a multilingual site the Associations tab links the
English and German versions of the same trail report, so that the language switcher leads from one to the other; see
xref:cms/joomla/multilingual-sites.adoc[Multilingual Sites].

The *Metadata* tab holds the *Robots* instruction (the default "Use Global" adds no robots tag), *Author* and *Rights*
tags. The *Keywords* field is not used by Joomla core and search engines no longer use it for ranking. To control crawlers
site-wide, edit `robots.txt` in the site root.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-metadata[Joomla User Guide: Article: Edit - Metadata]

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-access-restriction[Joomla User Guide: Article: Access Restriction]

== Article options: global, per article and per menu item

Many display settings of an article exist at three levels, and understanding which one wins is the main skill of a site
builder.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart TD
  G["Global: Content > Articles > Options&lt;br/&gt;(com_content parameters)"]
  A["Article: Options tab&lt;br/&gt;(attribs of the article)"]
  M["Menu item: Options tab&lt;br/&gt;(single article, blog, list, featured)"]
  R[Rendered article or blog]
  G -->|"default for everything"| R
  A -->|"overrides the global value&lt;br/&gt;when the article is shown alone"| R
  M -->|"overrides global and, for blog and list&lt;br/&gt;layouts, article, unless Use Article Settings"| R
----

The rules, as the User Guide documents them:

* *Global* values live in *Content → Articles → Options* (the Options button of the Articles list). Choosing *Use Global*
  in an article field means "take the value from here".
* An *article* value applies when the article is displayed on its own, and in blog views when the menu item is set to *Use
  Article Settings*.
* A *menu item* of a blog or list type has its own options and normally wins for the articles it shows. Two settings, *Linked
  Titles* and *Unauthorised Links*, depend on the menu item explicitly: the article's own value applies only when the menu
  item says *Use Article Settings*.

The Options tab of the article is organised in panels:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Panel |Fields

|Layout
|*Layout* (Use Global, Default, or any named layout override from the active template, see
xref:cms/joomla/template-overrides-and-alternative-layouts.adoc[Template Overrides and Alternative Layouts]); *Title*;
*Linked Titles*; *Tags*; *Intro Text*; *Position of Article Info* (above, below or split, where Hits moves below the text);
*Article Info Title*.

|Category
|*Category*, *Link Category*, *Parent Category*, *Link Parent Category*.

|Associations
|Multilingual sites only: *Associations* (show the list of language versions) and *Use Image Flags* (flags instead of
language codes).

|Author
|*Author* and *Link to Author's Contact Page*, which requires a contact linked to the user.

|Date
|*Create Date* and *Modify Date* are hidden by default; *Publish Date* is shown by default.

|Options
|*Navigation* (Previous and Next buttons), *Hits*, *Unauthorised Links*, *Position of the Links* (Links A, B and C above or
below the text, or hidden), *Read More Text* and *Browser Page Title* (an alternative title for the browser tab and search
engines, which defaults to the article title).
|===

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-options[Joomla User Guide: Article: Edit - Options]

The same Articles Options screen also holds the settings that govern editing: which tabs and fields editors see, the
*Editing Layout* tab with the version limit (below), the default access of new articles and the permission rules of the
component. Because *Use Global* is the default for almost every field, a change in *Content → Articles → Options*
affects every article that has not overridden it, which is the right place to set site-wide conventions such as hiding the
author on the Tallpine site while showing it on the News blog through its menu item.

== Versions and content history

Every time an article is saved in the edit form, Joomla stores a version of it. The *Versions* button of the toolbar opens
a dialog that lists the saved versions with these columns: a checkbox, the *Date* (a star marks the current version, and
clicking the date previews it), the *Version Note*, *Keep Forever*, the *Author* and the *Character Count* (which includes
the database column names).

The toolbar of the dialog offers five operations:

* *Restore*: replaces the current article with the selected version and reloads the form with that text in the editor.
  Nothing is saved until the form itself is saved.
* *Preview*: opens the version in a separate window.
* *Compare*: select two versions and a window shows a table with the field name, the older and the newer value, and the
  differences highlighted.
* *Keep On/Off*: toggles *Keep Forever*.
* *Delete*: removes the selected versions only, not the article.

How many versions are kept is a setting of *Content → Articles → Options → Editing Layout* (the default is 10; the setting
belongs to that screen, not to the Options tab of the article). When a save would exceed the limit, the oldest version is
deleted. A version marked *Keep Forever* is not counted towards the limit and is never deleted automatically. History is
also available for categories, contacts, banners and clients, news feeds and user notes. Custom fields are not stored in
the versions, so restoring a trail report does not restore its `distance-km` or `difficulty` values.

A practical convention for the Tallpine editors is to write a version note on each substantial save ("Added 2026 winter
closure"), and to tick *Keep Forever* on the version that was approved by the board.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-versions[Joomla User Guide: Article: Versions]

== Check-in and check-out

To prevent two editors from overwriting each other, Joomla locks an article while it is open in the edit form. The table row
has a `checked_out` column (the user ID) and a `checked_out_time` column. The lock is set when the form opens and cleared
when it is closed with *Save & Close* or *Close*. If the form is left in another way (the browser Back button, a closed
tab, an expired session), the lock stays and the article is shown with a padlock; hovering over the padlock shows who holds
the lock. The last editor can keep editing without checking in.

Who can release a lock:

* The editor who holds it, by opening the article and closing it properly.
* A Super User or Administrator, by clicking the padlock for one item, or by ticking several articles and choosing
  *Check-in* in the *Actions* menu.
* *System → Maintenance → Global Check-in*, available to Super Users and Administrators, lists every table that has locked
  rows with their counts. Selecting a table and choosing *Check-in* releases all locks in it, whoever holds them.

Global Check-in is a blunt instrument. If it is used while a colleague is editing, two people can work on the same article,
and whoever saves last wins. Modules, categories and other components use the same mechanism, so stale locks can occur there
too. The locks can be inspected with a query (run it against the database of a development copy, adjusting the prefix):

[source,sql]
----
SELECT id, title, checked_out, checked_out_time
FROM #__content
WHERE checked_out IS NOT NULL AND checked_out > 0
ORDER BY checked_out_time;
----

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-check-in[Joomla User Guide: Article: Check-in]

Check-in is not security: it is a courtesy lock based on the database, and anybody with the edit permission who bypasses the
form (for example through the Web Services API) is not stopped by it.

== Featured and Archived articles

=== Featured articles

*Content → Featured Articles* lists every article that is marked as featured. Any article of any category can be featured:
use the *Featured* switch of the form, click the star icon in the Articles list, or select several rows and choose *Actions →
Feature*. Featuring does not copy or move the article; it only marks it, which is why the same article can appear in a
category blog and in the featured view.

On the site, the featured view is the menu item type *Articles → Featured Articles*, normally the home page of the site. Its
*Blog Layout* tab sets the number of leading, intro and link articles, the columns and the ordering, for example *Featured
Articles Order* (the order dragged together on the Featured Articles page) or *Most Recent First*. Without a custom order, the
view uses ID descending, so the newest article appears first. The *Start Featured* and *End Featured* dates described above
let the Tallpine home page show the "Hike of the month" article only during that month, without any editor having to
remember to unfeature it.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-articles-featured[Joomla User Guide: Articles: Featured]

=== Archived articles

Archiving takes an article out of the active list while keeping it available. To archive, select the articles in the list and
choose *Actions → Archive*, or set *Status* to Archived in the form. Archiving does not unpublish the article: its direct URL
still works, and a single-article menu item that points to it still resolves. Archived articles are hidden from the default
list, and the status filter must be set to *Archived* (or *All*) to find them. To restore one, change its status back to
Published.

On the site, two items expose the archive:

* the menu item type *Articles → Archived Articles*, with month and year filters and a list limit from 5 to 100 or *All*
  (avoid *All* on large archives, because it can slow the page or cause server errors);
* the module *Articles - Archived*, which can be placed in any template position (see
  xref:cms/joomla/modules-and-positions.adoc[Modules and Positions]).

Logged-in users with front-end editing permission can also archive articles from the site.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-articles-archive[Joomla User Guide: Articles: Archive]

== Previewing and accessibility checking

The *Preview* button of the form shows the saved version of the article in a popup, not the unsaved edits, so save first. If
the popup says that the page cannot be found, log in to the front end and try again, because the preview uses the front-end
session. The *Accessibility Check* button runs the built-in checker over the article text and reports problems such as
missing alt text or skipped heading levels; it is described on its own User Guide page.

Source: https://guide.joomla.org/user-guide/articles/articles-article-preview[Joomla User Guide: Article: Preview]

== Front-end editing

Users who have the *Edit* or *Edit Own* permission can edit an article from the site: an edit icon appears next to the
article, and users who may create articles get a *Create an Article* menu item type (*Articles → Create Article*). The form
is a reduced version of the administrator form, uses the same editor and the same filters, and respects the same locks and
versions. Front-end editing is how Tallpine hike leaders submit a report without entering the administrator; the *Author*
group may create articles and an *Editor* may edit any article.

== Creating articles through the Web Services API

Articles are one of the resources of the Web Services API (see xref:cms/joomla/web-services-api.adoc[Web Services API]). With
an API token of a user who may create articles, a trail report can be created from a script. The token is read from the
environment:

[source,bash]
----
export TALLPINE_API_TOKEN="CHANGE_ME"   # token of an API user, never committed

curl -s -X POST "https://tallpine.example.org/api/index.php/v1/content/articles" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer ${TALLPINE_API_TOKEN}" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "Accept: application/vnd.api+json" \
  -d '{
        "title": "Lakeside Loop: trail report",
        "alias": "lakeside-loop-trail-report",
        "catid": 12,
        "state": 1,
        "access": 1,
        "language": "*",
        "articletext": "<p>Teaser.</p><hr id=\"system-readmore\"><p>Full report.</p>"
      }'
----

Source: https://github.com/joomla/joomla-cms/tree/6.2-dev/api/components/com_content[Joomla CMS source: api/components/com_content]

The payload uses the same field names as the database (`catid`, `state`, `access`, `language`), and the single field
`articletext` is split into intro text and full text at the read-more rule, exactly as in the editor. The category ID
(here 12 stands for *Trails › Alpine*) differs on every site.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Alias already in use*: two articles in the same category cannot share an alias. Rename one, or use *Save as Copy* and
  edit the alias of the copy.
* *Markup disappears on save*: the text filter of the user's group, or the TinyMCE prohibited elements, removed it. Check
  the group in *Global Configuration → Text Filters* and the *Use Joomla Text Filter* switch of the plugin. Only Super
  Users are exempt.
* *The article is "gone"*: it is Archived or Trashed, and the default list hides both.
* *The article is locked*: somebody left the form without closing it. Ask a Super User to check it in rather than using
  Global Check-in during working hours.
* *The article is published but invisible*: the access level excludes the visitor, the category is unpublished or not
  public, the start date is in the future, the finish date has passed, or the language does not match.
* *Wrong options applied*: a menu item overrides the article options. Look at the menu item first.
* *Lost custom field values after a restore*: custom fields are not part of the versions.
* *Large Archive page*: the *All* list limit on the archived articles menu item loads every article.
* *Images broken after tidying the media folder*: images placed in the text are plain HTML paths.

== Security notes

* Give editors the lowest toolset and the *Default Forbidden List* filter that fits; do not give Super User rights to
  authors. Raw HTML permission is the most powerful content permission, because cross-site scripting through an article
  executes in the browser of every visitor, including administrators.
* Never disable the text filter for a group that is not fully trusted, and do not switch to *Editor - None* to bypass it:
  the global filter still applies on save.
* Access levels hide articles from lists and pages, but media files in `images/` are public URLs. A members-only PDF must
  not live in a public folder if its URL must stay secret.
* Preview, Accessibility Check and the version dialogs require the edit permission; version history can reveal text that
  was removed from the live article, so restrict the Permissions of the component for users who should not see it.
* API tokens create articles with the permissions of their user; use a dedicated user with the *Author* group for
  integrations and keep the token in an environment variable or a secret store.

|===
|Version |Change relevant to articles

|Joomla 5.4
|Last 5.x release line; it receives security fixes only until October 2027.

|Joomla 6.1
|A Proof-of-Work captcha is added, which can protect front-end submission forms.

|Joomla 6.2
|The TinyMCE editor uses the Media Manager, a unified link picker, a Read More button and the global text filters by default;

Older books and tutorials such as *Joomla! 4 Masterclass* (Packt, 2023) describe the Joomla 4 editor and Media Manager. Since 6.2 the image picker opens the Media Manager, the Article, Contact
and Menu buttons share one link picker and Read More is also a toolbar button. Joomla 4 is end of life since October 2025, and links in older
material to `docs.joomla.org/J4.x` pages point to a legacy wiki; the current end-user documentation is the Joomla! User
Guide. Statements in older sources about jQuery being part of the core, or about filling the *Keywords* metadata field to
help search engines, no longer apply: Joomla core does not use the keywords field.
