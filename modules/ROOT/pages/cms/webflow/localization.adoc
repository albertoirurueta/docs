This page describes Webflow Localization as it was on 2026-10-28, the date on which its facts were checked against the
official documentation. Localization is a feature of the Webflow platform and has no version numbers, so everything below
is dated. Plans are named by tier only and this page never quotes prices. Publishing a secondary locale needs a paid Site
plan *and* a Localize add-on, in the tiers *Localize Essential*, *Localize Advanced* and *Localize Enterprise*, while the
Team workspace plan includes two locales; the tier names and limits are summarized in
<<_the_localize_tiers_and_what_they_unlock>>, and the xref:cms/webflow/plans-workspaces-seats-and-roles.adoc[Plans,
Workspaces, Seats and Roles] page explains how Site plans, Workspace plans and add-ons relate. A free preview of
Localization, including machine-powered translation and the localization of static pages, CMS content and SEO settings,
is part of every Site plan from Starter to Enterprise, so a team can try the workflow before buying anything. Webflow
Ecommerce is the one important exclusion: Localization does not support the features of an Ecommerce Site plan, so
products, categories and the Ecommerce pages of a shop cannot be localized; see
xref:cms/webflow/ecommerce-overview.adoc[Ecommerce Overview].

The running example is *Fernbrook Gardens*, the fictitious botanical garden of this sub-section. Its primary language is
English, and the garden adds Spanish (`es`, subdirectory `/es`) for the many Spanish-speaking visitors of the region and
German (`de`, subdirectory `/de`) for visitors from the partner garden in Hamburg. The Plants collection, the Garden
Journal and the static pages (Home, Visit, Events, About) are localized; the Volunteers area stays English and is not
published in the secondary locales; the seed shop stays English because Ecommerce cannot be localized.

== What Localization is and who it is for

Localization is the Webflow feature that lets *one* site, one Designer project and one CMS, be published in several
languages and regions. It is not a copy of the site per language. The site keeps a single structure of pages, components,
collections and fields; each language is a *locale* that overlays its own text, its own images, its own SEO settings, its
own style overrides and its own CMS item content on that shared structure. A visitor chooses a locale through a URL
prefix such as `/es`, and Webflow serves the same page with the Spanish values substituted.

Different people use it differently.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Person | What they do with Localization

| Site owner
| Decides which languages the garden serves, buys the Localize add-on tier that allows that many locales, and decides when
a locale is ready to be public

| Designer
| Builds the page structure once in the primary locale, adapts styles for longer German words or right-to-left scripts,
builds the locale switcher and checks each locale at every breakpoint

| Content editor and translator
| Switches the Designer to a locale, edits text, alt text and CMS items in that locale or reviews machine and AI
translations; this is described in <<_localizing_pages_styles_assets_and_alt_text>> and
<<_localizing_cms_items>>

| Marketer
| Localizes page names, slugs, titles, descriptions and Open Graph data, checks hreflang and the sitemap, and watches
indexing of each locale

| Developer
| Reads and writes localized content with the Data API (see
xref:cms/webflow/data-api-ecommerce-and-localization.adoc[Data API: Ecommerce and Localization]), connects a translation
management system, or reads the locale in custom code
|===

Localization features are available in the Designer. Content editors work with them through the Designer in edit mode,
the mode that replaced the legacy Editor; see xref:cms/webflow/content-editing-on-the-canvas.adoc[Content Editing on the
Canvas]. The legacy Editor never supported localization.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961240752147[Localization overview].

== The Localize tiers and what they unlock

A locale is a billable capability. The Site plan decides whether the site can be published at all; the Localize add-on
decides how many locales it can have and which routing and workflow features are present. On 2026-10-28 the tiers are:

[cols="2,4,5",options="header"]
|===
| Tier | Locales | What changes

| Free preview (every Site plan)
| Localization can be explored; no secondary locale can be published without the add-on
| Machine-powered translation, static page, CMS and SEO localization can be tried in the Designer

| Localize Essential
| Up to 3 locales
| Includes a monthly word allowance for machine translation (10,000 words per locale per month on the pricing page)

| Localize Advanced
| Up to 10 locales
| A larger monthly allowance (50,000 words per locale per month) and automatic domain-level routing

| Localize Enterprise
| A custom number of locales
| Custom allowance, automatic domain-level routing, a custom translation glossary, locale-specific access and the ability
to set individual static pages to draft in specific locales

| Team workspace plan
| Two locales included
| The Team plan bundles two locales with the plan itself; more locales need the add-on
|===

Three consequences follow.

* The tier sets how many locales a site can have, so a team that plans four languages for the Fernbrook site should check
  the tier limits before adding the fourth.
* Adding a locale does not publish it. A locale stays private until publishing is enabled for its subdirectory, so
  unfinished languages can be built in the background; see <<_per_locale_publishing>>.
* The Ecommerce exclusion applies at the plan level, as described in the introduction.

The word allowances are quoted from Webflow's pricing page and change when Webflow changes its pricing; check the pricing
page before sizing a project. Allowances apply to the built-in machine and AI translation only and do not limit manual
translation or a connected translation management system.

Source: https://www.webflow.com/pricing[Webflow pricing], https://webflow.com/feature/localize[Webflow Localization
feature page].

== Locales, languages and subdirectories

A *locale* is a language plus an optional country, with a display name, an optional display image and a subdirectory. The
Localize panel of the Designer, under *Locales*, lists them and starts the *Add new locale* flow.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Setting | Meaning

| Language
| The language of the locale, for example Spanish or German; it sets the language code used by `hreflang` and the `lang`
attribute

| Country (optional)
| A region such as Mexico; it produces a regional code like `es-MX` and lets a site serve Spanish for Spain and Spanish for
Mexico as two locales

| Display name
| The text the locale switcher shows; it defaults to the language and can be edited, for example `Espanol` instead of
`Spanish`

| Display image
| An optional flag or icon the locale switcher can show

| Subdirectory
| The URL path segment that selects the locale, for example `/es` or `/es-mx`
|===

The *primary locale* is the site's default language. It is chosen when Localization is set up, it cannot be changed later
and a secondary locale cannot be promoted to primary. This is the single most important decision of a localized project:
the primary locale is where the structure, the slugs and the fallback content live. Fernbrook's primary locale is English
without a subdirectory, so the existing English URLs, such as `/plants/lavender`, keep working and need no redirect.

Subdirectory guidelines:

* Choose short, stable subdirectories that follow the language code, such as `/es` and `/de`; use a regional code such as
  `/es-mx` only when a second Spanish locale exists.
* Do not choose a subdirectory that equals an existing static page slug, a folder or a Collection slug. A site that has a
  Collection with the slug `de` or a page `/es` would have two things claiming the same path.
* A locale that is edited in the Designer but not published has no public URL, so a typed `/de` returns the site's 404 page
  until publishing is enabled.

Webflow serves locales through subdirectories of the same domain, not through separate domains or subdomains. The effect
for search is that all locales share the domain's authority and appear in one Search Console property, and the effect for
operations is that one SSL certificate and one set of DNS records cover every language.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/53682971927571[Manage your site's locales].

== Locale routing

Routing is how a request ends up in the right locale. There are two mechanisms, and the second is narrower than most
people expect.

image::cms-webflow-localization-routing.svg["Routing of a Fernbrook request: the subdirectory prefix selects the locale, automatic routing applies only to the root homepage, and every page lists its locale variants in hreflang tags",width=900]

=== Explicit routing by subdirectory

Every URL carries its locale. A request without a prefix is served in the primary locale; a request starting with `/es` or
`/de` is served in that locale. The browser's language, the visitor's location and any cookie are *ignored* for these
URLs. This makes pages predictable: a link to `/de/pflanzen/lavendel` always shows the German page, can be indexed, shared
and cached, and is the same for every visitor.

=== Automatic routing of the root homepage

On the Advanced and Enterprise Localize tiers, Webflow can *automatically route* visitors by language. It applies only to
visitors who land on the root homepage (`/`). The browser's language preference is matched against the published locales
and the visitor is redirected to the matching one. Four details matter:

* A deep link, for example `/plants/lavender`, is never rerouted, so shared links stay stable.
* A browser configured for a language only, for example French, is *not* routed to a country locale such as French
  (Canada). If a site wants regional routing, it should publish the language-only locale as well.
* If no published locale matches, the visitor stays on the primary locale.
* On tiers without automatic routing, every visitor lands on the primary locale and has to use the locale switcher.

Automatic routing therefore never replaces a visible switcher. A visitor from Madrid whose browser is set to English must
still be able to choose Spanish, and a search crawler that does not send a language is routed nowhere.

=== The locale switcher

A switcher is built in the Designer with a Dropdown element that contains the *Locales list* element. The Locales list is a
collection-like list that has one item per published locale and exposes the locale's display name, display image and link.
Because it is built from the live locale list, adding the German locale later adds a link automatically. Place it in the
`SiteHeader` component so every page has it.

The links inside the list point to the same page in another locale. Internal links created with the *Page* or *Collection
page* link types are localized automatically: on the Spanish site a link to the Visit page points to the Spanish Visit
page. Links typed as plain URLs, or entered in rich text as raw addresses, are not rewritten and keep pointing wherever
they were typed.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961235675155[Localized SEO and locale routing],
https://university.webflow.com/resources/checklists/webflow-localize-checklist[Webflow Localize checklist].

== Localizing pages, styles, assets and alt text

Secondary locales *inherit* the primary locale until they are overridden. Switching the Designer to the Spanish locale with
the *Locale view* shows the English content in Spanish context; nothing is duplicated. As soon as a value is edited in the
Spanish view, it becomes a Spanish override; resetting the override returns the locale to inheritance.

The inheritance model gives the rule that governs the whole feature: *structure is edited in the primary locale; content
is edited in each locale.* Adding an element, changing a class, rearranging a section or creating a page happens once in
the primary locale and appears everywhere. Text, links, images, alt text, SEO fields and (optionally) styles are edited per
locale.

=== Text and links

Every text block, heading, button label, form label and placeholder can be translated in the Locale view. Text that is
bound to a CMS field is not edited on the page; it is edited on the CMS item. Three tools translate in bulk:

* *Machine translation* built into Localize, which translates the content of a locale in one action and draws on the
  monthly word allowance;
* *AI translation*, with tone and formality that can be set per target language, and exclusion rules that keep content such
  as SKUs, reference codes or legal text in the primary language (excluded content does not use the allowance);
* a *translation app* from the Marketplace or a translation management system connected through the Localize APIs.

The newer *agentic translation* can run once or continuously. Continuous mode keeps retranslating as the primary locale
changes and can *replace translations that people wrote*, so use the one-time mode when a team intends to edit the
translations by hand afterwards.

Machine translation is a draft, not a result. Fernbrook's plant care notes contain Latin names that must not change, units
that differ between regions and words such as _bloom_ that a generic engine renders as something else; a human reviewer
for each language is part of the workflow.

=== Pages and their settings

Page settings are localized per locale: the page name, the slug, the SEO title and meta description, the Open Graph title,
description and image, the sitemap inclusion and the custom code in the head and before the body. A localized slug is what
makes `/es/plantas` possible while the primary page stays `/plants`. Always translate the page *name* as well as the slug,
because the name appears in the Pages panel for the reviewer and in the breadcrumb element.

Static pages can be drafted per locale on Enterprise: the page exists in the primary and in one secondary locale but is not
published in the other. Drafting a localized page does *not* remove the links that point to it, so navigation that is
shared across locales can send a visitor to a 404. Review navigation and internal links before publishing a locale with a
missing page.

=== Localized styles

By default every locale uses the same classes and values. Because languages differ in length, Webflow also lets a designer
*localize styles*: a style property, for example `font-size`, `letter-spacing`, `padding`, `display` or `flex-direction`,
can be given a different value in one locale. The override applies only when the page is served in that locale and is
marked in the Style panel. A German heading, which is often longer than the English one, can then use a smaller
font size or a different wrap without affecting the English site.

The generated CSS keeps the locale override under an attribute selector on the root element, so the cascade stays
ordinary CSS. A simplified view of the output for a heading that is smaller in German is shown below.

[source,css]
----
/* base value, applies to every locale */
.u-heading-xl {
  font-size: 56px;
  line-height: 1.1;
}

/* German override, applies when the page is served in the de locale */
html[lang="de"] .u-heading-xl {
  font-size: 48px;
}

/* small screens keep the same ordering: base, then breakpoint, then locale */
@media screen and (max-width: 479px) {
  html[lang="de"] .u-heading-xl {
    font-size: 34px;
  }
}
----

The exact selector Webflow emits is an implementation detail and can change; the example shows the principle that a locale
override is an ordinary, more specific CSS rule layered on the base style. Localized styles are available to all customers
with Localization.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/lesson/localize-content-and-styles[Localize content and styles].

=== Assets and alt text

Images, videos and documents can be replaced per locale. An image that contains text, for example a poster that says
"Spring Plant Sale", must be replaced with a Spanish and a German version; an image with no text can be shared. Alt text is
translated with the same workflow as other text, and a locale without its own alt text shows the primary locale's alt text
until it is overridden. This inheritance means no locale is ever left with an empty alt attribute by accident, but it also
means that untranslated alt text silently stays in English for a screen-reader user of the Spanish site. Add alt-text
review to the checklist for every locale; see xref:cms/webflow/images-and-assets.adoc[Images and Assets] and
xref:cms/webflow/accessibility.adoc[Accessibility].

For the Fernbrook plant photographs the correct approach is to keep one asset, translate its alt text per locale and
translate the visible caption in the CMS item. The file name is not localized, so use neutral file names such as
`lavender-01.jpg` rather than words in one language.

=== Components and slots

Components are structural, so their elements are edited in the primary locale. The text inside a component instance, the
content of its slots and its prop values can be localized per locale, which lets the `PlantCard` show _Compact_ and
_Feature_ variants in all languages while each language supplies its own text. A component-level text that is not exposed
as a prop is translated inside the component and is therefore shared by every instance, which is correct for a fixed label
such as "Zone" and wrong for text that must differ per instance. Expose the latter as a prop, as described in
xref:cms/webflow/components-props-slots-and-variants.adoc[Components, Props, Slots and Variants].

== Localizing CMS items

The CMS follows the same model as pages: the *structure* is shared and the *content* is per locale. A collection has one
definition, with fields, validations and a template page, and its items exist once per locale.

=== Shared structure, per-locale items

When Localization is enabled, every item of every collection exists in each locale. The item of the primary locale is the
parent; the items of the secondary locales are linked to it and start by inheriting its values. In the Locale view the
editor chooses a locale, and the CMS panel then shows the item with the values of that locale.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Aspect | Behaviour

| Fields and validations
| Defined once, in the primary locale; a field added in the primary locale appears in every locale

| Field values
| Inherited until overridden in the locale; text, rich text, image, alt text, link and option fields can be localized

| Name and slug
| Both can be translated; the slug is what gives `/es/plantas/espliego` its own URL

| Draft and published state
| Set per locale; an item can be live in German and draft in Spanish

| Reference fields
| Point to an item; in a locale, the reference resolves to the same item's version in that locale

| Template page
| One template page; it renders the locale's item fields in the locale's URL space
|===

Fernbrook's Plants collection shows what that means. The `Latin name` field is not translated, so it is the same value in
every locale (the localization of a field can be switched off per locale so that the inherited value is retained). The
`name`, `care notes` and `alt text` fields are translated. The `hardiness zone` reference points to the `Zones` item, and
the Spanish page shows the Spanish name of the zone because the reference resolves to the Spanish version of the item. The
`bloom months` option field has option names, and they are translated at the field level so the Spanish page shows the
Spanish month names.

=== Collection lists in a locale

A Collection list or a Collection template page in a locale shows the items *of that locale*. A Collection list can also
be set to show the primary locale's items regardless of the page's locale, which is useful when a collection is not
translated yet: a German site with an untranslated Events collection can still show the English events instead of an empty
section. Decide this consciously for each list, because the fallback shows content in the wrong language. See
xref:cms/webflow/collection-lists.adoc[Collection Lists] and
xref:cms/webflow/collection-template-pages.adoc[Collection Template Pages].

=== Creating, editing and deleting

* Creating an item always happens in the primary locale; the item is then created in every locale.
* Editing content happens in the locale being translated.
* Duplicating an item in the primary locale creates a new item set for all locales.
* *Deleting an item from a secondary locale* removes it from that locale only. This is how an item that is relevant to one
  locale only, for example an event held in Hamburg, is hidden from the Spanish site.
* *Deleting an item from the primary locale deletes it from all secondary locales as well.* To hide an item from the
  primary locale only, set it to Draft in the primary locale instead of deleting it.
* Treat deletion in a secondary locale as final and rely on the backups described in
  xref:cms/webflow/backups-and-recovery.adoc[Backups and Recovery].

[source,text]
----
Plants / Lavender  (Item ID 65f0c2...a1)
  en  Lavender         /plants/lavender            published
  es  Espliego         /es/plantas/espliego        published
  de  Lavendel         /de/pflanzen/lavendel       draft
Delete in "de"  -> removes only the German item
Delete in "en"  -> removes the English, Spanish and German items
----

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961252209171[Localize Collection content].

=== Slugs and uniqueness

Slugs must be unique within a collection in a locale, not across locales. `lavender` in English and `espliego` in Spanish
are different URLs under different prefixes. If two languages share a word, for example `rosa`, the two URLs can use the
same slug because they live under different subdirectories. Keep the primary slug stable after the site is public, because
it is the identity from which the localized items are created, and redirects from an old slug need one rule per locale; see
xref:cms/webflow/redirects-and-site-migration.adoc[Redirects and Site Migration].

=== Import, export and the API

The CSV tools work on one locale at a time: the editor chooses the locale in the Locale view and the CSV contains the
values of that locale; the *Item ID* column is what links the rows of different locales, so it must be preserved. The Data
API addresses locales with locale IDs: a site lists its locales with an ID for each, an item has both a `cmsLocaleId` and
the item ID, and a request names the locale it reads or writes. See xref:cms/webflow/csv-import-and-export.adoc[CSV Import
and Export] and xref:cms/webflow/data-api-ecommerce-and-localization.adoc[Data API: Ecommerce and Localization]. A sync
script such as `fernbrook-sync` therefore creates the English item first and then updates the Spanish and German items by
their locale IDs.

== Machine and AI translation in practice

Webflow offers several translation paths. They can be mixed per locale.

[cols="2,5,4",options="header"]
|===
| Path | How it works | Typical use at Fernbrook

| Manual
| An editor types the translation in the Locale view
| Legal text and the garden's welcome message

| Machine translation
| Built into Localize; translates a locale's content in one action and counts against the monthly allowance
| First pass of the Spanish site

| AI translation
| Triggered by the editor; tone and formality are set per language and exclusion rules protect fixed content
| Journal posts, with a reviewer

| Agentic translation
| Can run once or continuously as the primary content changes; continuous mode can overwrite human edits
| Only for locales that nobody edits by hand

| Translation app or TMS
| A Marketplace app or a translation management system connected through the Localize APIs
| A professional translation vendor for the German site
|===

Practical rules:

. Finish the primary locale first. Machine translation of content that is still changing wastes words from the allowance.
. Set the exclusion rules before translating, so Latin names, SKUs and codes are not touched.
. Translate in batches by page or collection and review before enabling publishing for the locale.
. Wait for translations to finish processing before reviewing; reviewing a half-translated page leads to false fixes.
. Check for untranslated text and for unintended fallbacks to the primary locale on a staging or preview URL.
. A custom translation glossary, which fixes how terms are translated, is an Enterprise feature; on other tiers keep a
  glossary in the team's documentation and use the exclusion rules.

The allowance is a *quota*, so plan for it: ten thousand words per month for a locale on the Essential tier is a few
dozen Journal posts but not a whole catalog in one month. A site that exceeds its allowance can continue to translate
manually or connect a TMS; there is no overage fee quoted on the pricing page, so check it before a large import.

Source: https://webflow.com/feature/localize[Webflow Localization feature page],
https://university.webflow.com/resources/checklists/webflow-localize-checklist[Webflow Localize checklist].

== Right-to-left languages

For Arabic, Persian, Hebrew, Urdu and Yiddish locales, Webflow automatically applies right-to-left display to the
published site, even before any text is translated. The root element of such a page carries the direction, and the browser
mirrors the inline direction of text, the order of flex rows and the sides of padding written as logical properties.

[source,html]
----
<html dir="rtl" lang="he">
  <body>
    <header class="site-header">
      <!-- the flex row of the header is mirrored by the browser -->
    </header>
    <p dir="ltr">Lavandula angustifolia</p> <!-- Latin name kept left-to-right -->
  </body>
</html>
----

To opt an element out of the automatic direction, give it a custom `dir` attribute with the value `ltr` or `auto`. This is
the right way to keep the Latin plant names of the Fernbrook collection readable in a Hebrew or Arabic page. Fernbrook does
not publish an RTL locale, but the checklist for one is short: review all mirrored layouts, replace directional icons such
as arrows, check that absolute positions use `left` and `right` correctly, and test forms. Custom attributes are described
in xref:cms/webflow/custom-attributes-and-javascript.adoc[Custom Attributes and JavaScript].

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961240752147[Localization overview].

== hreflang, the sitemap and localized SEO

Search engines need to know that `/plants/lavender`, `/es/plantas/espliego` and `/de/pflanzen/lavendel` are the same page
in three languages. The mechanism is `hreflang`, and Webflow generates it.

=== hreflang in the page head

With automatic hreflang enabled in *Site settings > Localization*, every published page carries `link` elements that list
its variants, including itself, in the `head`.

[source,html]
----
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en" href="https://www.fernbrook.example/plants/lavender">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="es" href="https://www.fernbrook.example/es/plantas/espliego">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="de" href="https://www.fernbrook.example/de/pflanzen/lavendel">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://www.fernbrook.example/plants/lavender">
<link rel="canonical" href="https://www.fernbrook.example/es/plantas/espliego">
----

The last line is the canonical of the Spanish page: each locale's page is canonical to itself, never to the primary one.
Only locales with publishing enabled are listed, so enabling the German subdirectory adds the German line to every page
that has a German variant at the next publish.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961235675155[Localized SEO and locale routing].

=== hreflang in the sitemap

When Webflow generates the sitemap, it includes the same alternate entries for static and dynamic pages. Search engines
need *one* signal, either the page-level tags or the sitemap entries, though both can be used.

[source,xml]
----
<url>
  <loc>https://www.fernbrook.example/plants/lavender</loc>
  <xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang="es" href="https://www.fernbrook.example/es/plantas/espliego"/>
  <xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang="de" href="https://www.fernbrook.example/de/pflanzen/lavendel"/>
</url>
----

A hand-written custom sitemap has to carry the alternates by hand. Whichever form is used, an entry for a page that is
drafted or not published in a locale is a broken promise, so check the sitemap after publishing.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961235675155[Localized SEO and locale routing].

=== Language codes and x-default

* Leave the *site-level* language code empty on a multilingual site; the site-level value overrides the codes of the
  locales and would label every page with one language.
* `x-default` points to the version for visitors whose language matches none of the others, normally the primary locale.
* If a CMS item is unpublished in the primary locale but published in two or more other locales, Webflow cannot choose a
  fallback and each version gets its own `x-default`. Keep a published default version in the primary locale to avoid this.

=== Localized metadata

Page title, description, Open Graph tags and the structured data of each locale should be written in that locale, not
copied. A Spanish page with an English title is a common defect in machine-translated sites. The cross-cutting practices of
SEO, including structured data and answer-engine optimization, are on xref:cms/webflow/seo-and-aeo.adoc[SEO and AEO];
JSON-LD embedded in a template page should use the locale's name and description fields.

After launch, submit the sitemap, check indexing of each locale in Google Search Console and add separate 301 redirect
rules for localized paths if old URLs existed.

== Per-locale publishing

Localization changes what *publish* means. The publish dialog lists the site's domains and, for each, the locales that will
be published.

* Each locale has publishing *enabled or disabled*. A disabled locale is not served, not listed in the sitemap, not in
  hreflang and not in the locale switcher.
* A normal site publish publishes every enabled locale together, so a translator who saves Spanish text and a designer who
  publishes the English site publish both.
* Webflow documents publishing at the level of a page and of a CMS item, and localized pages and items follow the same
  rule: publishing a page publishes all its locales, while an item can be draft in a locale and live in another.
* Locale-specific access on eligible Enterprise sites restricts *who can edit* a locale; it does not restrict who can
  publish the site.

A reasonable launch order for Fernbrook is: build and translate Spanish, keep it disabled, review it on the webflow.io
staging domain, then enable the subdirectory and publish. Details of staging, scheduled publishing and the publish dialog
are on xref:cms/webflow/publishing-and-scheduling.adoc[Publishing and Scheduling] and
xref:cms/webflow/domains-hosting-and-publishing-options.adoc[Domains, Hosting and Publishing Options].

== Interactions, code export and other limits

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Area | Limit or behaviour on 2026-10-28

| Ecommerce
| Products, categories and Ecommerce pages cannot be localized

| Classic Interactions
| Classic Interactions defined on the primary locale run in the secondary locales; building new interactions on the newer
GSAP-powered engine inside a secondary locale is not yet possible, as noted in
xref:cms/webflow/classic-interactions.adoc[Classic Interactions] and xref:cms/webflow/interactions-with-gsap.adoc[Interactions with GSAP]

| Code export
| The code export contains the primary content; it does not give a multi-language static site, so a localized site cannot
be moved out of Webflow by export alone; see xref:cms/webflow/code-export-and-portability.adoc[Code Export and Portability]

| DevLink
| Components exported with DevLink carry their primary content; texts for other languages must be supplied by the host
application

| Localized pages with Page branches
| Check the current behaviour before relying on a branch to stage a translation; see
xref:cms/webflow/page-branching-releases-and-approvals.adoc[Page Branching, Releases and Approvals]

| Primary locale
| Fixed after setup; there is no way to swap languages later

| Structure
| Elements, classes and fields are changed in the primary locale only

| Static page drafting per locale
| Enterprise only

| Custom translation glossary
| Enterprise only

| Automatic routing
| Advanced and Enterprise tiers, root homepage only
|===

Locale-aware custom code is possible. The `lang` attribute of the root element tells scripts which locale is active, which
is how Fernbrook's script that formats ticket prices chooses a number format.

[source,html]
----
<script>
  // Format a number with the locale of the page, falling back to English
  const lang = document.documentElement.lang || "en";
  const formatter = new Intl.NumberFormat(lang);
  document.querySelectorAll("[data-count]").forEach((el) => {
    el.textContent = formatter.format(Number(el.dataset.count));
  });
</script>
----

Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Intl/NumberFormat[MDN: Intl.NumberFormat].
For the way Webflow includes custom code, see xref:cms/webflow/custom-code-head-footer-and-embeds.adoc[Custom Code in the
Head, Footer and Embeds].

== Common mistakes and pitfalls

[cols="3,5",options="header"]
|===
| Mistake | Consequence and remedy

| Choosing the wrong primary locale
| It cannot be changed; decide before adding the first secondary locale

| Subdirectory that collides with a page or Collection slug
| Two things claim one path; pick a prefix that no page uses

| Typing links as plain URLs in rich text
| They are not localized; use the Page or Collection page link types

| Continuous agentic translation over hand-edited text
| Human translations are replaced; use one-time mode

| Machine translation published unreviewed
| Wrong terminology, mistranslated Latin names; review before enabling publishing

| Expecting automatic routing on deep links
| It applies only to the root homepage

| Language-only browser, country locale
| French is not routed to French (Canada); publish the language-only locale as well

| Deleting a primary item to hide it
| It deletes the item in every locale; use Draft instead

| Site-level language code set
| Overrides the locales' codes; leave it empty

| Forgetting that a drafted localized page is still linked
| Visitors reach a 404; review navigation

| Untranslated alt text
| Screen-reader users hear English on the Spanish site; review per locale

| Images that contain text
| Replace the image per locale

| Translating the slug in the primary locale after launch
| Breaks indexed URLs; redirect each locale

| Expecting the shop to be localized
| Ecommerce cannot be localized; plan separate handling
|===

== Security and privacy

* *Who can see an unpublished locale.* An unpublished locale is not served publicly, but its content is visible to everyone
  with Designer or Workspace access. Treat unfinished translations of confidential text accordingly.
* *Locale-specific access.* On eligible Enterprise sites, access can be limited to the people who edit a locale. It
  controls editing, not publishing, so a contractor given access to the German locale should still not be given publishing
  rights beyond what the roles page in xref:cms/webflow/plans-workspaces-seats-and-roles.adoc[Plans, Workspaces, Seats and
  Roles] allows.
* *Third-party translation.* Machine, AI and TMS translation send site text to a service. Do not send personal data, internal
  notes or unreleased commercial text; use exclusion rules for fixed content, and check the data-processing terms of any
  connected vendor.
* *Redirects based on language.* Automatic routing reads the browser language header only to choose a locale; it does not
  need cookies or personal data, but a cookie-consent policy should still cover any custom locale-remembering script.
* *Password-protected pages.* A page protected by the password feature stays protected in each locale that publishes it;
  Fernbrook leaves the Volunteers area unpublished in Spanish and German rather than translating it.

== History and where older material is wrong

Since its introduction Localization has gained localized styles and components, Enterprise-oriented controls such as locale-specific access and a translation glossary, tiered Localize add-ons with a monthly word allowance on the add-on cards, and AI and agentic translation. The details of each change are
tracked on xref:cms/webflow/product-changes-and-deprecations.adoc[Product Changes and Deprecations].

The requester-provided book, _Webflow by Example_, predates Localization, so its chapters on the CMS describe a
single-language site. Two statements in older material therefore no longer hold. First, multilingual sites were once built
with a separate Webflow project per language or with a third-party translation overlay such as a proxy service; Webflow's
built-in Localization replaces that approach for new work, with one project, one CMS and subdirectory routing. Second,
the book describes the Editor for content changes, which never supported localization and was retired in 2026; localized
content is edited in the Designer in edit mode. Where the book and the official documentation differ, this sub-section
follows the official documentation. See
xref:cms/webflow/where-older-books-and-tutorials-are-outdated.adoc[Where Older Books and Tutorials Are Outdated].

== A launch checklist for Fernbrook Gardens

. Decide the primary locale (English) and the locale list (Spanish, German); confirm the tier allows three locales in total
  and that the Ecommerce shop is out of scope.
. Pick subdirectories `/es` and `/de` and check that no page or Collection uses those slugs.
. Finish page structure, components and collection fields in the primary locale.
. Add the locales; keep both disabled.
. Set exclusion rules for Latin names, SKUs and codes; run machine translation of the static pages.
. Translate the Plants, Zones, Events and Journal items, with a reviewer per language.
. Localize page names, slugs, SEO titles, descriptions and Open Graph data.
. Replace images that contain text; translate alt text.
. Adapt styles for long German words; test every breakpoint.
. Build the locale switcher in `SiteHeader`; use Page and Collection page links for navigation.
. Confirm automatic hreflang is on and the site-level language code is empty.
. Review on the staging domain; check for untranslated text and fallbacks.
. Enable publishing for one locale at a time, publish, and verify URLs, forms and key journeys on the production domain.
. Submit the sitemap and watch indexing per locale; add redirects for localized legacy URLs.
