This page describes the AI features of Webflow as they were on 2026-10-28, the date on which their facts were checked
against the official documentation. Webflow is a software-as-a-service product with no version numbers, and its AI
features change faster than most of it, so everything below is dated; the
xref:cms/webflow/product-changes-and-deprecations.adoc[Product Changes and Deprecations] page tracks what has changed
since, and Webflow announces changes at https://webflow.com/updates. Plans are named by tier only (Starter, Basic,
Premium, Team and Enterprise for sites; Starter, Core, Growth, Freelancer, Agency and Enterprise for Workspaces) and this
page never quotes prices; the xref:cms/webflow/plans-workspaces-seats-and-roles.adoc[Plans, Workspaces, Seats and Roles]
page explains how the two kinds of plan relate.

The running example is *Fernbrook Gardens*, the fictitious botanical garden of this sub-section, with English as its
primary language and Spanish (`es`) and German (`de`) as secondary locales. In this page the garden's marketer uses the AI
features to draft a campaign, audit the SEO of the Plants pages, and build a small interactive widget, and the site
manager decides which of the results are good enough to publish.

== What "Webflow AI" covers

Webflow uses the name *Webflow AI* for a family of features that share one switch, one pool of credits and one set of
rules. As of 2026-10-28 the official documentation describes these pieces.

[cols="2,4,3",options="header"]
|===
| Feature | What it does | Where it is used

| AI site builder
| Creates a new multi-page site from a text description of the business and its goals: a proposed structure of pages and sections, content and a theme that you can adjust
| The Dashboard, when creating a site

| AI Assistant
| A chat panel in the Designer that answers questions about Webflow, helps with design changes, writes copy, generates CMS collections and content, and starts SEO and AEO audits
| The Designer

| AI code components
| Generates a reusable React-based component from a prompt, which you refine by conversation or in code
| The Designer, through the AI Assistant

| SEO and AEO audit
| Finds missing alt text, titles, descriptions and schema markup, sitewide or per page, and can draft the missing content
| The Audit panel and the AI Assistant

| Source by Webflow
| A new platform for marketing teams and AI agents that brings codebase, CMS, hosting and marketing tools into one environment; in a limited research preview
| Application through webflow.com/source

| Webflow MCP server
| Lets an external AI agent act on a site through the Model Context Protocol; documented separately
| xref:cms/webflow/mcp-server-and-ai-agents.adoc[MCP Server and AI Agents]
|===

Two things are deliberately not part of this page. The external agents that connect through the MCP server are a developer
topic with their own permissions and are covered on the page named in the table. And machine translation for
Localization is a Localization feature: disabling Webflow AI does not turn off automatic translations, as the help article
that explains the switch states. See xref:cms/webflow/localization.adoc[Localization].

== Turning Webflow AI on and off

Webflow AI is enabled per Workspace. The AI site builder, for example, can be used only when Webflow AI is enabled for the
Workspace, which the help article places under Workspace settings, General. The same article describes the control as a
single toggle labelled *Enable Webflow AI*, limited to Workspace owners and admins, and it lists the AI Assistant among the
features the toggle covers. On Enterprise, changes to the setting are recorded in the Workspace audit log, and custom roles
can restrict a permission to use Webflow AI for individual members. Webflow also announced that Webflow AI is enabled by
default on Enterprise.

For the Fernbrook team this means a governance decision before any prompt is typed: the owner decides whether the
Workspace allows AI at all, and on Enterprise the garden can allow it to the marketer and the designer while withholding
it from freelancers with limited seats. Roles and seats are explained in
xref:cms/webflow/plans-workspaces-seats-and-roles.adoc[Plans, Workspaces, Seats and Roles].

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/49786374523923[Enable or disable Webflow AI] and
https://webflow.com/updates/webflow-ai-enabled-by-default[Webflow AI is now enabled by default on Enterprise].

== The AI site builder

The AI site builder generates a first version of a site from a description. It is a starting point, not a finished
product, and the official article is explicit about its scope.

* *It is for new sites.* It can be used when creating a brand-new site or when editing a site that the AI site builder
  itself generated. It cannot be applied afterwards to a site that was built by hand.
* *It generates a limited number of pages.* The help article describes up to five pages at generation time. More pages
  can be added later in the normal way.
* *It needs a plan for anything bigger than a small static site.* Sites with more than two static pages need a paid Site
  plan or a paid Workspace plan.
* *It produces three things:* a site structure (pages and sections), content, and a theme that you can adjust.

The flow in the Dashboard is: choose *New site*, choose the AI site builder, describe the business and its goals, ask
Webflow to generate the site structure, refine the proposed pages and sections, and then let the builder produce the
design.

=== Example: a first prompt for Fernbrook Gardens

A good prompt states the business, the audience, the pages that are wanted and the tone, and it keeps facts that the model
cannot know (opening hours, prices, species names) out of the request so that a person adds them afterwards.

----
Fernbrook Gardens is a botanical garden with a plant collection, seasonal
events and a small seed shop. Visitors are families, hobby gardeners and
school groups. Create a site with Home, Visit, Plants, Events and About
pages. Tone: warm, factual and calm. Use a green and cream colour scheme.
Do not invent opening hours, ticket prices or plant names; leave clearly
marked placeholders for them.
----

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/38840145286035[Build a site with Webflow's AI site builder].

=== What to do with the result

The output is an ordinary Webflow site: its elements, classes and styles can be edited in the Designer like any other. The
review that follows is the real work, and it is the same for every AI result on this page; see
<<_reviewing_ai_output>>. For a site that will carry real content, the usual next steps are to replace placeholder text,
to rebuild the generated classes into the garden's naming system (see
xref:cms/webflow/class-naming-systems.adoc[Class Naming Systems]), to move repeated sections into components (see
xref:cms/webflow/components-props-slots-and-variants.adoc[Components, Props, Slots and Variants]) and to define the
garden's colours and type as variables (see xref:cms/webflow/variables-and-modes.adoc[Variables and Modes]).

== The AI Assistant

The AI Assistant is the chat panel in the Designer. Webflow describes it in its Help Center as a way to get contextual
help while working, and its capabilities have grown since its introduction. As of 2026-10-28 the official sources describe
five kinds of request.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Kind of request | What to expect

| Help
| Answers questions about Webflow, drawing on Webflow University and the Help Center, without leaving the Designer. The Help Center describes the help feature as built on a third-party language model that receives the question and a chat identifier without identifiable personal information

| Design changes
| Modifies the design of a page. The Help Center states that, while this capability is in beta, modifying page designs is limited to a small number of free templates and to sites created with the AI site builder, so it is not yet a general tool for every existing site

| Copy
| Writes or rewrites text for headings, paragraphs, buttons and metadata

| SEO and AEO audit
| Audits a page or the whole site for missing alt text, titles, descriptions and schema markup and can generate the missing content

| CMS generation
| Generates CMS collections and sample content from a description
|===

The Assistant is a drafting aid. It acts on the canvas of the person who asked, it respects that person's permissions, and
every change it makes is a normal Designer change that can be undone, reviewed in a branch or discarded. A site that uses
page branching (see xref:cms/webflow/page-branching-releases-and-approvals.adoc[Page Branching, Releases and Approvals])
can have AI work reviewed in a branch before it reaches the live site.

=== Example: asking for copy in two locales

The garden's marketer wants a short banner for the autumn plant sale. The prompt gives the voice and the constraints and
asks for one version per locale. A person who reads Spanish and German must still approve the translations, because the
Assistant's output is a draft; the garden's real translations are managed in
xref:cms/webflow/localization.adoc[Localization].

----
Write a 12-word banner headline and a 25-word subheading for the Fernbrook
Gardens autumn plant sale. Voice: warm and factual, no exclamation marks.
Do not mention discounts or dates. Give the English, Spanish and German
versions as three labelled blocks.
----

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/articles/33961195523603[Get contextual help using Webflow with the Webflow AI Assistant].

=== Example: generating a CMS collection

CMS generation is best used to scaffold a collection that a person then reviews field by field. A prompt that names the
fields and their types produces a structure that matches the garden's content model instead of a guess.

----
Create a collection called Plants with these fields: Name (plain text),
Slug, Latin name (plain text), Light (option: full sun, partial shade,
shade), Blooms in (multi-option: spring, summer, autumn, winter), Photo
(image) and Description (rich text). Add five sample items for plants
that grow in temperate gardens.
----

Source: https://webflow.com/updates/webflow-ai-enabled-by-default[Webflow AI is now enabled by default on Enterprise]
(describes copy and CMS collection generation) and, for the resulting structure,
xref:cms/webflow/collections-and-field-types.adoc[Collections and Field Types].

The generated sample items are placeholders. They are useful to test a template page and a Collection list, and they
must be deleted or replaced before publishing, because published fiction is still published. The botanical details in
particular must be checked by the garden's horticulturists.

== SEO and AEO audits

Webflow's AI audit looks for the pieces of metadata that search engines and AI answer engines read. As of 2026-10-28 the
official documentation says it checks for missing image alt text, meta titles, meta descriptions and schema markup, that
it can run on the whole site or on a single page, and that Webflow AI can then draft the missing content. The Help Center
adds that a paid Workspace or Site plan is required for the AI-powered accessibility, SEO and AEO features.

Two products share the letters AEO and are easy to confuse.

* The *audit* is part of Webflow AI inside the Designer and fixes metadata on your pages.
* *Webflow AEO* is a separate product that measures and improves how a brand appears in AI search results. The Help Center
  describes it as available on the Team and Enterprise plans.

The page xref:cms/webflow/seo-and-aeo.adoc[SEO and AEO] explains both and the metadata they depend on. The accessibility
side of the Audit panel is covered in xref:cms/webflow/accessibility.adoc[Accessibility].

=== Example: auditing the Plants pages

----
Audit the Plants collection template page and list every missing alt text,
meta title, meta description and schema property. For each missing item
propose a draft that uses the plant's Name, Latin name and Light fields
through dynamic references instead of fixed text.
----

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/45696502240147-Audit-and-improve-SEO-AEO-with-Webflow-AI[Audit and improve SEO and AEO with Webflow AI].

A template page generates one page per item, so the best fix is a dynamic one: a meta description that combines fields
works for hundreds of plants, whereas a generated fixed sentence would repeat on every page and be flagged as duplicate
content. Review the drafts with that in mind.

== AI code components

AI code components let a person describe an interactive element in plain language and get a React-based component that is
rendered on the canvas. Webflow announced the feature as generally available on 2026-04-30; the announcement says that
the component can be refined through conversation or in code before it is placed in a site, and that publishing a site
that uses an AI code component needs a paid Workspace plan or a Site plan with a CMS or higher tier (which, after the May
2026 plan change, corresponds to Premium or above).

The Help Center article for the feature fixes the prerequisites. To create and use AI code components a person needs the
*Site manager* or *Designer* site role and Webflow AI enabled for the Workspace. It also names the two kinds of code
component that Webflow has.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| | AI code components | DevLink code components

| Created by
| A prompt to the AI Assistant inside the Designer
| A developer, in an external codebase, with React and the Webflow CLI

| Source of the code
| Generated, then editable in the Designer
| Written and versioned by the team in git

| Review and release
| Reviewed by the person who accepts the result
| Normal code review and CI

| Documented in
| This page
| xref:cms/webflow/code-components.adoc[Code Components]
|===

Because the generated code runs on the published page, the security expectations of any third-party script apply. Read the
generated code before publishing it, check which external requests it makes, and do not accept a component that loads
scripts from unknown origins.

=== Example: a prompt for a bloom calendar

----
Create a Bloom Calendar component for Fernbrook Gardens. It shows twelve
month tiles; a visitor can select a month and see the names of the plants
that bloom then. Take the month list and plants from props, not from
hard-coded text. Make it keyboard accessible, with visible focus and a
text alternative for any colour coding. Do not make network requests.
----

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/51168990228499-Build-AI-code-components[Build AI code components].

The prompt states the requirements that matter in review: data comes from props so that an editor can change it, keyboard
access is required, and network requests are forbidden. A prompt that omits them gets a component that looks right and
fails in use.

=== What the code looks like after review

The Help Center describes AI code components as one of two kinds of code components, the other being the kind that a
developer declares in an external project. When the garden's developers decide that a generated component is worth
keeping, they can rebuild it in their own codebase, where it is declared with `declareComponent` and gets reviewed like
any other code. The following snippet shows that shape for the same Bloom Calendar: a React component declared with
typed props that an editor sees in the Designer. It is a hand-written illustration of the developer path, not the literal
output of the AI feature.

[source,tsx]
----
// BloomCalendar.webflow.tsx
import { declareComponent } from '@webflow/react';
import { props } from '@webflow/data-types';
import { BloomCalendar } from './BloomCalendar';

export default declareComponent(BloomCalendar, {
  name: 'Bloom Calendar',
  description: 'Twelve month tiles that list the plants blooming in the selected month',
  group: 'Fernbrook Gardens',
  props: {
    heading: props.Text({
      name: 'Heading',
      defaultValue: 'What blooms when'
    }),
    layout: props.Variant({
      name: 'Layout',
      options: ['compact', 'spacious'],
      defaultValue: 'compact'
    })
  }
});
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/code-components/reference/hooks/declareComponent[declareComponent reference].

The declaration lives in a file named like `BloomCalendar.webflow.tsx`, and the imports come from `@webflow/react` and
`@webflow/data-types`. Prop names that start with `+__wf_+` are reserved by Webflow. The complete workflow, including the
CLI, shared libraries and versions, is explained in xref:cms/webflow/code-components.adoc[Code Components].

== Source by Webflow

At Webflow Conf 2026 (1 to 3 September 2026) Webflow announced *Source by Webflow*. As of 2026-10-28 the official
announcement describes it as a new platform, in a limited research preview, in which marketers, designers, developers and
AI agents work in one shared environment that brings together a codebase, the CMS, hosting and the marketing technology
stack, including sites already built in Webflow. Webflow says it will enrol selected agency partners and customers in
phases and that interested teams apply through a form on webflow.com/source. The announcement gives no public release
date and no price.

Practically, Source is not something the Fernbrook team can switch on. A research preview is an invitation-based programme
with no commitment to ship in its present form, so the garden's plans (including the
xref:cms/webflow/comparison-and-decision-guide.adoc[Comparison and Decision Guide]) are built on the Designer, the CMS and
the Data API, which exist today. The team can apply to the preview and revisit the decision when Webflow publishes
documentation for it.

Source: https://webflow.com/updates/introducing-source-by-webflow[Introducing Source by Webflow].

== AI credits

Most AI actions consume *AI credits*. Credits are included in the Workspace plan: since the May 2026 plan change every
Workspace plan includes a credit allowance, and the limits have been enforced since 2026-06-29. The Help Center article on
AI credits is the authority for the details.

=== Allowances per Workspace plan

As of 2026-10-28 the AI credits article lists these allowances. The numbers are credits, not currency.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Workspace plan | Credits | Period

| Starter
| 200
| Per month

| Core and Freelancer
| 300
| Per month

| Growth and Agency
| 400
| Per month

| Team
| 100,000
| Per year

| Enterprise
| Custom amount
| Per agreement
|===

Webflow can change these values, so check the article before budgeting. Credits are pooled at the Workspace level: they
can be used on any site in the Workspace, including sites on the free Starter Site plan, and they are not assigned to a
single site or a single person.

=== Reset rules

* Self-serve Workspace plans (Starter, Core, Growth, Freelancer and Agency) receive their allowance every month, and the
  allowance resets at the start of the next period.
* Team and Enterprise plans receive a yearly allowance and reset yearly.
* Unused credits expire at the end of each period. They do not roll over.

A practical consequence for the garden: a marketer who leaves a month's credits unused gains nothing, so work that
benefits from AI (the autumn campaign copy, the SEO audit) is best done in the month when the credits are available.

=== Enforcement and what happens at zero

Webflow began enforcing the credit limits on 2026-06-29. When a Workspace has used its allowance, AI actions stop until the
next reset or until credits are added. Reaching the limit does not trigger an automatic upgrade or a surprise charge; a
Workspace that needs more can buy an add-on credit pack. Everything else in the Workspace keeps working: the Designer, the
CMS, publishing and hosting do not depend on credits.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart LR
  A[Prompt to AI feature] --> B{Credits left in the Workspace?}
  B -- Yes --> C[Draft generated]
  B -- No --> D[Wait for reset or add a credit pack]
  C --> E[Human review: facts, accessibility, brand, privacy]
  E -- Accept --> F[Edit and publish]
  E -- Reject --> G[Discard or reprompt]
  G --> A
----

The diagram shows the loop the garden's team follows: each rejected draft costs another prompt, so a precise first prompt
saves credits as well as time.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/51060121155347[Webflow AI credits],
https://webflow.com/updates/ai-credits[AI credits update] and
https://webflow.com/blog/simplified-plans-and-updated-pricing-2026[We're updating our pricing and simplifying our plans].

=== Planning the garden's credits

The Fernbrook Gardens Workspace is on a self-serve plan with three people using AI: the marketer, the designer and the
developer. A simple budget keeps the pool from being drained by experiments.

* Reserve credits at the start of the month for the work that has a deadline, such as campaign copy.
* Do exploratory prompts (try ten layouts) in a separate scratch site, so they are easy to discard, and expect them to use
  the same pool.
* Write one precise prompt with the constraints, as in the examples above, instead of many vague ones.
* Check the remaining balance before a large generation job, for example generating a whole collection.

The article explains how credits are measured and where the balance is shown. Because the measure can change, the team
treats the Workspace's own usage display as the source of truth and not a number copied into a wiki.

== Reviewing AI output

AI features produce drafts. Publishing a draft makes it the garden's own statement, so the person who accepts a result is
responsible for it. The review has four parts, and each has a place in Webflow where it can be done.

[cols="2,4,3",options="header"]
|===
| Check | What to look for | Where in Webflow

| Facts
| Opening hours, prices, species names, dates and claims; the model can state invented details with confidence
| Reading the page against the garden's source of truth

| Accessibility
| Heading order, alt text that describes the picture, contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, text alternatives to colour, reduced motion
| The Audit panel and manual keyboard testing; see xref:cms/webflow/accessibility.adoc[Accessibility]

| Brand
| Voice, colours, type and tone against the garden's style; classes and variables that follow the naming system
| Style panel, variables and the shared library; see xref:cms/webflow/building-a-design-system.adoc[Building a Design System]

| Technical quality
| Duplicate or meaningless class names, hard-coded text that should be a field or a prop, external requests in generated code
| Navigator, Style panel and, for code components, the code editor
|===

=== Accessibility specifically

Generated alt text is often a literal description such as "a flower" or "an image of plants". Good alt text states what the
image contributes to the page. Generated components can omit focus styles or use colour alone to carry meaning. The
Audit panel finds some problems, but it cannot judge whether alt text is meaningful or whether a widget works with a
keyboard, so run those checks by hand with the keyboard and a screen reader before publishing.

=== Localized output

If the generated copy is published in Spanish or German, a speaker of the language reviews it. Machine-written text can be
fluent and still wrong for a botanical audience, for example in the common names of plants, which vary by region. Treat AI
copy as a first draft in every locale; see xref:cms/webflow/localization.adoc[Localization].

== Privacy and security

* *Data handling.* Webflow states in its description of its approach to AI that it does not use customer data to train
  generative AI models and that it has contracts with the vendors that process data for its AI features forbidding them to
  train their own models on it. For the help feature of the AI Assistant, the Help Center says the third-party model
  receives the question and a chat identifier that contains no identifiable personal information.
* *Do not paste secrets or personal data into prompts.* A prompt is data sent to a service. Keep visitor data, staff
  records and credentials out of it. The Fernbrook prompts above contain only public information.
* *Governance.* The Workspace owner or an admin decides whether Webflow AI is enabled; on Enterprise the change is
  audited and custom roles can restrict who may use it.
* *Generated code is code.* An AI code component runs in visitors' browsers with the same power as any script on the page.
  Review it, remove network requests that are not needed and keep it out of pages that handle payment or login unless it
  has been through the same review as other code. Webflow Optimize and machine translation use machine learning that is
  separate from the Webflow AI switch.
* *Agents are different.* An external agent that edits a site through the MCP server has its own OAuth scopes and site
  access. See xref:cms/webflow/mcp-server-and-ai-agents.adoc[MCP Server and AI Agents].

Source: https://webflow.com/de/ai/our-approach[Webflow AI: Our approach] and
https://help.webflow.com/hc/articles/33961195523603[Get contextual help using Webflow with the Webflow AI Assistant].

== Pitfalls

* *Treating the AI site builder as a way to rebuild an existing site.* It works for new sites and for sites it created, so
  an existing hand-built site cannot be regenerated with it.
* *Expecting design changes on every site.* While the capability is in beta, the Help Center limits page-design changes to
  a small number of free templates and AI-site-builder sites. A prompt that asks for a redesign of another site does not
  have the expected effect.
* *Confusing the two meanings of AEO.* The Designer audit fixes metadata; Webflow AEO is a separate product on Team and
  Enterprise.
* *Forgetting that credits expire.* Unused credits do not roll over, and the pool is shared by the whole Workspace.
* *Believing the credit table is permanent.* Allowances are plan data that Webflow can change; confirm them in the article.
* *Publishing placeholder content.* Generated sample items and invented facts end up on live pages if nobody removes them.
* *Fixed text on template pages.* A generated meta description repeated on every Plants page creates duplicates; use
  dynamic fields.
* *Assuming a generated component is accessible.* Test it with a keyboard.
* *Planning around Source.* It is a limited research preview with no release date; do not schedule a migration against it.
* *Using the book as a guide.* See the next section.

== Corrections to older material

The book on which parts of this sub-section were compared, Webflow by Example (Packt, 2021), predates every AI feature
described here. It has no AI site builder, AI Assistant, AI code components or credits, and its plan and Editor guidance
is also outdated. For AI, the official documentation is the only source. Many online tutorials from 2023 and 2024 describe
earlier AI behaviour, before credits were enforced, before AI code components became generally available and before the
May 2026 plans put credits into every Workspace plan. The page
xref:cms/webflow/where-older-books-and-tutorials-are-outdated.adoc[Where Older Books and Tutorials Are Outdated]
collects these differences.
