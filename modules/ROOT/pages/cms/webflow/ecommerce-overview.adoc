This page describes Webflow Ecommerce as it was on 2026-10-28, the date on which its facts were checked against the
official Help Center, the pricing page and the Data API reference. Webflow is a software-as-a-service product without
version numbers, so every statement below is dated and the xref:cms/webflow/product-changes-and-deprecations.adoc[Product
Changes and Deprecations] page tracks later changes. Ecommerce is sold as a *Site plan* in three tiers named *Standard*,
*Plus* and *Advanced*; this page names the tiers and their limits and never quotes prices, which are on the pricing page
and change. Ecommerce plans were not changed by the May 2026 plan update that merged the CMS and Business plans into
Premium; the Standard, Plus and Advanced names and their item limits are the ones on the pricing page on the check date.
The xref:cms/webflow/plans-workspaces-seats-and-roles.adoc[Plans, Workspaces, Seats and Roles] page explains how Site
plans, Workspace plans and add-ons relate.

The running example is *Fernbrook Gardens*, the fictitious botanical garden of this sub-section. Besides the Plants
collection and the Garden Journal, the garden runs a small *Fernbrook Gardens Shop* that sells seed packets, hand tools,
a gift membership and a printed garden guide. The site has Spanish (`es`) and German (`de`) locales for content, and the
shop is the one area that stays English-only, for a reason explained in <<_what_does_not_work_with_localization_code_export_and_devlink>>.

== What Ecommerce is and who it is for

Webflow Ecommerce adds a store to a Webflow site. It is not a separate product with its own admin: it is a set of
system Collections (products, SKUs, categories), a set of elements (Add to cart, Cart, Checkout form, Order summary), a set
of utility pages (Checkout, Order Confirmation, and the PayPal checkout), and a back-office (the Ecommerce panel in the
Designer and the Ecommerce area of the Dashboard). Because products are CMS-style items, everything learned about the CMS
applies: fields, template pages, Collection lists, filters and sorting, and the way a site is published. The shop pages
are ordinary Webflow pages that the designer styles with classes, and the transaction itself is handled by a payment
provider.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Person | What they do with Ecommerce

| Site owner
| Chooses the Ecommerce tier, connects Stripe or PayPal, decides on taxes and shipping, and watches the transaction fee on
the Standard tier

| Designer
| Builds the product template page, the product Collection lists, the cart and the checkout pages, and styles them at every
breakpoint

| Content editor
| Creates products, variants, categories, images and descriptions, and updates inventory; see
xref:cms/webflow/content-editing-on-the-canvas.adoc[Content Editing on the Canvas]

| Shop operator
| Reviews orders, fulfils them, adds tracking information, issues refunds and answers disputes

| Marketer
| Sets product SEO titles and descriptions, adds Open Graph data and structured data (see
xref:cms/webflow/seo-and-aeo.adoc[SEO and AEO]), and measures the funnel

| Developer
| Reads orders and writes products, SKUs and inventory with the Data API (see
xref:cms/webflow/data-api-ecommerce-and-localization.adoc[Data API: Ecommerce and Localization]), subscribes to order
and inventory webhooks, and adds custom code to the shop pages
|===

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961361368979[Connect a payment provider], https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/products/list[Data API: List Products and SKUs].

== Ecommerce plans and item limits

An Ecommerce Site plan includes the CMS and the standard Site-plan features (custom domain, hosting, forms, site search)
and adds the ability to accept payments, with a ceiling on the number of Ecommerce items. On 2026-10-28 the pricing page
lists these tiers:

[cols="1,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Tier | Ecommerce item limit | Notes

| Standard
| 500 items
| Adds a transaction fee of 2% on each sale on top of the fees of the payment provider

| Plus
| 5,000 items
| No Webflow transaction fee

| Advanced
| 15,000 items
| No Webflow transaction fee; the tier for the largest catalogs
|===

Four rules matter when sizing a shop.

* *The limit counts items, not products.* According to the Help Center article on product options, each product variant
  counts toward the item limit of the plan, so a catalog of 100 products with five variants each uses several times more of the
  allowance than 100 products with one variant. Categories are stored in their own Collection and are items too.
* *Variants are capped per product.* A product can have up to three option sets (for example, Packet size, Packaging and
  Pollinator mix) and up to 50 variants in total. A larger matrix needs several products, or a shop built on another
  platform; the xref:ecommerce/comparison.adoc[E-commerce comparison] page of this documentation set compares such
  platforms.
* *Ecommerce plans still follow the Site-plan rules.* An Ecommerce Site plan is a Site plan, so it is bought per site,
  and a Workspace plan is still needed for building features such as staging, code export and shared libraries.
* *The store can be designed before it is bought.* A site can be designed with Ecommerce enabled before any plan is bought; payments
  need an Ecommerce Site plan and checkout turned on. A site without Ecommerce enabled answers Data API ecommerce calls
  with the status code 409.

=== The Standard transaction fee

The Standard tier charges a transaction fee of 2% of each sale. It is charged by Webflow and is separate from the fees
that Stripe or PayPal charge for processing the payment. Plus and Advanced charge no Webflow transaction fee. The order
object returned by the Data API reports it in the fields `applicationFee` (the fee assessed by the platform), `netAmount`
(what remains after the application fee) and `customerPaid` (the total paid by the customer), so a reconciliation script
can check the fee against the tier.

A rough rule for Fernbrook Gardens: the garden expects a few hundred orders in spring, mostly seed packets of small value.
On Standard, the 2% fee applies to every order on top of the card fee, and the 500-item limit is enough for about twenty
products with a handful of variants each. If the garden adds a printed guide in four formats and several tool sets, the
variant count grows and Plus becomes the sensible tier, both for the larger limit and because it removes the Webflow fee.

Source: https://webflow.com/pricing[Webflow pricing], https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/orders/list[Data API: List Orders].

== Products, SKUs and variants

The Webflow product model has three layers.

[cols="1,5",options="header"]
|===
| Layer | Meaning

| Product
| What the visitor sees on a product page: name, slug, description, images, category, whether it needs shipping, tax
category and the option sets (`sku-properties`)

| SKU
| One purchasable combination of options. It carries the price, an optional compare-at price, the SKU code, the main image,
weight and dimensions for shipping, and inventory

| Variant
| The Designer term for a SKU other than the default: a product with the option sets Packet size (Small and Large) has a
SKU for each combination
|===

Every product has a *default SKU*, the combination of the first value of each option set. A product without options has
exactly one SKU. Products have a product type that decides which fields appear: physical, digital, a service, or an
*Advanced* type that shows every product and SKU field; creating SKUs through the API sets the product type to Advanced
so that all fields are shown in the Designer. Digital products carry download files, and subscription billing is a
property of the SKU (`ec-sku-billing-method` is `one-time` or `subscription`, and a subscription SKU has an
`ec-sku-subscription-plan` with an interval of day, week, month or year, a frequency and an optional trial).

Fernbrook Gardens sells four products:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Product | Option sets | Notes

| Foxglove seed packet
| Packet size: Small, Large
| Two SKUs; stock tracked per SKU

| Hand trowel
| None
| One SKU; weight set for shipping

| Gift membership
| Duration: 6 months, 12 months
| Digital, shippable false; a service sold through the shop

| Printed garden guide
| Language: English, Spanish, German
| Three SKUs; the language options are product data, not locales
|===

The last row is worth a remark: the three language editions of the printed guide are *variants*, not localized content.
Localization cannot translate Ecommerce items, so a shop that must be multilingual treats languages as variants or runs
separate sites; see <<_what_does_not_work_with_localization_code_export_and_devlink>>.

=== Categories

Categories group products. They are a system Collection with a name, a slug and optional extra fields, a product can belong
to several categories, and a *category template page* lists the products in each category with a Collection list bound to
the Products Collection and filtered by the category. Categories are the usual navigation of a shop (Seeds, Tools,
Memberships, Books) and, like any template page, they are the place for the category SEO title and description. See
xref:cms/webflow/collection-template-pages.adoc[Collection Template Pages] and
xref:cms/webflow/collection-lists.adoc[Collection Lists] for the shared mechanics.

=== Product pages in the Designer

A *product template page* is a template page for the Products Collection. It holds the product name, images, price,
description, the Add to cart form with its option dropdowns, and optional related-products lists. The design rules of the
rest of the CMS apply: the price uses a Collection-bound text element, conditional visibility hides the compare-at price
when it is empty (see xref:cms/webflow/conditional-visibility-and-dynamic-embeds.adoc[Conditional Visibility and Dynamic
Embeds]), and the page is tested at every breakpoint (see
xref:cms/webflow/responsive-design-and-breakpoints.adoc[Responsive Design and Breakpoints]).

Product import and export use a CSV file. The export contains all products with their variants and categories, including
archived items; import creates products, and updating existing Ecommerce items through CSV is not supported. The
xref:cms/webflow/csv-import-and-export.adoc[CSV Import and Export] page covers the format, and the Data API is the route
for bulk updates.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961334531347[Create product options and variants], https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/products/create[Data API: Create Product and SKU].

== Cart and checkout customization

Webflow customizes the shopping experience with elements and utility pages rather than a separate theme.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Piece | What it is

| Add to cart
| A form element with option dropdowns, a quantity field and a button. It sits on a product template page or in any
Collection list bound to the Products Collection

| Cart
| A cart icon with a count, plus either a dropdown, a modal or a right-hand sidebar. The Cart element settings choose the
type, the opening and closing animation (easing and duration) and the number of items in the preview

| Checkout page
| A utility page, reorderable and restylable. It contains the customer information, shipping address, shipping method,
payment and order summary blocks

| Checkout (PayPal)
| A second checkout utility page, relevant only if PayPal is enabled

| Order Confirmation
| The page shown after payment, with the order summary and a link back to the shop

| Order and email templates
| The emails sent to customers and the store owner, with a logo and a colour
|===

The designer may restyle and rearrange these pages, but required form components must stay. The Help Center warns that
deleting or hiding a component that contains a required field can break the checkout form. If the checkout page becomes
unusable, a new Checkout form element can be dropped on the page to restore the default layout.

=== What Webflow generates

Webflow outputs the Add to cart form and the Cart as ordinary HTML with Webflow classes and `data-node-type` attributes,
and the behaviour is provided by the `webflow.js` runtime. The markup below is abbreviated and illustrative; the exact
output changes with the elements and settings chosen and is not a stable interface.

[source,html]
----
<form data-node-type="commerce-add-to-cart-form" data-commerce-sku-id="SKU_ID_PLACEHOLDER"
      class="w-commerce-commerceaddtocartform default-state">
  <label for="quantity-1">Quantity</label>
  <input type="number" pattern="^[0-9]+$" inputmode="numeric" id="quantity-1" name="commerce-add-to-cart-quantity-input"
         min="1" class="w-commerce-commerceaddtocartquantityinput" value="1">
  <input type="submit" data-node-type="cart-add-to-cart-button" class="w-commerce-commerceaddtocartbutton"
         value="Add to cart">
  <div data-node-type="commerce-add-to-cart-out-of-stock" class="w-commerce-commerceaddtocartoutofstock">
    <div>This product is out of stock.</div>
  </div>
</form>

<div data-node-type="commerce-cart-wrapper" data-open-product="" data-wf-cart-type="rightSidebar"
     data-wf-cart-query="" data-wf-page-link-href-prefix="" class="w-commerce-commercecartwrapper">
  <a href="#" data-node-type="commerce-cart-open-link" class="w-commerce-commercecartopenlink">
    <div>Cart</div>
    <div class="w-commerce-commercecartopenlinkcount">0</div>
  </a>
  <div data-node-type="commerce-cart-container-wrapper" class="w-commerce-commercecartcontainerwrapper">
    <form data-node-type="commerce-cart-form" class="w-commerce-commercecartform">
      <a href="/checkout" value="Continue to Checkout" data-node-type="cart-checkout-button"
         class="w-commerce-commercecartcheckoutbutton">Continue to Checkout</a>
    </form>
  </div>
</div>
----

The classes starting with `w-commerce-` carry the default CSS that Webflow ships in its shared stylesheet; the designer's own classes
(for example `shop-button`) are generated next to them.

[source,css]
----
.shop-button {
  background-color: #2f5d3a;
  color: #fff;
  padding: 0.75rem 1.5rem;
  border-radius: 4px;
}

.shop-button:hover {
  background-color: #244a2e;
}

@media screen and (max-width: 479px) {
  .shop-button { width: 100%; }
}
----

Cart and checkout behaviour cannot be replaced by arbitrary custom code: the cart state, the totals and the payment form
belong to Webflow. Custom code can add behaviour *around* them, for example analytics events when a visitor clicks an
Add to cart button. Place such code in the page's custom code or in Site settings, and wrap it so that a missing element
does not throw.

[source,javascript]
----
document.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
  var button = event.target.closest('.w-commerce-commerceaddtocartbutton');
  if (!button) return;
  window.dataLayer = window.dataLayer || [];
  window.dataLayer.push({ event: 'fernbrook_add_to_cart' });
});
----

The snippet reads no personal data. A cookie-consent policy still has to cover any analytics tool that receives the event;
see xref:cms/webflow/custom-code-head-footer-and-embeds.adoc[Custom Code in the Head, Footer and Embeds].

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961364474643[Customize the shopping cart and checkout experience].

== Stripe and PayPal

Webflow Ecommerce supports two payment providers, *Stripe* and *PayPal*, and a site can connect both. The provider is
connected in *Site settings*, *Ecommerce*, *Payment* by choosing *Connect Stripe* or *Connect PayPal* and then signing in
to an existing account or creating one. Connecting a provider does not start sales: the site also needs an Ecommerce Site
plan and checkout switched on.

* *Stripe* handles card payments and web payments such as Apple Pay and Google Pay.
* *PayPal* handles PayPal payments and card payments through PayPal, and the Help Center lists PayPal Pay Later among its
  methods. Merchant accounts in Turkey are not supported by the Webflow PayPal integration.
* *Availability.* Neither provider is offered in every country. For an unsupported country, the Help Center points to
  Stripe Atlas as a way to set up a United States company that can accept payments through Stripe.
* *Fees.* Each provider charges its own fee per transaction. Together with the 2% fee of the Standard tier, a Standard shop
  can pay two fees on a sale.
* *Other providers are not available.* A merchant that needs another gateway uses a third-party commerce platform or a hosted
  checkout; compare the options on the xref:ecommerce/comparison.adoc[E-commerce comparison] page.

Payment details are entered on the provider's secure components or redirected pages, so card numbers never reach the
Fernbrook Gardens site code. Webflow's order object keeps only provider identifiers (`stripeDetails`, `paypalDetails`) and
the last digits and brand of the card (`stripeCard`), which is enough to look up a payment in the provider dashboard.

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961361368979[Connect a payment provider].

== Taxes and shipping

*Taxes.* The Ecommerce settings contain a Taxes area. Taxes can be set manually per country or region, or calculated
automatically through Stripe Tax when Stripe is the payment provider. Each product has a tax category field
(`tax-category`) used by automatic calculation. A shop must register for tax in each jurisdiction that requires it:
automatic calculation depends on the registrations configured in the provider account, as the Stripe documentation
describes. Check the tax settings and a test receipt before launching in a new country.

*Shipping.* Shipping is configured per *shipping zone*, a set of countries or regions, and each zone has one or more
shipping methods. The method types in the Help Center article are price-based (rates by order total), quantity-based
(rates by total item count), weight-based (rates by total weight) and flat-rate (one amount for the whole order or per
item, or free). The weight of each SKU therefore has to be entered correctly when weight-based rates are used. The
`shippable` field of a product turns shipping off for digital items, which is how the gift membership of Fernbrook Gardens
is sold without a shipping address.

[cols="2,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| Fernbrook zone | Method | Rule

| Domestic
| Weight-based
| Seed packets ship under a small rate; the tools use a higher weight band

| Europe
| Flat rate
| One amount per order, so that the Spanish and German visitors see a single predictable cost

| Digital items
| None
| The product is not shippable and no address is requested
|===

Source: https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961431933843[Configure shipping methods for different shipping zones], https://docs.stripe.com/tax/set-up[Stripe Tax: Set up].

== Orders and inventory

When a visitor pays, Webflow creates an *order*. The Orders area of the Dashboard and the Ecommerce panel list orders with
their status, and the Data API exposes the same information. The statuses are `pending`, `unfulfilled`, `fulfilled`,
`refunded`, `disputed` and `dispute-lost`.

[mermaid]
....
flowchart LR
    V["Visitor"] --> C["Cart and Checkout page"]
    C --> P["Stripe or PayPal"]
    P -->|paid| O["Order: unfulfilled"]
    P -->|not completed| N["Order: pending"]
    O --> F["Fulfilled, with tracking"]
    O --> R["Refunded"]
    F --> D["Disputed"]
    D --> L["Dispute lost"]
    O --> I["Inventory decreases"]
....

The operator can fulfil an order, optionally sending the Order Fulfilled email, add a shipping provider and tracking
information, refund it fully or partly through the payment provider, and answer a dispute in the provider dashboard.
Customer emails (confirmation, fulfilment and refund) are templates edited in the Ecommerce settings.

*Inventory* is tracked per SKU. A SKU is either `infinite` or `finite`; for a finite SKU the quantity decreases as orders are
placed, and a SKU that reaches zero shows the out-of-stock state of the Add to cart form. The Data API can read the
quantity and update it either absolutely (`quantity`) or incrementally (`updateQuantity`, which may be negative), and the
`ecomm_inventory_changed` webhook announces a change.

=== Reading and changing orders and inventory with the Data API

The examples use a *Developer workspace* site with Ecommerce enabled, because the ecommerce endpoints answer 409 on a site
without it. The token comes from the environment; the value shown is a placeholder.

[source,bash]
----
export WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN="your-site-token-with-ecommerce-read-and-write"
export WEBFLOW_SITE_ID="your-site-id"

# List the products and their SKUs (scope ecommerce:read)
curl --request GET \
  --url "https://api.webflow.com/v2/sites/${WEBFLOW_SITE_ID}/products?limit=100" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer ${WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN}" \
  --header "accept: application/json"
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/products/list[Data API: List Products and SKUs].

The response contains an `items` array; each element has a `product` object with `fieldData` (name, slug, description,
`shippable`, `sku-properties`, `category`, `tax-category`, `default-sku`, `ec-product-type`) and a `skus` array whose
`fieldData` holds the price, the compare-at price and the `sku` code. The `limit`, `offset` and `total` values of the
pagination object count products only, never SKUs, and `limit` has a maximum of 100.

[source,json]
----
{
  "product": {
    "fieldData": {
      "name": "Foxglove seed packet",
      "slug": "foxglove-seed-packet",
      "description": "Digitalis purpurea seeds from the Fernbrook meadow.",
      "shippable": true,
      "sku-properties": [
        {
          "id": "packet-size",
          "name": "Packet size",
          "enum": [
            { "id": "small", "name": "Small", "slug": "small" },
            { "id": "large", "name": "Large", "slug": "large" }
          ]
        }
      ]
    }
  },
  "sku": {
    "fieldData": {
      "name": "Foxglove seed packet - Small",
      "slug": "foxglove-seed-packet-small",
      "price": { "value": 399, "unit": "USD", "currency": "USD" },
      "sku": "FG-FOX-S"
    }
  },
  "publishStatus": "staging"
}
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/products/create[Data API: Create Product and SKU].

The body above is posted to the same `products` path with `ecommerce:write`. Webflow creates the default SKU from the first
value of each option set; the other combinations (here the Large packet) are added with the Create SKUs endpoint on
`+/sites/{site_id}/products/{product_id}/skus+`. The `publishStatus` field defaults to `staging`, so the product is not live
until it is published.

[source,bash]
----
curl --request POST \
  --url "https://api.webflow.com/v2/sites/${WEBFLOW_SITE_ID}/products" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer ${WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN}" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data @foxglove-seed-packet.json
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/products/create[Data API: Create Product and SKU].

Orders can be listed, filtered by status, and updated with shipping information.

[source,bash]
----
# Unfulfilled orders (scope ecommerce:read; status is one of
# pending, refunded, dispute-lost, fulfilled, disputed, unfulfilled)
curl --request GET \
  --url "https://api.webflow.com/v2/sites/${WEBFLOW_SITE_ID}/orders?status=unfulfilled&limit=100" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer ${WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN}"

# Add tracking information (scope ecommerce:write)
curl --request PATCH \
  --url "https://api.webflow.com/v2/sites/${WEBFLOW_SITE_ID}/orders/${ORDER_ID}" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer ${WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN}" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{"shippingProvider":"Correos","shippingTracking":"TRACKING-PLACEHOLDER","shippingTrackingURL":"https://tracking.example.com/TRACKING-PLACEHOLDER"}'

# Mark the order fulfilled without emailing the customer
curl --request POST \
  --url "https://api.webflow.com/v2/sites/${WEBFLOW_SITE_ID}/orders/${ORDER_ID}/fulfill" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer ${WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN}" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{"sendOrderFulfilledEmail": false}'
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/orders/list[Data API: List Orders], https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/orders/update[Data API: Update Order], https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/orders/update-fulfill[Data API: Fulfill Order].

The order object includes `orderId`, `status`, `customerPaid`, `netAmount`, `applicationFee`, `customerInfo`,
`purchasedItems`, `shippingAddress`, `billingAddress`, `totals`, `paymentProcessor` and the provider details. Orders
contain personal data (names, addresses, emails), so a script that reads them runs on trusted infrastructure with a
token that has only the needed scopes. The `x-webflow-signature` header of webhooks created through the API lets the
receiver verify that a request came from Webflow; webhooks created in the Dashboard do not carry the header.

Inventory is read and written on the SKU Collection:

[source,bash]
----
export SKU_COLLECTION_ID="your-sku-collection-id"
export SKU_ID="your-sku-id"

# Read the stock (scope ecommerce:read)
curl --request GET \
  --url "https://api.webflow.com/v2/collections/${SKU_COLLECTION_ID}/items/${SKU_ID}/inventory" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer ${WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN}"

# Set the stock to 120 (absolute), or use updateQuantity with a signed number for a delta
curl --request PATCH \
  --url "https://api.webflow.com/v2/collections/${SKU_COLLECTION_ID}/items/${SKU_ID}/inventory" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer ${WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN}" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{"inventoryType":"finite","quantity":120}'
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/inventory/list[Data API: List Inventory], https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/ecommerce/inventory/update[Data API: Update Inventory].

The SKU Collection ID comes from the site's list of Collections. Rate limits apply as for the rest of the Data API (60
requests per minute on Starter and Basic, 120 per minute on paid plans and the Developer workspace) and a failed request
should be retried with back-off; see
xref:cms/webflow/data-api-sdks-limits-and-errors.adoc[Data API: SDKs, Limits and Errors]. Ecommerce webhooks are
`ecomm_new_order`, `ecomm_order_changed` and `ecomm_inventory_changed`; see
xref:cms/webflow/webhooks.adoc[Webhooks].

== What does not work with localization, code export and DevLink

Several Webflow features that work for ordinary pages do not cover Ecommerce on the check date.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Area | What to know

| Localization
| Localization does not support the features of an Ecommerce Site plan. Products, categories and the Ecommerce pages of a
shop cannot be localized, so Fernbrook Gardens keeps the shop in English while Spanish and German visitors use the localized
Plants, Journal and static pages. Languages of a physical product (such as the printed guide) become variants. See
xref:cms/webflow/localization.adoc[Localization].

| Code export
| Exported code includes the static front end only. The CMS (lists render empty), Ecommerce, form processing and reCAPTCHA,
site search, password protection, localized content and code components are not part of the export, so a store cannot be
moved to another host by exporting code. Products and orders can be exported as CSV instead. See
xref:cms/webflow/code-export-and-portability.adoc[Code Export and Portability].

| DevLink
| The DevLink documentation describes exporting Webflow components as React components and importing React components into
Webflow, with an Ecommerce Site plan listed only as one of the qualifying plans. It documents no cart, checkout or product
support, so the shop pages stay in Webflow and DevLink is used for static design components such as the garden header.
See xref:cms/webflow/devlink-export.adoc[DevLink Export].

| Backups
| Restoring a backup made before 2024-03-25 resets Collection and item IDs, which also affects Ecommerce integrations. When
a backup is restored on a store, Webflow asks whether to include inventory; declining resets inventory and turns off
inventory tracking. See xref:cms/webflow/backups-and-recovery.adoc[Backups and Recovery].

| CSV update
| Importing a CSV into the Ecommerce panel creates products; it does not update existing Ecommerce items. See
xref:cms/webflow/csv-import-and-export.adoc[CSV Import and Export].
|===

== Pitfalls

* *Counting only products against the limit.* Variants count too; a growing matrix of options reaches 500 items on Standard
  sooner than expected.
* *Starting on Standard without modelling the fee.* The 2% fee plus the provider fee reduces the margin on low-priced items;
  compare it with the Plus tier before the first launch.
* *Deleting required checkout fields.* Hiding the email or address component breaks the checkout form; restyle, do not
  remove.
* *Treating the shop as translatable.* Localization does not cover Ecommerce, so plan languages as separate variants or
  separate sites.
* *Expecting export to carry the store.* Exporting code drops the CMS and Ecommerce; keep a CSV export and a documented
  Data API routine.
* *Using the dev site for real money.* Test the checkout with the provider's test mode and a staging domain before enabling
  live payments.
* *Sending order data to unsafe places.* Orders contain customer data; keep the token in an environment variable, scope it
  to ecommerce, and never log full order bodies.
* *Forgetting 409.* A call on a site without Ecommerce enabled fails with that status code, which looks like a permissions
  error but is not.

== History and where older material is wrong

Webflow introduced Ecommerce in 2018 with Standard-like tiers and has since added product variants, digital products, and
subscriptions, PayPal as a second provider, and a version-two Data API for products, SKUs, orders and inventory. The earlier
Data API (version one) was deprecated on 2025-03-31. In 2026 the CMS and Business Site plans were merged into Premium, but
the Ecommerce tiers kept their names. Details are on
xref:cms/webflow/product-changes-and-deprecations.adoc[Product Changes and Deprecations].

The requester-provided book, _Webflow by Example_ (2021), promises Ecommerce on page 37 but does not cover it, so nothing in
it describes products, checkout, payments, taxes, shipping or orders. Its plan descriptions (Lite, Pro and Team account
plans; Basic, CMS and Business site plans with 2,000 and 10,000 item limits) predate the current tiers and do not describe
the Ecommerce item limits above. Where the book and the official documentation differ, this sub-section follows the
official documentation. See
xref:cms/webflow/where-older-books-and-tutorials-are-outdated.adoc[Where Older Books and Tutorials Are Outdated].
