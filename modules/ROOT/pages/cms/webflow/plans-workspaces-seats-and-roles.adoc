This page was checked on 2026-10-28 against the Webflow Help Center, the Data API documentation and the public pricing
page. Webflow has no version numbers, so everything below describes the product on that date. The plan names and limits
changed in May 2026 and are still rolling out until 2026-11-16, and many Help Center articles still use the older
names, so treat the date as part of every statement. Plans are named by tier only: prices change often and are not
quoted here, so use the https://webflow.com/pricing[pricing page] for amounts.

Who pays for what is the first thing to understand before building on Webflow, because it decides who can open the
Designer, who can only edit content, whether a site can be served from a custom domain and whether custom code, code
export or the CMS are available at all. Webflow splits the answer into three separate things: a *Workspace plan* (the
people and the building tools), a *Site plan* (hosting one site) and *seats* (the people themselves, each with a
role). This page explains each, the May 2026 change that merged and renamed tiers, the add-ons, the roles and their
renamed vocabulary, the free Developer workspace that every code example in this sub-section uses, and a matrix that
maps features to the lowest tier that unlocks them. It follows the xref:cms/webflow/introduction-and-architecture.adoc[introduction and architecture]
page and precedes xref:cms/webflow/product-changes-and-deprecations.adoc[product changes and deprecations], which gives
the dated timeline.

The running example is *Fernbrook Gardens*, a fictitious botanical garden. A small team builds its site: a Site
manager who owns the project, a Designer who builds pages, a Marketer who runs campaigns and publishes landing pages,
a volunteer Content editor who updates the events calendar, and the board members who only leave comments. That mix of
people is exactly what the seat model is designed for.

== The three layers: Workspace, Site and seat

A *Workspace* is the container for people and sites. Every account has at least one. It holds the sites, the members
with their seats and roles, shared libraries of components and variables, billing for the Workspace plan and the
Workspace-level settings (integrations, apps, API tokens, security).

A *Site* is one website inside a Workspace. A site is created on the free Starter Site plan and can be upgraded to a
paid Site plan to be served on a custom domain with higher CMS, bandwidth and form limits. The site plan belongs to the
site, not to the Workspace, so one Workspace can hold sites on different plans.

A *seat* is the licence a person occupies in a Workspace. The seat type decides how much the person can do and whether
the Workspace is charged for them. The *role* (Owner, Admin, Site manager, Designer, Marketer, Content editor, Reviewer)
is what the person is allowed to do. Seats were introduced in December 2024 and replaced the old model in which each
plan capped the number of collaborators.

The diagram is the whole model. When a question reads "can this person do X on this site?", answer it in three steps:
which Workspace plan is the site's Workspace on (tooling), which Site plan is the site on (hosting and limits), and
which seat and role does the person hold (permission).

== Workspace plans

A Workspace plan pays for the tools used to build and manage sites and for the people who do it. In October 2026 the
Workspace tiers are:

* *Starter*: free. One person can build and publish sites on webflow.io staging domains, learn the Designer and try the
  platform. Features that need collaboration or code reuse are absent.
* *Core*: the entry paid tier for an individual who builds sites, with staging sites, custom code and code export.
* *Growth*: adds more seats, shared libraries and the collaboration features a small team needs.
* *Freelancer*: for people who build sites for clients. It adds client seats, so a client can edit content on the canvas
  without paying for a full seat, and guest access to client Workspaces.
* *Agency*: the same client model at larger scale, with more seats and sites.
* *Enterprise*: negotiated terms, custom roles, SSO and the security and scale features described later.

What a Workspace plan unlocks is mostly building features: staging sites, custom code, code export, shared libraries,
the number of seats and the collaboration tools. Which of those each tier includes is listed on the pricing page and
changes between revisions, so this page does not freeze a table for every tier. Where a limit is documented, it appears
below.

Webflow also states that *AI credits* are included in every Workspace plan. They meter the AI features (the AI
Assistant and the AI site builder, among others). The limits have been enforced since 2026-06-29, so a Workspace that
exhausts its credits stops using those features until the allowance renews or more credits are bought.

== Site plans

A Site plan pays for hosting one site on Webflow's infrastructure. It sets the things a visitor and a content team feel:
whether the site can use a custom domain, how many CMS items and Collections it can hold, how many static pages it can
have, how much bandwidth is included, how many form submissions are kept and how many API requests per minute are
allowed. In October 2026 the Site tiers are:

* *Starter*: free hosting on a webflow.io domain. It is meant for building and testing, with small limits, for example
  50 CMS items and 20 Collections, and 50 form submissions *in total* that are never reset.
* *Basic*: a static site on a custom domain, with no CMS. It keeps a 300-page limit.
* *Premium*: the tier that replaced the former CMS and Business plans. It has 300 static pages, 20,000 CMS items, 40
  Collections and 50 GB of bandwidth with add-ons.
* *Team*: a new annual all-in-one plan for one site. It includes 100 Collections, 10 seats, Localize with two locales,
  page branching, single-page publishing and the AEO agents (the answer-engine optimization tooling).
* *Enterprise*: custom limits, an SLA, custom certificates, security headers and custom hosting, and the next-generation
  CMS with larger limits.

Hosting on every tier is delivered through Cloudflare's network with free automatic TLS. Enterprise adds custom
certificates, security headers, custom hosting and a 99.99% SLA, as described in the
xref:cms/webflow/introduction-and-architecture.adoc[introduction and architecture] page.

=== Ecommerce plans

Webflow Ecommerce is sold as Site plans of its own: *Standard*, *Plus* and *Advanced*. They include the CMS and add
the store features (products, checkout, orders, inventory). The item limits are 500 products on Standard, 5,000 on Plus
and 15,000 on Advanced, and Standard adds a 2% transaction fee that the higher tiers do not charge. The Ecommerce tiers
were not changed by the May 2026 plans. The store itself is covered in the Ecommerce pages of this sub-section.

== The May 2026 plan change

On 2026-05-13 Webflow launched simplified plans for new purchases, and the Help Center article "Updated pricing and
simplified plans for May 2026" describes the change. The effects that matter when reading older material are these.

* The former *CMS* and *Business* Site plans were merged into *Premium*. If a tutorial says "you need the CMS plan" or
  "you need the Business plan", read it as Premium on a new site. Basic stays static-only.
* *Team* is new. It is annual, bundles Workspace and Site capabilities and is the first tier below Enterprise to include
  page branching, single-page publishing and the AEO agents.
* The CMS item add-ons were removed, because Premium raised the included allowance.
* AI credits are included in every Workspace plan, and their limits have applied since 2026-06-29.

Existing sites move on a schedule instead of all at once:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Date | What happens
| 2026-05-13 | New purchases use the May 2026 plans.
| 2026-06-29 | Existing CMS and Business sites migrate to Premium at their next renewal or billable change after this date. AI credit limits are enforced from the same date.
| 2026-11-16 | Sites in Freelancer and Agency Workspaces, and sites that still use pricing from 2022 or earlier, begin to migrate.
|===

During the roll-out the same site can therefore be on the old names in one Workspace and the new names in another. The
Help Center has not been rewritten everywhere, so many articles keep saying CMS or Business. The mapping to use is
CMS and Business become Premium, and "one site that needs everything plus branching" becomes Team. Features that older
articles describe as Enterprise-only, such as page branching, single-page publishing, the publishing workflow and the
site activity log, are available on Team as well; the matrix later in this page lists them.

== Add-ons

Add-ons extend a site or Workspace without changing its tier.

* *Localize* adds secondary locales to a site. It comes as *Localize Essential* (3 locales and 10,000 words per locale
  per month for machine translation) and *Localize Advanced* (10 locales and 50,000 words). Publishing a secondary
  locale needs a paid Site plan *and* a Localize add-on, except that Team includes two locales. See the localization
  pages of this sub-section.
* *Analyze* adds first-party analytics for traffic, conversions and, with Team or Enterprise, the AEO analytics that
  show how AI answer engines cite the site.
* *Optimize* adds A/B/n testing, personalization and AI-optimized variations.
* *AI credits* can be added when the included allowance runs out.
* Bandwidth and, on some tiers, form-submission allowances can be raised with add-ons on the Site plan.

Analyze and Optimize are add-ons, not part of any standard tier, so a feature that "needs Analyze" needs the paid
add-on on top of the site's plan.

== Seats

Seats were announced in December 2024. A seat is a person in a Workspace, and the seat type decides whether the
Workspace pays for that person and how deeply they can use the Designer.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Seat | Roles it can hold | Notes
| Full seat | Owner, Admin, Site manager, Designer | Paid. Full Designer access and, depending on the role, billing, members and site settings.
| Limited seat | Marketer, Content editor | Paid at a lower level. Work on the canvas with a restricted toolbox: edit text, images, CMS items and, for the Marketer, SEO and some page settings, but not the structure or styles.
| Free seat | Reviewer | Free, up to 100 per Workspace. Can view the site and leave comments but not edit.
| Client seat | Content editor (client) | Specific to one site. Exists only in Freelancer and Agency Workspaces so that a client can edit content on the canvas.
| Guest | Any role granted inside someone else's Workspace | Lets a Freelancer or Agency Workspace work inside a client's Workspace without moving the site.
|===

Three practical consequences follow. Reviewers cost nothing, so the garden's board members can comment on every draft
without increasing the bill. A client's content editor does not need a full seat if the agency is on Freelancer or
Agency. And a Workspace's headcount is a function of seat types, not of a per-plan cap, so the cheapest way to grow a
team is to put people on the least powerful seat that still lets them do their job.

=== The legacy Editor is gone

Before seats, content editing for people without Designer access happened in the *Editor*, reached by adding `/?edit`
to a published page's URL, with a bottom toolbar. It was retired in stages: client seats arrived on 2026-02-02,
automatic migration of Editor users began on 2026-05-04, the Editor became unavailable on 2026-08-04, and it was fully
deprecated on 2026-10-05, when the `?edit` URL stopped working. Content is now edited directly on the canvas by users
who hold a seat, usually a Limited seat or a Client seat. Guides that explain the Editor, including the whole
Editor-centred chapter of the reference book *Webflow by Example* (Packt, 2021), are obsolete on this point; the
concepts of editing text, images and CMS items are the same, but the entry point and the licensing are different.

== Roles

A role is a named set of permissions on the Workspace and on its sites. Webflow renamed three roles, which matters when
reading older articles and API documentation.

[cols="2,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Role (2026 name) | Older name | What it can do
| Owner | Owner | The single account owner of the Workspace. Everything an Admin can do, plus billing ownership, transferring the Workspace and deleting it.
| Admin | Admin | Manages members, seats, billing and Workspace settings, and has Site manager rights on every site.
| Site manager | Site admin | Manages one or more sites: publishing, site settings, domains, backups and restores, and the people assigned to the site. Only Site managers can restore a backup.
| Designer | Designer | Builds in the Designer: structure, styles, components, variables, interactions and the CMS schema.
| Marketer | (new) | Limited seat. Edits content and the marketing-relevant settings of a page, such as SEO fields and open graph data, without touching structure or styles.
| Content editor | Can edit | Limited seat. Edits text, images and CMS item content on the canvas, and publishes content within the limits set for the site.
| Reviewer | Commenter | Free seat. Views and comments only.
|===

Each role's exact permission list is long and changes as features ship, so the authoritative reference is the Help
Center article "Seats, roles, and permissions overview" and the per-role articles linked from it. Two patterns are
stable. First, structural power rises with the seat type: only full seats can change the design system. Second, role
assignment is per site as well as per Workspace: a Site manager of one site can be a Reviewer on another.

=== Custom roles

Enterprise Workspaces can define up to 20 *custom roles*. A custom role starts from the permissions of a built-in role
and adds or removes specific capabilities, for example "can publish but cannot change domains" for a release
coordinator. Custom roles are the way to apply least privilege when the built-in roles are too coarse, and they are
recorded in the Workspace audit log. They are an Enterprise feature; on every other tier the seven built-in roles are
the whole vocabulary.

=== Roles in the Fernbrook Gardens team

[cols="2,2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Person | Seat | Role | Why
| Garden director | Full | Owner | Holds billing and the Workspace.
| Web lead | Full | Site manager | Publishes, manages the domain, restores backups.
| Freelance designer | Full or Guest | Designer | Builds the templates and the design system.
| Communications officer | Limited | Marketer | Writes landing pages and SEO fields.
| Events volunteer | Limited | Content editor | Updates the events calendar CMS items.
| Board members | Free | Reviewer | Comment on drafts, free of charge.
|===

== The Developer workspace

The *Developer workspace* is a free Workspace designed for people who build apps, integrations and code on top of
Webflow. Every developer example in this sub-section runs on a Developer workspace site, so a reader does not need a
paid plan to follow along. Its documented characteristics are these.

* Free of charge, with up to 3 users.
* Unlimited unhosted sites, with custom code, page branching and the site activity log available for testing.
* An API limit of 120 requests per minute, the same as paid Site plans.
* It *cannot* publish to production domains, export code or transfer sites, and it is limited to 1,000 visitors per
  month, so it is a sandbox rather than a place to host a real site.

When a page in this sub-section needs a paid or Enterprise plan, it says so. The Developer workspace is also the right
place to experiment with webhooks and OAuth apps against sites whose content can be deleted safely.

[source,bash]
----
# List the Developer workspace sites visible to a site or workspace token.
# WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN is a placeholder: never commit a real token.
curl --request GET \
  --url https://api.webflow.com/v2/sites \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer $WEBFLOW_API_TOKEN" \
  --header "accept: application/json"
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/sites/list[Data API: List Sites]

The Data API applies a per-minute rate limit that depends on the plan: 60 requests per minute on Starter and Basic
sites, 120 on paid plans and on Developer workspaces, and custom limits on Enterprise. Team receives five times the
CMS API limits. Site publishing is limited to one per minute everywhere. When a request exceeds the limit the API
returns `429` with a `Retry-After` header, and the official SDKs retry with backoff automatically.

[source,typescript]
----
// Minimal manual back-off for a raw HTTP call; the SDKs already do this.
async function listSites(token: string): Promise<unknown> {
  for (let attempt = 0; attempt < 3; attempt++) {
    const response = await fetch("https://api.webflow.com/v2/sites", {
      headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${token}` },
    });
    if (response.status !== 429) {
      return response.json();
    }
    const waitSeconds = Number(response.headers.get("Retry-After") ?? "60");
    await new Promise((resolve) => setTimeout(resolve, waitSeconds * 1000));
  }
  throw new Error("Rate limited three times in a row");
}
----

Source: https://developers.webflow.com/data/reference/rate-limits[Data API: Rate limits]

== Feature to minimum plan

The matrix gives the lowest tier at which a feature becomes available, with tier names only. Official pages are not
always consistent about whether a Workspace plan or a Site plan unlocks a feature, so the column says which kind of
plan is the gate according to the most recent Help Center wording, and the entries marked "re-check" are the ones where
sources disagree.

[cols="3,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Feature | Minimum tier | Notes
| Build and publish on webflow.io | Starter (Workspace and Site) | Free.
| Custom domain with TLS | Basic (Site) | Automatic TLS on every tier.
| CMS Collections and items | Premium (Site) | Starter has a small CMS allowance for building; Basic has no CMS.
| 20,000 CMS items and 40 Collections | Premium (Site) | Replaces the former CMS and Business limits.
| 100 Collections | Team (Site) | Enterprise next-generation CMS goes further.
| Custom code (head, footer and Code Embed) | Paid plan (re-check) | Sources disagree on Workspace plan or Site plan. 50,000 characters per box.
| Code export | Core (Workspace) | Disabled on Developer workspaces. Drops the CMS, ecommerce, forms and search.
| Staging sites | Core (Workspace) | Developer workspaces have unhosted sites.
| Shared libraries | Paid Workspace plan (re-check) | Availability is stated inconsistently.
| Client seats and guest access | Freelancer (Workspace) | Also Agency.
| Page branching | Team (Site) | Also Enterprise. Older articles say Enterprise only.
| Single-page publishing | Team (Site) | Also Enterprise.
| Publishing workflow and site activity log | Team (Site) | Also Enterprise. The activity log is also in Developer workspaces.
| Scheduled CMS publishing | Premium (Site) | Not available on Starter or Basic.
| Localization with secondary locales | Paid Site plan plus Localize | Team includes two locales.
| Analyze and Optimize | Paid Site plan plus the add-on | AEO analytics needs Team or Enterprise plus Analyze.
| Ecommerce | Standard (Site) | Plus and Advanced raise the item limits.
| Custom roles (up to 20) | Enterprise (Workspace) | No other tier.
| Custom certificates, security headers, custom hosting, 99.99% SLA | Enterprise (Site) | See the introduction.
| Workspace API tokens | Enterprise (Workspace) | Documentation disagrees; re-check.
|===

== Correcting older advice

The reference book *Webflow by Example* describes Webflow as it was in 2021, and its plan chapter is out of date in
several ways that this page corrects.

* It names Workspace-style account plans Lite, Pro and Team, and Site plans Basic, CMS and Business with 2,000 and
  10,000 CMS items and 100-page caps. None of those names or limits is current. The 2026 Site tiers are Starter,
  Basic, Premium, Team and Enterprise with the limits above.
* It uses the words "project" and "Project Settings"; the current vocabulary is *site* and *Site settings*.
* It ties the Editor and its collaborators to the CMS plan and the `/?edit` URL; the Editor is gone and editing needs a
  seat.
* It ties code export to one plan in one place and to another in a different place, and the two statements
  contradict each other. Code export today needs a paid Workspace plan and is disabled on Developer workspaces.
* It states 30 fields per Collection; the limit is 60 (100 on Enterprise next-generation CMS).

== Pitfalls and good practice

* *Do not confuse the two plan layers.* Upgrading a Workspace plan does not host a site on a custom domain; the Site
  plan does. Conversely a Site plan does not give a person more seats.
* *Check the date of any plan article.* The Help Center still contains many pages that say CMS or Business plan. Before
  acting on one, map it to Premium and check the limits.
* *Put people on the smallest seat that works.* Reviewers are free, Marketers and Content editors are cheaper than
  Designers, and a client on a Freelancer or Agency Workspace can have a Client seat.
* *Restrict who can restore backups.* Only Site managers can restore, and restoring a backup made before 2024-03-25
  resets Collection and item IDs, which breaks API integrations that stored those IDs.
* *Use Developer workspaces for experiments.* They cannot publish to production, so a mistake cannot reach visitors.
* *Plan the migration window.* Sites that fall under the 2026-11-16 migration group should review their Premium or Team
  mapping before the renewal date.
* *Keep tokens least-privileged.* API tokens inherit the permissions of the person or Workspace that created them. Use
  a site token with only the scopes an integration needs, and rotate it when a person leaves the team.
