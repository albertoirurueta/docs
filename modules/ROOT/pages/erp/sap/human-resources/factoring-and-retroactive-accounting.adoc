= Partial Periods and Retroactive Accounting
:description: How SAP payroll pays an employee who did not work a whole payroll period (the partial period factor and the payment, deduction, hybrid, planned working time and all-or-nothing methods) and how it corrects a past period after a late change (retroactive accounting and recalculation differences), with checked example arithmetic, for SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 with SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA.

:stem: latexmath

include::partial$ai-disclaimer.adoc[]

Most months are simple for payroll: a person works the whole month and receives the whole monthly pay. Real life is not
that tidy. People join or leave in the middle of a month, take unpaid leave, change department, or get a raise whose start
date lies in the past. This page explains the two mechanisms SAP payroll uses for these cases. The first, called
factoring, pays only the part of a period that an employee should be paid for, by multiplying an amount by a fraction
between 0 and 1. The second, called retroactive accounting, goes back and recalculates a month that was already paid
because something that affects it was changed later, and then pays or claims back the difference in the current month.
After reading it you can compute a partial period factor with each of the five methods SAP offers, explain why the method
matters most for monthly payroll, and follow a retroactive correction from the changed record to the amount on the next
payslip. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 on premise and Private Edition, where payroll comes from SAP HCM for
SAP S/4HANA (H4S4), a separately licensed component. SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition and SAP SuccessFactors are not covered here; the landscape page compares the options.

All rates and amounts in the examples are generic and made up. They are not any country's rules, and the arithmetic was
checked by hand and with a small script, not by running an SAP system.

== Key terms

* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#factoring[Factoring]: reducing or splitting an amount in payroll by a partial period factor.
* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#partial-period-factor[Partial period factor]: the fraction by which an amount is multiplied when an employee is paid for only part of a period.
* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#personal-work-schedule[Personal work schedule]: the planned working time of one employee, day by day, for the period.
* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#retroactive-accounting[Retroactive accounting]: recalculating already paid periods when data that affects them was changed later.
* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#payroll-past[Payroll past]: all payroll periods for which payroll was already run and exited.
* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#retroactive-accounting-limit[Retroactive accounting limit]: the furthest date in the past up to which payroll data may be changed and recalculated.
* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#recalculation-difference[Recalculation difference]: the amount by which a recalculated period differs from what was paid, carried to the current period.
* xref:erp/sap/human-resources/glossary.adoc#wage-type[Wage type]: a pay component, such as basic pay, with an amount or a number of hours.

  A real company chooses its own in Customizing.

== Why payroll needs partial periods

SAP's documentation says that when an employee did not work for the whole payroll period, a partial remuneration is
calculated during the payroll run, so that only results for the current period are considered. It names three uses for
factoring:

* To reduce payments because an employee joins, leaves or is absent during a period.
* To calculate remuneration for an exact period, because of substitutions, changes in basic pay, organizational
  reassignments or changes in the personal work schedule.
* To calculate wage types for cost accounting for an exact period, so that different amounts can be assigned to
  different cost centers.

The remuneration amount is multiplied by a partial period factor to give the exact remuneration. The documentation lists
the infotypes that payroll evaluates for factoring: Actions (0000), Organizational Assignment (0001), Planned Working
Time (0007), Basic Pay (0008), Recurring Payments and Deductions (0014), Additional Payments (0015), Absences (2001)
and Substitutions (2003). The documentation's examples of reductions include joining or leaving on a day other than the
first or last day of a period, a week of unpaid leave, a transportation allowance that stops during leave, and a bonus
paid only if the employee is still employed the next month.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/ae5bdd5321e8424de10000000a174cb4.html[Partial Period Remuneration (Factoring)] and https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/9acade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Application Examples for Reductions].

== The partial period factor

The factor is a fraction. SAP describes its formula in words as planned working time (or the flat-rate period working
time) minus absence, divided by a divisor. Written as an equation, with stem:[T_{planned}] for the time the employee was
planned to work, stem:[T_{absent}] for the unpaid time in that stretch, and stem:[T_{period}] for the divisor:

[stem]
++++
PPF = \frac{T_{planned} - T_{absent}}{T_{period}}
++++

The amount to pay is then the basic amount times the factor. All parts of the formula must use the same calculation unit.
SAP's documentation names three: working hours, work days and calendar days. In the personnel calculation rules the unit
is marked by a letter (S for hours, A for work days, K for calendar days). The values come from the personal work
schedule (table PSP) and from the work center and basic pay data (table WPBP).

SAP also distinguishes the period on which a parameter is based. A parameter starting with `T` refers to a partial period
(used when a basic pay wage type has a WPBP split indicator), and one starting with `G` refers to the whole period (used
for items such as recurring payments or additional payments, which are not split). For example, `SSOLL` and `ASOLL` hold
the planned working time in hours and in work days, and their partial-period forms are `TSSOLL` and `TASOLL`.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/a0cade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Partial Period Factor] and https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/a3cade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Factoring Parameter].

== The five methods

The methods differ in what they put in the divisor and in what counts as the reduction. SAP's documentation names five.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Method |Factor |What SAP says about it

|PWS method (personal work schedule)
|stem:[(planned - absence) / individual period working time]
|Uses the employee's own planned working time as the divisor. Suitable for reductions when planned working time is
specified exactly and every deviation is entered in Absences, Substitutions and Planned Working Time. SAP says it is not
suitable if payroll is run for monthly periods.

|Payment method
|stem:[(planned - absence) / general period working time]
|The employee is paid for time worked or paid absences; a constant general divisor makes every day or hour worth the same
in short and long months. Suitable with many unpaid absences, or when the period length does not differ from the general
length (for example weekly payment). Not suitable for monthly payroll with few absences: a long month can pay more than
the basic amount, and a short month can reduce it too much.

|Deduction method
|stem:[(general period working time - absence) / general period working time]
|A pro-rata amount is deducted for the unpaid absence. Suitable with few unpaid absences or when period length is
constant. Not suitable for monthly payroll with many absences: in a long month the result could become negative, and in a
short month an employee absent the whole month still receives pay.

|Hybrid of payment and deduction
|payment factor or deduction factor, chosen per case
|SAP recommends deciding per individual: the payment method for few absences (the pay stays high), the deduction method
for many.

|All or nothing method
|1 or 0
|The employee receives the whole amount or nothing, for example if a minimum period was worked. SAP names the cost
transfer of a travel allowance and a savings plan allowance as uses.
|===

The choice is usually fixed by the collective agreement or the company agreement, and SAP says the question is extremely
important when payroll is run for monthly periods. The standard system delivers a few example factoring rules that a
company can use as they are or adapt in Customizing for Payroll under Factoring.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/a9cade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[PWS Method], https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/accade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Payment Method], https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/afcade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Deduction Method], https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/b2cade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Hybrid of Payment and Deduction Methods] and https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/b5cade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[All or Nothing Method].

== Worked example: Ben takes unpaid leave

Suppose Ben takes five days of unpaid leave, Monday 16.03.2026 to Friday 20.03.2026. His basic pay is 2,400.00 EUR, March
has 22 working days, and the general period working time is 21 days. In days, with a planned time of 22 and an absence of 5:

[cols="2,2,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Method |Factor |Value |Pay in EUR

|PWS
|(22 - 5) / 22
|0.7727
|1,854.55

|Payment
|(22 - 5) / 21
|0.8095
|1,942.86

|Deduction
|(21 - 5) / 21
|0.7619
|1,828.57
|===

With few absences the hybrid method would pick the payment method, so 1,942.86 EUR. The three methods differ by up to
about 114 EUR for the same five days, which is why a company agreement has to name the method.

The same five unpaid days in February 2026 (20 working days, absence 16.02.2026 to 20.02.2026) give a different picture:

[cols="2,2,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Method |Factor |Value |Pay in EUR

|PWS
|(20 - 5) / 20
|0.7500
|1,800.00

|Payment
|(20 - 5) / 21
|0.7143
|1,714.29

|Deduction
|(21 - 5) / 21
|0.7619
|1,828.57
|===

Observations that follow from the numbers, and that match the cautions in SAP's documentation:

* The deduction method takes the same amount, 5 / 21 of 2,400.00 or 571.43 EUR, off in both months. Under the PWS method
  the value of one day depends on the month (1 / 22 in March, 1 / 20 in February), so the same five days cost 545.45 EUR
  in March and 600.00 EUR in February.
* The payment method with no absence at all pays 22 / 21 of the basic pay in March, which is 2,514.29 EUR and more than
  the 2,400.00 EUR agreed. That is the long-month problem SAP describes.
* The deduction method for an employee absent all 20 working days of February still pays 1 / 21, which is 114.29 EUR.
  That is the short-month problem SAP describes.

SAP's documentation has its own examples with different numbers (a reduction for unpaid leave that spans two months, and
a comparison of the methods for one employee). They show the same pattern.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/b8d3de537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Reduction of Payments: Example] and https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/b2cade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Hybrid of Payment and Deduction Methods]. == Worked example: an exact period without any absence

Factoring is also used when nothing is lost but the pay must be split. Suppose Ben is reassigned on 16.03.2026 and his
monthly pay is to be booked in two partial periods. In March 2026 the first partial period (01.03.2026 to 15.03.2026) has
10 working days and the second (16.03.2026 to 31.03.2026) has 12, together the 22 of the month. Each partial period is
paid for the days worked in it:

[cols="2,2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Method |Factors (first, second) |First |Second |Total in EUR

|PWS
|10 / 22 and 12 / 22
|1,090.91
|1,309.09
|2,400.00

|Payment
|10 / 21 and 12 / 21
|1,142.86
|1,371.43
|2,514.29

|Deduction
|(21 - 12) / 21 and (21 - 10) / 21
|1,028.57
|1,257.14
|2,285.71
|===

In each partial period the "absence" is the working time that lies in the other partial period. Only the PWS method adds
up to the agreed 2,400.00 EUR, because it uses the real length of the month as divisor. That is the reason SAP's own
example of a change of cost center recommends the PWS method when an employee is not absent and only moves.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/bbd3de537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Calculating Period-Specific Remuneration: Example]. The figures above are this page's own example.

== How payroll applies the factor

Payroll runs factoring as a schema step. SAP's documentation describes the technical sequence of the international
schema: personnel calculation schema `X000` calls subschema `XAL9` (factoring and storage), and country-specific versions
exist (the letter `X` stands for the country indicator and the international versions). The documented prerequisites are
the reduction factors set in Customizing for Payroll under Factoring, Create Reduction Factors, and processing classes 10
(Mark wage types for factoring) and 31 (Split monthly lump sums for cost accounting) on the wage types to be factored.

In short, the documented flow is:

. Function `GEN/8` generates the secondary wage types `/801` to `/8nn`, one per partial period, each with the value 1
  multiplied by a constant to keep the arithmetic precise. The standard system generates 16; `/801` to `/809` are free for
  a company's own partial period factors, and `/810` to `/816` are used for monthly lump sums.
. Operation `PPPAR` reads the partial period parameters, for example whether the employee started a new job in the period
  or took unpaid leave. If no split indicator exists, the factor stays 1 and the wage type is paid in full.
. Personnel calculation rule `xPPF` determines the partial period factor for each partial period.
. Personnel calculation rule `xCMO` determines the flat-rate period for cost accounting.
. Personnel calculation rule `xVAL` multiplies the amount of each primary wage type by the factor and writes the reduced
  value back.

This runs only inside an SAP system. Requires an SAP system; not run here. The numbers in the previous sections were
computed outside SAP.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/d9b2188095ad4922bd9977126e0d92b7/a6cade537187424de10000000a174cb4.html[Technical Sequence for Partial Period Remuneration].

== Retroactive accounting

Payroll results cannot be corrected by editing them. Instead, SAP's documentation says that if you change master or time
data for a payroll period for which payroll has already run, the existing results are rechecked in the next regular
payroll run and included in the payroll again. Not every change causes this: SAP checks whether a change is relevant to
retroactive accounting, per infotype or per infotype field, set in Customizing for Personnel Administration under
Customize Procedures, Infotypes, Define Fields Relevant for Retroactive Accounting. Changes to Customizing itself do not
trigger retroactive accounting. To force it for all personnel numbers, SAP names the field Forced retro. accounting as of
on the payroll program's selection screen.

The documented recognition steps are:

. Editing a retro-relevant infotype writes the date of the changed record into the Payroll Status infotype (0003), in the
  field Earliest Master Data Change Since Last Payroll Run.
. At the next regular payroll run or correction run (off-cycle type B), the system compares that date with the date up to
  which payroll was already run for the employee.
. If the date lies before it, the system recalculates every period from the period containing the change up to the last
  period already paid.
. It then deletes the stored change dates from infotype 0003.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart TD
    A["Edit of a retro-relevant infotype in a paid period"] --> B["Infotype 0003 stores the earliest change date"]
    B --> C["Next regular or correction payroll run"]
    C --> D{"Change date before the paid-up-to date?"}
    D -- "No" --> E["Normal calculation of the current period"]
    D -- "Yes" --> F["Recalculate each period from the change date"]
    F --> G["Differences go to the current period"]
    G --> H["Change dates deleted from infotype 0003"]
----

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/ff854a903cbf4c10910a5825d787693f/493ec2531bb9b44ce10000000a174cb4.html[Retroactive Accounting].

=== The retroactive accounting limit

A correction cannot reach back forever. The retroactive accounting limit is the date furthest in the past up to which
payroll-relevant data may be changed. SAP's documentation says it is derived from three dates and that the most recent of
them is used: the limit stored for the payroll area in the payroll control record, the earliest personal limit in
infotype 0003, and the hiring date of the employee, which is the absolute limit. The payroll area's limit can only be
edited when its control record has the status End of payroll. For Example Trading Co. the payroll area is `E1`, so one
limit in its control record applies to Ana, Ben and Chloe unless an employee has a more recent personal limit.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/ff854a903cbf4c10910a5825d787693f/4c3ec2531bb9b44ce10000000a174cb4.html[Retroactive Accounting Limit] and https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/ff854a903cbf4c10910a5825d787693f/fb4ddd538636424de10000000a174cb4.html[Example: Determining the Retroactive Accounting Limit].

== Recalculation differences

The corrected period is not paid again. SAP's documentation describes this process. The system calculates a new payment
amount for the earlier period but does not save it as the period's result. It forms the difference to the original
payment amount and forwards it to the current period. For a period stem:[p] with a new result stem:[RT_{new}(p)] and the
original result stem:[RT_{old}(p)], the recalculation difference is

[stem]
++++
\Delta_p = RT_{new}(p) - RT_{old}(p)
++++

and the current period receives the sum over all recalculated periods, stem:[\sum_p \Delta_p]. A positive sum is an
arrears payment, a negative sum is a repayment withheld from the current pay.

SAP stores the differences in two wage types with opposite signs. In the retroactive accounting result the difference is
saved in wage type `/551` (Recalculation difference): negative when the new amount is greater (an arrears payment),
positive when it is less. The amount is transferred with the sign reversed to the current period's result in wage type
`/552` (Subs.clearing/prev.month). The original amount in wage type `/560` (Payment amount) is not changed.

[cols="2,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Change with retroactive effect |`/551` in the retro result |`/552` in the current result

|Remuneration element increased
|negative
|positive

|Remuneration element reduced
|positive
|negative
|===

=== Worked example: a raise with a past start date

Ben's basic pay rises from 2,400.00 EUR to 2,500.00 EUR, effective 01.01.2026. The change is entered after the payroll
for February 2026 was finished, so payroll for March 2026 is the next run. Following the steps above, and ignoring taxes
and contributions as SAP's own example does:

[cols="2,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Period recalculated |Old result |New result |Difference |`/551` (retro result)

|January 2026 (`01 in 03`)
|2,400.00
|2,500.00
|100.00
|-100.00

|February 2026 (`02 in 03`)
|2,400.00
|2,500.00
|100.00
|-100.00
|===

The sum of the differences is stem:[100.00 + 100.00 = 200.00] EUR. In March 2026 Ben's basic pay is 2,500.00 EUR and
wage type `/552` carries +200.00 EUR, so the basic pay and clearing items of the March result add up to 2,700.00 EUR. The
original January and February results are not deleted; SAP keeps them with different status indicators. The labels
`01 in 03` and `02 in 03` follow the notation of SAP's own example: the first number is the period the result is for, the
second the period in which it was calculated.

Requires an SAP system; not run here. SAP's example uses 3,000 and 3,500 USD for periods 05 and 06 and a run in period
07; it arrives at the same structure.

== What older books show, and what has changed

The book SAP ERP HCM: Technical Principles and Programming, 1st edition (2013), mentions retroactive accounting in its
payroll chapter: a change to a payroll-relevant infotype in a past period triggers recalculation back to the period of the
change, whether an infotype triggers it is a property that can be checked per infotype, and recalculated periods keep
their earlier results marked with status values. These ideas are unchanged in the 2025 FPS02 documentation. This page's
factoring material and the wage types `/551` and `/552` rest on the product documentation. What has changed is the
product around them: the book was written for SAP ERP HCM, while in SAP S/4HANA 2025 these functions come from SAP HCM for
SAP S/4HANA, which needs a separate license and whose business function is active by default, so the older compatibility
mode is no longer an option.

Source for the H4S4 statement: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/c6c3ffd90792427a9fee1a19df5b0925/18cbddfa5691416d8f004dc97ac98634.html[SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA].

== Summary

* Factoring pays an employee for part of a period by multiplying an amount by a partial period factor between 0 and 1.
* SAP documents five methods: PWS, payment, deduction, a hybrid of payment and deduction, and all or nothing. They differ
  in the divisor, and the choice matters most for monthly payroll, where short and long months distort some methods.
* In Ben's example, five unpaid days in March 2026 give 1,854.55 (PWS), 1,942.86 (payment) or 1,828.57 EUR (deduction), and
  only PWS splits a month into partial periods that add up to the agreed 2,400.00 EUR.
* Retroactive accounting recalculates already paid periods after a retro-relevant change, back to the retroactive
  accounting limit, and never edits the original results.
* The corrected difference, stem:[\Delta_p = RT_{new}(p) - RT_{old}(p)], is stored in `/551` and cleared in the current
  period through `/552`, so a raise with a past start date shows up as arrears in the next payslip.
