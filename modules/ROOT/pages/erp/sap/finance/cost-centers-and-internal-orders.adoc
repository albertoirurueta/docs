A cost center is a named place in the company where costs arise, such as the administration office or the marketing
team. An internal order is a short-lived container for the costs of one job, such as a trade fair, which is closed once
the job is done. Managers use cost centers to see what each team spends compared with what was planned, and internal
orders to see what one specific job cost in total. This page explains the master data of both objects, how cost centers
are grouped into a hierarchy, how costs are planned and compared with actual costs, and how an internal order passes its
costs on to the cost centers that should bear them (settlement). It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on
premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. Cost centers exist in both editions. Internal
orders are described by the on-premise help; the Public Edition help tells you to use projects instead, as explained
below.

== What a cost center is

SAP's help defines a cost center as an organizational unit within a controlling area that represents a clearly delimited
location where costs occur. You may divide the organization by function, by settlement, by activity, by space or by
responsibility. Cost centers are grouped into decision, control and responsibility units. They serve two purposes: to
assign overhead costs, such as rent or management salaries, to the organizational activities that use them, and to
control the costs that arise in the organization.

The help for Cost Center Accounting (CO-OM-CCA) distinguishes two basic kinds. Operational cost centers provide services
directly to production, service and maintenance processes. Support cost centers provide services to other cost centers,
and their costs must be allocated to the cost centers that requested the services. In a cost center, a manager is
responsible for recording costs with reference to plan values; a profit center calculates operating results.

Two rules matter early. First, a cost center belongs to exactly one controlling area and, in its master data, to exactly
one company code. Second, you can post revenue on a cost center only statistically. A real revenue posting needs a
revenue-carrying object such as an order, because true revenue cannot sit on a cost center (see the true and statistical
account assignment objects on the xref:erp/sap/finance/controlling-overview-and-cost-elements.adoc[previous page]).

== Cost center master data

The master data of a cost center is kept in groups of fields. SAP's help lists these groups and says that cost center
master data can be maintained with time-based dependencies, so that a field can have different values in different date
ranges. The next table shows the main fields, what each is for and how often it can change. The time reference letters
are the help's own: D for day-based, P for period-based, Y for fiscal-year-based and I for time independent.

[cols="2,4,1",options="header"]
|===
|Field |Use |Time reference
|Name and description
|The name and the longer description of the cost center.
|D

|Owner (person responsible)
|The cost center manager.
|D

|Department
|The department to which the cost center belongs.
|D

|Type (category) of cost center
|Classifies cost centers and holds planning and posting information, such as which primary and secondary postings are allowed.
|D

|Hierarchy area
|Places the cost center in the standard hierarchy.
|I

|Company code
|Assigns the cost center to one company code.
|Y

|Business area
|Assigns the cost center to a business area, where the company code requires it.
|Y

|Profit center
|Assigns the cost center to a profit center.
|P

|Currency
|The object currency of the cost center; its default value is the controlling area currency.
|Y

|Lock indicator
|Blocks plan and actual postings (primary, secondary, revenues, commitments).
|D

|Record quantity indicator
|Says that consumption quantities are to be recorded; a warning appears for actual postings without quantities.
|D

|Costing sheet
|Controls the calculation of overhead costs.
|P
|===

The Public Edition help gives the same classification for the app it describes: the name, the cost center category and
the person responsible are day-based, the profit center is period-based, the company code and the currency are
fiscal-year-based, and the hierarchy area is not time-based.

=== Validity periods and time-based fields

In the Public Edition app, each cost center has one or more validity periods, and a field behaves according to its time
dependency. SAP's help gives these rules:

* A day-based field can change on any day. If it is historically relevant, which most are, you either apply the change to
  the open validity period or create a new validity period that covers exactly the days affected.
* A period-based or fiscal-year-based field is changed for whole periods or whole fiscal years. If transaction data
  already exists for the current or a past period (or fiscal year), the change cannot be saved in the open validity
  period; you create a new one that starts with the next period (or fiscal year).
* A field that is not time-based can be changed only while the cost center has a single validity period.
* A new validity period cannot overlap another one of the same cost center. If it directly follows or precedes another
  with identical settings, the two are merged on saving.
* If the validity period already has postings or references from other objects, its duration can only be kept or
  extended.

An example from the help: a cost center is valid from 1 January 2018 to 31 December 2022 with the owner Denise Smith, but
another owner is needed from 15 February 2020 to 14 August 2020. Because the person responsible is a day-based,
historically relevant field, you create a validity period for exactly that range and enter the other owner there.
Changing the field without a new validity period would change the owner for the whole 2018 to 2022 range.

The on-premise help adds restrictions for the assignments. You can reassign a cost center to a different company code,
business area or profit center later within a fiscal year only if the new company code has the same currency as the old
one, no currency translation is running, only plan data (no actual data) is posted in that fiscal year, and the cost
center is not assigned to a fixed asset, a work center or an HR master record. You can change the object currency only
if no plan or actual data has been posted to the cost center, and it is valid for one fiscal year at a time. If the
company code currencies differ from the controlling area currency, the system adopts the company code currency
automatically. For the object currency see
xref:erp/sap/finance/controlling-overview-and-cost-elements.adoc[Controlling Overview and Cost Elements].

== Cost center groups and the standard hierarchy

You can collect cost centers into cost center groups, and groups into larger groups, so that the structure of the
organization shows in a tree. Individual cost centers form the lowest level. SAP's help states two rules. There must be
at least one cost center group that contains all cost centers of the controlling area and represents the whole business;
this group is the standard hierarchy, and further groups can be assigned to it. You can also create any number of
alternative groups, for example by function. A cost center can belong to only one group of the standard hierarchy but to
as many alternative groups as you like.

The tree below is an illustration for Example Trading Co. The cost center numbers are placeholders. The cost centers
`EX-ADM` (administration), `EX-MKT` (marketing) and `EX-SALES` (sales) are illustrative.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart TD
    A["Standard hierarchy EX00<br/>all cost centers"] --> B["Overhead"]
    A --> C["Selling"]
    B --> D["EX-ADM<br/>Administration"]
    C --> E["EX-MKT<br/>Marketing"]
    C --> F["EX-SALES<br/>Sales"]
    G["Alternative group<br/>Trade fair team"] -.-> E
    G -.-> F
----

Here the alternative group "Trade fair team" collects two cost centers that already sit elsewhere in the standard
hierarchy. That is allowed because the rule limits only the standard hierarchy to one place per cost center.

The group assignment of a cost center to the standard hierarchy holds for the whole lifetime of the cost center, and you
can only change it for the whole lifetime. For evaluations, the information system uses only the current assignment. If
you need an older structure, the on-premise help advises copying the hierarchy before you change it. Its example: you
plan the next fiscal year on a changed copy while the unchanged hierarchy keeps serving the reporting of the current
year.

== Planning and actual costs on a cost center

Cost center planning is part of the overall business planning process. You enter planned figures for costs, activities,
prices or statistical key figures for one cost center and one planning period. When actual costs are posted, you compare
them with the plan values and find the variances. SAP's on-premise help names three planning methods:

* *Primary cost planning* captures the costs and quantities of goods and services bought from outside, for each cost
  center or cost element: labor, external services, materials and operating supplies. Costs can be planned by quantity
  as well as by value.
* *Secondary cost planning* captures the costs and quantities of goods and services obtained internally, such as from
  other cost centers.
* *Activity type planning* captures the activity quantity that the cost center will provide in a period, its capacity
  and the price to be charged to other cost centers for that activity. If other cost centers plan the activity as input,
  their demand appears as scheduled activity.

An activity type divides what a cost center can produce, for example repair hours or assembly hours for a repair shop.
Statistical key figures, such as the number of employees, telephones or floor area, are the basis of indirect allocation
methods and of analyses. All actual allocations of a cost center, such as distribution and assessment, can also be
planned. How the allocations work is the subject of a later page; this page stops at the master data and the comparison.

For the comparison, the on-premise report Cost Centers: Actual/Plan Variance shows actual and plan costs within the
analysis period with their absolute and percentage variance, next to booked activity quantities and statistical key
figures. The report Cost Centers: Actual/Plan/Commitments additionally compares actual, commitment, assigned and plan
values per cost element for cost centers that have purchase orders assigned to them, with the values still available.
In the Public Edition, the analytical apps for plan/actual analysis show the deviations of actual against plan. SAP's
help marks the two cost center apps named Cost Centers - Actuals and Cost Centers - Plan/Actual as deprecated and asks you
to switch to the Cost Centers Review Booklet.

=== Numbers before notation: a variance

Suppose the planned annual costs of cost center `EX-MKT` in controlling area `EX00` are 12,000.00 EUR and the actual costs
reach 13,500.00 EUR. The absolute variance is the difference, 13,500.00 - 12,000.00 = 1,500.00 EUR, and the percentage
variance is that difference divided by the planned costs, 1,500.00 / 12,000.00 = 12.5 percent. In notation:

[stem]
++++
\Delta = A - P = 13\,500.00 - 12\,000.00 = 1\,500.00\ \text{EUR}, \qquad
\Delta\% = \frac{\Delta}{P} = \frac{1\,500.00}{12\,000.00} = 12.5\,\%
++++

where stem:[A] are the actual costs and stem:[P] the planned costs. SAP's help states exactly these two formulas, absolute
variance as actual costs minus planned costs and percentage variance as the absolute variance divided by the planned
costs, for the Product Cost Controlling plan/actual report. The cost center report shows absolute and percentage
variances as well; this page applies the same definition to the example, and the help warns that the variances of these
reports are not the same as those calculated by the separate variance calculation function.

== What an internal order is

SAP's help defines an internal order as an object used to monitor parts of the costs, and under certain circumstances the
revenues, of the organization. Internal orders are normally used to plan, collect and settle the costs of internal jobs
and tasks. They can add detail to one cost center, for example trade fair orders supported by the marketing cost center,
or they can stand for an activity apart from any cost center, such as a research project or an investment project. An
order is different from a commercial order such as a sales or production order: the costs of a commercial order are
ultimately billed to customers, while the costs of an internal order serve internal transparency.

The help describes these kinds of internal order:

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Kind |What it is for
|Overhead cost order
|Time-limited monitoring of overhead costs that arise when you execute a job, or long-term monitoring of part of the overhead costs.

|Investment order
|Monitoring investment costs that can be capitalized and are settled to fixed assets.

|Accrual order
|Monitoring the period-related accrual calculation between expenses posted in Financial Accounting and the costing-based costs in cost accounting.

|Order with revenues
|Monitoring costs and revenues from activities for external partners, or internal activities outside the core business.

|Model order
|A reference to copy when you create new internal orders.
|===

Order management is described as the most detailed operational level of cost and activity accounting. You use it when
cost element accounting or cost center accounting alone does not give enough detail, and to support decisions such as
making a part in-house or buying it.

=== Internal orders in the two editions

Internal orders continue to be supported in on-premise SAP S/4HANA, but the on-premise help notes that new development
focuses on projects rather than internal orders and refers to an SAP Note (2865342; SAP Notes need an S-user login).
The Public Edition help is stricter. On its page for projects, SAP says to use projects instead of internal orders,
because internal orders are not supported in SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition. That page describes projects as the way to
plan, collect and settle the costs of short-term projects, events or tasks, and names the apps Project Control -
Enterprise Projects and Create Internal Projects. For a trade fair in the Public Edition, the equivalent of the order in
the example below is therefore a project. The settlement ideas on this page, a settlement rule made of distribution rules,
apply to project senders too, as the Public Edition help for settlement rules shows.

=== Order status

An internal order has its own life cycle, and its status decides which business transactions are allowed. SAP's help says
every internal order passes through four system statuses, one of which is always active.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart LR
    A["Created<br/>no actual postings"] --> B["Released<br/>nearly everything allowed"]
    B --> C["Technically completed<br/>no planning changes"]
    C --> D["Closed<br/>no cost-relevant transactions"]
----

Each of the four statuses blocks something: in Created, for example, actual postings are not possible; in Released nearly
all business transactions are allowed; in Technically completed you cannot change the planning; in Closed no
cost-relevant business transaction is allowed. Changing the status is itself a business transaction, done with a function
such as Released in the master data maintenance, and you may save a plan date for each status except Created. The system
logs when a status was actually reached. Further system statuses are set by business transactions, for example Locked,
which allows nothing except removing the lock, and Settlement rule created, which the system sets when a settlement rule
exists. A user status, defined through general SAP status management, adds your own rules on when transactions are
allowed.

== Planning, budgets and actual postings on an order

Before an order starts, you can plan its costs roughly and calculate them exactly later. The on-premise help names
activity input planning (planning by cost element and by the activity input that cost centers supply) and the planning of
primary costs and revenues by cost element, also by cost element group with an automatic distribution. You can assign and
manage budgets for orders, and the information system tracks planned and assigned costs through every stage. When
integrated planning with Cost Center Accounting is active and the order is plan-integrated, cost center allocations can
send plan costs to orders: distribution moves the costs by distribution keys and keeps the original primary cost element,
and assessment uses an assessment cost element and assessment keys.

Actual costs reach an order in three ways, according to the help:

* *Postings in Financial Accounting.* You assign primary cost postings, such as external services and deliveries,
  directly to an internal order.
* *Goods movements in Materials Management.* Purchase requisitions, purchase orders, goods receipts, reservations and
  goods withdrawals can be posted statistically to an order. A purchase order posted this way creates a commitment on the
  order, and the commitment becomes actual cost when the goods are received. A goods withdrawal that refers to a reservation
  holding the order number posts actual costs to the order automatically.
* *Internal activity allocations in cost accounting.* Cost centers supply internal activities to the order that received
  them, using the activity type.

You can also create statistical key figures on an order for information only. The help's example is a trade fair order
with key figures for the number of visitors at the stand, requests for further appointments and the total number of
orders arising from the fair. A funds commitment lets you enter costs you know will occur but not yet through which
transaction, so that part of the order budget is reserved early.

An actual posting is possible only if the order's status allows the business transaction. This ties back to the status
list above: a posting to an order that is still in the status Created is rejected.

== Settlement of an internal order

An internal order is an interim collector of costs. When the job is finished, you settle the costs to one or more
receivers, such as a cost center, a fixed asset or a profitability segment. SAP's help gives the conditions and the two
ways to define where the costs go.

To settle an order, its sender needs a settlement rule. A settlement rule is made of one or more distribution rules.
A distribution rule names the settlement receiver, which part of the costs is settled, and the period for which the rule
holds (its Valid From and Valid To fields). Normally there is one distribution rule for each receiver. You must define the
settlement rule before the first settlement, and the scope for later changes is limited. You can settle in two ways, and
only one of them at a time:

* *Settlement to one receiver.* The system allocates 100 percent of the costs on the order either to a cost center or to
  a G/L account under a settlement cost element, and it generates the settlement rule from the order master data. For
  a cost center receiver the settlement cost element has category 21 (internal settlement) and settlement is by period.
  For a G/L account receiver it has category 22 (external settlement) and the system settles cumulatively all costs
  incurred up to and including the settlement period. The document type SA is always used here. If you posted revenues to
  the order, you cannot settle it to cost centers.
* *Comprehensive settlement.* You define a settlement rule yourself, which lets you settle to a wide range of receivers
  (project, sales order, profitability segment and so on), say how the costs are split between receivers, and define the
  cost elements under which the sender is credited and the receivers debited. If you first defined settlement to one
  cost center in the order master data, you must delete that rule before you can define a comprehensive one.

The two category numbers are those of the cost element categories explained on the
xref:erp/sap/finance/controlling-overview-and-cost-elements.adoc[previous page].

=== The share of each receiver

For each receiver you give the portion either as a percentage or as an equivalence number; within distribution rules of
the same settlement type you use one or the other. You can also settle fixed amounts, but only one fixed amount per period,
and an amount rule may settle more than the costs on the sender; that is why SAP advises combining it with percentage
rules. If the percentage check is active in the settlement profile, the system checks the percentages when you save the
settlement rule.

=== Settlement type: PER and FUL

Each distribution rule has a settlement type. Periodic settlement (`PER`) settles by period: only the costs of the
settlement period are included. Full settlement (`FUL`) settles all costs of the sender from all earlier periods that have
not yet been settled. The system proposes a default from the receiver type, for example `PER` for a cost center and `FUL`
for a fixed asset, and you can overwrite it. Investment management adds further types, such as preliminary settlement
(`PRE`) and capitalization to an asset under construction. When a sender has both `PER` and `FUL` rules and the processing
type is Automatic, the `PER` rules are applied first to the costs of the settlement period, and any remaining costs go to
the `FUL` rules.

=== Settlement parameters and the settlement profile

The settlement profile is taken for the order from the order type, and each settlement rule needs one. According to the
help, the profile and its linked structures control the following:

* whether actual costs are settled in full, may be settled, or are not for settlement; a sender set to settle in full
  produces an error when you try to close it or set the deletion flag with a non-zero balance, a sender that can be settled
  produces a warning, and a sender not for settlement produces no message
* the allocation structure, which groups cost elements into groups that are debited on the sender and says for each group
  whether settlement is by cost element or under a settlement cost element, which documents the purpose of an order or
  reduces data volume
* the source structure, which splits the costs into assignments so that primary and secondary costs can go to different
  receivers
* the PA transfer structure, needed only when you settle to Profitability Analysis
* whether a share may be given as a percentage, as an equivalence number or as an amount, whether the percentages must
  add up to 100 percent in periodic settlement, the permitted receivers (for example cost centers and orders but not fixed
  assets for overhead orders), the document type, and the maximum number of distribution rules

The settlement is carried out for one order (individual processing) or for many orders at once (collective processing,
which is normally part of period-end closing and can run in the background). You cannot settle across fiscal year
boundaries. The posting date is always the last day of the posting period, and the system determines it from the period
and the fiscal year. A distribution rule that has been used in a settlement is locked: to change the receiver afterwards
you delete the rule and create a new one for the new receiver. Distribution rules are not specific to a ledger; the same
rules are used for all ledgers. That matters for the two ledgers of Example Trading Co. (`0L` and `2L`): one rule serves
both, and the settlement history per ledger shows in which ledgers and periods it ran.

Planned settlement is separate from actual settlement. It posts the costs planned on an order to cost centers, business
processes or profitability analysis, and it requires that integrated planning of orders with cost center accounting is
active and that the settlement rule contains at least one `PER` rule or a `PRE` rule.

== A worked example for EX00

Example Trading Co. takes part in a trade fair in spring. In controlling area `EX00`, company code `EX01`, the company
opens an overhead cost order for the fair (on premise; in the Public Edition it would be a project, see above). Its
number is a placeholder. Two costs arise, both primary, posted from Financial Accounting with the order as the account
assignment object:

* in period 3, the stand rental of 3,000.00 EUR
* in period 4, travel of 1,200.00 EUR

The order therefore collects 3,000.00 + 1,200.00 = 4,200.00 EUR. The comprehensive settlement rule has two distribution
rules, both of type `PER`: 70 percent to the cost center `EX-MKT` and 30 percent to the cost center `EX-SALES`. The
settlement credits the order under a settlement cost element and debits each cost center under it; for a cost center
receiver SAP's one-receiver page gives category 21 (internal settlement), and its number is not important here. Each month the settlement run takes only
the costs of that period:

[cols="2,2,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Step |Order EX-FAIR (balance) |Cost center `EX-MKT` |Cost center `EX-SALES` |Total in `EX00`
|Period 3 posting of the stand rental |+3,000.00 EUR |0.00 EUR |0.00 EUR |+3,000.00 EUR
|Period 3 settlement (70 and 30 percent) |-3,000.00 EUR |+2,100.00 EUR |+900.00 EUR |0.00 EUR
|Period 4 posting of the travel costs |+1,200.00 EUR |0.00 EUR |0.00 EUR |+1,200.00 EUR
|Period 4 settlement (70 and 30 percent) |-1,200.00 EUR |+840.00 EUR |+360.00 EUR |0.00 EUR
|Balance after both settlements |0.00 EUR |2,940.00 EUR |1,260.00 EUR |4,200.00 EUR
|===

The last column, the total in `EX00`, shows the movements of the step, not a running total. Settlement moves costs from the order
to the cost centers without changing the total costs of the controlling area, and the order ends with a zero balance, so
it can be closed. The order is released before the first posting and closed after the last settlement. With stem:[s_i]
as the share of receiver stem:[i] and stem:[C] as the costs of a settlement period:

[stem]
++++
C_i = C \cdot s_i, \qquad \sum_i s_i = 1
++++

For period 3, with stem:[C = 3\,000.00] and stem:[s_1 = 0.7], stem:[s_2 = 0.3]: stem:[C_1 = 2\,100.00] and
stem:[C_2 = 900.00], which add up to 3,000.00 EUR. Over both periods the cost centers carry
2,100.00 + 840.00 = 2,940.00 EUR and 900.00 + 360.00 = 1,260.00 EUR, and 2,940.00 + 1,260.00 = 4,200.00 EUR, the
amount collected on the order.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Posting to an order in the wrong status.* In the status Created, actual postings are not possible; release the order
  first. In Closed, no cost-relevant transaction is allowed.
* *Mixing the two settlement methods.* A general settlement rule excludes settlement to one receiver, and the other way
  round; delete the first rule before you define the other.
* *Settling an order with revenues to a cost center.* Not possible; settlement to one receiver also refuses it.
* *Closing an order with a balance.* If the settlement profile says to settle in full, the system gives an error when you
  close the order or set the deletion flag while the balance is not zero.
* *Changing a used distribution rule.* A rule used in a settlement is locked. Delete it and create a new one for a new
  receiver, and use the Valid To field to end an old receiver cleanly.
* *Expecting a settlement to cross a fiscal year.* Settlement, unlike assessment, cannot cross fiscal year boundaries.
* *Percentages that do not add up.* If the settlement profile requires periodic settlement to total 100 percent, the
  system checks the percentages when you save the settlement rule.
* *Changing a time-based field in the middle of a period.* A period-based or fiscal-year-based field cannot change in the
  middle of a period or fiscal year once transaction data exists; create a new validity period for the next one.
* *Overlapping validity periods.* They cannot be saved.
* *Changing the cost center currency after posting.* Not possible once plan or actual data exists.
* *Placing a cost center twice in the standard hierarchy.* A cost center has one place there; use alternative groups
  for other views.
* *Expecting internal orders in the Public Edition.* Use projects instead.

== Summary

* A cost center is a place where costs occur; an internal order collects the costs of one temporary job. Both belong to a
  controlling area, and a cost center belongs to one company code.
* Cost center master data has day-based, period-based, fiscal-year-based and time-independent fields, and the Public
  Edition keeps them in validity periods. Some assignments and the currency are restricted once data is posted.
* Cost center groups build the hierarchy. The standard hierarchy contains every cost center once; alternative groups can
  overlap. The Public Edition manages the standard hierarchy as global hierarchy 1.
* Costs are planned on cost centers (primary, secondary and activity type planning) and compared with actual costs:
  absolute variance is actual minus planned, percentage variance is that difference divided by planned.
* An internal order passes through the statuses Created, Released, Technically completed and Closed, and the status decides
  which postings are allowed. The Public Edition does not support internal orders and uses projects.
* Settlement passes the order's costs to receivers by a settlement rule made of distribution rules, with percentages or
  equivalence numbers, by period (`PER`) or in full (`FUL`). It does not change the total of the controlling area.
