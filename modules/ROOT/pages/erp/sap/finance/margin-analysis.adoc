A cost center answers "where did the money go?" and a profit center answers "did this part of the company earn a
profit?". A sales or product manager asks a third question: "which products, customers or regions make money, and how
much is left after the cost of the goods?". Profitability Analysis answers it. It splits the revenues and the costs of
the company by market segments, for example by product group, customer or sales organization, and shows the margin of
each segment. This page explains the form of Profitability Analysis that SAP S/4HANA recommends, called margin
analysis: where its data lives, what a characteristic and a profitability segment are, how a posting receives its
characteristics (true and attributed assignment), how it differs from the older costing-based form, and how a
contribution margin is read. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP
S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608, and says where the two editions differ.

== Key terms

* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#margin-analysis[Margin analysis]: the form of Profitability Analysis that stores
  costs and revenues in accounts and is permanently reconciled with Financial Accounting.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#profitability-segment[Profitability segment]: one combination of values of the
  characteristics of an operating concern; it corresponds to a market segment.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#operating-concern[Operating concern]: the organizational unit for which the sales
  market has a uniform structure; it defines the characteristics that profitability analysis can use.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#characteristic[Characteristic]: a level at which you analyze profitability, such
  as sales organization, region, product or customer.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#true-account-assignment[True account assignment]: a line item whose profitability
  segment was determined by the sending application or entered by hand; it is available to follow-on processes.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#attributed-account-assignment[Attributed account assignment]: a profitability
  segment worked out for a line item from its other account assignment when the journal entry is created; it appears
  only in reports.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#co-pa-derivation[CO-PA derivation]: the tool that fills further characteristics
  from the data already available.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#costing-based-profitability-analysis[Costing-based profitability analysis]: the
  older form that stores values in value fields and needs reconciliation with Financial Accounting.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#contribution-margin[Contribution margin]: what remains of revenue after the costs
  you subtract at a given level of the margin structure.

== What margin analysis is

SAP's help defines Profitability Analysis (CO-PA) as the analysis of profit or contribution margins by market segment or
by strategic business unit, such as a sales organization or business area. Market segments can be classified by
products, customers, orders, or any combination of them. Two forms exist, and the difference between them is where the
values are kept.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Question |Margin analysis |Costing-based profitability analysis
|Where are values stored?
|In accounts, using cost and revenue elements.
|In value fields and costing-based valuation approaches that you define yourself.
|Is it reconciled with Financial Accounting?
|Permanently: SAP's help says the profitability report is always reconciled with the income statement.
|Not automatically. SAP says the cost elements must be converted into value fields, so reconciliation takes more
effort.
|What does SAP recommend in SAP S/4HANA?
|It is the recommended and default method.
|It can run in parallel with margin analysis on premise, but SAP calls margin analysis the preferred solution.
|===

A naming detail saves confusion. SAP states that the term "margin analysis" replaced "account-based profitability
analysis". The older name can still appear on screens and in documentation, and what it says is valid for margin
analysis. Sometimes you will read "CO-PA" for Profitability Analysis as a whole: the abbreviation names the application
component, and margin analysis is the way it works in SAP S/4HANA.

The central idea of the redesign is that margin analysis has no store of its own. The Universal Journal is the single
source of truth for the financial data, so profitability reporting reads the income statement items and can drill down
on any characteristic, for example the product group. Because the characteristics are part of the journal entry, the
numbers of margin analysis and of the income statement cannot drift apart. The Universal Journal page explains why one
table can carry all of these views at once.

SAP also states a boundary that follows from this design. The Universal Journal is integrated only with margin
analysis; costing-based profitability analysis can still run on premise, but as a separate set of data. In the Public
Edition only margin analysis exists (see <<_differences_between_the_editions,Differences between the editions>>).

== The operating concern, characteristics and profitability segments

Three structures carry margin analysis.

* *The operating concern* is, in SAP's words, an organizational unit for which the sales market has a uniform structure,
  and it is the valuation level for profitability analysis. It is structured into characteristics and value fields.
  Value fields are used only in costing-based profitability analysis, not in margin analysis. SAP recommends defining
  and working with only one operating concern.
* *Characteristics* are the levels you want to analyze, such as sales organization, region, product or customer. The
  possible reporting levels depend on which characteristics exist and on how they are created and derived.
* *A profitability segment* is a combination of characteristic values, for example the combination of customer, product,
  plant and distribution channel. Each segment is an analysis object, and the margin can be displayed per segment.

The system finds the operating concern through the organization structure: SAP's help says that when data is
transferred to Profitability Analysis, the operating concern is derived from the controlling area, and the controlling
area from the company code. In the running example, a posting in company code `EX01` leads to controlling area `EX00`,
and from there to the operating concern assigned to `EX00`. Because the operating concern should reflect the structure
of the sales market, SAP says one operating concern is normally enough for the whole organization, and that several
controlling areas are normally assigned to it.

Two more rules matter in practice.

* Not every characteristic is part of the profitability segment. SAP notes that characteristics which are almost always
  filled and nearly always different would make the volume of profitability segments too large, so you can deactivate
  them when a profitability segment is created. The sales order in repetitive manufacturing is the example SAP gives of
  a characteristic that should not be used.
* SAP states that the G/L account and the functional area also determine the margin structure. Both are required for
  multilevel contribution margin accounting, which is why a revenue account and a cost of goods sold account matter as
  much as the product does.

Which characteristics does a posting carry? In the Public Edition, the screen that assigns a profitability segment
shows only the characteristics contained in the operating concern, in four sections: Enterprise Structure, Product and
Customer, Account Assignment and Other. Custom characteristics created with the key user tool for the business context
Market Segment appear in the section Other. SAP's pages mention, as examples of characteristics, the customer, the
customer group, the sales office, the product, the product group, the plant, the sales organization, the distribution
channel, the division, the region and the billing type.

Creating or changing an operating concern, and assigning controlling areas to it, are Customizing activities (on
premise: in Customizing for Profitability Analysis, Structures, Define Operating Concern; the assignment in Customizing
for the company structure, under Assignment, Controlling). They need an SAP system and are not described step by step
here (requires an SAP system; not run here). When you work in the application, SAP's help describes a dialog
(SAP menu: Accounting, Controlling, Profitability Analysis, Environment, Set Operating Concern) in which you set the
operating concern and the type of profitability analysis, costing-based or account-based. If you work with one operating
concern only, you can store it and the type in your user master data, so the dialog no longer appears at logon.

== How a posting receives its characteristics

Every line of a journal entry that is relevant to margin analysis can carry the characteristics of a profitability
segment. SAP distinguishes two cases, and the distinction decides what you can do with the line afterwards.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Question |True account assignment |Attributed account assignment
|Who determines the segment?
|Normally the sending application, for example the sales order or the billing document. Also a manual assignment on the
account assignment screen, or an automatic assignment defined for the G/L account in Customizing.
|The system, when the journal entry is created, from the object the line is posted to (cost center, internal order,
project, sales order, production order, maintenance order or service transaction). The function must be activated in
Customizing.
|Where does it appear?
|In reports and in follow-on processes such as allocations, settlement and top-down distribution.
|In reports only. Most follow-on processes do not see it.
|Examples
|A goods issue or billing document item in a sell-from-stock scenario; a manual FI posting to a profitability segment.
|Labor cost posted to a cost center that serves one product group; a cost on a project that has a sales order.
|===

The reason for the second column is practical. Many costs, such as the wages of a packing team, are posted to a cost
center and not to a product. If the system waited for a period-end allocation, a product report would show those costs
only after closing. With attributed assignment a best guess is made at the moment of posting, using the information
available then, and the line appears in the product reports at once. SAP says a refinement at period end is still
possible with the closing transactions: settlement, allocation and top-down distribution.

=== CO-PA derivation

Characteristics are filled by a tool called CO-PA derivation. It holds a fixed set of rules that derive further
characteristics from the data already on the line. SAP gives two examples. The product group of the product sold is
derived from the material master when a revenue line is created by a billing document. And a derivation step can assign
a product group to a cost center: if cost center C100 works only for product group P1, every posting to C100, such as
labor costs, receives P1 and is read in product group reporting. Deriving market segments early in the process,
SAP adds, generally removes the need for some period-end closing activities.

The logic for attributed assignment depends on the object. SAP's page describes these chains, among others:

* *Cost center:* CO-PA derivation is called with the available data, and the enriched data is written back to the line.
* *Internal order:* if the order has a settlement rule to a profitability segment, that segment is used. Otherwise the
  system looks for a sales order item, then for a unique sales order, then for a billing document being posted, and
  derives from what it finds.
* *Sales order item:* the settlement rule of the item first, then a profitability segment the item carries, then the
  customer and product of the sales order with CO-PA derivation.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart TD
  A[Journal entry line] --> B{Segment already<br/>determined by the<br/>sending application?}
  B -- yes --> C[True account assignment:<br/>characteristics copied to the line]
  B -- no --> D{Attributed assignment<br/>active and object is cost center,<br/>order, project, sales order<br/>or service transaction?}
  D -- yes --> E[Look for settlement rule,<br/>sales order item or sales order]
  E --> F[CO-PA derivation fills<br/>further characteristics]
  F --> G[Attributed segment written to the line:<br/>reports only]
  D -- no --> H[No profitability segment]
  C --> I[Available in reports and<br/>in allocations and settlement]
----

Two mapping rules keep the line consistent. Fields of the organization structure that are relevant to the general
ledger, such as company code, controlling area, profit center, partner profit center, segment, business area and
functional area, are never overwritten by the profitability data. Account assignment and organization fields such as
cost center, order, plant or customer are filled from the segment only when they are empty, with one exception: for a
true assignment, fields such as plant, sales organization, distribution channel, division, customer and billing type
are always copied from the segment into the line, to avoid inconsistent data. SAP also notes that data of a sales order
is used only when the billing company code of the order equals the company code of the line being attributed.

In the Universal Journal, the segment itself is represented on the line by a profitability segment number; SAP's page on
deferred summarization names the field `PAOBJNR` in ACDOCA. When the operating concern is generated, SAP says, all
characteristics not yet available, including customer-specific ones, are appended to the journal entry and filled when
the posting is made.

== Where the data comes from

SAP's help lists the application components whose primary, profit-related postings margin analysis can assign to
profitability segments: Financial Accounting (G/L account postings), Materials Management (goods issues, invoice
receipts, purchase orders), Sales and Distribution (sales orders and billing documents) and period-end closing. In
period-end closing you can assign internal activities to segments, assess cost center costs and business process costs
to segments, and settle orders, projects and cost objects to segments. The page adds that the period-end processes of
cost center assessment and settlement are essentially a top-down distribution function for specific cost objects.

*Top-down distribution* is the function for an amount that was posted at an aggregated level, for example a freight
invoice booked on a product group. It spreads the amount to a finer level, such as the product, using a reference like
sales revenue. The sender rule is Posted Amounts and the receiver rule is Variable Portions, as the
xref:erp/sap/finance/allocations.adoc[Allocations] page explains. Allocation cycles created for the Margin Analysis
context can be scheduled with the job Universal Allocation Run - Margin Analysis, which supports test runs, live runs
and reversals, single or recurring, by cycle or by cycle group. On premise, SAP's page for deferred summarization names
the transaction `KE28` for top-down distribution (requires an SAP system; not run here).

== Contribution margins

A contribution margin is what is left of revenue after you subtract costs at one step of a cost structure. SAP does not
fix one list of steps: the structure is built from the G/L accounts and functional areas of the income statement, and
what you call a first or a second level is a choice of your reporting. This page uses two steps for the example, because
two are enough to show the idea. The margin of a segment is then

[stem]
++++
M_s = R_s - \sum_k K_{s,k}
++++

where stem:[R_s] is the revenue of profitability segment stem:[s] and stem:[K_{s,k}] is the cost of kind stem:[k], for
example cost of goods sold or freight, that is assigned to it. The margin rate is stem:[M_s / R_s].

=== Worked example for company code EX01

Example Trading Co. sells two product groups, `PG-A` and `PG-B`, from company code `EX01` (EUR, group currency GBP,
ledger `0L`, controlling area `EX00`). All numbers are illustrative values for this site, not delivered content, and
tax is left out. The made-up rate is 0.85 GBP for 1 EUR. Four events happen in the month:

. Billing documents of 12,000.00 EUR: 8,000.00 EUR for `PG-A` and 4,000.00 EUR for `PG-B`. The billing documents carry
  the segment, so this is true account assignment.
. Cost of goods sold posted at delivery: 4,800.00 EUR for `PG-A` and 2,200.00 EUR for `PG-B`, also true assignment.
. Packing costs of 600.00 EUR on the cost center `EX-PACK`, which works only for `PG-A`. A derivation step gives every
  posting to the cost center the product group `PG-A`, so the line has an attributed segment.
. A freight invoice of 900.00 EUR, posted at an aggregated level without a product group, is spread by revenue with
  top-down distribution.

The freight distribution uses the same arithmetic as the allocation page. With stem:[C] the amount to spread and
stem:[f_i] the revenue of receiver stem:[i],

[stem]
++++
C_i = C \cdot \frac{f_i}{\sum_j f_j}, \qquad C_{\text{PG-A}} = 900 \cdot \frac{8000}{12000} = 600, \qquad C_{\text{PG-B}} = 900 \cdot \frac{4000}{12000} = 300
++++

and 600 plus 300 equals the 900 that was posted. The result per product group, in EUR, follows.

[cols="2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Step |`PG-A` |`PG-B` |Total
|Revenue |8,000.00 |4,000.00 |12,000.00
|Cost of goods sold |-4,800.00 |-2,200.00 |-7,000.00
|*Margin after cost of goods sold* |*3,200.00* |*1,800.00* |*5,000.00*
|Margin rate |40.0% |45.0% |41.7%
|Packing cost (attributed) |-600.00 |0.00 |-600.00
|Freight (distributed) |-600.00 |-300.00 |-900.00
|*Margin after packing and freight* |*2,000.00* |*1,500.00* |*3,500.00*
|Margin rate |25.0% |37.5% |29.2%
|Same margin in GBP at 0.85 |1,700.00 |1,275.00 |2,975.00
|===

The checks are simple. Revenue minus cost of goods sold is 12,000 - 7,000 = 5,000, and 5,000 - 600 - 900 = 3,500. In
GBP, 3,500 x 0.85 = 2,975.00, and the two segments give 1,700.00 + 1,275.00 = 2,975.00. The result of the example is the
point of the exercise: on the first step `PG-B` looks better (45.0% against 40.0%), and after the costs that belong to
the products it is still better (37.5% against 25.0%) but the gap is wider, which only the attributed packing cost
explains. Without the derivation step the 600.00 EUR would have waited as unassigned cost until a period-end allocation.
The company result, 3,500.00 EUR, does not change when a cost is distributed between segments, in the same way that a
distribution to profit centers moves cost without creating any.

The same journal entry lines also carry the profit center. Margin analysis cuts the postings by market, Profit Center
Accounting cuts them by responsibility, and both read the same ACDOCA lines, so neither one needs a transfer from the
other.

== Data volume

SAP describes one option for very large data volumes. Deferred summarization for margin analysis reduces the number of
journal entry lines that carry profitability characteristics. SAP calls it a function for exceptionally large data
volumes with critical performance and storage problems, to be used only when other summarization options are already in
use, because it limits the flexibility of reporting and its effects normally cannot be reversed. A report named
`FINS_MA_DSUM_ANALYZE` estimates the reduction before you decide. Journal entries processed by it should not be handled
with top-down distribution through `KE28`. Treat it as a decision for a specialist who has checked the SAP Notes named on
the page (requires an SAP system; not run here).

== Differences between the editions

* *Operating concern.* On premise you define the operating concern in Customizing and generate it. In the Public
  Edition, SAP states that one account-based operating concern is predefined and runs in the background, that you cannot
  maintain it, that you cannot generate a user-defined one in parallel and that there are no template operating
  concerns. SAP's FAQ names the predefined operating concern A000.
* *Costing-based profitability analysis.* Available on premise, in parallel with margin analysis. In the Public Edition
  SAP states that it is not possible and not planned.
* *Derivation.* On premise, SAP's page describes CO-PA derivation as a tool with its own rules. In the Public Edition
  a fixed set of derivation rules is delivered for the characteristics of A000 and cannot be changed, and you define
  substitution rules for the business context Market Segment with the Manage Substitution/Validation Rules app. For
  extension SAP names the BAdI Market Segment Extension Fields Substitution (`FINS_MARKET_SEGMENT_SUB`).
* *Attributed assignment.* SAP says it is available in the Public Edition, and that SAP Best Practices content is
  shipped to activate it automatically. On premise it is a Customizing activation.
* *Deactivation.* SAP states that margin analysis cannot be deactivated in the Public Edition.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Expecting attributed lines in allocations.* Settlement, allocation and top-down distribution read only true
  assignments. A cost that shows in a product report through attribution is not a sender or receiver for them, and the
  newly created lines are attributed again by the system's logic.
* *Looking for value fields.* Value fields belong to costing-based profitability analysis. A report that you expect to
  find in "value fields" in margin analysis will not exist; use the accounts and the characteristics.
* *Too many characteristics in the segment.* A characteristic that is almost always filled and different on every line
  makes the number of profitability segments, and the data volume, grow without helping the analysis.
* *Reading an old name as a different thing.* "Account-based profitability analysis" on a screen or in an old guide
  means margin analysis.
* *Comparing a product report with the profit center report.* They cut the same postings differently, so a segment total
  is not expected to equal a profit center total; compare each with the income statement instead.

== Summary

* Margin analysis is the recommended form of Profitability Analysis in SAP S/4HANA. It stores costs and revenues in
  accounts, in the Universal Journal, so it is permanently reconciled with Financial Accounting.
* The operating concern defines the characteristics, such as product or customer. A combination of characteristic values
  is a profitability segment, and the system finds the operating concern from the company code through the controlling
  area.
* A line has a true assignment when the sending application or a manual entry gives it a segment, and an attributed
  assignment when the system derives one from the cost center, order, project or sales order. Only true assignments
  reach allocations and settlement; attributed ones are in reports.
* Contribution margins are revenue minus the costs at a chosen step. In the example, `PG-A` earns 2,000.00 EUR and
  `PG-B` 1,500.00 EUR after packing and freight, 3,500.00 EUR (2,975.00 GBP) in total.
* On premise you can define the operating concern and run costing-based profitability analysis in parallel; in the
  Public Edition one account-based operating concern, A000, is predefined and costing-based analysis does not exist.
