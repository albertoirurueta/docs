A plan says what a company expects to spend. A budget says what a manager is allowed to spend. Both are numbers kept next to
the actual postings, so that Controlling can show the difference. This page explains how cost centers and internal orders
are planned, why a budget is a different thing from a plan, how a budget is kept in step with the real postings by a
check called availability control, and where planning is done with SAP Analytics Cloud instead of in the ERP system. It is
written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. Cost
center planning with planning layouts and the order budget are on-premise topics in SAP's help; the budget availability
control for cost centers, the plan categories and the import app are described for the Public Edition, and the page says
which edition each statement comes from.

== Key terms

* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#planning[Planning]: entering expected costs, activities, prices or statistical key
  figures for a period, so that actual values can be compared with them.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#plan-version[Plan version]: a separate copy of plan data, so that alternative plans
  can be kept side by side.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#budget[Budget]: the approved, binding cost structure for an object such as an
  internal order or a cost center.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#availability-control[Availability control]: the check that compares the funds already
  used with the budget and reacts when a limit is reached.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#assigned-value[Assigned value]: the amount counted against a budget, which includes
  commitments as well as actual costs.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#commitment[Commitment]: a purchase requisition or purchase order that will cause
  a cost later but has not been posted as a cost yet.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#tolerance-limit[Tolerance limit]: the usage level or absolute amount at which
  availability control issues a warning or an error.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#integrated-planning[Integrated planning]: planning in which plan values are also
  written as plan line items and passed on, so that they can be used by other applications.

== Planning and budgeting are different things

SAP's help for internal orders puts the difference in one sentence: in contrast to planning, budget management is binding.
In the planning phase you estimate costs. During the approval phase, funds are prescribed by the budget. A plan can be
changed whenever the company revises its expectations and kept in several versions; a budget is an approval that the
system then enforces when costs are posted.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Aspect |Plan |Budget
|Purpose |An estimate of expected costs, activities and prices. |The approved limit for an object.
|Binding? |No. It is the basis for variance analysis. |Yes. Availability control can warn or block when it is reached.
|Versions |Several plan versions can be kept in parallel. |One current budget, derived from the original budget and its updates.
|Typical objects |Cost centers, activity types, internal orders, statistical key figures. |Internal orders, projects and, with budget availability control, cost centers.
|===

The two meet in the actual postings. Plan values are compared with actual costs to find variances. Budget values are
compared with actual costs and commitments to decide whether the next posting is allowed.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart LR
    A["Plan<br/>expected costs, versions"] --> B["Budget<br/>approved limit"]
    B --> C["Commitments<br/>purchase requisitions and orders"]
    B --> D["Actual costs<br/>journal entries"]
    C --> E["Assigned value"]
    D --> E
    E --> F{"Availability control<br/>within tolerance limits?"}
    F -->|"yes"| G["Posting accepted"]
    F -->|"limit reached"| H["Warning, mail or error"]
----

== Planning cost centers

SAP's help describes cost center planning as part of the overall business planning process: you enter plan figures for
costs, activities, prices or statistical key figures for one cost center and one planning period, and later determine the
variances against the costs actually incurred. The help divides the work into planning areas.

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Planning area |What you plan, as SAP's help describes it
|Primary cost planning
|Costs and consumption quantities of goods and services from outside sources for each cost center or cost element, so they can be planned by quantity as well as by value. SAP lists labor costs, external services, material costs and operating supplies.

|Secondary cost planning
|Costs and consumption quantities of goods and services from internal sources, such as other cost centers.

|Activity type planning
|The activity quantity the cost center provides in a period, its capacity, and the price charged to other cost centers for that activity.

|Statistical key figure planning
|Figures such as employees, telephones or floor area, used as the basis for distribution and assessment and for analysis in the information system.

|Revenue planning
|Expected revenue, planned like activity-independent primary costs on special cost elements. Revenue posted on a cost center is only statistical, and SAP reminds you to enter revenue planning with a negative sign.
|===

Activity type planning deserves a closer look because it feeds allocation. The help says its goal is to capture the
activity quantity, the capacity and the price. If activities are planned on other cost centers, their demand appears as
scheduled activity on the sender. The plan activity or the capacity is the basis for iterative price calculation,
depending on how accurately you can plan activity and whether you want seasonal variation to show in the price. When you
plan an activity type, the system also creates an unspecified credit on the sender cost center, posted with a secondary
cost element and based on the planned activity quantity and price. With each activity input, the credit on the sender is
reduced by the quantity consumed, and in reconciled planning (planned activity equals the total of the inputs) the
unspecified credit is zero. The page xref:erp/sap/finance/controlling-overview-and-cost-elements.adoc[Controlling Overview
and Cost Elements] explains the secondary cost elements that carry such credits.

Cost center costs can be planned manually or automatically. For automatic primary cost planning SAP names plan
distribution, and for automatic secondary cost planning it names assessment, driven by user-defined rules. Plan costs can
also be transferred to cost center planning from Human Resources and from Asset Accounting, and plan allocations from a
cost center to internal orders are possible when integrated planning is active.

=== How planning is done on premise

SAP's help for SAP S/4HANA on premise describes two ways to enter cost center plan data.

* *Planning layouts.* Entry screens held in planner profiles, subdivided into planning areas such as cost elements or
  activity type planning. The standard system ships profiles, and you can define your own layouts with the Report Painter
  tools. The help says planning is an iterative process that goes through several cycles, which is why alternative plan
  versions are saved in parallel.
* *Input-ready queries.* A newer approach combines data entry with input-ready queries and planning functions in SAP BW
  on the SAP S/4HANA database layer, which the help says simplifies cost center planning compared with planning layouts.
  It needs SAP Business Client and named roles and Web Dynpro application configurations, which the help lists on the
  cost center planning page.

Which of the two a company uses is a project decision. The queries and configuration names are delivered by SAP and are
listed on the pages cited here; this page does not repeat them. Before planning starts, the help's checklist asks you to
clarify distribution keys, planner profiles and layouts, the currencies to plan in, the versions you need, whether
changes must be documented, and which cost centers need detailed planning. One caution from the checklist is worth
remembering: once an area has been planned with detailed planning, you must keep using detailed planning for that area.

== Plan versions and integrated planning

When a controlling area is created, SAP's help says the system creates the plan/actual version 0, in which actual values
are posted. If you plan in only one version you can plan in version 0. If you want several plan versions, you define them
before you start (Customizing activity Maintain Versions). Copying a version needs an authorization to copy in the source
version.

*Integrated planning* decides whether plan values are written as plan line items and passed on to other applications. The
help sets two switches that work together for internal orders and projects: the Integrated Planning indicator in the
order master data (which can be defaulted from the order type) and the integrated planning indicator for Cost Center
Accounting and Activity-Based Costing in the version.

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Combination |Order plan-integrated |Order not plan-integrated
|Version indicator active |Case 1: integrated planning is active for the order. Activity inputs can be planned, scheduled activity is updated on the sender, and settlement to cost centers, internal orders, WBS elements and profitability segments is possible in the plan. |Case 2: the order is planned locally only, despite the active indicator in the version.
|Version indicator not active |Case 3: SAP says this is useful only to make a complete copy of a plan-integrated version to document the planning history. |Case 4: the order is planned locally only and no integrated planning occurs.
|===

Two further statements from the same page prevent surprises. A project cannot be plan-integrated and statistical at the
same time. And, because every change of an integrated plan updates plan line items, SAP advises postponing the transfer
to other applications until cost center and order planning are complete, since only the final results matter there. The
help's summary of planning types says integrated planning with Cost Center Accounting is allowed for overall planning,
primary cost planning, revenue planning and activity input planning, and not allowed for unit costing. When a version is
locked, you can no longer plan on plan-integrated internal orders, but you can still plan on non-plan-integrated ones.

Planning overhead costs adds one more rule. Overhead rates are applied from a costing sheet and an overhead key, and the
system debits orders and cost centers with overhead whether or not integrated planning is active, but credits the
credit objects only when integrated planning is active for the order and for all the credit objects.

== The budget of an internal order

SAP's help for internal orders defines the budget as the approved cost structure of an order or an order group. The budget
is built from three types, and the current budget is derived from them:

[stem]
++++
\text{current budget} = \text{original budget} + \text{supplements} - \text{returns}
++++

The original budget is what was first assigned, before any update. Supplements and returns are the updates for unforeseen
events, such as price rises for external activities. Every change saved in budgeting is documented as a budget line item.
The help adds that the method for assigning and updating a budget and for setting up availability control is similar to
that of projects, except that projects are organized in a hierarchy, and that investment programs manage budgets that
cover more than one order.

At year end, budget that has not been used up can be carried forward. SAP defines the unused budget mainly as the
difference between the planned budget and the actual costs incurred. Commitments are not taken into account when the
unused budget is calculated, so commitments are carried forward first. The run can be a test run or an update run, and
the help states that there is no availability control during the budget carryforward. Carryforward is allowed only for
objects that are created, released or technically completed, never for closed objects or those with a deletion indicator,
and negative budget cannot be carried forward.

== Availability control

Availability control monitors funds by comparing the current distributable budget (for projects, alternatively the
release) with the assigned value. SAP distinguishes two modes. Passive availability control gives an overview of the funds,
their assignment and their type. Active availability control prevents too many funds from being assigned: when you enter
a business transaction that creates costs on a Controlling object, for example posting an invoice, the system checks
whether enough budget is left, using the tolerance limits set in Customizing.

=== What counts as an assigned value

For internal orders, SAP's help calculates the assigned value from these items.

* Actual costs.
* Statistical actual costs.
* Commitments.
* Debits from settlement to the internal order.
* Credits from settlement to a budget-controlled object (an internal order or a WBS element).

The following do not count: actual costs on a cost element that Customizing defines as an exempt cost element, actual
costs from a business transaction that is not in a valid transaction group of availability control, credits from
settlement to an object that is not budget-controlling, such as a cost center, and revenues. Exempt cost elements are set
up in the optional Customizing step Define Exempt Cost Elements for Availability Control.

=== Tolerance limits and transaction groups

The tolerance limits are set for each controlling area, each budget profile and each business transaction group. SAP's
help gives these rules.

* Three reactions are possible: a warning (action 1), a warning plus a mail to the project manager (action 2), or an error
  message (action 3).
* A limit can be a *usage level*, the assigned funds as a percentage of the budget, or an *absolute variance*, the
  difference between assigned funds and budget as an amount, or both. If both are defined for a profile and a transaction
  group, the action is triggered when the assigned funds exceed either of them.
* An absolute variance can only be given in the controlling area currency, whatever the budget profile says.
* SAP recommends that you specify either a percentage or an absolute variance, not both.
* Every business transaction belongs to a transaction group. The general group, All transaction groups, avoids defining
  limits for every group separately; a limit defined for one specific group takes precedence over the general one, and the
  general limit is then ignored for that group. Groups named in SAP's help include purchase requisition, purchase order,
  goods withdrawal, Financial Accounting document (postings in Financial Accounting), Controlling document (postings in
  cost accounting), budgeting and funds reservation (a manual commitment).
* The budgeting group exists because a budget can be reduced after funds are already assigned. If that pushes the assigned
  funds over the tolerance limit, the system points you to an error log when you check or save the budget.

The setup is bundled in the budget profile, where you define all values the budgeting process needs, such as the activation
type. SAP's help says availability control is activated in the background when the budget profile uses activation type 2,
and that you can still activate it online for one object before the usage level is reached or the next batch run is
due. Once activated, every posting on the object is checked.

=== A worked tolerance example for EX00

SAP's help shows availability control of a current overall budget of EUR 100,000 with three limits. The same numbers
are used here for an internal order `EX-ORD1` (an illustrative order of controlling area `EX00`, company code `EX01`,
currency EUR). Suppose the order started with an original budget of 90,000.00 EUR, received a supplement of 15,000.00 EUR
and returned 5,000.00 EUR:

[stem]
++++
90\,000.00 + 15\,000.00 - 5\,000.00 = 100\,000.00\ \text{EUR}
++++

The limits of SAP's example, written as formulas with budget stem:[B], assigned value stem:[A], usage level stem:[p]
and absolute variance stem:[v], are reached when stem:[A \ge p \cdot B] for a usage level, and when stem:[A \ge B + v] for
an absolute variance.

[cols="1,1,2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Group |Action |Limit |Assigned value that reaches it |Reaction in SAP's example
|All transaction groups |1 |Usage level 80 % |stem:[0.80 \times 100\,000 = 80\,000] EUR |A warning is issued.
|All transaction groups |2 |Absolute variance 1,000 EUR |stem:[100\,000 + 1\,000 = 101\,000] EUR |A warning is issued and a mail is sent to the project manager.
|01 |3 |Usage level 110 % |stem:[1.10 \times 100\,000 = 110\,000] EUR |SAP describes the effect as preventing purchase orders by an error message.
|===

Now follow `EX-ORD1` through some postings, with illustrative amounts. The assigned value is actual costs plus commitments:

[cols="3,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Step |Actual costs |Commitments |Assigned value
|Supplier invoices posted |60,000.00 EUR |0.00 EUR |60,000.00 EUR
|Open purchase orders and requisitions |60,000.00 EUR |25,000.00 EUR |85,000.00 EUR
|A further purchase order of 20,000.00 EUR |60,000.00 EUR |45,000.00 EUR |105,000.00 EUR
|===

The assigned value is the sum of the two columns before it, and the table can be checked line by line:

[stem]
++++
60\,000 + 0 = 60\,000, \qquad 60\,000 + 25\,000 = 85\,000, \qquad 60\,000 + 45\,000 = 105\,000
++++

After step 1 the usage level is 60 %, below every limit. After step 2 it is 85 %, so the 80,000 EUR warning limit has been
passed and the user sees a warning. After step 3 the assigned value of 105,000.00 EUR is above both the 80,000 EUR and
the 101,000 EUR limits, so the mail limit is reached; it is still below 110,000 EUR, so the error for the purchase order
group in this example is not triggered yet. The amounts and the reactions come from the arithmetic above and SAP's
table; the posting steps are an illustration, not output of an SAP system.

== Budget availability control for cost centers

A cost center can be budget-controlled too. SAP's help says budget availability control lets you control budget consumption
in the cost centers you are responsible for, from budgeting to cost postings, and gives budget reporting for the budget
and cost-carrying objects. It starts with budget maintenance, runs with each commitment posting and actual cost posting,
and ends with the analysis of costs and budget.

The pieces, as the Public Edition help describes them:

* *Budget availability control profile.* It bundles the relevant cost items, the tolerance limits for budget consumption
  and the system response. You assign it to each budget-carrying cost center and switch availability control on in the
  cost center master data.
* *Budget currency type.* The currency type used to compare costs and commitments with the budget: company code currency or
  global currency. The budget data must exist in the currency type you choose.
* *Account groups.* The groups of accounts or cost items that the check considers. If an account is not in an account group
  of the profile, the Cost Center Budget Report app shows no data for it.
* *Tolerance limits per activity group.* Activity groups bundle posting procedures that should be handled identically. Each
  limit has a message type. A warning lets the budget be used past the limit; an error stops the posting, so commitments
  such as purchase orders or requisitions cannot be posted.
* *Plan category.* Cost center budgets are stored in plan categories. SAP delivers PLN (Plan Data Periodic), BUDGET01
  (Project Budgeting) and BUDGET02 (Cost Center Budgeting). The Customizing activity is Maintain Category for Planning.
* *Cost Center Budget Report.* The app (App ID F3871) shows budget, plan data, actual costs, commitments and available
  budget for budget-carrying cost centers, and lets you navigate to the line items for actual costs and commitments.

An existing profile cannot be deleted, and only its tolerance limits and currency settings can be changed. To change
anything else, SAP says to create a new profile and assign it to the cost center.

When the budget is imported for cost centers, SAP's help says to import for one or more accounts of each account group
in the profile, for a fiscal year, and in the currency the profile expects, and it recommends posting period 1: a budget
loaded in period 8 is not shown by a budget report that runs year to date June 30. The Public Edition FAQ adds that
purchase requisitions and orders produce commitments for the budget check only if scope item 2I3 (Predictive Commitments
Management) is active, that only projects and cost centers are supported as account assignments for such commitments,
and that the status of a purchase order, such as Rejected, does not reverse its commitment: delete the item or set a
closing indicator to do that.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Treating a plan as a limit.* A plan never stops a posting. Only a budget with availability control can warn or block.
* *Forgetting commitments.* The assigned value includes commitments, so a purchase order can trigger the check before any
  invoice arrives. Budget carryforward ignores commitments, so carry them forward first.
* *Mixing percentage and absolute limits.* If both are defined, either one triggers the action. SAP recommends using one.
* *An absolute variance in the wrong currency.* It can only be given in the controlling area currency.
* *A cost center report with no data.* The account is not in an account group of the profile, or the budget was imported
  with another currency type or in a period the report does not cover.
* *Editing a profile.* A budget availability control profile cannot be deleted and mostly cannot be changed; create a
  new one.
* *Planning a project as plan-integrated and statistical.* SAP states a project cannot be both.
* *Switching on integrated planning too early.* Every plan change then writes plan line items and slows the system; SAP
  advises transferring plan data after planning is complete.
* *Expecting a budget check without commitments.* In the Public Edition, requisitions and orders create commitments for
  the check only when scope item 2I3 is active.

== Summary

* A plan is an estimate kept in versions and compared with actual costs. A budget is an approval that the system can
  enforce.
* Cost center planning covers primary and secondary costs, activity types and prices, statistical key figures and revenue.
  On premise it uses planning layouts or input-ready queries, and both editions can use SAP Analytics Cloud.
* Integrated planning writes plan line items so other applications can use the plan, and it depends on indicators in the
  object and in the version.
* The current budget of an order is the original budget plus supplements minus returns.
* Availability control compares the assigned value, which includes commitments, with the budget, and reacts at tolerance
  limits given as a usage level, an absolute variance or both.
* Cost centers can be budget-controlled with a budget availability control profile, plan category BUDGET02 and the Cost
  Center Budget Report app.
