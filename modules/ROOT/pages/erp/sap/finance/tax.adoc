Almost every invoice a company sends or receives carries a tax, such as value added tax (VAT) in Europe or sales tax in the
United States. The tax belongs to the tax office, not to the company, so the books must show it on its own line and on its
own account. A clerk should not work it out by hand on every document. SAP solves this with a tax code: a short key that
stands for a tax rate and a set of rules, which the system uses to check, calculate and post the tax. This page explains
what a tax code holds, how the system calculates the amount from it, how input tax and output tax differ, what happens
when part of the tax cannot be reclaimed, and how sales tax in the United States adds a location (the jurisdiction) to the
calculation. After reading it you can read a tax line in a document, check the arithmetic, and know which setting to look
at when a tax amount or tax account is wrong. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private
Edition) and, where stated, SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. Tax rates and rules differ by country, so the SAP
pages cited here use country examples (Germany, Brazil, the United States) and the rates on this page are made up.

== Key terms

* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#tax-code[Tax code]: a short key that stands for a tax rate and its rules, entered on a
  line item so that the system can check, calculate and post the tax.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#input-tax[Input tax]: tax a company pays on purchases and expenses, which it can
  normally claim back from the tax office.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#output-tax[Output tax]: tax a company charges on sales, which it pays to the tax
  office.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#tax-type[Tax type]: the kind of tax a tax code carries, such as input tax, output
  tax or non-deductible input tax; it decides how the amount is calculated and posted.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#tax-calculation-procedure[Tax calculation procedure]: the country-specific list of
  tax types (condition types) and the rules that calculate and post them.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#condition-type[Condition type]: one line in a calculation procedure; in tax
  calculation it is a tax type with its base amount and account key.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#non-deductible-input-tax[Non-deductible input tax]: the part of an input tax that the
  company cannot claim back and that therefore becomes a cost.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#target-tax-code[Target tax code]: the tax code that a deferred tax is moved to when
  the tax is recognized.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#check-indicator[Check indicator]: a setting on a tax code that decides whether a
  manually entered tax amount that differs from the calculated one gives a warning or an error.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#tax-jurisdiction-code[Tax jurisdiction code]: a multi-level key for the taxing
  authorities of a location, used with the tax code to find the tax amount (United States and Brazil).

== The running example: Example Trading Co.

Example Trading Co. has company code `EX01`, books in euro (EUR), reports to a parent that consolidates in British pounds
(GBP), and posts to the ledgers `0L` and `2L` in client 100. The tax lines on this page are ordinary lines of the
accounting document, so they are stored for each ledger and currency like every other line (see
xref:erp/sap/finance/ledgers-and-currencies.adoc[Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates]). All amounts are euro amounts
in the company code currency. The tax rates (19 percent, 7 percent and the splits of a non-deductible tax) and the
account names are illustrative values chosen for this site, not the legal rates of a country and not SAP defaults. The
jurisdiction example near the end is a United States case and so stands outside the euro books of `EX01`.

== What tax on sales and purchases is

SAP describes input and output tax like this: the tax is calculated on revenue or expense items (the base amount), the tax
amounts are posted to separate tax accounts, and they are refunded by the tax office (input tax) or paid to the tax office
(output tax). The percentage rates vary from country to country and are set when the tax codes are defined. The system
posts the tax amount to the same side of the account as the G/L account items of the same document. So an expense with
input tax debits both the expense and the input tax account, and a sale with output tax credits both the revenue and the
output tax account.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/3cb1182b4a184bdd93f8d62e3f1f0741/2847d953189a424de10000000a174cb4.html[Tax on Sales/Purchases: Input and Output Tax (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== The arithmetic

A tax code carries a rate stem:[r], and the base amount is stem:[B], the sum of the revenue or expense items before tax.
The tax is

[stem]
++++
T = B \cdot r \qquad\text{and the gross amount is}\qquad G = B + T = B(1 + r).
++++

Invoices usually show the gross amount, so the system also works backward. SAP's description says that the tax is included
in the invoice amount: with a rate of 15 percent the invoice amount is 115 percent of the base, so to calculate the tax the
invoice amount is divided by 115 and multiplied by 15. In symbols

[stem]
++++
T = G \cdot \frac{r}{1 + r}.
++++

SAP's own example posts an invoice of EUR 1,150.00 with 15 percent input or output tax, and the tax is EUR 150.00:
stem:[1150 \times 15 / 115 = 150]. The same SAP topic notes that the base amount is determined "percentage included"
almost always (travel expenses in Germany are an exception) and gives the alternative, "percentage separate", as
stem:[1000 \times 15 / 100 = 150].

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/8fbeed5f2046489696a50ac7fd76f9c6/2547d953189a424de10000000a174cb4.html[Specifications for the Tax Type (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== Output tax: a sale by EX01

`EX01` sells goods for a net amount of EUR 10,000.00 with an illustrative output tax of 19 percent. The tax is
stem:[T = 10{,}000.00 \times 0.19 = 1{,}900.00] and the gross amount is
stem:[G = 10{,}000.00 \times 1.19 = 11{,}900.00]. Going backward from the gross amount gives the same tax,
stem:[11{,}900.00 \times 0.19 / 1.19 = 1{,}900.00].

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Debit |Credit

|Customer |11,900.00 |
|Revenue | |10,000.00
|Output tax account | |1,900.00
|===

The document balances: 11,900.00 on the debit side, stem:[10{,}000.00 + 1{,}900.00 = 11{,}900.00] on the credit side. The
customer line holds the invoice amount including tax, and the tax line is added automatically to the output tax account.

=== Input tax: a purchase by EX01

`EX01` receives a supplier invoice for EUR 5,000.00 net of consulting services with the same illustrative rate. The tax is
stem:[5{,}000.00 \times 0.19 = 950.00] and the gross amount is EUR 5,950.00.

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Debit |Credit

|Consulting expense |5,000.00 |
|Input tax account |950.00 |
|Supplier | |5,950.00
|===

Again the tax is on the same side as the expense. The supplier is credited with the gross amount that `EX01` owes. The
input tax account later offsets the output tax account when the tax return is prepared; this page ends at the posting.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/3cb1182b4a184bdd93f8d62e3f1f0741/2e47d953189a424de10000000a174cb4.html[Example: Posting of Input Tax (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== Several rates on one invoice

A document can contain lines with different tax codes, and the tax is calculated for each code. A supplier invoice for
`EX01` has two lines: goods of EUR 600.00 net with the illustrative 19 percent and printed material of EUR 400.00 net with
an illustrative 7 percent. The tax is stem:[600.00 \times 0.19 = 114.00] and stem:[400.00 \times 0.07 = 28.00], together
EUR 142.00, so the supplier is credited with stem:[1{,}000.00 + 142.00 = 1{,}142.00]. The system posts one tax line per
tax code and account, which is what lets the tax return show each rate separately.

== Tax codes

=== What a tax code does

SAP lists five jobs of a tax code: it checks the tax amount in the document, calculates the tax automatically on request,
calculates the non-deductible part of an input tax, checks whether a tax account with a given tax type may be posted to,
and determines the tax account. You define a tax code by entering a two-character code (the SAP pages use examples such
as `S0`, `S1`, `J9` and `I9`) that represents a tax percentage rate, plus technical specifications:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Characteristic |Meaning

|Tax type |Input tax or output tax. The system proposes the tax types that are relevant to the country, which it knows from the country key you enter when defining the code.
|Target tax code |The code a deferred tax moves to when the tax is recognized (see below).
|Check indicator |Whether the system gives a warning or an error when a tax amount entered by hand differs from the calculated one.
|EU indicator |Blank means not EU-relevant. The value 1 marks a goods delivery within the EU, shown in the EC sales list. The value 2 is services within the EU, 3 is subcontracting within the EU, and 9 is acquisition tax.
|===

SAP does not limit a code to one rate: you can specify rates for several tax types in one code. SAP's example is an input
tax of 15 percent with a non-deductible portion of 40 percent. The tax code then carries 9 percent under the tax type
"Input tax" and 6 percent under "Nonded.input tax", because stem:[15 \times 0.60 = 9] and stem:[15 \times 0.40 = 6].

=== Tax type, and which accounts accept which tax

The tax type states whether a tax is input or output. The system uses it to check that you post to an account with an
allowed code, and it raises an error otherwise. SAP's example is entering an input tax code while posting to a customer
account for which only output tax codes are allowed. The allowed kinds are held in the master record of the G/L account
in the field Tax category: a
minus sign allows input tax codes only, a plus sign allows output tax codes only and an asterisk allows all of them. The
page on xref:erp/sap/finance/business-partners-customers-and-suppliers.adoc[business partners] describes the same setting
for the reconciliation accounts of customers and suppliers.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/8fbeed5f2046489696a50ac7fd76f9c6/0247d953189a424de10000000a174cb4.html[Input or Output Tax (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== The check indicator

The system compares a tax amount entered by hand with the amount it calculates from the tax code. SAP explains the two
settings by the two directions of posting. For output tax you set the check indicator so that a wrong amount is an error
and must be corrected, which keeps a wrong tax from being invoiced. For input tax you leave it off, so that the system
only warns, because the tax amount printed on the supplier's invoice has to be taken over even if it differs slightly. A
message is always issued when the difference, in the smallest currency unit, is greater than the number of G/L account and
asset posting items in the document. SAP's example: eight such items carry the same tax code and the variance is EUR
0.09, which is more than 8 cents, so the system issues an error message.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/3cb1182b4a184bdd93f8d62e3f1f0741/ff46d953189a424de10000000a174cb4.html[Check Indicator (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== Exempt and zero-rate sales

For a sale that is tax-exempt you can create a tax code with a rate of zero percent. It is entered on the line, stored with
it, and lets the advance tax return show these sales separately. SAP also allows a posting without a tax code if the
G/L account master record has the indicator Posting without taxes allowed selected. This matters most for countries that
calculate tax with jurisdiction codes, because otherwise a dummy jurisdiction code would be needed on each posting. A
posting without a tax code produces no tax information, so tax reporting programs do not include it, and posting to a
tax-relevant account without a tax code gives a warning that Customizing can switch off.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/8fbeed5f2046489696a50ac7fd76f9c6/0547d953189a424de10000000a174cb4.html[Tax Codes for Tax-Exempt Sales (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== Never change a code that has been used

SAP states that a tax code and its definition must not be deleted or changed once items have been posted with it. The tax
amounts would otherwise be shown on the advance return with wrong rates, and a tax adjustment for payments would be
wrong. In the case of a tax increase you create a new entry with the new rate instead of changing the old one. The
exception is a definition with a jurisdiction code whose percentage rates are date-dependent. In Customizing the codes are
maintained in the activity Define Tax Codes for Sales and Purchases, and for the jurisdiction procedure SAP says to use
that menu path or transaction `FTXP`, never `SM31` except to display, because the links to other tables are not
maintained.

=== Deferred tax and the target tax code

Some countries recognize a tax later than the posting. SAP's Customizing for deferred taxes uses two tax codes for each
form of tax: a deferred tax code for the deferred item and a target tax code for the normal item once it is recognized. You
define the target code first, then the deferred code, and on its rates screen you enter the target code in the Target Tax
Code field. The program Deferred Tax Transfer then moves the amounts from the deferred tax accounts to the target tax
accounts.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/8fbeed5f2046489696a50ac7fd76f9c6/8377d0531d8b4208e10000000a174cb4.html[Customizing for Tax Codes (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== Cloud Public Edition

In SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition you do not use the Implementation Guide. SAP's country pages say that tax codes are
delivered for some countries, such as Peru and Saudi Arabia, and that you add new ones or edit the delivered ones in the
configuration activity Define Tax Codes for Sales and Purchases (ID 101016) in your configuration environment. The
Saudi Arabian codes are listed with a tax procedure (`0TXSA`), a code such as `S2` or `V2` and a description such as
"SA Output VAT 5% for domestic goods supplies", so one country has one tax procedure and its codes are sorted into output
and input tax.

The read model of a tax code is the CDS view `I_TaxCode`. Its important fields include `TaxCalculationProcedure`,
`TaxCode`, `TaxType`, `TargetTaxCode`, `EUTaxClassification` and `TaxCodeIsInactive`. SAP lists constraints for the Public
Edition: several fields of the view, for example the EU acquisition target codes, the tax return country and the One Stop
Shop fields, must always be empty, and the target tax code is used only where deferred tax is active. A tax code that you
deactivate with the Inactive field no longer appears in the field search help. The view `I_TAXCODERATE` joins codes with
their rates, validity periods, tax calculation procedure and condition types.

== The tax calculation procedure

A tax code does not carry the arithmetic itself. A calculation procedure is defined for each country, and it holds the
specifications to calculate and post tax on sales and purchases. A procedure contains several tax types, which are called
condition types inside it. When you define a tax code, the system proposes the condition types of the country. A condition
type says on which base amount the tax is calculated and which account key posts it. The base can be the total of the
expense and revenue items or a subtotal: incoming acquisition tax, for example, is calculated on the tax amount of outgoing
acquisition tax, and the column Fr.level (from level) holds the step whose amount is the base.

For each tax type the procedure answers four questions, according to SAP:

* What is the base amount: the expense or revenue amount, or a tax amount (as with sales equalization tax in Belgium)?
* Is the percentage included in the amount or separate? Normally it is included.
* To which side of the account does the tax post? Input and output tax follow the G/L account items. Acquisition and
  investment tax, for example, post to the opposite side.
* Is a non-deductible tax expense posted separately or shared among the G/L account and asset line items?

=== The same condition technique as pricing

In Materials Management (MM) and Financial Accounting (FI) the calculation procedure replaces a pricing procedure and uses
the condition technique, the search method of the page on
xref:erp/sap/finance/account-determination-and-automatic-postings.adoc[account determination]. SAP's description of tax
calculation for a United States procedure says that every condition type of the procedure has the same access sequence,
`MWST`, with two parameters: country and tax code. The tax condition records are created in a central place for tax code
condition records, so for an MM or FI document the country of the company code and the tax code entered form the key of the
tax condition record. If the tax code is not entered by hand in MM, an automatic tax code determination is needed. The
calculation procedure also determines the G/L accounts to which tax liability is posted, which needs correct account
determination.

In Sales and Distribution (SD), SAP states that tax codes are the link between the pricing procedure and the tax
calculation procedure. In the example of the external-system procedure `TAXUSX`, the pricing procedure carries the tax
rates and amounts in condition types `XR1` to `XR6`, while the tax accounts are not in the pricing procedure but in the tax
calculation procedure, so the condition type names must coincide in both. When SD finds the tax code through condition
records of the condition type `UTXJ`, the code and the departure country read the tax condition record kept in FI.

=== A country, a procedure, a choice of method

For the United States SAP offers three methods to calculate sales and use tax, set by the calculation procedure assigned to
the country, so every company code in the country uses the same method: the non-jurisdiction method (percentage rates are
assigned to tax codes; SAP calls it seldom used), the jurisdiction method (you define the jurisdiction of every region), and
the jurisdiction method with an external tax calculation system. SAP says that once you choose with or without jurisdiction
codes it is difficult to switch, and one SAP topic says the decision cannot be reversed, so decide it before the first
posting. SAP recommends an external system to avoid manual data entry.

== Non-deductible input tax

Sometimes a company cannot claim back all or part of an input tax. SAP says that you can post the non-deductible amount to
a separate expense account or distribute it to the G/L account and asset line items. You enter the rates of the deductible
and the non-deductible portion under one shared tax code. If the whole tax is non-deductible, the tax code has to say that
zero percent input tax is calculated. SAP's own example is an invoice of EUR 1,200.00 whose included tax is EUR 200.00
(20 percent), made of 12 percent input tax (EUR 120.00) and 8 percent non-deductible tax (EUR 80.00).

Here is the same idea for `EX01`, with illustrative numbers. A supplier invoice has a net amount of EUR 1,000.00 and a
tax code with 19 percent tax of which 25 percent is non-deductible. The deductible rate is
stem:[19 \times 0.75 = 14.25] percent and the non-deductible rate stem:[19 \times 0.25 = 4.75] percent. So

[stem]
++++
T_{\text{input}} = 1000.00 \times 0.1425 = 142.50, \qquad T_{\text{non-ded}} = 1000.00 \times 0.0475 = 47.50,
++++

and the two together are stem:[142.50 + 47.50 = 190.00 = 1000.00 \times 0.19]. The gross amount is EUR 1,190.00. The two
ways to post the non-deductible part give two documents. Variant A posts it to its own account:

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line (variant A) |Debit |Credit

|Expense |1,000.00 |
|Input tax account |142.50 |
|Non-deductible input tax expense account |47.50 |
|Supplier | |1,190.00
|===

Variant B spreads it over the expense line, so there is no separate tax expense line:

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line (variant B) |Debit |Credit

|Expense |1,047.50 |
|Input tax account |142.50 |
|Supplier | |1,190.00
|===

Both documents balance (1,000.00 + 142.50 + 47.50 = 1,190.00 and 1,047.50 + 142.50 = 1,190.00). In variant B the cost of
the expense includes the tax that could not be claimed. When none of the tax is deductible there is no input tax line at
all, and the whole 190.00 is a cost.

== Jurisdiction-based taxes

=== Why a location enters the calculation

In the United States the tax rate depends on where the goods are delivered, and there are more than 55,000 taxing
jurisdictions, according to SAP. A jurisdiction is the authority that imposes the tax, and the tax jurisdiction code is a key
that, together with the tax code and other parameters, determines the tax amount and how the total is divided among the tax
authorities. SAP's page on entering taxes says jurisdiction codes are used in the USA and Brazil.

A jurisdiction code is a freely definable field of up to 15 characters with up to four levels, such as state, county and
city. For the standard procedure `TAXUSJ` the structure is 2, 3 and 4 characters, nine in all. SAP's examples are
`PA0000000` for the state of Pennsylvania, `PA0010000` for Allegheny county and `PA0010100` for the city of Pittsburgh. The
procedure `TAXUSJ` has you enter the jurisdiction codes and percentages by hand, and `TAXUSX` calculates in an external
system that holds the codes and rates and supports up to six levels.

=== Where the code comes from

The jurisdiction code is defined in key master records. For sales it is determined from indicators on the customer and the
material. For purchasing, for simple tax scenarios, it comes from indicators on the material. For asset postings SAP gives a
search order: the code in the asset master record first, then the one of the cost center entered there, then the one of the
company code. In sales, condition records of the condition type `UTXJ` for country `US` give the tax code from the region,
the tax classification of the customer and the tax classification of the material (each 0 or 1). SAP says even an exempt
status needs a tax code, with a rate of 0.0 percent. SAP's example for the state of Pennsylvania gives code `S0` for the
combinations 0/0, 1/0 and 0/1, and `S1` for 1/1.

When you create a jurisdiction tax code, you enter a percentage for each jurisdiction level, and the percentages of the
levels are added together when applied. SAP's example sets up a code `J9` as input tax (type `V`) for accounts payable
sales tax.

=== An illustration with made-up rates

Suppose a US sale of USD 1,000.00 net to a customer whose code has three levels with the illustrative rates 6 percent
(state), 1 percent (county) and 2 percent (city). The three amounts are

[stem]
++++
1000.00 \times 0.06 = 60.00,\quad 1000.00 \times 0.01 = 10.00,\quad 1000.00 \times 0.02 = 20.00,
++++

so the sales tax is stem:[60.00 + 10.00 + 20.00 = 90.00] and the gross amount is USD 1,090.00. The system posts the tax
by level, so the payment can be divided among the authorities. When you enter taxes by hand on a document, SAP lets you
enter either the amount for the entire jurisdiction code, which is the default, or the amounts one by one for the state,
county and city code.

== What can go wrong

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Cause and check

|Error when posting a tax code to a customer or supplier account |The tax type of the code (input or output) is not allowed by the Tax category of the account. Check the master record, as in the section on tax type above.
|Error or warning "tax amount differs" |The manually entered tax differs from the calculated one by more than the permitted difference. The check indicator on the tax code decides between error and warning.
|Warning when posting to a tax-relevant account without a tax code |The account expects a tax code. Use a zero-rate code for an exempt sale or select Posting without taxes allowed in the account's master record.
|Wrong tax amounts on the advance return after a rate change |A code that had postings was changed instead of replaced. Create a new code with the new rate.
|Tax determination in purchasing finds no tax code |In MM the tax code is determined automatically when it is not entered. The determination is part of Customizing and has to be set up.
|Cannot switch between the jurisdiction methods later |SAP says the choice is difficult or impossible to reverse. Decide it before the first posting.

== Summary

* A tax code is a short key for a tax rate and its rules. It checks the tax amount, calculates it on request, calculates
  a non-deductible part, checks that a tax account accepts the tax type and determines the tax account.
* The tax is stem:[T = B \cdot r] on the base amount and the gross amount is stem:[B(1 + r)]. From a gross amount the tax
  is stem:[G \cdot r / (1 + r)]. A sale of EUR 10,000.00 net at an illustrative 19 percent gives EUR 1,900.00 output tax
  and a gross amount of EUR 11,900.00.
* Input tax is claimed back from the tax office and output tax is paid to it. Both post to the same side of the account as
  the G/L items, and the tax type and the tax category of an account must agree.
* The tax calculation procedure of a country holds the tax types as condition types, with a base amount and an account key,
  and uses the condition technique with the access sequence `MWST` in FI and MM. In SD the tax code links the pricing
  procedure to the tax procedure.
* A non-deductible part is posted to its own expense account or spread over the G/L and asset lines. A jurisdiction code
  adds the location, and with it the split among tax authorities, to the calculation for the United States and Brazil.
* A tax code that has been used must not be changed. In the Public Edition tax codes are maintained in a configuration
  activity and read through the CDS view `I_TaxCode`.
