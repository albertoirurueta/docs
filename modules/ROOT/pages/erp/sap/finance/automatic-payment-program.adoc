A company with a few hundred suppliers does not pay invoices one at a time. It lets the system look through all open
items, pick the ones that are due, group them into payments, post the payments and produce the files or forms the bank
needs. That function is the automatic payment program, often called the payment run. This page explains its three
steps (proposal, payment run, payment media), the parameters you enter, the rules that choose the payment method and
the bank, what you can change before money moves, and what to do when a run fails. It is written against SAP S/4HANA
2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The ideas are the same in both
editions; the screens differ, and the page says where. The page ends with a worked run for the fictitious company code
`EX01`.

== Key terms

* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#automatic-payment-program[Automatic payment program]: the program that selects due
  open items, creates payment documents and prepares the payment media.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#payment-run[Payment run]: one execution of the program, identified by a run date
  and an identification.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#payment-proposal[Payment proposal]: the list of payments the program would make,
  which you can check and change before anything is posted.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#payment-method[Payment method]: how a payment is made, for example check, bank
  transfer or bank direct debit.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#house-bank[House bank]: a bank at which your company code keeps an account.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#payment-medium[Payment medium]: the output of a run that goes to the bank or the
  payee, such as a file, a check or a payment advice.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#exception-list[Exception list]: the open items that the program did not propose,
  with the reason for each.

== What the program does and why it exists

SAP describes the payment program as able to process both outgoing and incoming payments, in Accounts Payable and in
Accounts Receivable, domestic and foreign, for suppliers and customers. It creates the payment documents and supplies
the data to the payment medium programs, which print a payment list or payment forms such as checks, or create files
for the bank. This page concentrates on paying suppliers, the case most beginners meet first.

The help describes the work in three steps:

. *Proposal.* The program determines the open items to be paid and creates a proposal list. You can process that list
  online: change payment methods or banks, block items, or cancel payment blocks.
. *Payment run.* The program carries out the payment using the proposal. Only the items in the proposal are included.
  It posts the payment documents and provides the data for the forms, the data carriers, the payment advice notes and the
  payment summaries.
. *Payment media.* A print or file program prints the forms and creates the data carriers.

The split matters because step 1 changes nothing in the books. Everything you do while editing a proposal affects only
the proposal; the invoices stay as they are. The money moves in step 2.

== What you need before the first run

The payment methods, house banks and forms you use must be defined before a run. SAP's Customizing documentation
describes the configuration as deciding what is paid (rules that select and group the open items), when (the due date,
which you can refine), to whom (the payee), how (rules that choose a payment method) and from where (rules that choose a
bank and bank account). Most of these settings are specific to a company code. The rules for controlling the program sit
in four places: the customer or supplier master record, the line items, the configuration of the payment program, and
the specifications you make for each payment run. The program also needs the document types, posting keys and accounts
for its automatic postings. Requires an SAP system; not run here.

* *House banks.* A house bank is a bank at which the company code has an account. It is defined under a house bank key
  and the accounts under an account ID, and each bank account has a G/L account. Bank master data holds the address and
  control data such as the SWIFT code, and the bank details are needed to print the payment forms.
* *Payment methods.* A payment method is the procedure used to pay, for example check, transfer or bill of exchange. For
  suppliers SAP lists check, bank transfer, postal giro transfer, bills of exchange, check/bill of exchange and a
  bill of exchange payment request; for customers it lists bank collection, bank direct debit, return check, refund
  and bank switching. A payment method is defined first per country and then per company code, with the conditions for
  its use.

== The run parameters

A run is identified by two values: the date of execution and an alphanumeric identification. The identification is
needed because several runs can be scheduled for the same day. In the classic system you reach the Automatic Payment
Transactions screen from the SAP Easy Access menu under Accounting, Financial Accounting, Accounts receivable/Accounts
payable, Periodic Processing, Payments. You enter the parameters on their own tab, or copy them from an earlier run.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Parameter |What SAP's help says

|Posting date
|The date printed on the payment media.

|Documents entered up to
|The date up to which documents are considered. The creation date, not the posting date, decides. Items entered later
are not processed.

|Due date
|The date by which an open item must be due to be considered. Without a due date, the posting date counts as the due
date.

|Company code
|The four-character company codes to pay.

|Payment methods
|The keys to use for every company code in this run. The sequence of the keys decides which method pays an item that has
no method in the item or the master record.

|Next posting date
|The posting date of the next run. The program uses it to decide whether an open item is paid in this run or in the next.

|Account restrictions
|Optional: limit the run to specific customers or suppliers. Entering both account numbers of a business partner that is
a customer and a supplier lets the program determine a joint balance.
|===

If you enter, for example, the payment methods `BTC`, the program first checks whether a bill of exchange can be
created, then a bank transfer, then a check. The Free selection tab accepts up to three more selection criteria on
fields of the document and master data tables, and the Additional log tab lets you ask for more detailed logging of the
due date check, the payment method selection and the payment document items for named accounts. SAP advises using the
additional log only in exceptional situations or for testing, because it makes the log longer and the run slower.

On the Printout/data medium tab you enter one or more variants for each payment medium program used for a payment
method, and variants for the payment proposal list (program `RFZALI00`) and the exception list (program `RFZALI10`).

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

== Which items are paid, by which method, from which bank

=== Selecting open items

The program selects items and pays them as late as possible without losing cash discount. SAP's list of the factors:

* The due date comes from the baseline date and the terms of payment in the open item. The program calculates the cash
  discount periods and the due date for net payment.
* A company-code-specific grace period for payables can be added to the due date, so that payment is made later.
* A minimum cash discount percentage can be set per company code for outgoing payments. If the item cannot reach it,
  the program pays on the net due date. Without a minimum, it pays with the highest possible cash discount.
* You decide which special G/L transactions are included, by entering their indicators in the company code
  specifications. A down payment request can be paid by the program if its indicator is entered.
* The next posting date of the run decides whether an item belongs to this run or the next.

=== Choosing the payment method

A payment method in the open item takes precedence over one in the master record. When several methods are in the
master record and none in the item, the program tries the methods of the run in the order you entered them and uses the
first that passes the checks. Among the conditions SAP lists: the method must be in the master record or item and the
master record must hold what the method needs (bank details for a transfer, for example); for foreign payments the
method must allow payees or banks abroad; for foreign currency the method must allow it; and the payment amount must lie
between the minimum and maximum set for the method. Then a permitted bank is determined.

Source:
https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/3cb1182b4a184bdd93f8d62e3f1f0741/0904c55368511d4be10000000a174cb4.html[Selection of the Payment Method by the Payment Program (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

=== Choosing the bank and the available amount

If several bank accounts serve the same payment method and currency, you can define the order in which the program uses
them: when the funds of the first account are used up, it takes the second, and so on. You can also set per account the
amount up to which payments or receipts may be made through it, for example to keep room for a payment the program does
not make. In the Public Edition the Enter Available Amounts for Payment Program app lets an accounts payable accountant
choose the house banks and amounts for exceptional or urgent payments; those settings override the bank determination
configuration for the payment runs of the Manage Automatic Payments app.

== Step 1: the payment proposal

You schedule the proposal with a start date and time or immediately. The status display shows which step the job is at.
When it finishes, SAP says you should read the log: it tells you about configuration errors, and in that case no
payment is possible. You correct the error, delete the proposal and create a new one.

Two lists help you judge the result. The proposal list shows the payments the program suggests. The exception list shows
the blocked items and all open items the program did not propose. SAP names three kinds: blocked items, special G/L
transactions (depending on the configuration), and items that could not be settled although they were due. Each
exception has an error number and an error text. A due item that is missing from the proposal is therefore either on the
exception list with a reason, or not yet due for this run.

=== Editing the proposal

You can divide the editing between clerks by the accounting clerk ID in the master record, so that several people work
through a large run in parallel, and you can later display who changed what. At the payment level you can change the
payment method, the house bank and the due date. A new method must be one set in the run parameters and must have what it
needs in the master record, such as bank details; the new due date may not be before the run date, and apart from bills of
exchange not more than 30 days after it. At the line item level you can block or unblock items, change the cash
discount, and reassign an item to another payment or a new one. A blocked item moves to the exception group and the
payment shrinks by its amount; only block indicators defined as editable in Customizing can be set or removed.
Changes take effect only when you save them.

=== Payment release list

The Payment Release List is an alternative to editing the proposal in the program. It lets several processors approve
and release items, with an approval process based on dual control. SAP's help says that once a Payment Release List
application is activated, you can no longer edit payment proposals in the payment program, and that it is available
only if you use Public Sector Management. Most companies therefore edit the proposal as described above.

== Step 2 and 3: payment run and payment media

Once you accept the proposal, you schedule the payment run. The help gives three ways to combine the programs: schedule
the payment program first and the payment medium program later; schedule both together; or run the payment program and
then print online. Before printing, check the payment log and the payment list, which you reach from the payment menu.

Scheduling both together needs the variants of the payment medium programs to exist, and you select the option with
print programs. SAP adds a caution: the documents of the payment program are first saved to a log file and posted
afterward. Some payment medium programs need information from the posted documents, so for those the medium programs
should be scheduled separately for a later time, once all payment documents are posted. The status display shows how many
documents were created and how many are posted.

Two families of programs produce the medium. The classic payment medium programs are named by country and payment method;
SAP's example is a program that generates bank transfers in ACH format for the USA. The Payment Medium Workbench is the
generic tool that SAP says will gradually replace the classic programs, with better control, better performance for mass
payments above 50,000 and better sorting of payment advice notes; not every classic format is supported yet. You choose
between them per country and payment method in Customizing. With the Workbench, the data media go to the data medium
exchange administration and the payment advice notes can be produced as IDoc, XML file, letter, e-mail or fax.

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

== Worked example: a payment run for Example Trading Co.

Company code `EX01` keeps its books in euro (EUR), uses British pounds (GBP) as group currency and writes ledgers `0L`
and `2L`. On 2026-04-10 it executes a run with identification `A` for two suppliers. The next posting date is 2026-04-17.
All amounts, dates and rates are made up. The terms are written as in the accounts payable page: "2/10 Net 30" means 2
percent discount within 10 days and net within 30. The tax effect of the discount is ignored, as on that page.

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Supplier |Invoice |EUR |Baseline date |Terms |Due date |Run result

|1 |I1 |4,000.00 |2026-04-02 |2/10 Net 30 |discount until 2026-04-12, net 2026-05-02 |Paid now with discount: the discount
period ends before the next run on 2026-04-17.
|1 |I2 |6,000.00 |2026-03-10 |Net 30 |2026-04-09 |Paid now: overdue.
|1 |I3 |2,500.00 |2026-03-12 |Net 30 |2026-04-11 |Not paid: payment block, shown on the exception list.
|2 |I4 |10,000.00 |2026-04-10 |Net 30 |2026-05-10 |Not proposed: not due before the next posting date.
|===

Supplier 1 has items for one payment method and one house bank, so the program creates a single payment. The discount of
I1 and the amount paid are:

[stem]
++++
4\,000.00 \times 0.02 = 80.00, \qquad 4\,000.00 - 80.00 = 3\,920.00, \qquad 3\,920.00 + 6\,000.00 = 9\,920.00
++++

Suppose that, while editing the proposal, the clerk finds that the payment block on I3 was set in error and removes it,
which is possible if that block indicator is editable. Then I3 joins the payment:

[stem]
++++
9\,920.00 + 2\,500.00 = 12\,420.00
++++

The example continues with the proposal as the program created it, with I3 blocked, so the payment is EUR 9,920.00. The
payment document clears I1 and I2. With a made-up rate of 0.85 GBP per EUR, the group currency amounts are:

[stem]
++++
10\,000.00 \times 0.85 = 8\,500.00, \qquad 9\,920.00 \times 0.85 = 8\,432.00, \qquad 80.00 \times 0.85 = 68.00
++++

[cols="1,1,3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Side |Account |EUR |GBP

|1 |Debit |Supplier 1 (clears I1 and I2) |10,000.00 |8,500.00
|2 |Credit |Bank account of the house bank |9,920.00 |8,432.00
|3 |Credit |Cash discount received (found through automatic postings) |80.00 |68.00
|*Total debit* | |  |*10,000.00* |*8,500.00*
|*Total credit* | | |*10,000.00* |*8,500.00*
|===

Check: stem:[8\,432.00 + 68.00 = 8\,500.00], so the entry balances in both currencies. Each line is written to `0L` and
`2L`. After the run, supplier 1 still has I3 open (EUR 2,500.00) and supplier 2 has I4 open (EUR 10,000.00). The sum of
open items is stem:[2\,500.00 + 10\,000.00 = 12\,500.00]. The account and posting key of the discount line are
determined by the system, not chosen by the clerk; the name used here is generic. Requires an SAP system; not run here.

https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/3cb1182b4a184bdd93f8d62e3f1f0741/afead353ca9f4408e10000000a174cb4.html[Selecting Open Items (SAP Help Portal, on premise)]

== When a run fails

In the Public Edition SAP describes a rare case where a payment run stops unexpectedly and leaves the status Payment
Cancelled or Payment Run Failed, or where the background job is deleted (Payment Run Job Deleted or Proposal Run Job
Deleted). Then the payment is locked, which blocks the supplier or customer accounts and freezes the items. In the Manage
Automatic Payments app you select the item on the Payments Processed tab, or the proposal on the Proposals Processed tab,
and choose Delete Output or Delete Proposal. What happens next depends on the status:

* *Nothing posted and no payment orders:* the data of the run is deleted and the status goes back, so you reschedule.
* *Payment orders created:* delete the payment orders and create a new payment.
* *Payment documents posted:* reverse the payment documents in the Manage Journal Entries app (`F0717A`), then create a
  new payment.

SAP advises acting on a failed run quickly, so that the job does not expire and the items are released.

== What older books show, and what has changed

The book Configuring SAP S/4HANA Finance, 3rd edition (2025), places the payment program in the accounts payable menu
and names it with the transaction code `F110`, and it lists run date, identification, posting date, documents entered
up to, company codes, payment methods, next posting date and accounts as the important fields. These match the
parameters above, and the code is still how classic users call the program. The SAP help pages used for this page give
the menu path and the screen name, Automatic Payment Transactions, instead of the code. The book SAP S/4HANA Financial
Accounting Configuration, 2nd edition (2023), lists the code too. What is new since the classic program is the set of
Public Edition apps `F0770`, `F0771` and `F1868`, which split the work into a proposal and payment app, a revision app and a
payment media app.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Expecting the proposal to post anything.* It posts nothing; only the payment run does.
* *Reading a missing item as a bug.* Check the exception list for a block or a missing payment method, bank detail or
  master record specification, and check the next posting date.
* *Listing a method in the run that the supplier cannot use.* The payment method must be in the master record or the
  item, and the master record must hold what the method needs, such as bank details, or the method is not used.
* *Setting the additional log on in production.* It is meant for exceptional situations or tests, and slows the run.
* *Printing before the documents are posted.* Some medium programs need the posted documents; schedule them separately.
* *Leaving a failed run alone.* The accounts stay blocked and the job can expire; reset the run.

== Summary

* The automatic payment program pays due supplier and customer open items in three steps: proposal, payment run,
  payment media. Only the payment run posts.
* A run is identified by a run date and an identification, and its parameters name the posting date, the company codes,
  the payment methods and the next posting date that decides which items are due now.
* The program chooses the payment method and the house bank by rules; you can edit method, bank, due date, blocks and
  discounts in the proposal, and the exception list explains what was left out.
* Payment media come from the classic programs or the Payment Medium Workbench. In the Public Edition the apps are
  Manage Automatic Payments, Revise Payment Proposals and Manage Payment Media.
* In the worked run, supplier 1 receives one payment of EUR 9,920.00 after a discount of EUR 80.00, while a blocked item
  and a not yet due item stay open.
