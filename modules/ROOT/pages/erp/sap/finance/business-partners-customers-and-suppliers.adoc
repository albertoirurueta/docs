Every sale on credit creates a receivable from a customer, and every purchase on credit creates a payable to a supplier.
Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable keep one account per customer and per supplier, and each of those accounts
hangs on a master record. In SAP S/4HANA that master record is no longer a stand-alone "customer" or "vendor" record: it
is a business partner that has been given a customer role or a supplier role. This page explains the finance side of
that arrangement: what the roles are, which finance data sits in the company code part of the record, what a
reconciliation account is and why every customer and supplier needs one, which transaction and apps you use, why the
familiar create transactions `XK01` and `FD01` no longer work as before, and what Customer/Supplier Integration (CVI)
does behind the scenes. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA
Cloud Public Edition 2608. The business partner model applies to both; transaction `BP` and the conversion topics
apply to the on-premise side, and the Public Edition works through apps.

== One partner, two kinds of account

SAP's help for the business partner says that you can centrally manage master data for business partners, customers and
suppliers, and that transaction `BP` is the single point of entry to create, edit and display all three, depending on
the roles that are configured. The Public Edition says the same about its leading object: a business partner record
exists only once, whatever business processes it takes part in, and when a company is both customer and supplier you
need one record for both roles.

For finance this has a plain consequence. The partner holds the data that is true everywhere, such as name and address.
A customer role and a supplier role add the data that Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable need. Posting still goes to
a customer account or a supplier account, because those accounts are what the subledgers are made of; the business
partner is the record that those accounts hang on.

== The finance roles

A role is the view of a business partner for one business function. SAP's definition of the business partner role is
that it describes rights and obligations a partner can take on in business transactions, and that the attributes of a role
are tailored to the transaction behind it. The central data (name, address, bank details) is created only once, however
many roles the partner has.

The help page of the Manage Business Partner Master Data app names the roles that matter for a beginner in finance:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Role |What SAP's help calls it

|`FLCU00`
|FI customer (the financial accounting view of a customer).

|`FLCU01`
|Sales customer.

|`FLVN00`
|FI supplier (the financial accounting view of a supplier).

|`FLVN01`
|Supplier (the purchasing view).
|===

A partner can hold any combination. The app lets you delete a role only if it is not the only role of that category: the
help says you can delete all but one role for the categories customer and supplier. Roles also have a validity period,
which the Simplification List describes with an example: a partner with the prospect role can be moved to the
sold-to-party role by closing the validity of the first and starting the second.

Two sources name the roles slightly differently. The Manage Business Partner Master Data app page calls the `00` roles the
financial accounting views. One Simplification List item, which deals with specific fields for US federal financials,
calls `FLVN01` and `FLCU01` the financial accounting part. This page follows the app page; in your own system, open the
role list of transaction `BP` to see what each role is called.

== What the finance data holds

SAP's definition of the customer master record divides it into areas so that each company code and each sales
organization can store its own information. The finance-relevant ones are:

* *General data*: valid for all company codes, such as name, address and language.
* *Company code data*: specific to one company code. SAP's examples are the reconciliation account number, the payment
  terms and the dunning procedure.
* *Sales data* (customers): per sales organization and distribution channel. This is not finance data.
* *Purchasing data* (suppliers): per purchasing organization. Also not finance data.

SAP's example makes the split concrete: a company with two company codes that trade with the same customer stores the
address once, and each company code stores its own reconciliation account and payment terms in its own company code area.
The supplier record follows the same pattern. SAP's description of it lists the tax numbers, bank details, account
control data such as the reconciliation account, and the payment methods and payment terms agreed with the supplier.

The field mapping pages of the on-premise help show how the company code fields of the old Customizing screens appear in
the Fiori apps. Use them as the checklist of what finance data a record has:

[cols="2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Group |Customer company code fields |Supplier company code fields

|Account management
|Reconciliation account, planning group, sort key, head office, release group, value adjustment
|Reconciliation account, planning group, sort key, head office, minority indicator, release group, withholding tax country

|Payment data
|Payment terms, credit memo payment terms, payment block, tolerance group, payment history record
|Payment terms, credit memo payment terms, payment block, tolerance group, double invoice check

|Automatic payment
|Payment methods, alternative payer, house bank, clearing with vendor, payment advice by EDI
|Payment methods, alternative payee, house bank, clearing with customer, payment advice by EDI

|Correspondence
|Accounting clerk contact data, local processing, collective invoice variant
|Accounting clerk, clerk ID and account at the supplier, local processing, account memo
|===

In the Fiori apps the customer fields sit under Company Code, Finance, with Accounting, Payment Data and Automatic
Payment Transactions as sub-areas, and the supplier's reconciliation account sits under Company Code, Finance, Accounting.
The names in the table are the field labels shown in the Fiori apps as listed on those pages. Later pages explain what payment terms do for the due date and discount, and how the payment program uses
payment methods and house banks.

== Reconciliation accounts

A subledger such as Accounts Receivable holds a separate account per customer, but the balance sheet needs one number for
"receivables". The reconciliation account connects the two. SAP states that when you post items to a subledger, the
system automatically posts the same data to the general ledger at the same time. Each subledger has one or more
reconciliation accounts in the general ledger, and these keep the balance sheet current without any transfer of totals
from the subledgers. You must enter a reconciliation account in every customer and supplier record, in the company code
data.

Two settings make it work:

. *In the G/L account.* You mark a G/L account as a reconciliation account by entering the account type it serves, for
  example customer or vendor, in the field Reconciliation account for account type. SAP's example is the account
  "Receivables from goods and services" with the entry Customer. The account can then be assigned only to accounts of
  that type, and the system checks that the account named in a customer master record is allowed for customers.
. *In the customer or supplier record.* The company code data names the reconciliation account that the subledger
  account posts to.

SAP adds that you cannot post to a reconciliation account manually. That is the point of the construction: the subledger
and the general ledger cannot drift apart, because the only way to change the reconciliation account is to post to a
customer or supplier account.

What else a reconciliation account controls, from SAP's descriptions:

* *Posting screens.* You can use it to configure the screens for posting items to customer or supplier accounts, for
  example to hide fields for exchange rate hedges if you do not use them. This is the reason why the field status of
  customers and suppliers is partly set in the reconciliation account, as the page on xref:erp/sap/finance/number-ranges-field-status-and-tolerance-groups.adoc[number ranges and field status]
  explains. The field status of the master record screens is a different setting: it comes from the account group's
  screen layout, in the activity Define Account Groups with Screen Layout (Customers) or (Vendors) on premise.
* *Currencies.* You can use it to say which currencies may be posted to the connected accounts. You usually enter the
  local currency in the master record, and postings are then possible in all currencies. If you enter a foreign currency
  key in the reconciliation account, postings to the connected accounts are possible only in that currency.
* *Tax code field.* The reconciliation account's tax category field decides whether the tax code field is hidden when you
  post to a customer or supplier account or whether it is ready for input. SAP lists three indicators: a hyphen allows only
  input tax codes, a plus sign only output tax codes and an asterisk all tax codes.

=== Changing a reconciliation account

You can change the reconciliation account in a customer or supplier record. If you do so after items were posted, the
payables or receivables in the balance sheet are off, because the old items stay on the old reconciliation account. SAP
says you must then adjust the account for the balance sheet at year end, using the regrouping program for changed
reconciliation accounts: it posts the items from the old accounts to the new ones through temporary adjustment accounts
and reverses those postings after the financial statements. The adjustment accounts and posting keys come from the
Customizing activity Define Adjustment Accounts for Changed Reconciliation Accounts. SAP also recommends to protect the
reconciliation account field with the field status or authorizations, so that nobody changes it by mistake.

=== The account for a single posting

The reconciliation account of the master record is the normal case. In sales, billing documents can be posted to
alternative reconciliation accounts that the system finds with the condition technique, based on, for example, the
sales organization and the account assignment group of the customer. The Public Edition help gives the account
determination type for this as `KOAB`. A related on-premise page, about settlement management documents, adds that when a
G/L account is determined this way, the system ignores the reconciliation account in the customer or supplier master
record. The page on xref:erp/sap/finance/account-determination-and-automatic-postings.adoc[account determination and automatic postings] shows how the condition technique finds an account.

== Worked example: one partner, both roles

Example Trading Co. (company code `EX01`, euro, ledgers `0L` and `2L`) trades with the fictitious haulage firm Nordwind
Logistics, introduced on the master data page. The firm has one business partner record with the roles `FLCU00` and
`FLVN00`, and `EX01` has maintained company code data for each: a customer reconciliation account for receivables and a
supplier reconciliation account for payables. Account names are descriptive, and all amounts and the tax rate of 19 percent
are made up for the example.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart TB
    BP["Business partner Nordwind Logistics<br/>name, address: entered once"]
    C["Role FLCU00 customer<br/>company code data EX01"]
    S["Role FLVN00 supplier<br/>company code data EX01"]
    CA["Customer account<br/>subledger Accounts Receivable"]
    SA["Supplier account<br/>subledger Accounts Payable"]
    RR["G/L: reconciliation account<br/>Receivables from goods and services"]
    RP["G/L: reconciliation account<br/>Payables from goods and services"]
    BP --> C
    BP --> S
    C --> CA
    S --> SA
    CA -. "automatic posting" .-> RR
    SA -. "automatic posting" .-> RP
----

*Sale.* `EX01` invoices Nordwind Logistics for freight handling: net EUR 1,000.00, tax stem:[1\,000.00 \times 0.19 = 190.00],
gross stem:[1\,000.00 + 190.00 = 1\,190.00]. The customer line goes to the customer account, and the system adds the
matching line to the customer reconciliation account.

[cols="3,3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Account |Debit EUR |Credit EUR

|1 |Customer Nordwind Logistics (posts also to the customer reconciliation account) |1,190.00 |
|2 |Revenue account | |1,000.00
|3 |Output tax account | |190.00
|*Total* | |*1,190.00* |*1,190.00*
|===

*Purchase.* `EX01` buys fuel cards from the same firm: net EUR 500.00, tax stem:[500.00 \times 0.19 = 95.00], gross
stem:[500.00 + 95.00 = 595.00]. The supplier line goes to the supplier account and, automatically, to the supplier
reconciliation account.

[cols="3,3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Account |Debit EUR |Credit EUR

|1 |Expense account |500.00 |
|2 |Input tax account |95.00 |
|3 |Supplier Nordwind Logistics (posts also to the supplier reconciliation account) | |595.00
|*Total* | |*595.00* |*595.00*
|===

Both documents balance. The two subledger accounts of the one partner now show a customer debit balance of EUR 1,190.00
and a supplier credit balance of EUR 595.00. In the general ledger the customer reconciliation account shows the same
EUR 1,190.00 debit and the supplier reconciliation account the same EUR 595.00 credit, so the reconciliation check of a
subledger against its account passes. The two balances stay separate because they are different accounts; the net
position with this partner is

[stem]
++++
1\,190.00 - 595.00 = 595.00
++++

in favor of `EX01`, which the company can use when it decides how to settle the two accounts. The company code data of the
customer role has a field Clearing with vendor, and that of the supplier role has Clearing with customer; SAP's field
mapping pages list them, and the pages used here do not describe the setting further. Every line above is written to both ledgers `0L` and `2L`.

== What replaced XK01 and FD01

In SAP ERP, a supplier was created with `XK01` (centrally) or `FK01` (accounting only), and a customer with `XD01` (centrally)
or `FD01` (accounting only). The Simplification List item "Business Partner Approach" states what happens in SAP S/4HANA:
the business partner is the mandatory, leading object, and transaction `BP` is the user interface. There is no
separate customer or supplier interface, and the old transactions are not available. They are redirected to `BP`.

The list of redirected transactions in the Simplification List item includes the create, change, display, block and
delete-flag transactions of the three families:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Family |Redirected to `BP`

|Customer (FI)
|`FD01`, `FD02`, `FD03`, `FD05`, `FD06`

|Customer (sales and central)
|`VD01`, `VD02`, `VD03`, `VD05`, `VD06`, `XD01`, `XD02`, `XD03`, `XD05`, `XD06`

|Supplier (FI, purchasing and central)
|`FK01`, `FK02`, `FK03`, `FK05`, `FK06`, `MK01`, `MK02`, `MK03`, `MK05`, `MK06`, `XK01`, `XK02`, `XK03`, `XK05`, `XK06`
|===

The same item names a short list of transactions that are obsolete and not redirected: `MK12`, `MK18`, `MK19`, `XD07`,
`XK07`, `pass:c[V+21]`, `pass:c[V+22]` and `pass:c[V+23]`. Another item of the Simplification List confirms the effect from the point of view of
custom code: code that creates or changes customer or supplier master data must be changed so that the business partner
is created or changed instead. The business process is not affected, only the transaction to use.

Because the business partner is the leading object, SAP's guidance for integrations changes too. The Simplification List
says the IDocs `DEBMAS` and `CREMAS` are not recommended for data integration between S/4HANA systems, and that
business partner web services are the choice, with IDocs only when the source system has customer or supplier data and no
business partner.

== Customer/Supplier Integration (CVI)

The old customer and supplier tables still exist, because much of SAP's own code and many interfaces read them. CVI is the
mechanism that keeps them and the business partner consistent. SAP describes the process as bidirectional: you can
process customer or supplier records from business partner maintenance, and data created in the old customer or supplier
processing is populated to the business partner. The Customer, Supplier and Business Partner records are three
synchronization objects in the configuration of master data synchronization.

When it matters:

* *In a conversion from SAP ERP.* The conversion process and its pre-checks check whether CVI is completely introduced,
  and SAP states that a system without CVI is declined for the transition. All customers and suppliers, and the contacts
  that relate to them, must be converted to business partners, including those with a deletion flag. SAP recommends, but
  does not require, that the business partner ID and the customer or supplier ID are the same.
* *In a system that already runs on S/4HANA.* Creating a business partner with a customer or supplier role creates the
  customer or supplier record at the same time, using the number assignment rules below.

The Simplification List names the preparation steps: implement the conversion pre-checks, activate the business function
`CA_BP_SOA`, check the CVI Customizing (for example a missing business partner role category and the number
assignments), check and maintain the business partner Customizing (for example missing tax types), check master data
consistency with the report `CVI_MIGRATION_PRECHK` and clean up data such as a missing at sign in an email address. The
data load itself runs in the Synchronization Cockpit (transaction `MDS_LOAD_COCKPIT`), where the Logs button and the
Monitor tab help to find errors. A separate Compare Tool for Master Data Synchronization (transaction
`MDS_COMPARE_TOOL`) compares customer or supplier data with business partner data at object level, to find
inconsistencies before a migration. Each of these needs an SAP system; none was run for this page.

== What older books show, and what has changed

The book SAP S/4HANA Financial Accounting Configuration, 2nd edition (2023), lists `FD01` to `FD06` and `FK01` to `FK06`
in its appendix of useful transaction codes as the transactions to create, change, display, block and flag customer and
supplier master records. In SAP S/4HANA these are redirected to transaction `BP`, as the Simplification List says, so a
reader who follows the appendix literally ends up in `BP` anyway. The book Configuring SAP S/4HANA Finance, 3rd edition
(2025), describes the business partner approach as mandatory and `BP` as the one transaction, and its list of obsolete
transactions maps `FK01` to `BP`. Material written for SAP ERP describes customers and vendors as two separate master
records with separate create transactions; that no longer holds.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Looking for `XK01` or `FD01` to create an account.* They are redirected to `BP`; create the business partner and
  select the role.
* *Creating a business partner without the finance role.* A partner without the `FLCU00` or `FLVN00` role (or the
  customer or supplier role in the Fiori apps) has no finance data to fill in, such as the reconciliation account.
* *Choosing the wrong grouping.* The business partner category cannot be changed afterwards, and neither can the grouping
  the partner was created with. Decide them at creation.
* *Treating the reconciliation account like a normal account.* You cannot post to it manually; you post to the customer or
  supplier account and the system posts to it.
* *Changing the reconciliation account after posting.* Plan the regrouping adjustment at year end, and restrict the field.
* *Archiving a customer or supplier that is still linked.* SAP states that deletion of the customer or supplier
  record is not possible while it is connected to the business partner; the partner must be archived first, with its link.
* *Marking non-standard fields as required in a Public Edition account group.* SAP warns that this triggers errors in the
  Fiori apps that the screen cannot resolve.

== Summary

* In SAP S/4HANA a customer or a supplier is a business partner with the roles for financial accounting (`FLCU00`,
  `FLVN00` in SAP's role list) and for sales or purchasing. General data is kept once; company code data is kept per
  company code.
* The company code data holds the finance settings: the reconciliation account, payment terms, payment methods, house
  bank, tolerance group and clearing settings.
* The reconciliation account is a G/L account marked with the account type it serves. Every posting to a customer or
  supplier account also posts to it automatically, and nobody can post to it manually.
* `XK01`, `FD01` and their relatives are redirected to transaction `BP`; the Public Edition offers the Maintain Business
  Partner app and the three Manage Master Data apps.
* CVI keeps the business partner and the old customer and supplier records in step in both directions and decides how
  numbers are assigned. A conversion from SAP ERP needs it completely in place.
* In the example, the one partner Nordwind Logistics has a customer balance of EUR 1,190.00 and a supplier balance of EUR
  595.00, each mirrored in its reconciliation account.
