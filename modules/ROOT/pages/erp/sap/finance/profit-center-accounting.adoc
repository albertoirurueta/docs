A cost center answers "where did the money go?". A manager who runs a whole line of business needs a bigger question
answered: "did my part of the company make a profit?". The profit center is the unit that answers it. You divide the
company into profit centers, for example by product line, by region or by function, and the system then shows the
revenues and costs of each one, so that its manager can be held responsible for the result, like the head of a small
company inside the company. This page explains what a profit center is, how its master data works, how a profit center
ends up on each line of a journal entry without anyone typing it, what the dummy and default profit centers are for,
and how you report on profit centers. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition)
and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. Profit centers exist in both editions; the page says where the apps or the
settings differ.

== What a profit center is

SAP's help defines a profit center as an organizational unit in accounting that reflects a management-oriented
structure of the organization for the purpose of internal control. The operating result of a profit center can be
analyzed by the cost-of-sales approach or by the period accounting approach. If you also assign balance sheet items
(fixed assets, payables and receivables, material stock, work in process), the profit center becomes an investment
center, and key figures such as return on investment, working capital and cash flow can be calculated for it.

The difference from the units that you already know is who is responsible for what.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Unit |What SAP says it is for
|Cost center
|The unit where capacity costs arise. Its manager answers for costs only.
|Profit center
|An area of responsibility whose manager answers for the balance of costs and revenues.
|Business area (legacy)
|SAP's help says the essential difference is that profit centers serve internal control, while business areas are more
geared toward an external viewpoint. This site treats the business area as legacy, see
xref:erp/sap/finance/organizational-units.adoc[Organizational Units of Accounting].
|===

You can divide the company by product (product lines, divisions), by geography (regions, offices, production sites) or
by function (production, sales). SAP states that the concept fits any industry and any form of manufacturing. A profit
center is not a legal entity: the books of the company code stay as they are, and the profit centers are a second view
over the same postings.

== Profit center master data

The master data of a profit center holds its name, the controlling area it belongs to, its period of validity, the
person responsible, its place in the standard hierarchy and communication data such as an address. The rules that
follow come from SAP's pages on profit centers.

* *One controlling area.* Every profit center belongs to exactly one controlling area, because profit center data is
  shown by G/L account. Data can be added up across profit centers only if they use the same chart of accounts, the same
  fiscal year variant and a single currency. Profit Center Accounting always works inside one controlling area; SAP does
  not support profit center accounting across all controlling areas.
* *Time dependency.* Like a cost center, a profit center is valid for a period. You enter the period in which actual or
  plan data may be posted to it, and you can define time-based fields, such as the person responsible, so that a change
  of responsibility at the fiscal year change needs no new master record and loses no history.
* *The standard hierarchy.* Every profit center must be assigned to the standard hierarchy, a special profit center
  group that contains all profit centers of the controlling area. Reporting, allocations and planning use it. Other
  trees of profit centers are profit center groups.
* *A copy of the cost center structure.* If your profit centers follow your cost centers closely, SAP's help says you can
  create them by copying the cost center master data.

=== Creating a profit center

The on-premise help describes the individual processing of a profit center in these steps. The steps need an SAP system
and Customizing authorization; they were read from SAP's help and not run here (requires an SAP system; not run here).

. Enter the name of the new profit center. If you copy from an existing one, enter its name in the Copy from field.
. On the Basic Data tab, enter the validity period first as one analysis period, then divide it later if needed. Enter
  the profit center manager and the department.
. Assign the profit center to an end node of the standard hierarchy in the Hierarchy Area field. If the enterprise
  organization is active in the controlling area, the assignment made there has priority over a manual one.
. On the Indicators tab, set the lock indicator if no postings may be made to the profit center for a period.
. On the Company Codes tab, check which company codes may post to it. SAP says the system proposes the profit center to
  all company codes of the controlling area, and that a posting to a profit center that is not assigned to the company
  code gives an error.
. Activate the profit center. Until then its status is Inactive: Create and no postings are possible.

Three details from the same page prevent common mistakes. A purely numeric name has leading zeros added (333 becomes
0000000333), which makes the search 3* find nothing, so include at least one letter in the name. A locked profit center
stops the posting in every application, not only in Finance. The hierarchy area applies to the whole validity period, so
different analysis periods cannot have different areas.

== How a line item gets its profit center

The profit center is not an account assignment object that you post to directly. SAP's help puts it plainly: the data is
posted to other objects and passed on from there to a profit center, according to the assignment set in Customizing or in
the master data. So the posting names a cost center, an order, a project or a material, and the profit center comes with
it. This is why a manager's report by profit center needs no extra work from the people who post.

=== Assigning objects

The assignment of an object to a profit center is stored in that object's master record. SAP lists these objects: sales
orders, manufacturing orders (including production, CO production and process orders), cost objects, cost centers,
internal orders, business processes, maintenance orders, projects, materials, assets, real estate objects and
profitability segments. By the kind of cost, the help gives the usual route:

* revenues and goods usage through the assignment of sales document items,
* direct costs through the assignment of production orders and cost objects,
* overhead costs through the assignment of the account assignment objects of overhead cost controlling, such as cost
  centers and orders.

Some details that readers meet early:

* *Cost center.* Assigning a cost center to a profit center brings all primary costs from Financial Accounting and all
  secondary allocations of Cost Center Accounting to that profit center, and implicitly assigns the assets of the cost
  center to it. When Profit Center Accounting is active in the controlling area, the system issues a warning if the
  cost center master data has no profit center. SAP suggests, for an administration cost center that cannot be tied to
  one profit center, a service or allocation profit center that is cleared by assessment or distribution at period end.
* *Sales order.* The profit center is assigned per order item, the finer level of detail. The system proposes the profit
  center of the material in the supplying plant, which suits a division by product or by geography. The assignment
  travels along the chain sales order, delivery, goods issue and billing document, so the revenue arrives at the profit
  center of the sales order. If you divide the company by a sales-oriented structure instead, you define substitution
  rules and activate them for each controlling area.
* *Project.* A profit center can be set on the project definition and on each WBS element. For a network with its
  assignment at the header, the profit center of the header is updated; if none is entered there, the profit center of
  the related WBS element is used.
* *Manual G/L posting.* You can enter the profit center or the partner profit center yourself. For primary cost elements
  it is derived from the cost-relevant account assignment. For payables, receivables and automatically generated lines
  you cannot enter it by hand; with document splitting the system can supply it.

An incorrect assignment produces incorrect transaction data that, SAP warns, can usually be corrected only with
considerable effort. For that reason the help recommends checking assignments with the assignment monitor, which lists
the objects not yet assigned (for example materials without a profit center) or the objects assigned to a given profit
center, and jumps to the maintenance transaction to fix them. The menu path of the classic monitor is under Profit
Center Accounting, Master Data, Assignment Monitor; for General Ledger Accounting it is under Financial Accounting,
General Ledger, Master Records, Profit Center, Current Settings, Assignment Overview.

=== Derivation by inheritance

Most objects get their profit center by inheritance from another object. SAP's example: a purchase order item with a
product inherits the profit center from the product, and when the item is assigned to a controlling object such as a
cost center or a WBS element, it inherits the profit center from that object instead. This standard logic is defined by
the system, and it is also what the organizational change function relies on, described below.

== Dummy and default profit centers

Some postings reach an object that has no profit center. SAP's help names two tools for that case, and the Universal
Journal design changed the first of them.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Tool |What SAP says |Advice in SAP's help
|Dummy profit center
|A central default value in each controlling area, applied when a posting goes to an object with no profit center.
Analyzing what sits on it shows which objects are not assigned. In the Universal Journal design it is not necessary: the
Profit Center field stays empty in the line items.
|Do not use a dummy profit center on purpose as a default. If you use document splitting, a dummy profit center can have
undesired effects: payables are distributed to it when the expense lines have no profit center, and they can then not be
transferred manually.
|Default (clearing) profit center
|An ordinary profit center that is not an organizational area of responsibility but collects costs, revenues or balance
sheet postings during a period. At period end, assessment or distribution moves the data to the right profit centers.
Unlike the dummy profit center, it can be derived from other details such as company codes or accounts.
|Define separate profit centers for this purpose. SAP names three ways to derive one: the Customizing activity Assign
Default Profit Center to Accounts, substitution rules, or BAdIs.
|===

If you need every line item to have a profit center, SAP says you can make the Profit Center field a required entry
field in Customizing. The system then stops each posting to an object without a profit center and issues the error
message "Balancing field "Profit Center" is not filled in line item". If you still run classic Profit Center Accounting
next to the new General Ledger Accounting, a dummy profit center must exist.

== Profit centers and document splitting

A cost line carries a profit center because the object does. A payable, a receivable or a bank line does not belong to
any cost object, so without help it would have no profit center, and a balance sheet by profit center could not be
drawn. Document splitting fixes this by giving those lines the profit center of the lines they relate to. SAP's page on
the Profit Center Update scenario says that this is the way to display payables and receivables specific to the profit
centers where they occurred, and to create balance sheets at profit center level. The scenario is assigned to the ledger,
and the segment scenario needs it: if you use the Segment Reporting scenario with the segment characteristic, you also
activate the Profit Center Update scenario. How the splitting itself works is on
xref:erp/sap/finance/document-splitting.adoc[Document Splitting].

== Goods and services between profit centers

When a material leaves one profit center for another, the sending profit center delivered something and the receiving
one consumed it. The classic EC-PCA pages say that Profit Center Accounting then creates additional lines, such as
internal revenues, besides the original postings, so that the activity of each profit center is complete. They can be
valued at external prices, at the group costs of goods manufactured or at special transfer prices. If no transfer
prices are used, the postings are valued at the legal (external) prices.

For the Universal Journal design, SAP's Profit Center Update page gives the prerequisites for those additional lines to be
updated in General Ledger Accounting: transfer prices are used, the profit center valuation approach is used, and the
currency type and valuation type of the profit center valuation are assigned as a parallel currency to the leading
ledger and the other relevant ledgers. Without the profit center valuation, the Business Add-In named Update of
Internal Revenues Between Profit Centers (`FAGL_INTERNAL_ACCOUNTS`) activates the update of the lines created in the legal
valuation view, in all ledgers. The ledger and currency side is on
xref:erp/sap/finance/universal-parallel-accounting.adoc[Universal Parallel Accounting] and
xref:erp/sap/finance/ledgers-and-currencies.adoc[Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates].

== A worked example: one month in EX01

Example Trading Co. uses company code `EX01` (EUR, group currency GBP, ledger `0L`), controlling area `EX00` and two
profit centers, `EX-RETAIL` and `EX-WHOLE`, as in
xref:erp/sap/finance/organizational-units.adoc[Organizational Units of Accounting]. All numbers are illustrative values
for this site, not delivered content, and tax is left out. For the group currency the example uses the made-up rate of 0.85
GBP for 1 EUR. The month has four events:

. Sales invoices of 12,000.00 EUR. The sales order items were assigned to profit centers: 8,000.00 EUR to `EX-RETAIL`
  and 4,000.00 EUR to `EX-WHOLE`.
. Office rent of 1,000.00 EUR on the cost center `EX-ADM`. The profit center in the cost center's master record is
  `EX-RETAIL`.
. Freight of 400.00 EUR on an internal order that is assigned to `EX-WHOLE`.
. A consulting fee of 900.00 EUR on an object that has no profit center. In the Universal Journal design the Profit
  Center field stays empty on this line.

Reading the profit center of each line item and subtracting, the result of a profit center is

[stem]
++++
P_c = R_c - K_c
++++

where stem:[R_c] is the revenue and stem:[K_c] the cost of profit center stem:[c]. The first column of numbers below is
the situation after the four events.

[cols="2,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Profit center |Revenue (EUR) |Costs (EUR) |Result (EUR) |Result (GBP at 0.85)
|`EX-RETAIL` |8,000.00 |1,000.00 |7,000.00 |5,950.00
|`EX-WHOLE` |4,000.00 |400.00 |3,600.00 |3,060.00
|(no profit center) |0.00 |900.00 |-900.00 |-765.00
|*Total* |*12,000.00* |*2,300.00* |*9,700.00* |*8,245.00*
|===

The total is 12,000 - 1,000 - 400 - 900 = 9,700 EUR, and 9,700 x 0.85 = 8,245.00 GBP. The line without a profit center is
visible, which is the use of the empty field that SAP's help describes: you see that an object is not assigned and you
can correct the assignment, or you can forward the amount afterwards by assessment or distribution. Suppose the company
decides to spread the 900.00 EUR by revenue. With stem:[C] the amount to spread and stem:[f_i] the key of receiver stem:[i]
(here the revenue), receiver stem:[i] gets

[stem]
++++
C_i = C \cdot \frac{f_i}{\sum_j f_j}, \qquad C_{\text{EX-RETAIL}} = 900 \cdot \frac{8000}{12000} = 600, \qquad C_{\text{EX-WHOLE}} = 900 \cdot \frac{4000}{12000} = 300
++++

and the 600 plus 300 equals the 900 that is taken off the empty line. The cycles, the sender and receiver rules and the
tracing factors for this step belong to the allocation functions of Controlling, which are not described on this page.
After the distribution:

[cols="2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Profit center |Result before (EUR) |Distributed cost (EUR) |Result after (EUR / GBP)
|`EX-RETAIL` |7,000.00 |600.00 |6,400.00 / 5,440.00
|`EX-WHOLE` |3,600.00 |300.00 |3,300.00 / 2,805.00
|(no profit center) |-900.00 |-900.00 (taken off) |0.00 / 0.00
|*Total* |*9,700.00* |*0.00 net* |*9,700.00 / 8,245.00*
|===

The company result has not changed (6,400 + 3,300 = 9,700; in GBP 5,440 + 2,805 = 8,245), because a distribution moves
cost between profit centers and creates none. This is the property to check after any allocation to profit centers.

== Reporting on profit centers

Reporting is always tied to a controlling area; SAP's information system pages say that Profit Center Accounting does
not support analyses across controlling areas. You can store the controlling area as a user parameter with the ID `CAC`
and the system then uses it automatically.

* *Drilldown reports for profit centers (General Ledger Accounting).* SAP delivers standard reports for the Profit Center
  Update scenario: `0SAPBSPL-03` Profit Center Group Plan-Actual Difference, `0SAPBSPL-04` Plan/Plan/Actual, `0SAPBSPL-05`
  Key Figures and `0SAPBSPL-06` Profit Center Comparison: Return on Investment. They can be called for one profit
  center, a range, or a profit center group, and drilled down by profit center, partner profit center or secondary cost
  element. The prerequisites are the activated scenario Profit Center Update (or Segment Reporting), financial
  statement versions with accounts assigned, and the reports imported from client 000. From a report you can reach the
  G/L line items (`FAGLL03`), the document (`FB03`) and its Controlling document.
* *Plan and actual by profit center (CDS view).* The consumption view `C_PROFITCENTERPLANACTQ2701`, "Profit Centers -
  Plan/Actuals", answers the question of actual and plan amounts per profit center and G/L account in company code or
  global currency, with the absolute and percentage differences. It is built on the cube
  `I_ActualPlanJrnlEntryItemCube`, its parameters include ledger, fiscal period, plan category, company code, profit
  center, segment and the G/L account hierarchy, and SAP's page lists its release status as not released, so it is not
  a stable interface for your own code.
* *Asset Accounting.* The asset history sheet can show assets by profit center or segment, and the logical database ADA
  offers Profit Center and Segment as selections in the standard asset reports.
* *Classic Profit Center Accounting.* SAP's classic pages describe five report groups (drilldown, Report Painter,
  summary records, line item reports and a master data index), based on the summary table `GLPCT`, and say that line
  items must be updated in Profit Center Accounting for line item reports to work.

Profit center reporting needs the group tree. A profit center group gives a subtotal, such as all retail profit centers,
and the standard hierarchy gives the complete one. SAP's help for the Manage Profit Center Groups app also notes that a
new root node item must be set as report-relevant before it is used in reporting.

== Changing the profit center of an object: organizational changes

A reorganization, for example when a product line moves from one profit center to another, would be risky if you simply
edited the master records, because open items and past postings refer to the old profit center. SAP provides the
organizational changes feature (the Manage Organizational Changes app) for it. You enter the root objects, such as
products, and the new profit center; dependent objects such as purchase documents, sales documents and production orders
inherit the new assignment through the derivation logic described earlier. Two rules from SAP's help show what the system
guarantees:

* An open item keeps the profit center it had when it was posted. The change creates an additional entry in `ACDOCA` that
  transfers the item from the old to the new profit center, and the clearing later considers both.
* A new journal entry with a posting date on or after the effective date gets the new profit center. One with an
  earlier posting date is posted with the old profit center, and the system generates a transfer posting on the effective
  date to correct the balance caused by it.

SAP also warns not to let a substitution change the profit center of the master data being processed during the change,
and to complete the previous period before processing the change when you use periodic result analysis and settlement,
which are not time-dependent in the derivation. For a cost center, you do not enter the new profit center in the
organizational change app; you create a new validity period of the cost center (Manage Cost Centers or `KS02`) with the
new profit center.

== Common errors and pitfalls

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Cause and remedy
|Posting to an object is refused with "Balancing field "Profit Center" is not filled in line item"
|The Profit Center field is a required entry field and the object has no profit center. Assign one in the object's master
record, or do not make the field required.
|Posting to a profit center gives an error about the company code
|The profit center is not assigned to the company code on its Company Codes tab, or it is locked, or its status is still
Inactive: Create.
|Search finds no profit center called "3*"
|A numeric name was stored with leading zeros. Include a letter in the name.
|Payables sit on a dummy profit center and cannot be moved
|Document splitting distributed them to the dummy profit center. Do not use a dummy profit center with document splitting.
|A report by profit center shows a large amount on an empty profit center
|Objects without an assignment. Run the assignment monitor, correct the master records, then distribute what remains.
|Cost center posting gives a warning
|No profit center in the cost center master data while Profit Center Accounting is active.
|Amounts of two profit centers cannot be added
|Different chart of accounts, fiscal year variant or currency. Aggregation needs all three the same.
|Costs from a reorganized cost center cannot be allocated with Universal Allocation
|Postings after the effective date already exist, so the sender has different profit centers for one object. Choose the
effective date with existing postings in mind.
|===

== Summary

* A profit center is an area of responsibility whose manager answers for costs and revenues. It belongs to one
  controlling area, is valid for a period, and sits in the standard hierarchy.
* In SAP S/4HANA the profit center is a field of the Universal Journal line item. The line gets it from the object that is
  posted to, by assignment in the object's master record, by inheritance (purchase order item from product or controlling
  object), by substitution or by a default profit center for the account, and by document splitting for payables and
  receivables.
* The dummy profit center belongs to the classic design. In the Universal Journal an unassigned object leaves the field
  empty, and SAP advises against dummy profit centers when document splitting is active; use default (clearing) profit
  centers on purpose instead.
* Profit centers are maintained in Manage Profit Centers (F3516 on premise, F3516A new version in the Public Edition),
  grouped with Manage Profit Center Groups, and checked with the where-used list and the assignment monitor.
* Reports exist as drilldown reports for profit center groups, as the plan and actual view per profit center, and as
  asset reports. All are bound to one controlling area.
* In the worked example the result of 9,700.00 EUR (8,245.00 GBP) is split 7,000 and 3,600 with 900 unassigned, and a
  distribution of that 900 by revenue gives 6,400 and 3,300 without changing the total.
