Accounts payable is the part of accounting that keeps track of what the company owes its suppliers: an invoice arrives,
it has to be checked and recorded, and later it has to be paid on time and, if the supplier offers a discount for quick
payment, at the right amount. In SAP the supplier's account is a subledger, a detailed list of every open invoice and
payment of that supplier, that adds up to one account in the general ledger. This page follows one purchase of the
fictitious Example Trading Co. (company code `EX01`) from the goods receipt to the bank statement. It explains the two
ways to enter a supplier invoice, what the goods receipt and invoice receipt (GR/IR) clearing account does between them,
how terms of payment and cash discount decide the amount and the date, and how an outgoing payment clears the invoice.
After reading it you can say which entry route fits an invoice, read the postings it produces and find the apps that show
what is open. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud
Public Edition 2608. The ideas are the same in both; the names of the apps and transactions differ, and the page says
where.

== The big picture: from purchase to payment

A purchase moves through four events, and each of them is an accounting document.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Step |Business event |What it does to the books

|1 |The ordered goods arrive. |The goods receipt raises the stock account and credits the GR/IR clearing account.
|2 |The supplier's invoice is entered. |The invoice clears the GR/IR account and the supplier account is credited, so the
amount becomes a payable.
|3 |The company pays. |The payment debits the supplier account, which clears the invoice, and credits a bank account
or a bank clearing account.
|4 |The bank statement arrives. |The bank statement posting moves the amount from the clearing account to the real bank
account.
|===

When goods are not involved, because the invoice is for a service or an expense that nobody ordered through purchasing,
steps 1 and 2 shrink to one posting: the invoice is entered straight in Finance and debits an expense account. The next
sections explain the two routes, then payment, then the numbers.

== Entering a supplier invoice

SAP's help describes two families of invoice entry and the apps that belong to each.

=== Route 1: through purchasing (logistics invoice verification)

If the invoice refers to a purchase order, the invoice is entered in the procurement part of the system, in the area
that SAP's menu names Logistics Invoice Verification. The apps of this route are:

* *Manage Supplier Invoices*, app ID `F0859`. The Create Supplier Invoice tile creates an invoice, a credit memo, a
  subsequent credit or a subsequent debit, with or without purchase order references, and the Supplier Invoices List app
  opens existing invoices.
* *Create Supplier Invoice - Advanced*, app ID `MIRO`. SAP describes it as the app to create and verify supplier
  invoices for content, prices and arithmetic; it updates the invoice documents in Materials Management and Financial
  Accounting, and it completes the material procurement process from the purchase requisition via purchasing to the goods
  receipt.

What the system does on posting is the reason this route exists. If you choose Simulate or Post, it compares the
quantity and amount of each invoice item with the purchase order item. If goods-receipt-based invoice verification has
been defined for the item, it compares the invoice with the goods receipt as well. When the deviation is outside the
tolerances that are defined, the invoice is still posted, but it is automatically blocked for payment, and an approval
process starts if the system finds a responsible person. So the invoice, the order and the receipt are checked against
each other before money can leave.

The same route lets you:

* post straight to a G/L account, a material account or an asset account when there is no purchase order reference,
* park an invoice, so that one clerk starts it and another completes and posts it,
* enter tax, cash discount (gross or net) and foreign currency,
* reduce an invoice that has a deviation in amount or quantity: the app then posts an invoice and a credit memo at the
  same time and creates a letter of complaint,
* enter planned and unplanned delivery costs, and
* trace the account movements that a posting will cause, before posting it.

A detail that surprises beginners: when a supplier invoice is posted in Manage Supplier Invoices, journal entries are
posted in Finance as a result, and the supplier invoice and its journal entries have different document numbers. You can
display the journal entries in the Manage Supplier Line Items app.

=== Route 2: directly in Finance

For a supplier invoice or credit memo that has no purchase order, SAP's help names the transaction `FB60`, titled Create
Incoming Invoices. It is the Finance-side entry: you give the company code, the supplier and the amounts, and you post.
The classic invoice entry of the Finance menu (Accounts payable, Document entry, Invoice) works the same way. SAP's page for it describes these screens:

. The header screen, where you enter the document date, the document type, the company code, the posting date and the
  currency.
. The first line item, for which you enter the posting key and the supplier account number. The posting key and the
  reconciliation account of the supplier decide which fields appear next.
. The remaining line items. You need at least one supplier line item and one G/L account line item, and the document can
  be posted when debits equal credits.

In the standard system, a credit memo uses posting key 21 for the supplier line item, according to SAP's credit memo
page. SAP's installment example shows posting key 31 on the supplier line of an invoice and 40 on the G/L account
line; the page on xref:erp/sap/finance/journal-entries-document-types-and-posting-keys.adoc[posting keys] explains what the numbers mean. On posting, the system generates the document and
updates the supplier account balance and the G/L account balance. When you enter an invoice, an interface lets you check
whether it was entered before, without changing the standard system.

The Public Edition lists `FB60` Create Incoming Invoices among the settings and apps related to supplier invoices
together with Edit Supplier Invoice Settings, and its Manage Supplier Invoices app also creates invoices without a
purchase order reference, so a user of the Public Edition can enter such an invoice in Manage Supplier Invoices.

== The GR/IR clearing account

The goods usually arrive before the invoice, and the two events are done by different people, often in different
departments. The GR/IR clearing account lets the books stay balanced in between. SAP's help gives the mechanism in one
sentence: goods receipts and invoice receipts are offset to a clearing account. When goods are delivered, the system
posts the value to the stock account (or to the account of the purchase order) and the offsetting entry to the GR/IR
clearing account. When the invoice is posted, the GR/IR account is cleared and the amount is credited to the supplier
account.

SAP says that postings to the same purchase order and purchase order item are updated on the account but not cleared
automatically. Open items are cleared with automatic clearing (`F.13` or `F.13E`), which analyzes the items by purchase
order number and item and clears those with a zero balance. Items that do not produce a zero balance, or cannot be
assigned to each other, stay open. You may post manually to a GR/IR clearing account, but you cannot clear it manually:
for correction, SAP offers the Clear GR/IR Clearing Account app (app ID `MR11`).

Reading the balance is simple:

* a credit balance means that goods have been received but not yet invoiced, and
* a debit balance means that goods have been invoiced but not yet received.

If the quantity invoiced is larger than the quantity received, the system expects further goods receipts for the
purchase order; if the quantity received is larger, it expects further invoices. SAP says that before you create a
balance sheet you analyze the GR/IR clearing account with a program that displays everything posted and not cleared, and
that it posts adjustment entries that are reversed after the closing. SAP also says that the GR/IR clearing account is
usually cleared at the end of a period or fiscal year for those order items for which no further goods receipts or
invoices are expected.

How the clearing posting is balanced depends on the material, according to the app's help:

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Case |Offsetting entry of the GR/IR clearing posting

|Material with moving average price
|The stock account, unless there is no stock coverage. If the stock is smaller than the quantity to clear, only the actual
stock quantity is debited or credited proportionally and the rest goes to a price difference account.

|Material with standard price
|A price difference account.

|Purchase order assigned to an account
|The cost or fixed asset account shown in the account assignment of the purchase order.
|===

The account determination page of this section shows how the accounts of the goods receipt and of the price difference
are found.

== Terms of payment and cash discount

The terms of payment tell the company when an invoice is due and what it may deduct if it pays early. SAP's help puts it
this way: terms can be agreed on three levels, for example within 5 days 3 percent cash discount, within 10 days 2
percent, within 20 days net. A terms-of-payment key, defined once in Customizing, carries these conditions and also the
baseline date as of which the days are counted, so that a clerk only enters the key. The buyer usually defines the terms
in the purchase order header and they are suggested when the invoice is entered, where they can be overwritten. If the
supplier master record holds a key, the system proposes it whenever you enter an item for this supplier.

On the invoice screens, a rate such as "2/10 Net 30" is entered as 10 days and 2 percent, and the due date for net
payment is 30 days. You may enter up to three decimal places in the percentage, for example 2.125. The amount on which the
discount is calculated is the discount base. If you leave it empty, the system calculates it, depending on whether the
configuration says the discount is calculated on the net or the gross amount, and it leaves out G/L account line items
that are marked as without cash discount (the shipping line of an invoice for goods and shipping, for example). You can
also type the discount amount, which overrides the system's calculation. SAP warns that, once the terms have been
adopted, they are no longer changed automatically: if you change the terms or the supplier while creating an invoice,
check the terms and reset them manually.

=== Example: due dates and discount for EX01

An invoice of EUR 1,428.00 (the gross amount of the worked example below) has the terms 2/10 Net 30. The baseline date
in this made-up example is the invoice date, 2026-03-01.

[cols="2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Period |Last day |What the company pays

|Within 10 days |2026-03-11 |Gross amount less 2 percent discount
|Within 30 days |2026-03-31 |The full gross amount
|===

Taking the gross amount as the discount base, which the configuration may or may not do:

[stem]
++++
1\,428.00 \times 0.02 = 28.56, \qquad 1\,428.00 - 28.56 = 1\,399.44
++++

The tax effect of the discount is ignored here to keep the lines visible, as on the account determination page. Whether
the effect on tax is posted depends on the configuration of the company code.

=== Posting an invoice net

Some countries require that the cash discount is withheld from the cost, typically for property, fixed assets and
equipment. SAP's help then speaks of posting the invoice net: the net amount, gross less discount, is shown in the
expense or balance sheet account, you use a net document type, and the system creates an additional document line item for
the discount. If the invoice is paid through the payment program, the system compares the discount taken at posting
with the percentage valid at payment time, and the difference goes to a separate expense account whose accounts, rules
and posting key are set up in Customizing.

=== Installment payments

A terms-of-payment key can also split an invoice into installments, each with its own payment term. SAP's example posts
an invoice of 1,000.00 with posting key 31 for the supplier and 40 for the expense, and the simulation then shows three
supplier lines of 300.00, 400.00 and 300.00:

[stem]
++++
300.00 + 400.00 + 300.00 = 1\,000.00
++++

The original supplier line shows 0.00 until the posting is simulated. After posting, the total amount is replaced by the
individual installments. A later page on payment terms in this section goes deeper into discount rates and
installments.

== Parking, blocking and releasing

Three mechanisms stop an invoice before it is final.

* *Parking.* SAP's document parking lets you save a document without posting it, for supplier, customer, G/L account and
  asset data. When you park, the system only checks that the data exists; you can ask for a completeness check as well,
  and authorizations can separate users who may park from users who may also post. In the purchasing route the same idea
  is called holding or parking an invoice: one clerk parks it and another completes it.
* *Payment blocks.* An invoice or a whole supplier account can be blocked for payment. In the Public Edition the
  Manage Payment Blocks app (app ID `F0593A`) sets and removes blocks on invoices or supplier accounts, with a block
  reason and notes; the Manage Supplier Line Items app can also set or remove a payment block.
* *Invoices blocked by the check.* An invoice that is blocked because of variances or marked by hand is released with the
  Release Blocked Invoices app (app ID `MRBR`). SAP lists the filters Blocked Due to Variances, Manual Payment Block and
  Stochastically Blocked; the system checks each blocking reason and deletes a reason that is no longer valid. A blocked
  invoice must be released for payment, possibly after consultation with purchasing or the supplier.

== Paying the supplier

The payment is the event that clears the invoice. SAP's help distinguishes the usual way from the manual one.

*Automatic payment.* The automatic payment program selects open items, creates payment documents and
supplies the data to the payment medium programs, which print a payment list or payment forms such as checks, or create
files for the bank. The program processes domestic and foreign payments for suppliers and customers. In the Public
Edition the apps for the run are Manage Automatic Payments, Revise Payment Proposals and Manage Payment Media. Another page
of this section explains the program in detail.

*Single payment.* If an invoice has to be paid at once, the Post Outgoing Payments app (app ID `F1612` in the Public
Edition help) posts and clears a single outgoing payment in one step. It lists the open items that can be cleared,
lets you add or change the discount for each invoice, creates residual items for a residual amount with a reason code,
posts a payment on account or to a G/L account, simulates the resulting journal entry, and clears the payment against the
selected items. The classic clearing functions of accounts payable, according to SAP's table, are the outgoing payment,
the outgoing payment with printed form for an immediate payment by check, the internal transfer posting (for an invoice
cleared because of a product defect, for example) and the incoming payment, which SAP uses for a refund from the
supplier such as an annual quantity discount.

*Bank statement.* SAP states that the system also supports the automatic entry of the bank postings for outgoing payments
by importing bank statement data with the Electronic Bank Statement function.

Whichever way the payment is made, the supplier line of the invoice and the supplier line of the payment have equal and
opposite amounts, so the open item management of the supplier account clears them and enters the clearing document
number in both. The open items page of this section covers what a clearing is.

== Worked example: procure-to-pay for Example Trading Co.

Company code `EX01` keeps its books in euro (EUR), uses British pounds (GBP) as group currency and writes the ledgers
`0L` (leading) and `2L` (local principle). Every line below is written to both ledgers, and the group currency amount
follows the exchange rate of the posting date, which this example does not need. All amounts are made up, the tax rate of
19 percent and the discount of 2 percent are illustrative, and the document numbers are left out. The material is valued
at a price that equals the order price, so there is no price difference.

*Step 1, goods receipt.* `EX01` orders 100 pieces of a trading good at EUR 12.00 and receives all 100:

[stem]
++++
100 \times 12.00 = 1\,200.00
++++

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Debit EUR |Credit EUR

|Stock account of the material |1,200.00 |
|GR/IR clearing account | |1,200.00
|*Total* |*1,200.00* |*1,200.00*
|===

*Step 2, supplier invoice.* The supplier invoices the same price, with tax:

[stem]
++++
1\,200.00 \times 0.19 = 228.00, \qquad 1\,200.00 + 228.00 = 1\,428.00
++++

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Debit EUR |Credit EUR

|GR/IR clearing account |1,200.00 |
|Input tax account |228.00 |
|Supplier (reconciliation account of the supplier) | |1,428.00
|*Total* |*1,428.00* |*1,428.00*
|===

The GR/IR account was credited 1,200.00 in step 1 and is debited 1,200.00 now, so its balance is zero and nothing is
left to clear for these 100 pieces.

*Step 3, payment within the discount period.* The terms 2/10 Net 30 apply and `EX01` pays on 2026-03-10, inside the 10 days of the
earlier section. The payment clears the supplier item, the discount is an automatic posting, and the cash leaves through
a bank clearing account (a bank subaccount, SAP's term on the charts of accounts page, for payments in transit), which
open item management keeps open until the bank reports:

[stem]
++++
1\,428.00 - 28.56 = 1\,399.44
++++

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Debit EUR |Credit EUR

|Supplier account (clears the invoice item) |1,428.00 |
|Bank clearing account | |1,399.44
|Cash discount gain account (automatic) | |28.56
|*Total* |*1,428.00* |*1,428.00*
|===

*Step 4, bank statement.* The bank statement reports the debit of EUR 1,399.44. Importing it posts the bank side and
clears the bank clearing account, which is the transfer pattern of the open items page:

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Debit EUR |Credit EUR

|Bank clearing account |1,399.44 |
|Bank account | |1,399.44
|*Total* |*1,399.44* |*1,399.44*
|===

image::sap-finance-procure-to-pay-t-accounts.svg["T-accounts for stock, GR/IR clearing, input tax, supplier, bank clearing, bank and cash discount gain after the four postings of the example, with the GR/IR clearing, supplier and bank clearing accounts at zero",width=1000]

After the four postings the stock account holds 1,200.00 and the input tax account 228.00 on the debit side, the bank
account is credited 1,399.44 and the discount gain account 28.56:

[stem]
++++
1\,200.00 + 228.00 = 1\,428.00 = 1\,399.44 + 28.56
++++

Debits and credits are equal, as in every posting. The lines of steps 3 and 4 show the pattern SAP's help describes
for payment and clearing and the bank subaccount purpose from the charts of accounts page; the pages used here do not list
the exact lines of the bank statement posting, so the accounts and the use of a clearing account depend on the
Customizing of the company code. This example requires an SAP system to reproduce; not run here.

=== A partial invoice leaves a GR/IR balance

If `EX01` receives all 100 pieces (EUR 1,200.00) but the supplier invoices only 60 pieces, the invoice clears 60 pieces at
the order price:

[stem]
++++
60 \times 12.00 = 720.00, \qquad 1\,200.00 - 720.00 = 480.00
++++

The GR/IR clearing account keeps a credit balance of EUR 480.00, which means goods received and not yet invoiced. The
system still expects an invoice for the other 40 pieces. If the supplier will never invoice them, this balance is what
the GR/IR account maintenance clears at period end.

=== Example: an invoice without a purchase order

`EX01` receives a bill of EUR 500.00 for a software subscription, with the same illustrative tax of 19 percent, and no
purchase order exists:

[stem]
++++
500.00 \times 0.19 = 95.00, \qquad 500.00 + 95.00 = 595.00
++++

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Line |Debit EUR |Credit EUR

|Expense account entered on the invoice |500.00 |
|Input tax account |95.00 |
|Supplier (reconciliation account of the supplier) | |595.00
|*Total* |*595.00* |*595.00*
|===

This is the minimum SAP's help names for direct entry: at least one supplier line and one G/L account line, with
balanced debits and credits. No goods receipt and no GR/IR account are involved, so nothing compares the bill with an
order.

== Looking at the supplier account

The classic way to see the open and cleared items of a supplier is the supplier line item report `FBL1N`, named on the
open items page. The Fiori counterpart is the Manage Supplier Line Items app (app ID `F0712` in the Public Edition
help). SAP describes it for ad hoc requests or recurring reports: you can see all line items of a supplier account, or all
open supplier invoices of a company code that are overdue at a key date. Besides displaying, you can set or remove
payment blocks, change line item attributes such as payment data, assignment or line item text, create a manual payment,
save queries as variants, export to a spreadsheet and send emails. The app shows line items from the point of view of
financial operations, which is why you cannot use it to display information related to General Ledger accounting.

SAP's overview of the apps for accounts payable also lists reports for managers: Aging Analysis, Overdue Payables, Future
Payables, Cash Discount Forecast, Cash Discount Utilization, Days Payable Outstanding, Invoice Processing Analysis and the
Supplier Payment Analysis variants.

== Correcting a posted invoice

A wrong supplier invoice is reversed, not deleted. SAP's rules for document reversal say a document can be reversed only
if it contains no cleared items, contains only customer, supplier and G/L account items, was posted using Financial
Accounting, and all entered values such as business area, cost center and tax code are still valid. A cleared line item
must first have its clearing reset. Documents from Materials Management must be reversed with the function of that
component, because the reversal function of Finance does not reverse all the values needed. For an invoice posted in the
Manage Supplier Invoices app this means the reversal is done on the invoice with status Posted, which you open from the
Supplier Invoices List. You usually post the reversal in the same period as the original; if that period is closed, enter a
date in an open period.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Expecting an invoice for a purchase order to post like a plain Finance invoice.* Through purchasing, a deviation
  outside tolerance blocks the invoice for payment even though it is posted.
* *Changing the supplier or the terms and trusting the old terms.* The terms adopted by the system are not recalculated;
  check them and reset them by hand.
* *Trying to clear a GR/IR item by hand.* SAP says manual clearing of these accounts is not possible; use automatic
  clearing or the Clear GR/IR Clearing Account app.
* *Ignoring a long-lived GR/IR balance.* A credit balance is goods received but not invoiced and a debit balance is goods
  invoiced but not received; both should be explained before the balance sheet is created.
* *Reversing a goods-based invoice with the Finance reversal.* Documents from Materials Management must be reversed in that
  component, and a document with cleared items cannot be reversed until its clearing is reset.
* *Looking for the supplier invoice number in the journal entry.* In Manage Supplier Invoices the invoice and the journal
  entries have different document numbers.
* *Forgetting that a discount base can be net or gross.* The configuration decides when the discount base is left empty,
  and G/L lines marked without cash discount are left out of it.
* *Entering the same invoice twice.* The system offers a duplicate-entry check for invoices; it is part of the settings of
  the invoice apps.

== Summary

* Accounts payable keeps one subledger account per supplier, and every open invoice and payment of that supplier is an
  open item that adds up to the reconciliation account in the general ledger.
* An invoice for a purchase order is entered through purchasing (`F0859` Manage Supplier Invoices, `MIRO` Create Supplier
  Invoice - Advanced) and is compared with the order and the goods receipt; an invoice without a purchase order is entered
  in Finance (`FB60`) and needs at least one supplier line and one G/L account line.
* The GR/IR clearing account sits between the goods receipt and the invoice: a credit balance means received and not
  invoiced, a debit balance invoiced and not received. It is cleared by automatic clearing or the Clear GR/IR Clearing
  Account app, not by hand.
* The terms of payment key holds the days, the discounts and the baseline date. In the example, 2/10 Net 30 on EUR 1,428.00
  gives a discount of EUR 28.56 and a payment of EUR 1,399.44 if paid within ten days.
* A payment, whether from the payment program or the Post Outgoing Payments app, clears the supplier item; the bank
  statement then moves the amount from the clearing account to the bank account.
* A posted invoice is corrected by reversal, which requires that nothing in it is cleared; a goods-based document is reversed
  in Materials Management.
