A general ledger can hold thousands of accounts, and nobody reads a balance sheet that lists every one of them. A financial statement is a short, ordered summary: assets on one side, liabilities and equity on the other, and a profit and loss statement with revenue and expenses grouped into a few lines. In SAP S/4HANA the rule that groups accounts into those lines is a financial statement version, usually shortened to FSV. This page explains what an FSV is, what it is made of, how the same account balances give different statements under different versions, and how an FSV is built and put into use. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The concepts are the same in both; the page names where the maintenance tools differ. Everything about maintaining an FSV requires an SAP system, so the step lists are labeled as not run here.

== Key terms

* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#financial-statement-version[Financial statement version (FSV)]: a hierarchical arrangement of G/L accounts, used to generate financial statements and structured reports such as a trial balance.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#fsv-node[FSV node]: one element of an FSV, also called a financial statement item or FS item, that holds a group of G/L accounts.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#special-node[Special node]: one of seven nodes that the system creates in every FSV and that carries a fixed meaning in reporting.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#contra-node[Contra node]: a node paired with another node that holds the same accounts with the opposite debit or credit setting, so that the accounts show on the side that matches their balance.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#global-hierarchy[Global hierarchy]: a hierarchy that is maintained in the app Manage Global Hierarchies and shared by many reports; the FSV is one hierarchy type.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#balance-sheet[Balance sheet]: the statement of assets, liabilities and equity at a date.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#profit-and-loss-statement[Profit and loss statement]: the statement of revenue and expenses for a period.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#trial-balance[Trial balance]: a list of account balances that shows debits equal credits.

== The idea: a tree of accounts, kept apart from the accounts

SAP defines a financial statement version as a hierarchical arrangement of G/L accounts. The accounts can follow the legal rules for your financial statements, or any order you need. The important point for a newcomer is that the tree is separate from the accounts. A G/L account does not know which line of the balance sheet it appears on. The FSV says so. That is why one company can hold several FSVs over the same chart of accounts and produce, from the same postings, one statement for the tax office, another for management and a third, shorter, for the group.

SAP gives three purposes for an FSV. You need one to generate financial statements. You need one for reports that are structured lists of account balances, such as the trial balance and the trial balance comparison. And it is a basis for planning in General Ledger Accounting, for example through the Balance Sheet/Income Statement app. SAP's examples of different versions are one for each organizational unit, such as the corporate group or a single company code, and one for each level of detail: the accounting department may want every account listed with its balance while senior management wants the same information summarized.

== What an FSV is made of

=== Header attributes

An FSV has a few attributes at header level.

* An FSV ID and a description.
* The chart of accounts. Only accounts of that chart can be assigned to the FSV. For a group financial statement the FSV can instead use a group chart of accounts, as described below.
* A valid-from and a valid-to date. The FSV can be applied in a report only if the report period lies within this time frame.
* Optional settings that change what the FSV may contain: contra nodes (which also enable bank account grouping), functional areas, group account numbers and aging.

Because an FSV is time dependent, SAP lets you create several hierarchy versions under one FSV ID as long as their time frames do not overlap. The app shows them as time frames. They share the header attributes but can have completely different structures, which is how a company changes its statement layout from one fiscal year to the next without losing the old layout for old periods.

=== Nodes and what each carries

The elements of an FSV are its nodes, which SAP also calls financial statement items or FS items. Each node contains a group of G/L accounts, and nodes can contain other nodes, so the tree can be as deep as the statement needs. A node has these attributes.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Attribute |What it does

|Node ID and description
|Identifies the node. The description can be kept in every language that SAP S/4HANA supports; the description of the FSV itself is kept only in your logon language.

|Debit Balance and Credit Balance indicators
|Select both and the node shows whether its total is in debit or in credit. Accounts assigned to the node inherit these indicators unless you set them differently. Selecting only one is possible only when the node has a contra node.

|Contra node
|Pairs two nodes that hold the same accounts, one with Debit Balance only and one with Credit Balance only.

|Bank account grouping
|Lets a node accept only accounts of type Cash Account, to group bank balances by business partner (see below).

|+/- sign change
|Reverses the sign of the node total in reports. SAP's example is revenue, which the ledger holds as a negative number (a credit): with the indicator it appears as positive. The indicator changes only how the total of that node is displayed, not the accounts in it, and the help notes that Balance Sheet/Income Statement does not support it yet while Balance Sheet/Income Statement - Multidimensional does.

|Reclassification increment
|Sets an aging attribute so that the balance sheet can show short-term, mid-term or long-term items of liabilities and receivables by due date. Subnodes and accounts inherit it unless an account is set to No Reclassification.

|Semantic tags
|Mark a node so that analytical reports can calculate measures and key figures from it.
|===

Accounts that belong to the chart of accounts but are not assigned to any node appear under a special node called Not Assigned, which makes a forgotten account visible instead of silently missing from a statement.

=== The seven special nodes

When you create an FSV the system generates a basic structure of seven special nodes. They carry a fixed meaning in reporting, and you can edit only three of them by assigning accounts: Assets, Liabilities and Equity, and Financial Statement Notes. The system fills the others.

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Special node |Editable |What it holds and means

|Assets
|Yes
|The accounts that make up the asset side. Together with Liabilities and Equity, the accounts here determine the balance sheet result.

|Liabilities and Equity
|Yes
|The accounts of the liability and equity side.

|Net Result: Loss
|No
|The loss shown in the balance sheet when the balance that the system calculates is in debit, displayed as a positive amount.

|Net Result: Profit
|No
|The profit shown in the balance sheet when the calculated balance is in credit, displayed as a negative amount.

|P&L Result
|No
|The accounts that are not assigned to any other special node. The balance of these accounts is the result of the profit and loss statement.

|Financial Statement Notes
|Yes
|A node for quick control. SAP's FAQ says customers often use it for accounts that should have a zero balance at month end, so that a balance shows at a glance that something was not cleared. These accounts do not count toward the balance sheet or profit and loss result; the reports list them in a Notes section.

|Not Assigned
|No
|The accounts of the chart that are in no node. They are not counted in the results either; the reports list them in an Unassigned Accounts section.
|===

Behind the names there are fixed semantic tags (for example ASSET, LIAB_EQUIT, NETRESULT, PL_RESULT, FS_NOTES and UNASSIGNED). They apply only to the hierarchy type Financial Statement Version and cannot be assigned by hand to other nodes.

=== Assigning accounts: one account, one place

You assign G/L accounts to a node one by one or as a range from one account number to another. Two details matter. A range can be changed only as a whole: you cannot delete single accounts from it or give them different debit and credit settings. And if the FSV allows functional areas, you can assign functional areas to a node instead of accounts, but not a mixture of both in one node. Functional areas make profit and loss statements by cost of sales accounting possible, and SAP stresses that the functional area logic works for profit and loss accounts by the functional area on the postings, not by the one in the account master data. Balance sheet accounts may carry a functional area in postings, but the Balance Sheet/Income Statement app ignores it.

The indicators on each assignment give the three ways an account can be placed, which the next section shows in a worked example.

== Worked example for Example Trading Co.

This site uses one fictitious business, Example Trading Co., with company code `EX01`, leading ledger `0L` and euro (EUR) as the company code currency. Its operating chart of accounts is called `YCOA` on this site (the key is a placeholder, as on the page about xref:erp/sap/finance/charts-of-accounts-and-gl-accounts.adoc[charts of accounts]). We define one FSV for `EX01`, with the invented ID `YEX1`, and use accounts that appear elsewhere on this site plus one invented payables account. All keys, account numbers and amounts are illustrative values, not SAP content.

The FSV adds the accounts below each node. The P&L result is the sum of the profit and loss accounts:

[stem]
++++
-90\,000 - 30\,000 + 70\,000 + 30\,000 = -20\,000
++++

A negative total is a credit, which the Net Result: Profit special node displays as a negative amount, so `EX01` made a profit of 20,000 EUR. The balance sheet totals are:

[cols="3,1",options="header"]
|===
|Node |EUR

|Assets: 40,000 + 1,000 + 89,000
|130,000

|Liabilities and Equity: -60,000 - 50,000
|-110,000

|Net Result: Profit
|-20,000

|Sum of the three
|0
|===

[stem]
++++
130\,000 + (-110\,000) + (-20\,000) = 0
++++

A statement whose result nodes do not sum to zero has an account in the wrong place, or a posting that is not balanced; the Not Assigned and Financial Statement Notes nodes are where to look first.

The same balances give a different statement under a different FSV. A second version for the group could put cash and inventories under one node, "Current assets", and give a total of 130,000 without listing the accounts, with no change to any posting. That is the purpose of keeping the tree apart from the accounts.

=== The three ways to place an account

The Debit Balance and Credit Balance indicators give three display rules. SAP's help page on balance-dependent display describes them.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Rule |Indicators |Use for

|Not balance-dependent
|Debit Balance and Credit Balance both selected on the account.
|Accounts that always appear under one node, such as fixed asset accounts. In `YEX1` this is how `120000` Inventory and `113500` Petty cash are assigned.

|Dependent on the balance of the individual account
|Debit Balance only on the account under one node, and Credit Balance only under another node.
|SAP's example: three bank accounts are assigned to "Cash in bank" if the balance is in debit and to "Payable to banks" if it is in credit, account by account.

|Dependent on the total balance of the node
|A node with Debit Balance only, a contra node with Credit Balance only, holding the same accounts.
|Showing a whole group of accounts, with all of them, under assets or under liabilities depending on the total.
|===

The third rule is the one used for `113100` in `YEX1`. Suppose the bank account has the 40,000 EUR debit balance of the table above in 2026. In 2027 the company overdraws it and the balance becomes a credit of 10,000 EUR. The statements show:

[cols="2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Node |Fiscal year 2026 (EUR) |Fiscal year 2027 (EUR)

|Cash at bank (assets)
|40,000
|0

|Payables to banks (liabilities and equity)
|0
|-10,000
|===

The account appears under exactly one of the two nodes in each year, selected by its total balance, which is how SAP's own example with the nodes "Receivables Bank 1" and "Payables Bank 1" behaves. Two caveats come from the help. The contra node setting applies only in the Balance Sheet/Income Statement and Balance Sheet/Income Statement - Multidimensional reports. And an FSV with contra nodes or an aging option can hold the same account twice in its structure, so it can be selected only in the Balance Sheet/Income Statement app and is not supported in other analytical apps.

=== Bank account grouping

A company with many bank accounts often posts them to one G/L account of type Cash Account, the reconciliation account, with a bank subaccount per bank. Bank account grouping makes the balance sheet list the banks one by one, grouped by business partner, with each balance on the side that fits it. To set it up SAP asks for two nodes that are contra nodes of each other, each with the Bank Account Grouping checkbox, and the same accounts in both. The accounts must have the account type Cash Account and the subtype Bank Reconciliation Account or Bank Subaccount. SAP's example has a node 1020 (Bank Balances - Debit) under Assets and a node 2020 (Bank Balances - Credit) under Liabilities and Equity: positive balances with bank 1 and bank 2 are listed under Assets, one row per bank, and the negative balance with bank 3 under Liabilities and Equity. The setting needs the option to allow contra nodes on the FSV, and it is a feature of the Manage Global Hierarchies tool.

== Building an FSV: Manage Global Hierarchies

=== Two tools, and which to use

An FSV can be maintained in two different tools whose FSVs are independent of each other. SAP recommends Manage Global Hierarchies for all new FSVs, and every reporting app can use the FSVs of either tool. The FAQ page lists the differences.

=== The steps for YEX1

The steps below build `YEX1`. They are taken from the help pages and cannot be tried without an SAP system.

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

. Open the app Manage Global Hierarchies and choose Create. Select Financial Statement Version as the hierarchy type.
. Enter the hierarchy ID `YEX1`, a description and the chart of accounts `YCOA`. Because the FSV is time dependent, enter Valid From 1 January 2026 and Valid To 31 December 2026. Choose Create.
. On the Hierarchy tab select Allow Contra Node and Bank Account Grouping, which `YEX1` needs for the pair Cash at bank and Payables to banks. Leave Allow Functional Areas, Group Account Number and Aging off.
. On the Timeframe tab the seven special nodes are already there. Save the FSV.
. Choose Edit. Add the nodes under Assets (Cash at bank, Petty cash, Inventories), under Liabilities and Equity (Equity, Trade payables, Payables to banks) and under the nodes for the profit and loss accounts, by Add for single nodes or More, Fast Entry for several at once. A node can also be imported from another FSV with More, Import Node.
. On Cash at bank set Debit Balance only, on Payables to banks Credit Balance only, and set the two nodes as contra nodes of each other.
. Assign the accounts: Add in the node row, then G/L Account, single accounts or a range. In every other node keep both Debit Balance and Credit Balance selected.
. Save. The FSV has the status Draft and cannot yet be used by reports.
. Optional: switch on the Simulate flag and save. The system generates a version whose ID has the suffix `+_DRAFT+`, which a reporting app such as Balance Sheet/Income Statement can select to show the data in the new structure. Switching the flag off deletes the generated version.
. Open the FSV in display mode and choose Activate. If you add a second time frame, the system checks that the time frames do not overlap.

To change an active FSV, deactivate it first, which returns it to Draft, and activate it again when you are finished. An active FSV cannot be deleted until you deactivate it. The status In Revision that other hierarchy types have does not apply to the FSV; it has only Draft and Active.

=== Copy, import and the sample versions

You rarely start from an empty tree. In Manage Global Hierarchies you can copy an existing FSV to a new FSV ID (the whole structure and attributes are copied, and the chart of accounts is inherited and cannot change), copy it into an existing FSV ID to create a new time frame (the chart of accounts of both must be equal and the time frames must not overlap), or import an FSV made in the classic app by choosing Import Hierarchy and the source Maintain Financial Statement Versions. For the 2027 layout of `YEX1`, for example, you copy the 2026 time frame to the existing ID with the dates 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2027 and change what is different.

In the Public Edition SAP recommends starting from sample FSVs. For a profit and loss statement the FAQ names YPL0 (a sample by function) and YPL1 (a sample by nature), which you import and then extend with functional areas, G/L accounts or both. The same FAQ points to an SAP Note about the IFRS 18 layout, which needs a login and was not opened for this page.

=== Production systems and transport

The FAQ says that you can create or change FSVs only in the configuration system and then transport them to the production system. In a two-system landscape of the Public Edition you can instead download the FSVs from the configuration system and upload them to the production system with the Synchronize G/L Account Master Data app, which needs no transport. FSVs are replicated for runtime automatically when activated, so the app Replicate Runtime Hierarchy is not needed for FSVs of Manage Global Hierarchies. An FSV can be exported to a spreadsheet from the Export/Import menu, which does not need edit authorization, so read-only users can do it too.

== Where an FSV is used

An active FSV is consumed by reports. Two Fiori apps are the ones to know, both documented in the on-premise help.

* Balance Sheet/Income Statement (App ID F0708) displays balance sheets and profit and loss statements for companies using operational, local and global charts of accounts. The reports are generated on the fly from all relevant line items, with access to the line items of a G/L account and to customer and supplier line items. It can show a key period against a comparison period, and actual against plan data. It can export a statement to PDF. A key date can be selected to display the FSV version valid on that date, which is why time frames matter.
* Balance Sheet / Income Statement - Multidimensional (App ID W0161) shows a balance sheet in a grid, can compare fiscal years, ledgers and actual against plan, and exports to Excel. It reads the hierarchies from special tables built for analysis, which SAP says you must fill with the Replicate Runtime Hierarchy app; for an FSV of Manage Global Hierarchies the FAQ says activation does this automatically.

The first app's help says that the assignment of G/L accounts to an FSV is changed with the Manage Financial Statement Versions app, a sentence that reflects the classic tool; for a global hierarchy FSV you use Manage Global Hierarchies. SAP also releases two CDS views for FSVs defined in Manage Global Hierarchies, named in the help as Financial Statement Hierarchy and Financial Statement Hierarchy Node; the technical names are on the SAP Business Accelerator Hub, which was not used for this page. The Public Edition's generic financial statement report also takes the FSV as its Hierarchy ID parameter; its page lists the other parameters.

== What can go wrong

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Symptom |Likely cause |What to do

|A node or the whole FSV is missing in the reporting app
|The FSV is still in Draft.
|Activate it, or switch on Simulate and select the version with the suffix `+_DRAFT+`.

|An account appears under Not Assigned
|It belongs to the chart of accounts but no node holds it.
|Assign the account to a node, or if it is correctly outside the statement, leave it; Not Assigned is a reminder, not an error.

|The statement shows an account twice
|The FSV uses contra nodes or aging, which allow duplicates, or the same account was assigned to two nodes with different indicators.
|Check the indicators; with a contra pair the account shows once, depending on the balance.

|Revenue shows as a negative number
|The ledger holds credits as negatives.
|Set +/- sign change on the revenue node for reports that support it; Balance Sheet/Income Statement does not yet.

|The FSV edit is refused
|The FSV is active.
|Deactivate it, edit, then activate again.

|A range of accounts cannot be partly changed
|A range is stored and edited only as a whole.
|Delete the range and assign the accounts you need again.

|A time frame cannot be activated
|It overlaps another time frame of the same FSV.
|Change the valid-from and valid-to dates.

|The production system has no FSV
|FSVs are created in the configuration system.
|Transport it, or use the Synchronize G/L Account Master Data app in a two-system landscape of the Public Edition.
|===

== What older books show, and what has changed

The book _SAP S/4HANA Financial Accounting Configuration_, 2nd edition (2023), by Andrew Okungbowa, spends a chapter on financial statement versions. It explains why an FSV exists, that several versions can serve the tax authority, investors and management, and that an FSV is a combination of G/L accounts in hierarchy levels tied to a chart of accounts. It then builds one in the classic customizing activity: define the version key (it recommends the chart of accounts key, at most four characters), create items, reassign the system's predefined items as subordinates of a Balance Sheet heading and a Profit and Loss heading, add texts and totals, assign accounts to items, set the debit and credit shift and run the consistency checks. The ideas match the current help. What has changed is the tooling and some details. SAP now recommends Manage Global Hierarchies for new FSVs, which adds time frames, a simulation version and spreadsheet maintenance, and treats the classic activity and `OB58` as an independent tool for existing versions. The seven special nodes are generated for you, so you do not have to create headings for unassigned accounts by hand. The book mentions the Fiori app and the business catalog by a name without the GL in it, where the current help pages name `SAP_SFIN_BC_GL_MD_COA`. Where the book and the help pages differ, the help pages win. The publisher page is https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4842-8957-0[SAP S/4HANA Financial Accounting Configuration (Apress, 2nd edition)].

== Summary

* A financial statement version (FSV) is a tree of G/L accounts, separate from the accounts, that turns balances into a balance sheet and a profit and loss statement. One chart of accounts can have many versions.
* Every FSV starts with seven special nodes. Assets, Liabilities and Equity and Financial Statement Notes you fill; Net Result: Loss, Net Result: Profit, P&L Result and Not Assigned the system fills.
* An account is placed by its Debit Balance and Credit Balance indicators: always under one node, under a node chosen by the account balance, or, with a pair of contra nodes, under the side chosen by the total balance of the group.
* For Example Trading Co., assets of 130,000 EUR, liabilities and equity of -110,000 EUR and a profit of -20,000 EUR sum to zero.
* New FSVs are built in Manage Global Hierarchies (App ID F2918): create, add nodes, assign accounts, optionally simulate, then activate. An active FSV must be deactivated before it is edited.
* The classic tool, transaction `OB58`, builds independent FSVs; the two kinds can both be used by reporting apps.
