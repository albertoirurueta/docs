Every company has to answer two different questions about its money. The first is outward-facing: how much does the company own and owe, and did it make a profit, in a form that tax authorities, banks and investors accept? The second is inward-facing: which department, product or project spent the money, and was that planned? SAP answers the first question with Financial Accounting (FI) and the second with Controlling (CO). A newcomer to SAP meets both at once, because almost every business process, from buying a pallet of goods to paying a salary, ends in a posting that both areas read. This page explains what FI and CO contain, how they fit together, and the five changes that SAP S/4HANA made to them, so that the later Finance pages have a map to hang their detail on. It is written against the SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 documentation (on premise and Private Edition). It describes the on-premise and Private Edition documentation; the Cloud Public Edition 2608 has its own help pages for each topic.

== Key terms

* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#financial-accounting[Financial Accounting (FI)]: the application area that keeps the legal books of each company code and reports to the outside world.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#controlling[Controlling (CO)]: the application area that tracks where costs and revenues arise inside the company, for planning and decisions.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#general-ledger[General ledger (FI-GL)]: the central record of all accounts of a company code.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#subledger[Subledger]: a detailed ledger, such as accounts payable or asset accounting, whose totals feed the general ledger.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#universal-journal[Universal Journal]: the single table of journal entry line items that FI and CO both read and write in SAP S/4HANA.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#cost-element[Cost element]: a classification of a cost or revenue for management accounting; in SAP S/4HANA it is a type of G/L account.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#material-ledger[Material Ledger]: the component that values materials and can keep their value in several currencies.
* xref:erp/sap/finance/glossary.adoc#business-partner[Business partner]: the one master record that holds a customer, a supplier or a contact.

== Two views of the same money

Financial Accounting is the external view. The SAP Help portal describes the purpose of its core component, General Ledger Accounting, as fully representing external accounting and the accounts involved in it, and says that it is integrated with all application components that produce posting data of relevance to it: Asset Accounting, Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable, Controlling, Materials Management, Human Capital Management and Treasury and Risk Management. Controlling is the internal view. SAP describes it as the component that provides information for management decision-making, showing where costs were incurred in the organization and for what purpose, and whose main task, besides documenting actual costs, is planning, so that variances between actual and plan data can be found.

The two areas are owned by different organizational units. The company code is the unit for which FI keeps a complete set of books. The controlling area is the unit in which CO analyzes costs and revenues, and it can span several company codes (see xref:erp/sap/organizational-structures.adoc[Organizational Structures]).

FI is split into components, each with a short technical name that you will see in menus, documentation and support messages:

* General Ledger Accounting (FI-GL): all accounts, parallel ledgers for different accounting principles, and the financial statements.
* Accounts Payable (FI-AP): what the company owes its suppliers.
* Accounts Receivable (FI-AR): what customers owe the company.
* Asset Accounting (FI-AA): the fixed assets, such as machines and vehicles, with their depreciation. SAP calls it a subsidiary ledger to the general ledger.

Accounts Payable and Accounts Receivable are subledgers: they hold the detail per supplier and per customer, and every posting in them is mirrored automatically in the general ledger on a reconciliation account. The SAP page on General Ledger Accounting lists this as the "automatic and simultaneous posting of all subledger items" in the general ledger.

CO is split the same way. The SAP page on Controlling lists these components:

* Cost Element Accounting (CO-OM-CEL): an overview of the costs and revenues that occur.
* Cost Center Accounting (CO-OM-CCA): costs by the place in the organization where they arise, for example a department.
* Internal Orders (CO-OM-OPA): costs collected per job, with an optional budget.
* Product Cost Controlling (CO-PC): the cost of making a product or providing a service.
* Profitability Analysis (CO-PA): profit and loss by market segment.
* Profit Center Accounting (EC-PCA): the profit and loss of independent areas of the organization. The page lists it in the classic component list; later Finance pages show how profit centers are used in SAP S/4HANA.

== What the Universal Journal changed

In SAP ERP, financial accounting and management accounting kept their line items in different tables, and a reconciliation step made sure that the two agreed. In SAP S/4HANA both write to one place. SAP describes the Universal Journal as the book of original entry for business transactions in FI and CO, and as the single source of truth. A journal entry is one accounting document, and its line items are the individual debit and credit lines. The line items are stored once, and totals are calculated when they are needed.

The single line item table is named ACDOCA. SAP states that each business transaction in these application components generates a journal entry: General Ledger Accounting, Asset Accounting, Controlling, Profitability Analysis (margin analysis only, not costing-based) and the Material Ledger. The Universal Journal can hold up to 10 currency fields: two are preconfigured (the company code currency and the controlling area currency), and 8 are freely definable.

The old tables did not all disappear. SAP's FAQ says that many tables were replaced by ACDOCA but not all: the table BKPF still holds the journal entry header, and the table BSEG still holds the source documents and the open item entries. The line items of the new general ledger tables (FAGLFLEXA among others), of the Controlling tables COEP, COSS and COSP, of the Material Ledger table MLIT and of the asset table ANEP are now contained in ACDOCA.

A small example makes the effect concrete. Example Trading Co. (company code `EX01`, leading ledger `0L`) pays the monthly salary of its sales department, 1,000 in the local currency. The account names and the cost center in the table are illustrative values for this site, not delivered content.

[cols="1,2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Line | G/L account | Cost center | Debit | Credit
| 1 | Salaries (expense) | CC-SALES | 1,000 | 
| 2 | Bank account | | | 1,000
|===

A journal entry must balance, which is the accounting equation applied to one posting:

[stem]
++++
\sum \text{debits} = \sum \text{credits} \qquad 1{,}000 = 1{,}000
++++

In SAP ERP the salary expense would have been written once to the general ledger and again to the Controlling tables, with a reconciliation between them. In SAP S/4HANA line 1 is one row of ACDOCA that carries the G/L account and the cost center together, so a financial statement and a cost center report read the same row and cannot disagree. SAP states that no reconciliation between financial accounting and management accounting is ever required for this reason.

SAP also says that the Universal Journal is integrated only with margin analysis: costing-based Profitability Analysis can still run in parallel, as a separate set of data.

== Cost elements are G/L accounts

A cost element classifies a cost or revenue for Controlling, for example "labor costs". In SAP ERP each cost element was its own master record in CO, next to the matching G/L account. SAP's Cost Elements page says that in SAP S/4HANA the Universal Journal contains the Account field, which covers both G/L accounts and cost elements, and that cost elements are now part of the chart of accounts, so you no longer maintain cost element master data separately.

The G/L account master record has a field Cost Element Category that only Controlling uses. A G/L account with a primary cost element category denotes operating expenses such as wages (a CO account assignment such as a cost center is then required), and one with a secondary category, such as 21 (internal settlement), 41 (overhead rates) or 42 (assessment), denotes costs from value flows inside the company. You edit them with transaction FS00 or the Manage Chart of Accounts Fiori app (F0763A), and default account assignments are maintained with transaction OKB9. Cost element groups still exist. The Customizing for costing sheets and allocations still shows cost elements.

== The Material Ledger is mandatory

The Material Ledger values materials and can keep that value in more than one currency. SAP's installation page for Actual Costing and the Material Ledger says that the Material Ledger is mandatory as of SAP S/4HANA, while Actual Costing, which uses it to compute actual prices at period end, is still optional. A system converted from an earlier release such as SAP ERP has to convert data with transaction FINS_MIG_STATUS, and the production startup of the Material Ledger cannot be reversed. In the component map the Material Ledger therefore sits beside Controlling and writes to the Universal Journal like the other components.

Source: https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/5e23dc8fe9be4fd496f8ab556667ea05/97f1d353ca9f4408e10000000a174cb4.html[Installation: Actual Costing/Material Ledger (SAP Help, on premise 2025 FPS02)]

== The Business Partner is the leading object

In SAP ERP, customers and suppliers were separate master records. In SAP S/4HANA the business partner is the leading object when customer and supplier master data is created: you can maintain the customer or supplier from the business partner maintenance. SAP's page on the Business Partner approach (customer and supplier integration, CVI) says that only customers with this integration in place can move to SAP S/4HANA, and that all customers and suppliers must be converted to business partners before the conversion. The xref:erp/sap/master-data-and-business-partner.adoc[Master Data and the Business Partner] page explains the business partner model, its roles and its API.

== New Asset Accounting

Asset Accounting is the subsidiary ledger for fixed assets. SAP S/4HANA uses what SAP calls new Asset Accounting. SAP's migration guide describes the path from classic to new Asset Accounting, in which the asset documents are migrated into the universal journal entry, and it states that new Asset Accounting builds on the new depreciation calculation of the Financials Extension (EA-FIN) and on new General Ledger Accounting. The Asset Accounting page says that you can flexibly assign the depreciation areas to the ledgers of the general ledger for parallel valuation, and that the system posts parallel values with the actual values in real time, with a separate document for each accounting principle. The asset table ANEP is now contained in ACDOCA (see the FAQ cited above). A system that is being converted cannot post in Asset Accounting between the installation of SAP S/4HANA and the end of the migration, so the closing steps before the conversion matter; a migration project is a topic for the implementation pages.

== Where this leaves a newcomer

* The question "is this FI or CO?" now concerns the view of a posting, not where it is stored: both read the same line items.
* Material written for SAP ERP describes separate cost element master data, separate Controlling tables and a reconciliation step between FI and CO. Those descriptions are outdated for SAP S/4HANA. Technical names such as FI-AP or CO-PC survive in the documentation and menus.
* The later Finance pages (organizational units, fiscal year, charts of accounts and G/L accounts, and so on) build on this map one component at a time.

== Summary

* Financial Accounting (FI) keeps the legal books and Controlling (CO) tracks costs and revenues inside the company; FI has the components FI-GL, FI-AP, FI-AR and FI-AA, and CO has cost element, cost center, internal order, product cost and profitability components.
* In SAP S/4HANA both areas write their line items to one table, ACDOCA, the Universal Journal, so no reconciliation between FI and CO is needed.
* Cost elements are a type of G/L account, kept in the chart of accounts, with a Cost Element Category field for Controlling.
* The Material Ledger is mandatory, Actual Costing is optional.
* The business partner is the leading object for customers and suppliers.
* New Asset Accounting is the version used in SAP S/4HANA, and its postings go to the Universal Journal.
