Financial Accounting answers the question "what does the company own and owe, and what did it earn?". Controlling answers a
different question: "where in the company did the costs arise, and for what purpose?". It lets a manager see that the
office rent belongs to administration, compare what was planned with what was spent, and charge one department for the
services of another. This page explains what Controlling covers, why it needs no reconciliation with Financial
Accounting in SAP S/4HANA, what a controlling area is, which currencies Controlling uses, and what a cost element is and
how its category decides what you can do with it. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private
Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The ideas are the same in both editions; the page says where the
tools or the lists differ.

== What Controlling covers

SAP's help describes Controlling as the component that gives information for management decisions: where costs arose in
the organization and for what purpose. It provides the basis for matching the cost of sales with the revenue earned.
Besides documenting actual costs, its main task is planning, so that actual data can be compared with plan data and
variances found. Analysis of profitability, for example by contribution margin, monitors how cost-efficient the parts of
the organization are.

The help lists the sub-components in the table below. Each is taught on its own page of this section; this page covers
the foundation they all share, the controlling area and the cost elements.

[cols="2,1,4",options="header"]
|===
|Sub-component |Component |What it does, in SAP's words
|Cost Element Accounting
|CO-OM-CEL
|Gives an overview of the costs and revenues that occur in the organization.

|Cost Center Accounting
|CO-OM-CCA
|Monitors the costs arising within the organization; useful for assigning overhead costs to the place where they occurred.

|Internal Orders
|CO-OM-OPA
|Collect costs by the job that caused them; budgets can be assigned to keep costs within limits.

|Product Cost Controlling
|CO-PC
|Calculates the costs of making a product or providing a service, for example to find the minimum profitable price.

|Profitability Analysis
|CO-PA
|Analyzes profit and loss by market segment, with the corresponding costs allocated to the revenues of each segment.

|Profit Center Accounting
|EC-PCA
|Evaluates profit and loss of independent areas that are responsible for their costs and revenues. SAP describes it as a statistical accounting component.
|===

The names and component codes are those of the on-premise help for SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02. Other pages of this section
treat the cost center, the internal order, the profit center, planning, allocations and product costing in turn.

== Controlling and Finance are one posting

In SAP ERP, Controlling and Financial Accounting kept separate documents and had to be reconciled. SAP's help for
SAP S/4HANA says the two are integrated and need no reconciliation to stay consistent, because the financial data in
CO and FI is based on the same line items. Each journal entry holds the G/L accounts to be debited and credited and
also the Controlling account assignment objects: a cost center for wages and salaries, a project or order for material
costs, or a combination of characteristics such as product, customer and region for sales revenues. The Universal Journal
FAQ states the consequence: accounting documents and CO documents are superseded by journal entries, and the journal
entry is the single source of truth for both views.

[mermaid]
----
flowchart LR
    A["Posting: rent 1,000.00 EUR<br/>account 'rent', cost center EX-ADM"] --> B["One journal entry line<br/>account + cost center + amounts"]
    B --> C["Financial Accounting view<br/>balance of the G/L account"]
    B --> D["Controlling view<br/>costs on cost center EX-ADM in EX00"]
----

Two details from SAP's help matter for a beginner. First, Controlling normally shows the leading valuation, because
recording values by different accounting principles is not a classic requirement of Controlling. The values of
allocations inside Controlling, which move costs between segments and functional areas, are transferred to Financial
Accounting, and with parallel ledgers they can be posted to a ledger other than the leading one. Second, what flows from
Financial Accounting to Controlling depends on the design: with parallel ledgers, all values of the leading ledger that
are relevant to CO are transferred, and postings that only ever update parallel ledgers are not available in Controlling.
See xref:erp/sap/finance/universal-parallel-accounting.adoc[Universal Parallel Accounting] for the ledger side.

== The controlling area

SAP defines the controlling area as an organizational unit within an enterprise for which a complete cost accounting can
be performed within a closed system. It can contain one or several company codes, which may operate in different
currencies, but all of them must use the same operating chart of accounts. All internal allocations refer only to objects
of the same controlling area, so a cost of one controlling area can never be allocated to a cost center of another. When
you create master data, the system always assigns the Controlling object to a controlling area and a company code.

For Example Trading Co. the controlling area is `EX00` and it contains the company code `EX01` (EUR, group currency GBP,
ledgers `0L` and `2L`, as in xref:erp/sap/finance/organizational-units.adoc[Organizational Units of Accounting]).

=== One company code or several

SAP names two basic relationships between controlling areas and company codes.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Relationship |Meaning |When SAP recommends it

|1:1, one company code per controlling area
|The financial accounting and the cost accounting views of the organization are identical.
|Consolidated analysis of settled transactions across company codes in Profitability Analysis (several controlling areas are then assigned to one operating concern), and intercompany processes where the production plant is the same as the delivering plant.

|1:n, several company codes per controlling area
|Cost accounting is done across the company codes in one controlling area, so internal allocations between company codes are possible. The external and internal views differ.
|Cross-company-code transactions that must be handled in one controlling area, group costing, Profit Center Accounting with transfer prices, and multilevel product cost management across company codes.
|===

`EX00` with its single company code `EX01` is the 1:1 case today. If Example Trading Co. later adds a second company code
in the same group, the 1:n relationship would let the two share cost centers and allocate costs between them. The help
warns that changing the relationship after the assignment can be difficult or very time-consuming, that allocations
across controlling areas are not possible, and that a 1:n relationship comes with conditions:

* a consistent chart of accounts, and all cost elements treated the same way in all company codes (for example as
  primary cost elements or as accrual cost elements),
* fiscal year variants of the company codes that match the one of the controlling area (see
  xref:erp/sap/finance/fiscal-year-and-posting-periods.adoc[Fiscal Year and Posting Periods]),
* period-end closing in Controlling for all company codes together, and only after the closing in Financial Accounting
  is complete, and
* authorizations that stop postings across company codes if that is not wanted.

Using only one controlling area also means using only one operating concern, and profit center allocations and transfer
prices can be shown in only one controlling area.

== Currencies in Controlling

Controlling uses three currencies, and a posting can pass through all of them.

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
|Currency |Meaning
|Transaction currency
|The currency in which a Controlling document is posted. It can differ from the other two. Amounts are converted automatically to the controlling area currency at the specified exchange rate.

|Controlling area currency
|The currency of cost accounting and the default currency of Controlling objects. It is set when the controlling area is created.

|Object currency
|The currency in the master data of one Controlling object such as a cost center or internal order. There is an object currency for the sender and one for the receiver.
|===

SAP's help gives this example: if the transaction currency is USD, the controlling area currency is EUR and the object
currency is SFR, the amount is converted twice, from USD to EUR and then from EUR to SFR. Actual data is translated with
the average rate (exchange rate type M), and the version of a plan can name another exchange rate type for planning data.

=== A worked conversion for EX00

Example Trading Co. receives a freight bill of 1,200.00 USD and posts it on the cost center `EX-ADM`. `EX00` uses the
company code currency EUR as its controlling area currency, so the amount is converted from the transaction currency to
EUR. With an illustrative average rate of 0.92 EUR for 1 USD (a made-up value, not a market rate):

[stem]
++++
1\,200.00\ \text{USD} \times 0.92\ \frac{\text{EUR}}{\text{USD}} = 1\,104.00\ \text{EUR}
++++

The cost center `EX-ADM` is then charged 1,104.00 EUR in the controlling area currency. The object currency of
`EX-ADM` is EUR as well, because `EX00` has no different company code currency, so no second conversion is needed. The
group currency GBP of `EX01` is kept in the same journal entry line as a parallel currency by the translation rules of
the ledgers; see xref:erp/sap/finance/ledgers-and-currencies.adoc[Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates].

=== How the controlling area currency is chosen

The controlling area currency depends on two settings of the controlling area: how it is assigned to company codes and
the currency type. SAP's help lists the combinations.

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Assignment of company codes |Currency type |Controlling area currency
|Controlling area same as company code |10 |Use company code currency
|Cross-company-code cost accounting |10 |Use company code currency
|Cross-company-code cost accounting |20 |Independent controlling area currency
|Cross-company-code cost accounting |30 |Transfer group currency
|Cross-company-code cost accounting |40 |Transfer hard currency
|Cross-company-code cost accounting |50 |Index-based currency transfer
|Cross-company-code cost accounting |60 |Transfer global company currency
|===

The keys 10 to 60 in this table are those of the controlling area setting. They are not the currency type keys of
the ledgers (there 10 is the company code currency, 20 the controlling area currency and 30 the group currency), so do
not mix the two lists. For `EX00` the illustrative choice is type 10, which gives EUR. A choice of type 30 would give
GBP, but only if every assigned company code carries the group currency as a parallel currency and the group currency is
stored in the client. In that case the company code currency EUR would become the object currency of the cost centers,
because SAP's help says the company code currency is recorded automatically as the object currency whenever the
controlling area currency differs from it. SAP's examples also show the effect in a mixed group: with group currency EUR
as the controlling area currency, objects of a Mexican subsidiary stay in MXP and objects of a US subsidiary in USD.

Two rules from the same help pages prevent surprises. With several company codes in one controlling area under currency
type 10, all of them must use the same currency. And once an assigned company code is productive, the currency type can
no longer be changed; you can only set the assignment control and the currency type when you redefine the controlling
area or have not yet assigned a productive company code.

The Universal Journal can hold up to 10 currency fields. Two are preconfigured, the company code currency and the
controlling area currency, and neither can be changed. The controlling area currency is only available if you use
Controlling. The other eight are freely definable.

== Cost elements are G/L accounts

A cost element classifies a cost or a revenue for Controlling, for example "labor costs". SAP's help defines G/L accounts
used this way as classifying the valued consumption of production factors of a company within a controlling area, giving
information about value flow and value consumption. In SAP ERP every cost element was a master record of its own next to
the G/L account. In SAP S/4HANA the Universal Journal has one Account field that covers G/L accounts and cost elements,
cost elements are part of the chart of accounts, and you no longer maintain cost element master data separately. The
Universal Journal FAQ puts it as: a cost element is a special type of G/L account, maintained as a master record in the
general ledger.

What this means in practice:

* *Where you edit them.* On premise, SAP names transaction `FS00` (Edit G/L Account Centrally) and the Manage Chart of
  Accounts app (`F0763A`). The Manage G/L Account Master Data app (`F0731A`) lists `KA01`, `KA02`, `KA03` and `KA06`
  among its related back-end transactions, next to `FS00`, `FSP0` and `FSS0`. In the Public Edition, SAP says you edit
  cost element master data in the Manage G/L Account Master Data app.
* *What the account type is.* An account of type primary costs or revenue is a primary cost element, one of type
  secondary costs a secondary cost element (see
  xref:erp/sap/finance/charts-of-accounts-and-gl-accounts.adoc[Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts]).
* *Which data is only for Controlling.* The cost element category, whether quantities are recorded and the internal
  unit of measurement belong to the controlling-area part of the account. They are kept per controlling area, so the
  same account can have a different category in another controlling area. In the Public Edition SAP adds that changing
  a cost element attribute for one company code affects all company codes of the controlling area.
* *Default account assignments.* A default Controlling account assignment for a cost element is defined with
  transaction `OKB9` rather than in a cost element master record.
* *What stays visible.* Customizing settings such as costing sheets, cost component structures and allocations continue
  to display cost elements, and cost element groups still exist.

A caution on `KA01`: SAP says cost element master data is no longer a separate object, yet transaction names
with the prefix KA still appear on SAP pages. SAP's help for the Manage G/L Account Master Data app lists them as
related transactions of the app. One on-premise help page, Configuration of Units of Measure, Reconciliation, and
Controlling, also tells you to specify all revenue accounts as cost elements with `KA01` and `KA02` if you have integrated
Profitability Analysis (margin analysis). This page found no help text that explains how those transactions behave in
detail on SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02, so it does not describe it. Treat the G/L account master data as the place where a
cost element lives, and check what your system offers (requires an SAP system; not run here).

== Cost element categories

The cost element category is a field in the G/L account master record that only Controlling uses. It classifies the
account and so decides with which transactions the cost element can be used, for example internal settlement or
allocation. It also decides how the account is posted:

* *Direct posting*: you post an amount to the account by naming it. This is possible for all primary cost elements.
* *Indirect posting*: the account is determined automatically at the time of posting. This is possible only for secondary
  cost elements.

On premise you see the category on the Control Data tab of the G/L account master record; in the Public Edition the same
data is on the Controlling Data tab of the Manage G/L Account Master Data app.

|03 Accrual/deferral per surcharge

== Account assignment of Controlling objects

A posting to a cost element needs a Controlling object to be charged. SAP's help calls these account assignment objects
and distinguishes two kinds.

* *True Controlling objects* can be sender or receiver of costs. The list includes cost centers (for costs), true
  orders, true projects, networks, make-to-order sales orders, cost objects, profitability segments, real estate objects
  and business processes.
* *Statistical Controlling objects* are for information only; you cannot allocate costs from them to other objects. They
  include cost centers (for revenues, and when a true object already exists), statistical internal orders, statistical
  projects and profit centers. An internal order or project is made statistical in its master record.

The rules, in plain words:

* Every posting item needs one true Controlling object.
* Besides the true object you can name up to three statistical objects.
* You cannot name two objects of the same type, such as a true order and a true project, in one item. The exception is a
  cost center together with one other true object: the posting is then true for the other object and statistical for the
  cost center.
* The same object cannot be both true and statistical in one item.
* Profit centers can only be posted to in addition to a true object, and the profit center you enter must match the one
  derived from that object.
* A P&L account must be created as a cost element before you can post to it together with a Controlling object.
* Revenue is posted truly to profitability segments, make-to-order sales orders, projects or orders with revenues and
  real estate objects, and only statistically to cost centers and profit centers.

SAP's two examples show the logic. Costs of 100 USD on statistical order 40010 that also stores cost center 2330: the
cost center is the true object, and the system also posts statistically to the order and to the profit center of the
cost center. Costs of 100 USD on true order 40020 and cost center 2330: the order gets true costs, the cost center
statistical costs, and the profit center statistical costs again.

== Cost element groups

A cost element group is a hierarchy of cost elements with similar characteristics. It serves two purposes in SAP's help:
it defines the row structure of reports, with a total calculated at each node, and it lets you process several cost
elements in one transaction, for example in cost center planning, distribution or allocation. SAP's examples are a group
Personnel Costs, used to plan personnel costs in one business transaction, and a group Services and Operating Supplies,
used to assess operating supply costs in one step. A group does not represent cost elements by itself; it only structures
them.

|Public Edition

== A worked example for EX00

Example Trading Co. pays office rent of 1,000.00 EUR in company code `EX01`. The rent account is a primary cost element
(category 01) in `EX00`; its number is not important here. The posting names the cost center `EX-ADM`, so the line
item carries both. This is a primary posting: Financial Accounting entered the account directly.

Later the administration cost center passes part of its costs on. Suppose the company assesses 400.00 EUR of the rent to a
second cost center, `EX-SALES` (an illustrative cost center), using the secondary cost element `940000` of category 42.
Assessment is an allocation, so the system determines the secondary account; nobody posts to it by hand. The cost center
`EX-ADM` is credited and `EX-SALES` debited. The result for the controlling area:

[cols="2,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Step |Cost center `EX-ADM` |Cost center `EX-SALES` |Total in `EX00`
|1 Rent posted (primary, category 01) |+1,000.00 EUR |0.00 EUR |+1,000.00 EUR
|2 Assessment (secondary, category 42) |-400.00 EUR |+400.00 EUR |0.00 EUR
|Balance after both steps |600.00 EUR |400.00 EUR |1,000.00 EUR
|===

An allocation moves costs between objects but does not change the total of the controlling area, so the sum over all
cost centers stays at the amount of the original primary posting:

[stem]
++++
600.00 + 400.00 = 1\,000.00\ \text{EUR}
++++

The secondary cost element is what keeps the allocation visible in reports: the
1,000.00 EUR stays one primary cost, and the 400.00 EUR transfer shows as an assessment on its own line.

== Configuration pointers

The following steps change the setup of a system and need an SAP system; they were not run here.

* *Define the controlling area and assign company codes.* On premise, in the Implementation Guide choose Controlling,
  General Controlling, Organization, Maintain Controlling Area, select the controlling area, choose Assignment of company
  code(s), then New Entries and enter the company codes. They must be fully maintained before. The assignment control
  indicator and the currency type are specified there. In the Public Edition this page found no help page that gives the
  equivalent configuration path, so it names none. The read-only data model view `I_ControllingArea` (Public Edition
  help) exposes, among others, the fields `CONTROLLINGAREA`, `CHARTOFACCOUNTS`, `FISCALYEARVARIANT`,
  `CONTROLLINGAREACURRENCYROLE` and `CONTROLLINGAREACURRENCY`; the help does not state in the page used here whether
  the view is released for ABAP Cloud development.
* *Assign business transactions to number ranges.* When you activate a Controlling component, SAP says all business
  transactions of the component must have number ranges, defined for each controlling area, or the transactions cannot
  be called. Transaction `OKC1` displays all CO business transactions, and plan and actual transactions belong in separate
  transaction groups. The sample client 000 contains defaults.
* *Create or change cost elements.* Edit the G/L account (see above) and fill the cost element category in the
  controlling-area data.
* *Default account assignments.* Maintain them with transaction `OKB9` on premise. The Public Edition Customizing
  activity named in SAP's starter-content help is Manage Default Account Assignments, with the fields Company Code and
  Cost Element and the PrfSeg checkbox for a profitability segment.

== Common errors and pitfalls

* *Posting to a cost element without a Controlling object.* SAP states that postings to cost elements always need a CO
  object; every posting item needs a true Controlling object.
* *Posting to a P&L account that is not a cost element together with an object.* Create the account as a cost element
  first.
* *Using category 22 for settlement inside Controlling.* Category 22 is for settlement to assets, materials or G/L
  accounts and creates a journal entry; use 21 for settlement to cost centers, orders or projects.
* *Treating cost-reducing revenue as sales.* Rent received that reduces your own rent expense uses category 01, not 11.
* *Expecting to allocate revenue on a cost center.* Revenue on cost centers is only statistical; other allocations are
  not possible.
* *Mixing the two currency type lists.* Keys 10 to 60 of the controlling area setting differ from the ledger currency
  type keys.
* *Planning to change the currency type later.* Once an assigned company code is productive it cannot be changed.
* *Assuming the Public Edition lists the same categories.* The two help pages differ; use the list of your release.
* *Skipping number ranges for a new Controlling component.* The transactions cannot then be called.

== Summary

* Controlling shows where costs arose and why, and plans and compares them. In SAP S/4HANA it needs no reconciliation
  with Financial Accounting, because both read the same journal entry lines.
* The controlling area is the closed unit of cost accounting. It holds one or several company codes that share the
  operating chart of accounts, and allocations never cross controlling areas.
* Controlling uses the transaction currency, the controlling area currency and each object's currency. The controlling
  area currency is chosen by an assignment control and a currency type, and cannot be changed once a company code is
  productive.
* A cost element is a G/L account of type primary costs or revenue or secondary costs, with a cost element category kept
  per controlling area. The category decides the transactions it can be used with and whether it is posted directly or
  indirectly.
* A posting needs one true Controlling object and may add statistical ones. Allocations move cost between objects without
  changing the total of the controlling area.
* Cost element groups structure cost elements for reports and for processing several of them at once.
