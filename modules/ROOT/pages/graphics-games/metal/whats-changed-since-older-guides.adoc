= What Has Changed Since the Older Metal Guides
:description: Row-by-row corrections for the two Metal books this sub-section was checked against (Metal by Tutorials, 4th edition, and the Metal Programming Guide), with what each says, what is current in 2026, the operating system release of the change and the page to read.

The ideas in the two older Metal books are still sound: a device that owns queues, command buffers that record encoders, pipeline
state objects built from shaders, resources bound to a pass, a render loop that keeps a few frames in flight and a shading
language that is a dialect of {cpp}. What has changed is the newest layer of the API and almost everything that is typed: the
command-recording model, the way resources are bound and kept resident, the capabilities that need an upgrade of the hardware
baseline, and the tools and the Xcode needed to run the code. The books were written for Metal 2 (2017) and Metal 3 (2022), for
Swift 4 and for Xcode 9 or 15; this sub-section documents Metal 4 (the 26 operating systems), MSL 4.1 (the 27 operating
systems), Swift 6.4 and Xcode 27. This page lists, book by book, the statements that are out of date and what to do today, so that
you can keep reading the books for their explanations and use the pages of this sub-section for current code. The facts were
checked in November 2026 against Apple's Metal documentation, the Metal Shading Language specification, the Metal Feature Set
Tables and the Xcode release notes. For the changes that affect all graphics libraries, not only Metal, see
xref:graphics-games/whats-changed-since-older-guides.adoc[What Has Changed Since the Older Guides].

The books are listed in the xref:graphics-games/index.adoc#bibliography[section bibliography] and have the short names used in
the tables: MBT (Caroline Begbie and Marius Horga, _Metal by Tutorials_, 4th edition, Kodeco) and CLA (Janie Clayton, _Metal
Programming Guide_, Addison-Wesley, 2017). Chapter numbers are those of the books. The current edition of MBT is the 5th, published
in November 2025: it moved to Swift 6 and the 26 SDKs but still teaches Metal 3, so most of the rows apply to it too.

The left panel is the style of both books; the right panel is the structure of

and, for a few items, deprecation warnings.

|xref:graphics-games/metal/whats-new-in-metal-4.adoc[What's New in Metal 4]

|1 to 3 and onward
|A new `MTLCommandBuffer` is created from the queue for every frame, and buffers and textures are bound to the encoder one call at a time.
|Metal 4 uses a reusable `MTL4CommandBuffer`, a command allocator per frame in flight, an `MTL4ArgumentTable` and explicit residency. The old model is still supported.
|26 OSes
|xref:graphics-games/metal/hello-triangle-metal4.adoc[Hello Triangle with Metal 4], xref:graphics-games/metal/metalkit-mtkview-and-swiftui.adoc[MetalKit, MTKView and SwiftUI]

|2 and 4
|Buffers on a Mac use managed storage, and the CPU calls `didModifyRange` after a write.
|Use shared storage for data the CPU writes and private storage for data only the GPU reads. Managed storage is deprecated in the 27 operating systems.
|27 OSes
|xref:graphics-games/metal/buffers-vertex-descriptors.adoc[Buffers and Vertex Descriptors]

|25 and 26
|Resources are made available to shaders with `MTLArgumentEncoder`.
|Write `gpuResourceID` and `gpuAddress` values straight into buffers (Metal 3), or fill an `MTL4ArgumentTable` and add resources to an `MTLResidencySet` (Metal 4). The encoder is not deprecated.
|iOS 16 and macOS 13, then 26 OSes
|xref:graphics-games/metal/argument-buffers-and-bindless.adoc[Argument Buffers and Bindless Resources], xref:graphics-games/metal/synchronization-and-barriers.adoc[Synchronization and Barriers]

|19
|Tessellation is the way to amplify geometry on the GPU.
|Mesh shaders (object and mesh functions) do amplification and level of detail with meshlets. Metal 4 render encoders have no tessellation configuration.
|iOS 16 and macOS 13
|xref:graphics-games/metal/mesh-shaders.adoc[Mesh Shaders], xref:graphics-games/metal/tessellation.adoc[Tessellation in Metal]

|27 to 29
|"Ray tracing" is raymarching and signed distance fields in a fragment shader.
|Hardware ray tracing uses `MTLAccelerationStructure` objects, intersectors and intersection queries, and runs in hardware on A17 and M3 or later. `MPSRayIntersector` is deprecated.
|iOS 14 and macOS 11, deprecation in iOS 17 and macOS 14
|xref:graphics-games/metal/ray-tracing.adoc[Ray Tracing in Metal]

|31
|Profiling uses the older GPU-capture interface.
|Xcode adds the Performance HUD, the Summary and Shaders tabs, a GPU trace export, more validation, and since WWDC26 the command-line tools `gpucapture`, `gpudebug` and `metalperftrace`.
|Xcode 26 and 27
|xref:graphics-games/metal/debugging-and-profiling.adoc[Debugging and Profiling Metal], xref:graphics-games/metal/performance-best-practices.adoc[Performance Best Practices]

== What to keep from the books

* The explanations of the render pipeline, of the model, view and projection chain, of texture mapping, of lighting models, of
  render passes and of compute remain correct. Only the names of the calls around them have changed.
* The structure of the book projects (create a device, a queue, a pipeline, resources, then encode and present each frame) is the
  structure of xref:graphics-games/metal/hello-triangle-metal4.adoc[Hello Triangle with Metal 4], with a Metal 3 equivalent
  shown next to it.
* Where a book and Apple's documentation or the Metal Shading Language specification disagree, follow the documentation.
* The books' chapters on tile-based deferred rendering and on instancing explain concepts that Metal 4 did not change; read them
  with xref:graphics-games/metal/deferred-and-tile-shading.adoc[Deferred and Tile Shading] and
  xref:graphics-games/metal/gpu-driven-rendering-icb.adoc[GPU-Driven Rendering and Indirect Command Buffers].
