= Image-Based Lighting and HDR in Metal
:description: Image-based lighting in Metal with compute kernels: a procedural HDR environment cube, a diffuse irradiance map, a roughness-prefiltered specular chain and the BRDF lookup table, the shading code that combines them for the Bookshelf, and HDR and extended dynamic range output with a half-float CAMetalLayer, EDR headroom, tone mapping and PQ or HLG metadata.
:keywords: Metal, image-based lighting, IBL, irradiance map, prefiltered environment map, BRDF LUT, HDR, EDR, extended dynamic range, CAMetalLayer, wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent, rgba16Float, tone mapping, texturecube, MSL, split-sum

include::partial$ai-disclaimer.adoc[]

A desk lamp is a point light; the room around it is not. Real surfaces are lit by everything they can see: the window, the ceiling, the bright wall. *Image-based lighting* (IBL) captures that surrounding light in a texture, an *environment map*, and uses it as a light source. Two pre-computed maps (one for diffuse light, one for glossy reflections) plus a small lookup table turn the physically based shading of xref:graphics-games/metal/pbr-materials.adoc[PBR Materials] into a handful of texture reads per pixel. The environment is HDR: sky and lamp are far brighter than white paper, and the renderer must keep those values until the very last step. That last step, getting values above white onto a display that can show them, is *extended dynamic range* (EDR) output in Apple's terminology, and it is the second half of this page.

The facts were checked in November 2026. The versions documented are Metal 4 on the 26 operating systems and Metal Shading Language 4.1 on the 27 operating systems (Xcode 27); everything here works on Metal 3 as well, and the setup code below uses the Metal 3 command buffer for brevity (the compute kernels are identical on Metal 4, which binds their resources through an `MTL4ArgumentTable`, see xref:graphics-games/metal/hello-triangle-metal4.adoc[Hello Triangle with Metal 4]). Writing to half-float cube textures from compute kernels is covered by the Metal Feature Set Tables for the target GPU family; EDR output needs a display that supports it (current iPhones, iPads and Macs with XDR or HDR-capable displays, Apple Vision Pro) and the layer settings shown below.

In the running example this page sits between PBR with a shadow map and the many-books stage: the shelf gets an environment (warm room, bright lamp) that lights the books and appears in the glossy board. The same maps are reused by the ray-traced reflections in xref:graphics-games/metal/ray-tracing.adoc[Ray Tracing in Metal] as the fallback colour for rays that miss. General background is in xref:graphics-games/lighting-and-shading-models.adoc[Lighting and Shading Models] and xref:graphics-games/color-spaces-hdr-and-tone-mapping.adoc[Color Spaces, HDR and Tone Mapping]; the same techniques in OpenGL are in xref:graphics-games/opengl/pbr-basics.adoc[PBR Basics] and xref:graphics-games/opengl/antialiasing-and-hdr.adoc[Antialiasing and HDR].

== The idea in one picture

.Image-based lighting for the Bookshelf: four steps
image::graphics-games-metal-image-based-lighting-and-hdr-ibl-storyboard.svg["Storyboard of four panels: environment cube, irradiance map, prefiltered specular map, shading with the BRDF lookup",width=720]

. *Environment*: a cube texture in `rgba16Float`, either generated procedurally (as below), baked from a capture, or decoded from an equirectangular HDR image offline. Values above 1.0 are the point: the lamp is thousands of times brighter than the walls.
. *Irradiance map*: for each direction `N`, the cosine-weighted sum of the environment over the hemisphere around `N`. A diffuse surface reads it with its normal. It has no detail, so 32 by 32 texels per face is plenty.
. *Prefiltered specular map*: the environment blurred by the specular lobe for a given roughness, stored one roughness per mip level. A glossy surface reads it with the *reflection vector* and picks the mip from its roughness.
. *BRDF lookup table*: a 2D texture indexed by the angle between normal and view vector and by roughness, returning a scale and bias for the Fresnel term. It does not depend on the environment and is computed once for every material.

This is the *split-sum approximation* that real-time engines use. It assumes the surface is viewed head-on when it prefilters, so grazing reflections are slightly too sharp, a trade-off worth the cost.

=== Irradiance, visually

.From environment to irradiance map
image::graphics-games-metal-image-based-lighting-and-hdr-irradiance-map.svg["An HDR environment cube is convolved into a small blurry irradiance cube; each texel sums light over the hemisphere around its normal",width=720]

Irradiance is the integral of incoming light weighted by the cosine of the angle to the normal. Written as math, for a normal \( n \) and the environment radiance \( L(\omega) \):

\[ E(n) = \int_{\Omega} L(\omega)\,\max(n \cdot \omega, 0)\,d\omega \]

The diffuse term of the surface is then \( \frac{\text{ albedo }}{\pi} E(n) \). The kernel below evaluates the integral with a regular grid over the hemisphere, which is simple and accurate enough for a 32 by 32 map computed once.

=== The prefiltered chain

.Prefiltered specular mips and the BRDF lookup
image::graphics-games-metal-image-based-lighting-and-hdr-prefiltered-chain.svg["Five cube mip levels for roughness 0 to 1 and the 2D BRDF integration lookup texture",width=720]

Mip 0 is the sharp environment (roughness 0); each next mip stores a rougher blur. Because blurry images need few texels, the chain is also cheap in memory. Shading selects the mip with `pass:c[level(roughness * (mipCount - 1))]` using trilinear filtering, so intermediate roughness values blend between neighbours.

== Building the maps with compute kernels

All four maps come from compute kernels. The Metal Shading Language writes to a cube texture through `texturecube<float, access::write>` with a face index, and the Swift side creates the textures with shader-write usage.

=== MSL: environment, irradiance, prefilter and BRDF LUT

[source,metal]
----
#include <metal_stdlib>
using namespace metal;

constant float PI = 3.14159265359;

// Direction of a cube texel. Metal cube faces are +X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, -Z and the texel
// coordinate has its origin at the top left, so v grows downwards.
float3 cubeDirection(uint face, float2 uv) {      // uv in -1 ... 1
    switch (face) {
        case 0:  return normalize(float3( 1.0, -uv.y, -uv.x));
        case 1:  return normalize(float3(-1.0, -uv.y,  uv.x));
        case 2:  return normalize(float3( uv.x,  1.0,  uv.y));
        case 3:  return normalize(float3( uv.x, -1.0, -uv.y));
        case 4:  return normalize(float3( uv.x, -uv.y,  1.0));
        default: return normalize(float3(-uv.x, -uv.y, -1.0));
    }
}

constexpr sampler cubeSampler(filter::linear, mip_filter::linear, address::clamp_to_edge);

// 1. Procedural environment: a warm room with a window and a very bright lamp.
kernel void make_environment(texturecube<float, access::write> env [[texture(0)]],
                              uint3 gid [[thread_position_in_grid]])    // x, y, face
{
    uint size = env.get_width();
    if (gid.x >= size || gid.y >= size || gid.z >= 6) { return; }
    float2 uv = (float2(gid.xy) + 0.5) / float(size) * 2.0 - 1.0;
    float3 d = cubeDirection(gid.z, uv);

    float3 ceiling = float3(0.85, 0.80, 0.70);
    float3 floorColor = float3(0.20, 0.13, 0.08);
    float3 color = mix(floorColor, ceiling, smoothstep(-0.4, 0.8, d.y)) * 0.6;

    float window = smoothstep(0.92, 0.95, dot(d, normalize(float3(-0.6, 0.4, 0.7))));
    color += float3(0.7, 0.85, 1.0) * 4.0 * window;                  // daylight, above white

    float lamp = smoothstep(0.995, 0.998, dot(d, normalize(float3(0.5, 0.7, 0.3))));
    color += float3(1.0, 0.75, 0.45) * 80.0 * lamp;                  // the desk lamp

    env.write(float4(color, 1.0), uint2(gid.xy), gid.z);
}

// 2. Diffuse irradiance: cosine-weighted integral over the hemisphere around N.
kernel void make_irradiance(texturecube<float> env [[texture(0)]],
                            texturecube<float, access::write> irradiance [[texture(1)]],
                            uint3 gid [[thread_position_in_grid]])
{
    uint size = irradiance.get_width();
    if (gid.x >= size || gid.y >= size || gid.z >= 6) { return; }
    float2 uv = (float2(gid.xy) + 0.5) / float(size) * 2.0 - 1.0;
    float3 n = cubeDirection(gid.z, uv);
    float3 up = abs(n.y) < 0.999 ? float3(0, 1, 0) : float3(1, 0, 0);
    float3 right = normalize(cross(up, n));
    up = cross(n, right);

    float3 sum = 0.0;
    float count = 0.0;
    const float delta = 0.05;
    for (float phi = 0.0; phi < 2.0 * PI; phi += delta) {
        for (float theta = 0.0; theta < 0.5 * PI; theta += delta) {
            float3 t = float3(sin(theta) * cos(phi), sin(theta) * sin(phi), cos(theta));
            float3 dir = t.x * right + t.y * up + t.z * n;
            sum += env.sample(cubeSampler, dir, level(2)).rgb * cos(theta) * sin(theta);
            count += 1.0;
        }
    }
    float3 result = PI * sum / count;
    irradiance.write(float4(result, 1.0), uint2(gid.xy), gid.z);
}

// 3. Specular prefilter: GGX importance sampling, one dispatch per mip level.
float radicalInverse(uint bits) {
    bits = (bits << 16u) | (bits >> 16u);
    bits = ((bits & 0x55555555u) << 1u) | ((bits & 0xAAAAAAAAu) >> 1u);
    bits = ((bits & 0x33333333u) << 2u) | ((bits & 0xCCCCCCCCu) >> 2u);
    bits = ((bits & 0x0F0F0F0Fu) << 4u) | ((bits & 0xF0F0F0F0u) >> 4u);
    bits = ((bits & 0x00FF00FFu) << 8u) | ((bits & 0xFF00FF00u) >> 8u);
    return float(bits) * 2.3283064365386963e-10;
}

float2 hammersley(uint i, uint n) { return float2(float(i) / float(n), radicalInverse(i)); }

float3 importanceSampleGGX(float2 xi, float3 n, float roughness) {
    float a = roughness * roughness;
    float phi = 2.0 * PI * xi.x;
    float cosTheta = sqrt((1.0 - xi.y) / (1.0 + (a * a - 1.0) * xi.y));
    float sinTheta = sqrt(1.0 - cosTheta * cosTheta);
    float3 h = float3(sinTheta * cos(phi), sinTheta * sin(phi), cosTheta);
    float3 up = abs(n.z) < 0.999 ? float3(0, 0, 1) : float3(1, 0, 0);
    float3 tangent = normalize(cross(up, n));
    float3 bitangent = cross(n, tangent);
    return normalize(tangent * h.x + bitangent * h.y + n * h.z);
}

struct PrefilterParams { float roughness; uint sampleCount; };

kernel void prefilter_environment(texturecube<float> env [[texture(0)]],
                                  texturecube<float, access::write> target [[texture(1)]],  // one mip as a cube view
                                  constant PrefilterParams& params [[buffer(0)]],
                                  uint3 gid [[thread_position_in_grid]])
{
    uint size = target.get_width();
    if (gid.x >= size || gid.y >= size || gid.z >= 6) { return; }
    float2 uv = (float2(gid.xy) + 0.5) / float(size) * 2.0 - 1.0;
    float3 n = cubeDirection(gid.z, uv);
    float3 v = n;                                       // split-sum assumes V = N

    float3 sum = 0.0;
    float weight = 0.0;
    for (uint i = 0; i < params.sampleCount; ++i) {
        float3 h = importanceSampleGGX(hammersley(i, params.sampleCount), n, params.roughness);
        float3 l = normalize(2.0 * dot(v, h) * h - v);
        float nDotL = saturate(dot(n, l));
        if (nDotL > 0.0) {
            sum += env.sample(cubeSampler, l, level(0)).rgb * nDotL;
            weight += nDotL;
        }
    }
    target.write(float4(sum / max(weight, 1e-4), 1.0), uint2(gid.xy), gid.z);
}

// 4. BRDF integration LUT: scale and bias for F0, indexed by N.V and roughness.
float geometrySchlickGGX(float nDotV, float roughness) {
    float k = (roughness * roughness) / 2.0;
    return nDotV / (nDotV * (1.0 - k) + k);
}

kernel void make_brdf_lut(texture2d<float, access::write> lut [[texture(0)]],
                          uint2 gid [[thread_position_in_grid]])
{
    uint size = lut.get_width();
    if (gid.x >= size || gid.y >= size) { return; }
    float nDotV = (float(gid.x) + 0.5) / float(size);
    float roughness = (float(gid.y) + 0.5) / float(size);
    float3 v = float3(sqrt(1.0 - nDotV * nDotV), 0.0, nDotV);
    float3 n = float3(0.0, 0.0, 1.0);

    float a = 0.0, b = 0.0;
    const uint sampleCount = 256;
    for (uint i = 0; i < sampleCount; ++i) {
        float3 h = importanceSampleGGX(hammersley(i, sampleCount), n, roughness);
        float3 l = normalize(2.0 * dot(v, h) * h - v);
        float nDotL = saturate(l.z), nDotH = saturate(h.z), vDotH = saturate(dot(v, h));
        if (nDotL > 0.0) {
            float g = geometrySchlickGGX(nDotL, roughness) * geometrySchlickGGX(nDotV, roughness);
            float gVis = g * vDotH / (nDotH * nDotV);
            float fc = pow(1.0 - vDotH, 5.0);
            a += (1.0 - fc) * gVis;
            b += fc * gVis;
        }
    }
    lut.write(float4(a / float(sampleCount), b / float(sampleCount), 0.0, 1.0), gid);
}
----

Source: https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Shading-Language-Specification.pdf[Metal Shading Language Specification, texturecube and texture functions] and https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/performing-calculations-on-a-gpu[Performing calculations on a GPU]

Details that matter:

* The irradiance kernel samples the environment at a *lower mip* (`level(2)`) instead of mip 0. Sampling the blurred level removes the fireflies a very bright lamp would cause with a fixed hemisphere grid, a standard trick. The environment therefore needs a mip chain (`generateMipmaps` in a blit pass after step 1).
* The lamp has a value of 80, the window 4. With an 8-bit target the lamp would clip to white and its energy would be lost; `rgba16Float` holds values up to 65504.
* The prefilter kernel must read the environment with trilinear filtering and write into a single mip level of the output; the Swift code below creates a texture *view* for each mip.
* The BRDF table is two numbers per texel and does not depend on the scene. Store it as `rg16Float`; many projects ship it as a precomputed texture instead.

=== Swift: creating and baking the maps

[source,swift]
----
import Metal

final class EnvironmentBaker {
    let device: MTLDevice
    let queue: MTLCommandQueue
    private let makeEnvironment: MTLComputePipelineState
    private let makeIrradiance: MTLComputePipelineState
    private let prefilter: MTLComputePipelineState
    private let makeLUT: MTLComputePipelineState

    private(set) var environment: MTLTexture!
    private(set) var irradiance: MTLTexture!
    private(set) var prefiltered: MTLTexture!
    private(set) var brdfLUT: MTLTexture!
    var prefilteredMipCount: Int { prefiltered.mipmapLevelCount }

    init(device: MTLDevice, queue: MTLCommandQueue, library: MTLLibrary) throws {
        self.device = device
        self.queue = queue
        func pipeline(_ name: String) throws -> MTLComputePipelineState {
            try device.makeComputePipelineState(function: library.makeFunction(name: name)!)
        }
        makeEnvironment = try pipeline("make_environment")
        makeIrradiance = try pipeline("make_irradiance")
        prefilter = try pipeline("prefilter_environment")
        makeLUT = try pipeline("make_brdf_lut")
    }

    private func cube(size: Int, mipped: Bool, label: String) -> MTLTexture {
        let d = MTLTextureDescriptor.textureCubeDescriptor(pixelFormat: .rgba16Float,
                                                           size: size, mipmapped: mipped)
        d.usage = [.shaderRead, .shaderWrite]
        d.storageMode = .private
        let t = device.makeTexture(descriptor: d)!
        t.label = label
        return t
    }

    /// Generates all maps. Blocks until the GPU has finished: call it while loading.
    func bake() {
        environment = cube(size: 256, mipped: true, label: "Environment")
        irradiance = cube(size: 32, mipped: false, label: "Irradiance")
        prefiltered = cube(size: 128, mipped: true, label: "Prefiltered environment")

        let lutDescriptor = MTLTextureDescriptor.texture2DDescriptor(pixelFormat: .rg16Float,
                                                                     width: 128, height: 128,
                                                                     mipmapped: false)
        lutDescriptor.usage = [.shaderRead, .shaderWrite]
        lutDescriptor.storageMode = .private
        brdfLUT = device.makeTexture(descriptor: lutDescriptor)!
        brdfLUT.label = "BRDF LUT"

        let commandBuffer = queue.makeCommandBuffer()!
        commandBuffer.label = "Bake IBL"

        // 1. Environment, then its mip chain.
        dispatchCube(commandBuffer, makeEnvironment, size: 256) { $0.setTexture(self.environment, index: 0) }
        let blit = commandBuffer.makeBlitCommandEncoder()!
        blit.generateMipmaps(for: environment)
        blit.endEncoding()

        // 2. Irradiance.
        dispatchCube(commandBuffer, makeIrradiance, size: 32) {
            $0.setTexture(self.environment, index: 0)
            $0.setTexture(self.irradiance, index: 1)
        }

        // 3. Prefiltered chain, one dispatch per mip level, each writing to a cube view of that level.
        let mips = prefiltered.mipmapLevelCount
        for level in 0..<mips {
            let size = max(prefiltered.width >> level, 1)
            let view = prefiltered.makeTextureView(pixelFormat: .rgba16Float, textureType: .typeCube,
                                                   levels: level..<(level + 1), slices: 0..<6)!
            var params = PrefilterParams(roughness: Float(level) / Float(max(mips - 1, 1)),
                                         sampleCount: level == 0 ? 1 : 256)
            dispatchCube(commandBuffer, prefilter, size: size) {
                $0.setTexture(self.environment, index: 0)
                $0.setTexture(view, index: 1)
                $0.setBytes(&params, length: MemoryLayout<PrefilterParams>.stride, index: 0)
            }
        }

        // 4. BRDF LUT.
        let encoder = commandBuffer.makeComputeCommandEncoder()!
        encoder.setComputePipelineState(makeLUT)
        encoder.setTexture(brdfLUT, index: 0)
        encoder.dispatchThreads(MTLSize(width: 128, height: 128, depth: 1),
                                threadsPerThreadgroup: MTLSize(width: 8, height: 8, depth: 1))
        encoder.endEncoding()

        commandBuffer.commit()
        commandBuffer.waitUntilCompleted()
    }

    private func dispatchCube(_ commandBuffer: MTLCommandBuffer, _ pipeline: MTLComputePipelineState,
                              size: Int, bind: (MTLComputeCommandEncoder) -> Void) {
        let encoder = commandBuffer.makeComputeCommandEncoder()!
        encoder.setComputePipelineState(pipeline)
        bind(encoder)
        encoder.dispatchThreads(MTLSize(width: size, height: size, depth: 6),
                                threadsPerThreadgroup: MTLSize(width: min(size, 8), height: min(size, 8), depth: 1))
        encoder.endEncoding()
    }
}

struct PrefilterParams {
    var roughness: Float
    var sampleCount: UInt32
}
----

Source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtltexture[MTLTexture] and https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlblitcommandencoder[MTLBlitCommandEncoder]

Notes on the Swift:

. Cube textures are created with `textureCubeDescriptor(pixelFormat:size:mipmapped:)`; a cube must be square, and `.shaderWrite` is needed for the compute writes. `storageMode = .private` keeps the maps GPU-only, which is the right place for them on every device.
. The grid size of a dispatch uses the *face* as the third dimension, so one dispatch covers all six faces.
. `dispatchThreads` handles sizes that are not multiples of the threadgroup size, which is why the kernels also test the bounds.
. The compute encoders are serial on one command buffer; Metal tracks hazards between them for tracked textures, so the irradiance pass waits for the environment mips. With a Metal 4 command buffer or untracked resources you add barriers explicitly, see xref:graphics-games/metal/synchronization-and-barriers.adoc[Synchronization and Barriers].
. Baking at load is a few milliseconds on current devices. Cache the result if the environment is fixed, and re-run steps 2 and 3 only when it changes.

== Shading with the maps

[source,metal]
----
struct IBLUniforms {
    float3 cameraPosition;
    float  prefilteredMipCount;
    float  exposure;
};

constexpr sampler iblSampler(filter::linear, mip_filter::linear, address::clamp_to_edge);
constexpr sampler lutSampler(filter::linear, address::clamp_to_edge);

float3 fresnelSchlickRoughness(float cosTheta, float3 f0, float roughness) {
    return f0 + (max(float3(1.0 - roughness), f0) - f0) * pow(clamp(1.0 - cosTheta, 0.0, 1.0), 5.0);
}

float3 evaluateIBL(float3 position, float3 normal, float3 albedo, float metallic, float roughness, float ao,
                   texturecube<float> irradianceMap, texturecube<float> prefilteredMap,
                   texture2d<float> brdfLUT, constant IBLUniforms& u)
{
    float3 n = normalize(normal);
    float3 v = normalize(u.cameraPosition - position);
    float nDotV = saturate(dot(n, v));
    float3 r = reflect(-v, n);

    float3 f0 = mix(float3(0.04), albedo, metallic);
    float3 f = fresnelSchlickRoughness(nDotV, f0, roughness);
    float3 kD = (1.0 - f) * (1.0 - metallic);

    float3 diffuse = irradianceMap.sample(iblSampler, n).rgb * albedo * kD;

    float3 prefiltered = prefilteredMap.sample(iblSampler, r,
                            level(roughness * (u.prefilteredMipCount - 1.0))).rgb;
    float2 brdf = brdfLUT.sample(lutSampler, float2(nDotV, roughness)).rg;
    float3 specular = prefiltered * (f0 * brdf.x + brdf.y);

    return (diffuse + specular) * ao * u.exposure;
}
----

Source: https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Shading-Language-Specification.pdf[Metal Shading Language Specification, texture sampling]

The function returns linear HDR radiance; it never clamps. It is added to the direct lighting of the lamp (see xref:graphics-games/metal/lighting-fundamentals.adoc[Lighting Fundamentals]) and the result goes to a `rgba16Float` colour target, which is the input of the next section. The sampler needs `mip_filter::linear` for the prefiltered map: with `mip_filter::nearest` the roughness steps are visible as bands in the reflection.

Common mistakes:

* *Reflections too sharp or too blurry*: the mip count in the shader does not match the texture's real `mipmapLevelCount`, or the roughness is squared in one place and not the other.
* *Everything washed out*: irradiance is multiplied by albedo twice, or the environment was written as 8-bit sRGB and then treated as linear.
* *Fireflies*: the lamp is sampled directly at mip 0 in the irradiance integral.
* *Mirrored lighting*: the environment generation and the shading disagree about the cube-face convention; always use the same `cubeDirection` for generation and test with an asymmetric pattern.
* *Black faces*: `.shaderWrite` missing, or the view levels do not match the dispatch size.

== HDR and EDR output

By default an `MTKView` shows 8-bit sRGB: 1.0 is the brightest white the pixel can be, and anything above is clamped. Apple displays can do better. An *EDR* (extended dynamic range) display reports *headroom*: the factor by which content may exceed SDR white. A headroom of 4 means a value of 4.0 is displayed four times brighter than a white sheet of paper (while the SDR white level follows the user's brightness setting). The headroom is not constant; it grows when the screen is dimmed or the room is bright enough, and shrinks with brightness, thermals and power state. Values within 0 to 1 look exactly as before, so enabling EDR does not change SDR content.

.From linear HDR to an extended-range display
image::graphics-games-metal-image-based-lighting-and-hdr-edr-output-chain.svg["Chain of five stages: linear half-float scene colour, tone map, encode into an extended linear Display P3 layer, compositor with headroom, display",width=720]

The pipeline has four requirements:

. Render in a half-float target. `rgba16Float` carries values above 1 and negative values for wide gamut.
. Make the layer an *extended-range* layer: pixel format `.rgba16Float`, `wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent = true`, and an extended-range colour space such as extended linear Display P3.
. Tone map to the *current headroom*, not to 1.0: the display can show up to that value, and anything higher is clipped or compressed by the system.
. Keep UI and SDR video in SDR range: values up to 1 are treated as SDR white.

=== Swift: configuring the layer

[source,swift]
----
import MetalKit
import QuartzCore

#if os(macOS)
import AppKit
#else
import UIKit
#endif

enum HDROutput {
    /// Configures an MTKView for extended dynamic range.
    static func enable(on view: MTKView) {
        view.colorPixelFormat = .rgba16Float
        view.clearColor = MTLClearColor(red: 0, green: 0, blue: 0, alpha: 1)
        guard let layer = view.layer as? CAMetalLayer else { return }
        layer.wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent = true
        layer.colorspace = CGColorSpace(name: CGColorSpace.extendedLinearDisplayP3)
    }

    /// The factor above SDR white the current screen can show, at least 1.0.
    @MainActor
    static func currentHeadroom(for view: MTKView) -> Float {
        #if os(macOS)
        let value = view.window?.screen?.maximumExtendedDynamicRangeColorComponentValue ?? 1.0
        #else
        let value = view.window?.windowScene?.screen.currentEDRHeadroom ?? 1.0
        #endif
        return Float(max(value, 1.0))
    }
}

/// Optional: HDR10 (PQ) or HLG output instead of extended linear.
func configurePQ(layer: CAMetalLayer, maxLuminanceNits: Float) {
    layer.pixelFormat = .bgr10a2Unorm                 // PQ is encoded by your shader
    layer.wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent = true
    layer.colorspace = CGColorSpace(name: CGColorSpace.itur_2100_PQ)
    layer.edrMetadata = CAEDRMetadata.hdr10(minLuminance: 0.005, maxLuminance: maxLuminanceNits,
                                            opticalOutputScale: 100)
}

Source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametallayer[CAMetalLayer] and https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/caedrmetadata[CAEDRMetadata]

The first approach, extended linear Display P3 in a half-float layer, is the right default for a game: the system converts for the display and applies its own tone mapping to anything above the headroom. The PQ (`itur_2100_PQ`) or HLG variants are for pipelines that mastered for a specific reference white and peak, such as video or photo viewers; you encode the transfer function yourself and describe the mastering range with `CAEDRMetadata`.

On SwiftUI the layer is reached through the representable of xref:graphics-games/metal/metalkit-mtkview-and-swiftui.adoc[MetalKit, MTKView and SwiftUI]; call `HDROutput.enable(on:)` in `makeUIView` or `makeNSView`, and update the headroom once per frame. Display changes (moving the window to another screen, brightness changes) change the value, so do not cache it.

=== MSL: a headroom-aware tone mapper

[source,metal]
----
struct ToneMapParams {
    float headroom;       // 1.0 on SDR displays
    float exposure;
};

// Leaves 0 ... 1 untouched and compresses everything above SDR white
// smoothly into 1 ... headroom, so highlights keep detail without clipping.
float3 edrRolloff(float3 c, float headroom) {
    float range = max(headroom - 1.0, 1e-3);
    float3 over = max(c - 1.0, 0.0);
    return min(c, 1.0) + range * over / (over + range);
}

// Narkowicz ACES approximation, for SDR displays.
float3 acesFilm(float3 x) {
    const float a = 2.51, b = 0.03, c = 2.43, d = 0.59, e = 0.14;
    return saturate((x * (a * x + b)) / (x * (c * x + d) + e));
}

kernel void tone_map(texture2d<float, access::read>  hdr [[texture(0)]],
                     texture2d<float, access::write> out [[texture(1)]],
                     constant ToneMapParams& params [[buffer(0)]],
                     uint2 gid [[thread_position_in_grid]])
{
    if (gid.x >= out.get_width() || gid.y >= out.get_height()) { return; }
    float3 color = hdr.read(gid).rgb * params.exposure;
    float3 mapped = params.headroom > 1.05 ? edrRolloff(color, params.headroom)
                                           : acesFilm(color);
    out.write(float4(mapped, 1.0), gid);
}
----

Source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlcomputecommandencoder[MTLComputeCommandEncoder] and https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametallayer[CAMetalLayer]

The kernel writes the drawable's texture, which therefore needs `framebufferOnly = false` on the `CAMetalLayer`; if you prefer to keep the drawable framebuffer-only, run the same function as a fragment shader in a full-screen pass. On an SDR display (`headroom` of 1) the ACES curve gives the familiar filmic look; on an EDR display the lamp and the window rise above paper white, which is what the HDR environment was built for. For a comparison of tone-mapping curves see xref:graphics-games/color-spaces-hdr-and-tone-mapping.adoc[Color Spaces, HDR and Tone Mapping].

Performance and practical notes:

* A half-float colour target doubles the memory traffic compared with 8-bit; on a tile-based GPU the cost is mostly in the resolve and the tone-map pass, which is cheap compared with the scene. See xref:graphics-games/metal/render-passes-and-load-store.adoc[Render Passes, Load and Store Actions] to avoid storing the HDR target when the tone map runs in the same render pass.
* `bgr10a2Unorm` is cheaper in bandwidth than `rgba16Float` and suits PQ output; it cannot hold values above its encoded maximum, so tone map first.
* Bloom and other post-processing run in linear HDR before the tone map (see xref:graphics-games/metal/image-processing-and-mps.adoc[Image Processing and Metal Performance Shaders]).
* Test the SDR path: external displays and low-brightness phones report a headroom of 1.0.

== Where the books are outdated

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Source | Then and now

| Metal by Tutorials, 4th edition (IBL and reflection chapters) | The maths of IBL (irradiance, prefiltering, BRDF table) is unchanged and still correct. The book targets Metal 3 with a new `MTLCommandBuffer` per frame; on the 26 operating systems the same kernels can run on a Metal 4 command buffer with an argument table.
| Metal by Tutorials and Metal Programming Guide | The books target SDR output. Neither covers EDR: `wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent`, extended linear colour spaces and the headroom API are newer than the books' target OS versions, and apps that ignore them look dull on current displays.
| Metal Programming Guide | Written for iOS 11 and Metal 2 with Swift 4; calls such as `device.newDefaultLibrary()` are now `makeDefaultLibrary()`. The compressed cube formats of that period (PVRTC) are deprecated since iOS 18 and macOS 15 in favour of ASTC (or BC on Mac).
|===
