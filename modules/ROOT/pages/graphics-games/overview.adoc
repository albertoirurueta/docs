== Graphics APIs and game engines

A *graphics API* is the programming interface to the GPU. It exposes buffers, textures, shaders and commands, and it leaves
everything else to you. OpenGL, OpenGL ES, WebGL, WebGPU, Metal, Vulkan and Direct3D are graphics APIs. A *game engine* sits
above one or more APIs and adds the parts of a game that are not drawing: an editor, an asset pipeline, a scene system,
physics, audio, animation, scripting and one-click builds for many platforms. Unity and Unreal Engine are engines. Between
the two lie *rendering frameworks* such as three.js, which give you meshes, materials and a camera without taking over the
whole project, and *scene-graph APIs* such as the historical Java 3D, which draw a tree of objects for you.

== The Bookshelf scene and shared assets

All pages share one original scene that grows into a tiny game, so the same concept can be compared across libraries. The
*Bookshelf* is a wooden shelf holding textured books (cover, spine and page textures), lit by a desk lamp. The general pages
describe it conceptually and show neutral pseudocode, GLSL or WGSL; the library parts build it step by step:

. a triangle
. a textured book quad
. a lit, rotating book box (model-view-projection transform and Blinn-Phong lighting)
. the shelf with shadows
. physically based materials
. many instanced books
. picking a book
. post-processing and particles ("dust")
. a tiny game, "catch the falling books", with a score and a timer

.The steps of the Bookshelf, shared by every library
image::graphics-games-index-bookshelf-progression.svg["Nine numbered panels from a triangle through a textured quad, a lit box, shadows, materials, instancing, picking and post-processing to the catch the falling books game",width=720]

Every library part uses the same original assets, made for this site and free of licence restrictions:

* xref:attachment$bookshelf.glb[bookshelf.glb]: a glTF 2.0 binary of 70 KB with a shelf made of six boards and nine book
  nodes that share one book mesh. It has four materials (wood for the shelf, and cover, spine and pages for the books) using
  the PBR metallic-roughness model, with the textures embedded.
* The base-colour textures as PNG files in sRGB: xref:attachment$bookshelf-textures/bookshelf-wood.png[wood] and
  xref:attachment$bookshelf-textures/bookshelf-cover.png[cover] (256 by 256 pixels), and
  xref:attachment$bookshelf-textures/bookshelf-spine.png[spine] and
  xref:attachment$bookshelf-textures/bookshelf-pages.png[pages] (64 by 256 pixels).
* A tangent-space xref:attachment$bookshelf-textures/bookshelf-cover-normal.png[normal map for the cover] as a linear PNG (256 by 256
  pixels), derived from the cover texture and not embedded in `bookshelf.glb`.

The textures are published as PNG because the KTX 2.0 command-line tool was not available when the assets were produced.
To get KTX 2.0 versions, convert the PNG files yourself with `toktx` (or `ktx create` from the KTX-Software tools), choosing
Basis Universal compression or a GPU format such as BC7 or ASTC; the page
xref:graphics-games/textures-sampling-and-mipmaps.adoc[Textures, Sampling & Mipmaps] explains the formats and the page
xref:graphics-games/scene-graphs-and-asset-formats.adoc[Scene Graphs and Asset Formats] walks through the structure of
`bookshelf.glb`.
