= Vulkan
:description: Landing page for the Vulkan sub-section of Graphics & Games -- what Vulkan is and where it stands in 2026, its place in the stack, how it differs from OpenGL, Metal and WebGPU, the re-verified version baseline (Vulkan 1.4, SDK 1.4.363.0, Roadmap 2026), a decision tree, a learning path, every page grouped by topic, the Vulkan Bookshelf running example and a coverage map of the official documentation and of two books.
:keywords: Vulkan, Vulkan 1.4, Roadmap 2026, SPIR-V, Slang, GLSL, HLSL, Vulkan-Hpp, VMA, dynamic rendering, synchronization2, timeline semaphores, descriptor heap, ray tracing, mesh shaders, Vulkan Video, MoltenVK, KosmicKrisp, Vulkan SC, learning path, Bookshelf, coverage map, bibliography

*Vulkan* is the explicit, cross-platform graphics and compute API of the Khronos Group. It gives a program direct control of the
GPU on Windows, Linux, Android, embedded systems and, through translation layers, Apple platforms, with a small, regular C API,
a binary shader format (SPIR-V) that several shading languages compile to, and a layered design in which validation, capture and
profiling are optional layers instead of costs paid by every program. This sub-section teaches Vulkan 1.4, the generation released
in December 2024, with Vulkan 1.3 as the minimum of the running example and the 2026 roadmap features marked where they apply. It
covers the instance and the device, the swapchain, synchronisation, memory with the Vulkan Memory Allocator, shaders in GLSL, HLSL
and Slang, descriptors and bindless access, the graphics and compute pipelines, ray tracing, mesh shaders, Vulkan Video, the
platforms, the {cpp} bindings and the debugging tools. It follows one running example, the *Vulkan Bookshelf*, from a first
triangle to a small game, and it says plainly where Vulkan stands in 2026: the main low-level API outside Apple platforms, a
specification that is still growing every year and an API whose newest features, such as descriptor heaps, are moving away from
the first design of 2016. The pages build on the general pages of xref:graphics-games/index.adoc[Graphics & Games]; read those
first if the rendering pipeline or the transformation chain are new to you.

[discrete]
=== What Vulkan and its neighbours are

[cols="1,3",options=header]
|===
|Name |What it is

|Vulkan
|The GPU API: instances, devices, queues, command buffers, pipelines, descriptors and synchronisation primitives, specified by the Khronos Group and implemented by the driver of each GPU vendor.

|SPIR-V
|The binary intermediate language of Vulkan shaders. GLSL, HLSL and Slang compilers produce it, and the driver turns it into GPU code.

|The Vulkan loader and layers
|The library that finds the drivers, forwards every call to them and inserts optional layers such as the Khronos validation layer.

|Vulkan-Hpp, volk and VMA
|The {cpp} bindings with RAII handles, a meta-loader that loads the function pointers and the allocator that sub-allocates memory.

|Slang
|A shading language with modules and generics that compiles to SPIR-V. It is hosted by Khronos and is the primary language of the Khronos Vulkan Tutorial.

|MoltenVK and KosmicKrisp
|Vulkan on top of Metal for macOS and iOS: a portability layer (MoltenVK) and a Vulkan 1.4 conformant driver built on Metal 4 (KosmicKrisp).

|OpenGL, Metal and WebGPU
|The other graphics APIs. xref:graphics-games/opengl/index.adoc[OpenGL] is frozen at 4.6, xref:graphics-games/metal/index.adoc[Metal] is the Apple API and
WebGPU is the browser API, implemented on top of Vulkan, Metal or Direct3D 12 (see
xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgpu-overview-and-comparison.adoc[WebGPU: Overview and Comparison with WebGL]).
|===

Vulkan gives you the GPU and little else: you manage memory, synchronisation, frame pacing and the lifetime of every object, and
you decide what a scene is. That is why most games use an engine that already speaks Vulkan, and why learning Vulkan first makes an
engine's behaviour predictable. The reasoning about when each layer pays off is in
xref:graphics-games/choosing-an-api-or-engine.adoc[Choosing an API or Engine], and the idea of an explicit API is in
xref:graphics-games/explicit-vs-implicit-apis.adoc[Explicit vs Implicit APIs].

[discrete]
=== Where Vulkan sits

.Specifications, hardware and API milestones, 2016 to 2026
image::graphics-games-vulkan-index-timeline.svg["Timeline from 2016 to 2026 with Vulkan 1.0, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4, the two books on Vulkan 1.0, the Roadmap 2022 profile, Slang at Khronos, Roadmap 2026 with the descriptor heap, and MoltenVK 1.4.2 with KosmicKrisp",width=720]

Vulkan 1.0 appeared in February 2016. Vulkan 1.1 (2018) added the Features2 queries and subgroups, 1.2 (2020) timeline
semaphores, descriptor indexing and buffer device address, 1.3 (2022) dynamic rendering and synchronization2, and 1.4 (December
2024) push descriptors, dynamic rendering local read and host image copy, and made several features mandatory. Khronos publishes a
*roadmap profile* about every two years that lists what a current device is expected to support, and on 23 January 2026 it
announced Roadmap 2026 together with the `VK_EXT_descriptor_heap` extension. No Vulkan 1.5 had been announced when this page was
checked. What each release promoted is on
xref:graphics-games/vulkan/versions-profiles-and-extensions.adoc[Vulkan Versions, Profiles and Extensions].

.The layers between a game and the GPU
image::graphics-games-vulkan-index-stack.svg["Five stacked layers from the application or engine, through helper libraries, the Vulkan loader and layers and the installable client driver, to the GPU, with ANGLE, Zink, wgpu and Dawn built on top of Vulkan and MoltenVK and KosmicKrisp built on top of Metal",width=720]

The same layers appear in the general
xref:graphics-games/graphics-apis-vs-game-engines.adoc[Graphics APIs vs Game Engines] page. The loader and its layers are what make
Vulkan debuggable: xref:graphics-games/vulkan/instance-loader-and-layers.adoc[Instances, the Loader and Layers] and
xref:graphics-games/vulkan/validation-and-debug-utils.adoc[Validation and Debug Utils] explain them, and every later page uses them.

=== How Vulkan differs from OpenGL, Metal and WebGPU

.The same five questions asked of four APIs
image::graphics-games-vulkan-index-api-comparison.svg["Table of five rows, pipeline state, synchronisation, memory, shaders and platforms, for OpenGL, Vulkan, Metal and WebGPU, with Vulkan highlighted as immutable pipeline objects, explicit barriers and semaphores, memory the application allocates, SPIR-V shaders and Windows, Linux and Android",width=720]

OpenGL hides memory, synchronisation and most state behind the driver, so a small program is short and a large one is slow in ways
that are hard to see. Vulkan moves those decisions to the application: more code for the first triangle, predictable cost for the
thousandth. Metal sits between the two on Apple hardware, and its fourth generation moved closer to Vulkan with explicit barriers
and residency. WebGPU keeps the explicit pipeline objects but leaves synchronisation and memory to the browser. Porting between
them is covered on
xref:graphics-games/opengl/porting-to-vulkan-metal-webgpu.adoc[Porting from OpenGL to Vulkan, Metal and WebGPU], and the browser
side by xref:programming-languages/rust/webassembly.adoc[WebAssembly] and
xref:programming-languages/javascript/browser-canvas-webgl.adoc[Browser Canvas and WebGL] for the wgpu and WebGPU context.

=== When to choose what

[mermaid]
....
flowchart TD
    A[Draw 3D graphics] --> B{Need your own renderer or to learn the GPU?}
    B -- yes --> C{Target platforms?}
    C -- Windows, Linux, Android --> D[Vulkan 1.3 or 1.4 with Vulkan-Hpp and Slang]
    C -- Apple only --> E[Metal 4]
    C -- Apple plus others --> F[Vulkan with MoltenVK or KosmicKrisp, or a Metal backend]
    C -- Browser --> G[WebGPU or WebGL]
    B -- no --> H{Scene, physics and tools ready made?}
    H -- yes --> I[Unity, Unreal Engine or Godot, which use Vulkan underneath]
    H -- no --> J{Existing OpenGL code?}
    J -- yes --> K[Port to Vulkan, or keep OpenGL with Zink or ANGLE]
    J -- no --> D
....

Choose *Vulkan* when you are building a renderer or an engine feature, when you need to share one code base between Windows,
Linux and Android, when you want ray tracing, mesh shaders, hardware video or the lowest overhead, or when you must understand what
an engine does for you. Choose an *engine* when the scene, the physics and the tools matter more than control. Choose *Metal*
for an Apple-only product: see xref:graphics-games/metal/index.adoc[Metal]. The general criteria are in
xref:graphics-games/choosing-an-api-or-engine.adoc[Choosing an API or Engine].

=== The Vulkan Bookshelf running example

The *Bookshelf* scene of the section landing page, described in
xref:graphics-games/overview.adoc#_the_bookshelf_scene_and_shared_assets[The Bookshelf scene and shared assets], is built in *{cpp}20
with Vulkan-Hpp RAII handles, VMA, GLFW or SDL3 and Slang shaders* (GLSL and HLSL shown as alternatives). It needs Vulkan 1.3 as a
minimum and switches on Vulkan 1.4 and roadmap features, and the optional ray-query and mesh-shader stages, only when the device
offers them. The model and the textures are the shared xref:attachment$bookshelf.glb[bookshelf.glb] and the files in the same
folder, and frames in flight use timeline semaphores. The pages grow the program in ten stages. The {cpp} language features used
are explained on
xref:programming-languages/cpp/memory-management-and-smart-pointers.adoc[Memory Management and Smart Pointers],
xref:programming-languages/cpp/build-and-tooling.adoc[Build and Tooling],
xref:programming-languages/cpp/threads-and-synchronization.adoc[Threads and Synchronization] and
xref:programming-languages/cpp/performance.adoc[Performance].

.The ten stages from an instance to a game
image::graphics-games-vulkan-index-running-example.svg["Ten stages in two rows, an instance with validation, a cleared window, a triangle, the textured book, depth and the shelf, light and shadows, many books, dust particles, the optional ray query and mesh shader extras and the game with ray picking and an ImGui score",width=720]

. an instance with validation (xref:graphics-games/vulkan/instance-loader-and-layers.adoc[Instances, the Loader and Layers])
. a cleared window with a swapchain and a frame loop (xref:graphics-games/vulkan/window-system-integration-and-swapchain.adoc[Window System Integration and the Swapchain])
. a triangle with dynamic rendering and synchronization2 (xref:graphics-games/vulkan/bookshelf-01-triangle-to-textured-book.adoc[Bookshelf 1])
. the book box with a VMA-staged vertex buffer, a model-view-projection matrix and a KTX2 cover texture
  (xref:graphics-games/vulkan/memory-heaps-types-and-vma.adoc[Memory Heaps, Types and VMA] and
  xref:graphics-games/vulkan/textures-samplers-and-mipmaps.adoc[Textures, Samplers and Mipmaps in Vulkan])
. depth and the shelf mesh (xref:graphics-games/vulkan/depth-stencil-blending-and-msaa.adoc[Depth, Stencil, Blending and MSAA in Vulkan])
. Blinn-Phong and PBR lighting with a shadow map (xref:graphics-games/vulkan/bookshelf-02-scene-and-game.adoc[Bookshelf 2])

. compute dust particles (xref:graphics-games/vulkan/compute-shaders-and-subgroups.adoc[Compute Shaders and Subgroups])
