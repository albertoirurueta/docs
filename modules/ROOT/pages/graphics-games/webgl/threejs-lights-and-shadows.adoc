= three.js Lights and Shadows
:description: How lighting and shadows work in three.js r187 with WebGLRenderer: physically based light units (candela, lux, nits and lumens) and decay = 2, the light classes (ambient, hemisphere, directional, point, spot, rect area), the shadow map pipeline, shadow camera bounds, mapSize, bias and normalBias, shadow map types and the PCFSoftShadowMap deprecation, helpers for debugging, performance costs, and a complete desk-lamp example for the Bookshelf scene, with the changes since the 2015 books.
:keywords: three.js, lights, shadows, DirectionalLight, PointLight, SpotLight, HemisphereLight, RectAreaLight, shadow map, shadow bias, normalBias, shadow acne, peter panning, PCFShadowMap, VSMShadowMap, castShadow, receiveShadow, candela, lux, decay, physically based lighting

include::partial$ai-disclaimer.adoc[]

A light in three.js is an `Object3D` that contributes radiance to every lit material in the scene, and a shadow is an extra rendering
pass that asks, for every light that is allowed to cast one, "what does the nearest surface look like from here?". This page explains
the light classes and their units, the shadow map technique that three.js uses, every shadow option that matters, how to tune the
artifacts it produces, and what the older books on the library got wrong because the defaults changed.

The facts were checked in November 2026 against three.js r187 (npm `three@0.187.0`) and its manual and API documentation. Everything
here applies to the `WebGLRenderer`, which runs on WebGL 2 in every evergreen browser; the `WebGPURenderer` uses the same light classes
and similar shadow properties, with the differences noted in the final sections and in
xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-webgpurenderer-and-tsl.adoc[WebGPURenderer and TSL]. The examples are ES modules built with Vite,
as set up in xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-getting-started.adoc[three.js Getting Started].

In the Bookshelf three.js track this page is the step where the shelf, loaded from xref:attachment$bookshelf.glb[bookshelf.glb] in
xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-loading-models-gltf.adoc[Loading Models and glTF], gets a desk lamp and soft shadows. The materials
that react to these lights are in xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-materials-and-pbr.adoc[Materials and PBR], and the same ideas written
by hand in a shader are in xref:graphics-games/webgl/lighting-in-shaders.adoc[Lighting in Shaders]. The renderer-independent theory is
in xref:graphics-games/lighting-and-shading-models.adoc[Lighting and Shading Models] and
xref:graphics-games/shadows-and-global-illumination.adoc[Shadows and Global Illumination].

== The light classes

.The light classes, their shadow support and the units of their intensity
image::graphics-games-webgl-threejs-lights-and-shadows-light-types.svg["Six panels for AmbientLight, HemisphereLight, DirectionalLight, PointLight, SpotLight and RectAreaLight, with the intensity units and the number of shadow renders per light",width=720]

All lights share the base properties `color`, `intensity` and the `castShadow` flag (honoured only by the classes that can cast one).
Lights are scene graph objects: a light can be a child of a group, moves with it, and is switched off with `visible = false`. Changing
the number of lights, or the number of shadow-casting lights, makes three.js recompile the affected materials, so decide the set of
lights up front and animate their intensity rather than adding and removing them.

|===
|Class |What it models |Main options |Shadows

|`AmbientLight`
|Uniform light from every direction. It lifts the shadows evenly and carries no direction, so it flattens form.
|`color`, `intensity`
|No

|`HemisphereLight`
|A sky colour from above blended to a ground colour from below, the cheap stand-in for an outdoor or room environment.
|`color` (sky), `groundColor`, `intensity`
|No

|`DirectionalLight`
|Parallel rays from an infinitely distant source, such as the sun. Direction runs from `position` to `target.position`.
|`color`, `intensity`, `target`
|Yes, orthographic shadow camera

|`PointLight`
|A bare bulb that emits in all directions from a point.
|`color`, `intensity`, `distance`, `decay`
|Yes, six renders into a cube map

|`SpotLight`
|A cone from a point toward a `target`, like a desk lamp or a torch.
|`color`, `intensity`, `distance`, `angle`, `penumbra`, `decay`
|Yes, perspective shadow camera

|`RectAreaLight`
|A rectangular emitter such as a window or a softbox. It replaced the old `AreaLight`.
|`color`, `intensity`, `width`, `height`
|No
|===

Three classes need a short note.

* `RectAreaLight` works only with `MeshStandardMaterial` and `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, it has no `castShadow`, and it needs a one-time
  `RectAreaLightUniformsLib.init()` call (from `three/addons/lights/RectAreaLightUniformsLib.js`) before the first render.
* `DirectionalLight` and `SpotLight` aim at their `target` object. The target is not in the scene by default, so its position is
  `(0, 0, 0)` unless you set it; if you move the target, add it to the scene (`scene.add( light.target )`) so that its world matrix is
  updated.
* `LightProbe` and the `environment` texture are image-based lighting rather than punctual lights. They are covered with environment maps
  in xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-textures-and-color-management.adoc[Textures and Color Management].

Source: https://threejs.org/manual/#en/lights[three.js manual: Lights], https://threejs.org/docs/pages/DirectionalLight.html[three.js docs: DirectionalLight]

== Physically based units

Since r155 the renderer works in physical units by default, and the documentation of the light classes states them. The defaults
changed in several steps (see <<_where_the_books_are_outdated>>), so older code shows lights that look much darker or brighter than the
numbers suggest.

|===
|Light |`intensity` unit |Notes

|`PointLight`
|candela (cd)
|`power` is the same quantity in lumens (lm); the relationship in the source is `pass:c[power = intensity * 4 * Math.PI]`. Setting one changes the other.

|`SpotLight`
|candela (cd)
|`power` is also exposed; for a spot it also depends on the cone `angle`.

|`DirectionalLight`
|lux (lx)
|Independent of distance, because the source is at infinity.

|`RectAreaLight`
|nits (cd per square metre)
|Brightness of the emitting surface, so a larger rectangle lights more.

|`AmbientLight`, `HemisphereLight`
|illuminance-like value
|No distance falloff. Treat the number as a fill level and tune it by eye.
|===

The falloff of point and spot lights is controlled by two properties:

* `decay` defaults to `2`, the physically correct inverse-square law. The documentation advises leaving it alone for physically
  correct rendering. Before r147 the default was `1`, which is why legacy scenes needed much smaller intensities.
* `distance` defaults to `0`, which means "no limit": the light attenuates by the inverse-square law all the way to infinity. A non-zero
  value fades the light smoothly to zero at that distance. This is useful as a performance and art-direction cutoff but is not physically
  correct.

Because the unit is candela, a point light at three metres from a surface with `intensity = 100` delivers about
\( 100 / 3^2 \approx 11 \) lux there. The scene scale matters: model the Bookshelf in metres (a shelf about a metre wide) and the
numbers in the example below behave sensibly. Colour management and tone mapping decide how those values turn into display values; they
are covered in xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-textures-and-color-management.adoc[Textures and Color Management].

Source: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PointLight.html[three.js docs: PointLight], https://threejs.org/manual/#en/lights[three.js manual: Lights]

== How a shadow map works

three.js implements shadows with *shadow mapping*, in two passes per shadow-casting light, shown below.

.Shadow mapping in two passes
image::graphics-games-webgl-threejs-lights-and-shadows-shadow-map-projection.svg["Left panel: a light renders depth of the book and the shelf board into a shadow map. Right panel: the camera pass transforms each fragment into light space and compares its depth with the stored depth to decide lit or in shadow",width=720]

. *Depth pass.* The renderer draws every object with `castShadow = true` from the point of view of the light, using the light's
  `shadow.camera`, into a depth texture of `shadow.mapSize` pixels. The result is `light.shadow.map`.
. *Lookup in the main pass.* While shading, each fragment of an object with `receiveShadow = true` is transformed into the light's clip
  space with `light.shadow.matrix`. Its depth is compared with the stored depth (plus the bias); if the fragment is farther, something
  is between it and the light, and the light's contribution is removed.

This is the same technique taught in xref:graphics-games/shadows-and-global-illumination.adoc[Shadows and Global Illumination] and
implemented by hand in the Bookshelf raw WebGL track. Three consequences follow, and every shadow option below is a way to manage one
of them:

* the depth texture has finite resolution, so shadows are blocky or blurry where one texel covers a large screen area;
* the depth comparison has finite precision, so surfaces can shadow themselves ("acne") unless offset by a bias;
* only the region covered by the shadow camera is shadowed, so it must be fitted to the scene.

== Enabling shadows

Three switches must all be on, and forgetting one is the most common reason for "no shadows":

|===
|Where |Property |Meaning

|Renderer
|`renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true`
|Turns the whole feature on. It is off by default (older books used `renderer.shadowMapEnabled`).

|Light
|`light.castShadow = true`
|This light renders a shadow map. Only `DirectionalLight`, `PointLight` and `SpotLight` honour it.

|Caster
|`mesh.castShadow = true`
|The mesh is drawn into the shadow map.

|Receiver
|`mesh.receiveShadow = true`
|The mesh is darkened by shadow maps. A mesh can be both caster and receiver, which is what a book on a shelf board needs.
|===

The flags are per `Mesh`, and `Group` and `Object3D` ignore them, which matters for models loaded from glTF: set them in a `traverse`
loop, as in the example. The glTF format itself carries no shadow flags.

=== Shadow map types

`renderer.shadowMap.type` selects the filtering used when the map is sampled.

|===
|Constant |Behaviour |Use it when

|`THREE.BasicShadowMap`
|One depth comparison per fragment: hard, aliased edges. Cheapest.
|Prototyping, or a deliberately hard-edged look.

|`THREE.PCFShadowMap`
|Percentage-closer filtering: several comparisons averaged, giving soft edges. The default, and since r182 it is soft as well. `shadow.radius` controls the width of the blur.
|The normal choice.

|`THREE.PCFSoftShadowMap`
|Deprecated with `WebGLRenderer` in r182 (the migration guide says to use `PCFShadowMap`, "which is now soft as well"). With `WebGPURenderer` it was removed in r186.
|Do not use in new code.

|`THREE.VSMShadowMap`
|Variance shadow maps: blurred with `radius` and `blurSamples`. Soft, but can show light bleeding where casters overlap.
|Large soft shadows when the PCF result is too noisy.
|===

Source: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html[three.js docs: WebGLRenderer], https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/wiki/Migration-Guide[three.js Migration Guide]

== The shadow object

Every shadow-capable light has a `light.shadow` object (`DirectionalLightShadow`, `PointLightShadow` or `SpotLightShadow`, all derived
from `LightShadow`).

|===
|Property |Default |Purpose

|`mapSize`
|`512 x 512`
|Resolution of the depth texture, a `Vector2`. Doubling both sides quadruples memory and fill cost. Typical values are 1024 to 4096. Set it before the first render or call `shadow.map.dispose()` after changing it.

|`camera`
|depends on the light
|The camera that renders the depth pass. Directional: an `OrthographicCamera`; spot: a `PerspectiveCamera` whose field of view follows the light `angle`; point: a 90 degree `PerspectiveCamera` used six times.

|`bias`
|`0`
|A constant offset added to the compared depth. Small negative values such as \( -0.0005 \) remove acne. Too large and shadows detach from their casters.

|`normalBias`
|`0`
|Offsets the lookup position along the surface normal. It scales with slope, so it handles grazing angles better than `bias` and is the better first knob.

|`radius`
|`1`
|Blur width for `PCFShadowMap` and `VSMShadowMap`.

|`blurSamples`
|`8`
|Sample count for the `VSMShadowMap` blur.

|`autoUpdate`, `needsUpdate`
|`true`, `false`
|With `autoUpdate = false` the map is rendered only when `needsUpdate = true`, then the flag resets. This is how static scenes avoid the depth pass entirely.
|===

=== Fitting the shadow camera

The shadow camera is the main quality lever, because the map's texels are spread across whatever the camera sees. A directional light's
orthographic camera defaults to a 10 by 10 unit box (`left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom` of -5 to 5) with `near = 0.5` and `far = 500`.
For a one metre shelf that wastes most of the resolution, so shrink it to the shelf:

[source,js]
----
const d = 1.2;                       // half-size of the shadowed region, metres
const cam = sun.shadow.camera;
cam.left = -d; cam.right = d;
cam.top = d;   cam.bottom = -d;
cam.near = 0.5; cam.far = 8;
cam.updateProjectionMatrix();        // required after changing the frustum
----

Source: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/DirectionalLightShadow.html[three.js docs: DirectionalLightShadow]

The most useful debugging tool is the camera helper:

[source,js]
----
import { CameraHelper } from 'three';

const helper = new CameraHelper( sun.shadow.camera );
scene.add( helper );
// after changing the frustum:
sun.shadow.camera.updateProjectionMatrix();
helper.update();
----

Source: https://threejs.org/manual/#en/shadows[three.js manual: Shadows]

The picture of the frustum shows at once whether a caster is outside it (no shadow at all), whether `far` clips the receiver, and how
much of the box is empty.

=== Bias, acne and peter panning

.The three bias regimes
image::graphics-games-webgl-threejs-lights-and-shadows-bias-artifacts.svg["Three panels: bias too small gives striped self-shadowing on lit surfaces, a balanced bias gives a tight contact shadow, bias too large detaches the shadow from the object",width=720]

Tune in this order and stop as soon as the artifact is gone:

. Fit the shadow camera and `mapSize` to the scene (above).
. Raise `normalBias` in small steps (0.01 to 0.05 for a scene in metres).
. If acne remains on flat surfaces, add a small negative `bias` (for example `-0.0005`).
. If shadows float away from objects, the offsets are too large; lower them.

A related trick is `material.shadowSide`: rendering back faces into the map (`THREE.BackSide`) moves the depth comparison away from
the lit surface and removes acne on closed meshes, at the price of leaks on thin geometry. Notice also that after r183 the
`WebGPURenderer` shadows were improved, so bias values that were added to hide acne there may now need to be reduced or removed.

== The Bookshelf desk lamp

The complete example loads the shelf, adds a warm spot lamp that casts shadows, a weak hemisphere fill and a floor, and wires the
renderer. It assumes the Vite project from the getting-started page, with `bookshelf.glb` placed in the `public` folder so that it is
served at `/bookshelf.glb`.

.index.html
[source,html]
----
<!doctype html>
<html lang="en">
  <head>
    <meta charset="utf-8" />
    <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1" />
    <title>Bookshelf lights and shadows</title>
    <style>
      html, body { margin: 0; height: 100%; background: #111; }
      canvas { display: block; width: 100%; height: 100%; }
    </style>
  </head>
  <body>
    <script type="module" src="/src/main.js"></script>
  </body>
</html>
----

.src/main.js
[source,js]
----
import {
  ACESFilmicToneMapping, Box3, CameraHelper, Color, HemisphereLight, Mesh,
  MeshStandardMaterial, PCFShadowMap, PerspectiveCamera, PlaneGeometry,
  Scene, SpotLight, SpotLightHelper, Vector3, WebGLRenderer,
} from 'three';
import { GLTFLoader } from 'three/addons/loaders/GLTFLoader.js';
import { OrbitControls } from 'three/addons/controls/OrbitControls.js';

const renderer = new WebGLRenderer( { antialias: true } );
renderer.setPixelRatio( Math.min( window.devicePixelRatio, 2 ) );
renderer.setSize( window.innerWidth, window.innerHeight );
renderer.toneMapping = ACESFilmicToneMapping;

// 1. Shadows must be enabled on the renderer first.
renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true;
renderer.shadowMap.type = PCFShadowMap;          // soft by default since r182
document.body.appendChild( renderer.domElement );

const scene = new Scene();
scene.background = new Color( 0x15171c );

const camera = new PerspectiveCamera( 45, window.innerWidth / window.innerHeight, 0.05, 50 );
camera.position.set( 1.6, 1.3, 2.4 );

const controls = new OrbitControls( camera, renderer.domElement );
controls.target.set( 0, 0.6, 0 );
controls.update();

// 2. A weak sky/ground fill, so the shadows are not pitch black.
scene.add( new HemisphereLight( 0x9fb4d8, 0x2a2118, 0.35 ) );

// 3. The desk lamp: a SpotLight, intensity in candela, decay = 2 by default.
const lamp = new SpotLight( 0xffd9a8, 80, 0, Math.PI / 5, 0.5 );
lamp.position.set( 0.9, 1.8, 1.2 );
lamp.castShadow = true;
lamp.shadow.mapSize.set( 2048, 2048 );
lamp.shadow.camera.near = 0.5;
lamp.shadow.camera.far = 6;
lamp.shadow.bias = -0.0004;
lamp.shadow.normalBias = 0.02;
lamp.shadow.radius = 3;
scene.add( lamp );
scene.add( lamp.target );                        // lets the target's matrix update

// 4. A floor that only receives shadows.
const floor = new Mesh(
  new PlaneGeometry( 6, 6 ),
  new MeshStandardMaterial( { color: 0x3a3f4a, roughness: 0.9 } ),
);
floor.rotation.x = -Math.PI / 2;
floor.receiveShadow = true;
scene.add( floor );

// 5. The shelf: set the shadow flags on every mesh of the loaded model.
const loader = new GLTFLoader();
const gltf = await loader.loadAsync( '/bookshelf.glb' );
gltf.scene.traverse( ( o ) => {
  if ( o.isMesh ) {
    o.castShadow = true;
    o.receiveShadow = true;
  }
} );
scene.add( gltf.scene );

// Aim the lamp at the middle of the model, whatever its size.
const box = new Box3().setFromObject( gltf.scene );
const centre = box.getCenter( new Vector3() );
lamp.target.position.copy( centre );

// Debug helpers: toggle with the "h" key.
const helpers = [ new SpotLightHelper( lamp ), new CameraHelper( lamp.shadow.camera ) ];
helpers.forEach( ( h ) => { h.visible = false; scene.add( h ); } );
window.addEventListener( 'keydown', ( e ) => {
  if ( e.key === 'h' ) helpers.forEach( ( h ) => { h.visible = ! h.visible; } );
} );

window.addEventListener( 'resize', () => {
  camera.aspect = window.innerWidth / window.innerHeight;
  camera.updateProjectionMatrix();
  renderer.setSize( window.innerWidth, window.innerHeight );
} );

renderer.setAnimationLoop( () => {
  helpers.forEach( ( h ) => h.visible && h.update() );
  renderer.render( scene, camera );
} );
----

Source: https://threejs.org/manual/#en/shadows[three.js manual: Shadows], https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SpotLight.html[three.js docs: SpotLight]

The example uses top-level `await`, which Vite supports for modern build targets; wrap it in an async function if your target does not.
Because the lamp, the shadow map and the flags all exist before the first frame, the first render already shows shadows and the
shaders compile once. If the shelf appears black, check the units first: a lamp of 80 candela about two metres away delivers roughly
20 lux, so a model exported in centimetres (100 times larger than expected) needs a much higher intensity or a scale of `0.01`.

=== Static scenes: render the shadow once

A shelf that does not move needs its shadow map only once. Disable automatic updates and request a refresh when something changes:

[source,js]
----
lamp.shadow.autoUpdate = false;
lamp.shadow.needsUpdate = true;      // renders the map on the next frame, then resets

// later, when a book moves:
function onBookMoved() {
  lamp.shadow.needsUpdate = true;
}
----

Source: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LightShadow.html[three.js docs: LightShadow]

This removes one full scene render per light per frame. For the mini game later in the track, where books fall continuously, keep
`autoUpdate = true`.

== Point lights and cube shadows

A shadow-casting `PointLight` renders the scene six times into a cube-map layout, once per face, so it costs six depth passes. One lamp
of this kind can cost more than a whole directional and spot rig, and each extra shadow-casting point light multiplies the cost. A
common compromise is to light the room with a spot or a directional light that casts shadows and add point lights without shadows for
colour. `distance` is useful here to stop the shadow camera from rendering far geometry: it also sets the far plane of the point
light's shadow.

[source,js]
----
import { PointLight } from 'three';

const bulb = new PointLight( 0xffe2b8, 40, 6 );  // 40 cd, cut off at 6 m, decay stays 2
bulb.position.set( 0, 2, 0 );
bulb.castShadow = true;
bulb.shadow.mapSize.set( 1024, 1024 );           // per face
bulb.shadow.bias = -0.002;
scene.add( bulb );
----

Source: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PointLightShadow.html[three.js docs: PointLightShadow]

== Area lights

`RectAreaLight` gives the soft, window-like highlight on glossy surfaces that point lights cannot, without shadows. It requires the
uniforms library to be initialised once and works only with the physical material family:

[source,js]
----
import { RectAreaLight } from 'three';
import { RectAreaLightUniformsLib } from 'three/addons/lights/RectAreaLightUniformsLib.js';

RectAreaLightUniformsLib.init();                 // once, before the first render

const window1 = new RectAreaLight( 0xcfe6ff, 6, 1.2, 0.8 );   // colour, nits, width, height
window1.position.set( -1.5, 1.4, 0.5 );
window1.lookAt( 0, 0.8, 0 );
scene.add( window1 );
----

Source: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html[three.js docs: RectAreaLight]

== Pitfalls

|===
|Symptom |Usual cause

|No shadows at all
|One of the four flags is missing (renderer, light, caster, receiver), or the caster is outside the shadow camera frustum. Show the `CameraHelper`.

|Shadows only on part of the scene
|The orthographic box of a directional light is too small, or `far` ends before the receiver.

|Stripes on every lit surface
|Acne: raise `normalBias`, then add a small negative `bias`.

|Shadow detached from the object
|Bias too large (peter panning) or `shadowSide` on thin geometry.

|Blocky shadow edges
|`mapSize` too small for the area covered. Shrink the camera box before raising the resolution.

|Shadow disappears when the light moves
|The light is a child of an object whose matrix is not updated, or the target is not in the scene.

|Hitch when adding a light
|Changing the number of lights recompiles materials. Create the lights at start-up.

|Model black after loading
|Intensity units and scale mismatch, or the model has only a `MeshStandardMaterial` and the scene has no lights or environment.
|===

== Performance notes

* Every shadow-casting directional or spot light adds one extra render of the shadow casters; a point light adds six. Limit
  shadow-casting lights to one or two.
* Memory per map is about \( \text{ mapSize }^2 \times 4 \) bytes for a 32-bit depth target; a 4096 map is roughly 64 MB. Shrink the map
  before the camera box is fitted and you pay for resolution you do not use.
* Mark small decorative meshes `castShadow = false`, and distant or hidden ones `receiveShadow = false`; both reduce work without being
  visible.
* Use `autoUpdate = false` for static lighting, and prefer baked lighting (a lightmap texture) for scenery that never moves.
* The fragment cost of lights grows with the number of lights, shadowed or not; many cheap lights are better replaced by an
  environment map.
* Dispose of the shadow map when removing a light: `light.dispose()` releases `shadow.map`, as described in
  xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-performance-and-disposal.adoc[Performance and Disposal].

== Where the books are outdated

The books are _Learning Three.js_, 2nd edition (LTJ, three.js r69) and the _Three.js Cookbook_ (TCB, r69); _WebGL: Up and Running_ (PAR,
2012) predates both. The concepts they teach (light types, shadow maps, bias) are still right; the code and the numbers are not.

|===
|Book code or statement |Today

|`renderer.shadowMapEnabled = true` (LTJ ch. 3-4, TCB ch. 4-5)
|`renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true` (since r72).

|`light.shadowMapWidth` and `shadowMapHeight`, `shadowCameraNear`, `shadowCameraFar`, `shadowDarkness`
|`light.shadow.mapSize`, `light.shadow.camera.near` and `far` (since r74). `shadowDarkness` no longer exists: use the intensity of the light and ambient fill.

|`light.shadowCameraVisible = true`
|`new CameraHelper( light.shadow.camera )`.

|`light.onlyShadow = true`
|Removed; a `ShadowMaterial` on a plane renders only the shadow.

|`AreaLight`, `LensFlare` (LTJ ch. 3)
|`RectAreaLight` (with `RectAreaLightUniformsLib`) and the `Lensflare` addon (r90).

|Intensities around 1 for every light, `decay` of 1
|Candela, lux and nits with `decay = 2`; scale and numbers must be chosen again (r147 and r155).

|`renderer.gammaInput` and `gammaOutput`
|`ColorManagement` and `outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, defaults since r152.

|Global `<script>` tags and `examples/js` helpers
|ES modules from `three/addons/` (the `examples/js` folder was removed in r148).

|Only `BasicShadowMap` and `PCFSoftShadowMap` (r69)
|`PCFShadowMap` (default, soft), `BasicShadowMap` and `VSMShadowMap`; `PCFSoftShadowMap` is deprecated.
|===
