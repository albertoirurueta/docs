*WebGL* is the JavaScript binding of OpenGL ES that lets a web page draw hardware-accelerated 2D and 3D graphics on a
`<canvas>` element, with no plug-in. This sub-section teaches raw WebGL 2 with GLSL ES 3.00, the *three.js* library (revision
r187) that most real projects use on top of it, and *WebGPU*, the successor API that three.js is moving to, which it covers as
context and as a comparison. It follows two tracks of one running example, the *Bookshelf* scene, from a first triangle to a
small game, and it says plainly where WebGL stands in 2026: finished and stable, available in every evergreen browser, still the
default renderer of three.js, and no longer where new features arrive. The pages build on the general pages of
xref:graphics-games/index.adoc[Graphics & Games]; read those first if the rendering pipeline or the transformation chain are new
to you. For a fast first taste, the page
xref:programming-languages/javascript/browser-canvas-webgl.adoc[Canvas, WebGL & Three.js] draws a 2D canvas, a short WebGL 2
triangle and a minimal three.js cube.

== What WebGL, three.js and WebGPU are

[cols="1,3",options=header]
|===
|Name |What it is

|WebGL 1.0 and 2.0
|Khronos specifications of a JavaScript API, based on OpenGL ES 2.0 and 3.0, with the shading languages GLSL ES 1.00 and 3.00. The browser implements it; there is nothing to install. The API is a state machine on a rendering context obtained from a canvas.

|three.js
|An open-source JavaScript library that wraps WebGL (and, since the `WebGPURenderer`, WebGPU) in a scene graph of objects, cameras, materials, lights and loaders. Two of the three books of this sub-section are about it.

|WebGPU and WGSL
|The W3C successor API and its shading language, with explicit pipelines, bind groups and compute shaders. It is still a W3C draft and, according to MDN, not yet Baseline.

|Babylon.js
|Another browser 3D engine with its own scene graph and tools. It is only named here; see its documentation for details.
|===

WebGL gives you the GPU and nothing else: you write the shaders, upload the buffers, manage the memory and handle the lost
context yourself. That is why most applications use a library, and why learning raw WebGL first makes a library's behaviour
predictable. The reasoning about when each layer pays off is in
xref:graphics-games/choosing-an-api-or-engine.adoc[Choosing an API or Engine].

== The family tree

.Specifications, browser milestones and three.js changes, 2011 to 2026
image::graphics-games-webgl-index-timeline.svg["Three timelines from 2011 to 2026, the WebGL 1 and 2 and WebGPU specifications, the browser milestones for WebGL 2, OffscreenCanvas and WebGPU, and the three.js revisions that removed Geometry and WebGL 1 support",width=720]

WebGL 1.0 appeared in 2011 as the browser binding of OpenGL ES 2.0, years before Safari or Internet Explorer shipped it
reliably. WebGL 2.0, based on OpenGL ES 3.0, arrived in Chrome 56 and Firefox 51 in 2017 and in Safari and iOS 15 in 2021, which
completed support in every evergreen browser. WebGPU reached Chrome and Edge 113 in 2023 and Safari 26 in 2025. The details, the
feature matrix and the support grid are on
xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl-versions-and-browser-support.adoc[WebGL Versions and Browser Support] and
xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgpu-overview-and-comparison.adoc[WebGPU: Overview and Comparison with WebGL].

.What a WebGL call passes through between the page and the GPU
image::graphics-games-webgl-index-stack.svg["Six stacked layers from the JavaScript of the page through the WebGL API, the browser GPU process, ANGLE or a native driver, the native graphics API, and the driver and GPU",width=720]

The same ES lineage runs through native OpenGL ES on Android (see
xref:apps/android/graphics-canvas-and-opengl-es.adoc[Graphics: Canvas, OpenGL ES and GLTextureView]) and through the
xref:graphics-games/opengl/index.adoc[OpenGL sub-section], which also explains how the GL, GLSL and WebGL versions map to each
other on xref:graphics-games/opengl/opengl-and-webgl.adoc[OpenGL and WebGL].

== When to choose what

[mermaid]
....
flowchart TD
    A[Draw 3D graphics in a web page] --> B{Need your own renderer or to learn the pipeline?}
    B -- yes --> C[Raw WebGL 2 with GLSL ES 3.00]
    B -- no --> D{Scene graph, loaders and ready materials wanted?}
    D -- yes --> E{Need compute shaders or the newest GPU features?}
    E -- no --> F[three.js with WebGLRenderer]
    E -- yes --> G[three.js with WebGPURenderer and TSL, or raw WebGPU]
    D -- no --> H{Need a full engine with editor tools?}
    H -- yes --> I[Babylon.js or another engine]
    H -- no --> C
    G -.-> J[WebGL 2 fallback keeps older browsers working]
....

Choose *raw WebGL 2* when you are learning how the pipeline works, when the program is small and special (a visualisation, a
shader toy, a custom renderer) or when a library's weight is not acceptable. Choose *three.js* for nearly everything else:
it manages shaders, state, loaders and animation for you and runs everywhere WebGL 2 does. Choose *WebGPU*, directly or
through the `WebGPURenderer`, for compute shaders, for large scenes that are limited by the CPU cost of draw calls, or for new
projects that can accept the current browser support. The general criteria are in
xref:graphics-games/choosing-an-api-or-engine.adoc[Choosing an API or Engine].

....

. Read xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl-versions-and-browser-support.adoc[WebGL Versions and Browser Support] to know which

  xref:graphics-games/webgl/framebuffers-and-render-to-texture.adoc[Framebuffers and Render-to-Texture in WebGL 2].

== The Bookshelf running example

The *Bookshelf* scene of the section landing page, described in
xref:graphics-games/index.adoc#_the_bookshelf_scene_and_shared_assets[The Bookshelf scene and shared assets], is built twice. Both
tracks use the shared xref:attachment$bookshelf.glb[bookshelf.glb] and the textures in the same folder. The PNG textures ship as
they are; the KTX2 versions mentioned on the pages are produced by the reader with `toktx` or `ktx create`.

*Raw WebGL track* (one HTML page and a few ES modules):

. a triangle (xref:graphics-games/webgl/first-triangle-webgl2.adoc[Your First Triangle in WebGL 2])
. a textured book-cover quad (xref:graphics-games/webgl/textures-and-samplers.adoc[Textures and Samplers in WebGL])
. a lit rotating book with the cover texture and a normal map (xref:graphics-games/webgl/lighting-in-shaders.adoc[Lighting in WebGL Shaders])
. an instanced shelf of books (xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl2-advanced-features.adoc[WebGL 2 Advanced Features])
. a shadow pass through a framebuffer object (xref:graphics-games/webgl/framebuffers-and-render-to-texture.adoc[Framebuffers and Render-to-Texture in WebGL 2])
. colour-ID picking (xref:graphics-games/webgl/framebuffers-and-render-to-texture.adoc[Framebuffers and Render-to-Texture in WebGL 2] and xref:graphics-games/webgl/performance-and-debugging.adoc[WebGL Performance and Debugging])
. recovery from a lost context (xref:graphics-games/webgl/context-loss-and-robustness.adoc[Context Loss and Robustness])
. the same scene in a worker (xref:graphics-games/webgl/offscreencanvas-and-workers.adoc[OffscreenCanvas and Workers])

*three.js track* (Vite and `three@0.187`):

. the shelf and the books from the GLB (xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-loading-models-gltf.adoc[Loading Models and glTF in three.js])
. physically based materials (xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-materials-and-pbr.adoc[three.js Materials and PBR])
. an orbit camera (xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-interaction-picking-controls.adoc[three.js Interaction, Picking and Controls])
. a click that pulls a book out of the shelf with a raycaster and an animation clip (xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-animation-system.adoc[three.js Animation System])
. a tiny game, "catch the falling books", with an HTML score overlay (xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-interaction-picking-controls.adoc[three.js Interaction, Picking and Controls])
. bloom (xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-postprocessing.adoc[three.js Post-processing])
. a switch to `WebGPURenderer` with a glowing-title TSL material (xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-webgpurenderer-and-tsl.adoc[three.js WebGPURenderer and TSL])

xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgpu-overview-and-comparison.adoc[WebGPU: Overview and Comparison with WebGL] renders the same lit
rotating book with raw WebGPU.
