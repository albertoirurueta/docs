= What Has Changed Since the Older WebGL and three.js Guides
:description: Row-by-row corrections for the three WebGL and three.js books this sub-section was checked against (WebGL: Up and Running, Learning Three.js, 2nd edition, and the Three.js Cookbook), with what each says, what is current in 2026, the three.js revision of the change and the page to read.
:keywords: outdated three.js tutorials, WebGL Up and Running, Learning Three.js, Three.js Cookbook, THREE.Geometry removed, Face3, CubeGeometry, ImageUtils.loadTexture, Projector, Raycaster, MeshFaceMaterial, PointCloud, CanvasRenderer, GLTFLoader, AnimationMixer, outputColorSpace, ColorManagement, examples/js, Physijs, WebGL 2, corrections

include::partial$graphics-games-webgl-disclaimer.adoc[]

The ideas in the three older WebGL books are still sound: a scene graph of objects, cameras that project it, geometry that becomes
buffers, materials and lights that become shaders, textures, picking with a ray, animation as keyframes, and a render loop. What
has changed is almost everything that is typed: class names, loader names, the way a script is included, the colour rules, the
browser support and the format used to bring a model into the page. The books were written for three.js revisions r49 to r69
(2012 to 2015) and for WebGL 1 only; this sub-section documents r187 (npm `three@0.187.0`, 30 October 2026), WebGL 2 and
WebGPU. This page lists, book by book, the statements that are out of date and what to do today, so that you can keep reading
the books for their explanations and use the pages of this sub-section for current code. The facts were checked in November
2026 against the three.js Migration Guide and release notes, the three.js manual and the WebGL specifications. For the changes
that affect all graphics libraries, not only WebGL, see
xref:graphics-games/whats-changed-since-older-guides.adoc[What Has Changed Since the Older Guides].

The books are listed in the xref:graphics-games/index.adoc#bibliography[section bibliography] and have the short names used in
the tables: PAR (Tony Parisi, _WebGL: Up and Running_, 2012), LTJ (Jos Dirksen, _Learning Three.js_, 2nd edition, 2015) and TCB (Jos
Dirksen, _Three.js Cookbook_, 2015). Chapter numbers are those of the books. The revision numbers in the *Revision* column are
three.js release numbers, so `r125` is the release that removed the item, and the Migration Guide is the primary source for each.

== The corrections, book by book

=== PAR: WebGL: Up and Running (2012)

[cols="1,3,3,1,2",options=header]
|===
|Chapters |What the book says |What is current |Revision or date |Read more

|7
|Internet Explorer has no WebGL, Safari needs a developer toggle and iOS supports WebGL only inside iAd.
|WebGL 2 runs in every evergreen browser (Chrome 56, Firefox 51, Edge 79, Safari and iOS 15), about 97 percent of global usage in November 2026. Internet Explorer is retired.
|2017 and 2021
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl-versions-and-browser-support.adoc[WebGL Versions and Browser Support]

|1 and 7
|The context is requested with `"experimental-webgl"`, animation uses a vendor-prefixed `requestAnimationFrame` and Detector.js tests for support.
|Ask for `getContext("webgl2")` and fall back only if you must. In three.js drive the loop with `renderer.setAnimationLoop`. Detector.js was replaced by the `WebGL` compatibility module.
|r97 and r107
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/first-triangle-webgl2.adoc[Your First Triangle in WebGL 2], xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl-versions-and-browser-support.adoc[WebGL Versions and Browser Support]

|3
|Geometry is built from `THREE.Geometry`, `Face3` and `CubeGeometry`, and the scene is described with Sim.js.
|`BoxGeometry` and the other primitives are `BufferGeometry` subclasses. `Geometry` was removed in r125 and `Face3` in r126. Sim.js is not needed: the scene graph and the examples' lil-gui cover its role.
|r66, r125 and r126
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-geometry.adoc[three.js Geometry], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-scene-graph-and-transforms.adoc[three.js Scene Graph and Transforms]

|5
|Picking converts the mouse position with `THREE.Projector` and an unprojected vector.
|Convert the pointer to normalised device coordinates and call `Raycaster.setFromCamera`. For many objects consider GPU colour-ID picking.
|Since the 2010s
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-interaction-picking-controls.adoc[three.js Interaction, Picking and Controls]

|7
|Models are loaded from the three.js JSON format, from COLLADA or from the binary format produced by the Blender exporter.
|Use glTF 2.0 (`.glb` or `.gltf`) with `GLTFLoader` and Blender's glTF exporter. The legacy three.js loaders were removed between r97 and r111, and COLLADA is no longer the recommended exchange format.
|r97 to r111
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-loading-models-gltf.adoc[Loading Models and glTF in three.js]

|3
|Shaders are GLSL ES 1.00 with `attribute`, `varying` and `gl_FragColor`.
|WebGL 2 shaders start with `#version 300 es` and use `in`, `out` and a declared fragment output. In three.js set `glslVersion: THREE.GLSL3` on the material.
|WebGL 2 (2017)
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/glsl-es-100-and-300.adoc[GLSL ES 1.00 and 3.00 in WebGL], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-custom-shaders-glsl.adoc[three.js Custom Shaders (GLSL)]

|3 and 8
|Particle systems are built from the early three.js particle classes and a list of vertices, and sound uses a custom audio helper.
|Use `Points` with `PointsMaterial`, instancing or transform feedback for GPU particles, and the three.js `Audio` classes or the Web Audio API for sound.
|r72 for `Points`
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-geometry.adoc[three.js Geometry], xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl2-advanced-features.adoc[WebGL 2 Advanced Features]

|4
|Animation is done with Tween.js tweens.
|Tween.js still works for UI-style tweens, but model animation uses `AnimationMixer`, clips and actions, with the `Timer` class replacing `Clock`.
|r86 and r183
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-animation-system.adoc[three.js Animation System]
|===

=== LTJ: Learning Three.js, 2nd edition (2015)

[cols="1,3,3,1,2",options=header]
|===
|Chapters |What the book says |What is current |Revision or date |Read more

|1
|Development uses the IEWebGL plugin for Internet Explorer and disables the browser's security checks to open pages from `file://`.
|Serve the page from a development server such as Vite, or use an import map with any static server. Textures from `file://` are blocked by design.
|ES modules since r137
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-getting-started.adoc[three.js: Getting Started], xref:graphics-games/webgl/security-cors-and-privacy.adoc[WebGL Security, CORS and Privacy]

|2 to 6
|`THREE.Geometry`, `Face3` and `CubeGeometry` describe shapes, and textures come from `THREE.ImageUtils.loadTexture`.
|Only `BufferGeometry` remains, and `TextureLoader` loads images.
|r66, r89, r125 and r126
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-geometry.adoc[three.js Geometry], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-textures-and-color-management.adoc[three.js Textures and Colour Management]

|3
|`AreaLight` and the core `LensFlare` object light and decorate a scene, and light intensities are unitless numbers.
|`RectAreaLight` replaces `AreaLight`, the lens flare is the `Lensflare` addon, light intensities use physical units (candela, lux and lumens) and point and spot lights have `decay = 2`.
|r90, r147 and r155
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-lights-and-shadows.adoc[three.js Lights and Shadows]

|3 and 4
|Shadows are enabled with `renderer.shadowMapEnabled` and sized with `light.shadowMapWidth`.
|Use `renderer.shadowMap.enabled`, `light.shadow.mapSize` and `shadow.bias`. `PCFSoftShadowMap` is deprecated since r182.
|r72 and r74
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-lights-and-shadows.adoc[three.js Lights and Shadows]

|4
|One object uses several materials through `MeshFaceMaterial` (or `MultiMaterial`).
|Pass an array of materials and give the geometry groups.
|r85
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-geometry.adoc[three.js Geometry], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-materials-and-pbr.adoc[three.js Materials and PBR]

|7
|`PointCloud` and `PointCloudMaterial` draw particles, and `CanvasRenderer` is the fallback for browsers without WebGL.
|`Points` and `PointsMaterial`. `CanvasRenderer` was removed in r98 and every supported browser has WebGL 2.
|r72 and r98
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-geometry.adoc[three.js Geometry]

|8
|CTM, AWD, Babylon and AssimpJSON loaders import models.
|These loaders were removed. Convert the asset to glTF 2.0.
|r108 to r110
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-loading-models-gltf.adoc[Loading Models and glTF in three.js]

|9
|`MorphAnimMesh`, `THREE.Animation` and `AnimationHandler` play animations, and materials need the `morphTargets` and `skinning` flags.
|`AnimationMixer` with clips and actions plays every animation, and the material flags were removed.
|r86 and r129 to r130
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-animation-system.adoc[three.js Animation System]

|11
|Post-processing with `EffectComposer` is the only path.
|It is still valid with `WebGLRenderer`, now ending with `OutputPass` for tone mapping and colour conversion. `WebGPURenderer` uses `RenderPipeline` instead.
|`RenderPipeline` r183
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-postprocessing.adoc[three.js Post-processing], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-webgpurenderer-and-tsl.adoc[three.js WebGPURenderer and TSL]

|12
|Physijs, an unmaintained wrapper around Ammo.js and cannon.js, adds physics, and dat.GUI builds control panels.
|Choose a current engine from the physics page of the three.js manual and the general
xref:graphics-games/physics-audio-and-input-basics.adoc[Physics, Audio and Input Basics]. The examples bundle lil-gui in place of dat.GUI.
|Since the late 2010s
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-animation-system.adoc[three.js Animation System]
|===

=== TCB: Three.js Cookbook (2015)

[cols="1,3,3,1,2",options=header]
|===
|Chapters |What the book says |What is current |Revision or date |Read more

|1
|Detector.js and the `"experimental-webgl"` context name decide whether WebGL is available, and a local web server is optional.
|Call `getContext("webgl2")` or catch the renderer constructor error, and always use a development server.
|r97 and r107
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl-versions-and-browser-support.adoc[WebGL Versions and Browser Support], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-getting-started.adoc[three.js: Getting Started]

|1, 2 and 7
|The three.js exporters and loaders for JSON, OBJ with `OBJMTLLoader` and Collada bring models in.
|Export glTF 2.0 from Blender. For OBJ, `MTLLoader` and `OBJLoader` replace `OBJMTLLoader`, and both are legacy formats today.
|r97 to r111
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-loading-models-gltf.adoc[Loading Models and glTF in three.js]

|1 and 3
|Picking uses `THREE.Projector` and mouse events, and the camera controls are loaded as global scripts from `examples/js`.
|`Raycaster.setFromCamera` with pointer events, and controls imported from `three/addons/`. The `examples/js` folder was removed in r148.
|r148
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-interaction-picking-controls.adoc[three.js Interaction, Picking and Controls]

|4
|`MeshFaceMaterial` combines materials, and light and material values are tuned in a gamma-unaware workflow with `renderer.gammaInput` and `gammaOutput`.
|Material arrays, `renderer.outputColorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace`, `ColorManagement.enabled` (the default since r152) and `texture.colorSpace`. Book lighting values look different today.
|r85, r152 and r155
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-materials-and-pbr.adoc[three.js Materials and PBR], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-textures-and-color-management.adoc[three.js Textures and Colour Management]

|5
|Custom shader recipes use GLSL ES 1.00 with the built-in `ShaderMaterial` uniforms.
|The recipes still work with `WebGLRenderer`, and GLSL 3.00 is available with `glslVersion: THREE.GLSL3`. `ShaderMaterial` does not work with `WebGPURenderer`, which uses TSL.
|r163 for WebGL 1 removal
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-custom-shaders-glsl.adoc[three.js Custom Shaders (GLSL)], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-webgpurenderer-and-tsl.adoc[three.js WebGPURenderer and TSL]

|6
|`PointCloud` particles and the composer's post-processing passes add effects.
|`Points`, and the `EffectComposer` with `OutputPass` on `WebGLRenderer` or `RenderPipeline` on `WebGPURenderer`.
|r72 and r183
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-postprocessing.adoc[three.js Post-processing], xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-geometry.adoc[three.js Geometry]

|7
|Animation uses `MorphAnimMesh` and skinned meshes with the `THREE.Animation` class, and Physijs adds physics.
|`AnimationMixer`, and a maintained physics engine chosen from the three.js manual.
|r86 and r129
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-animation-system.adoc[three.js Animation System]
|===

=== All three books

[cols="3,3,1,2",options=header]
|===
|What the books say |What is current |Revision or date |Read more

|Every example is a global `<script>` that loads `three.min.js` and files from `examples/js`.
|ES modules: `import * as THREE from "three"` with `three/addons/` for the add-ons, resolved by Vite or by an import map. The `examples/js` folder was removed in r148.
|r137 and r148
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-getting-started.adoc[three.js: Getting Started]

|Colours and lights are defined in a gamma-unaware way and the renderer shows them unchanged.
|The renderer converts from a linear working space to `SRGBColorSpace` on output. Colour textures need `texture.colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, and data textures stay linear.
|r152
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-textures-and-color-management.adoc[three.js Textures and Colour Management], xref:graphics-games/webgl/lighting-in-shaders.adoc[Lighting in WebGL Shaders]

|Only GLSL ES 1.00 and WebGL 1 exist.
|WebGL 2 with GLSL ES 3.00 is the baseline, `WebGLRenderer` has not supported WebGL 1 since r163, and WebGPU is the successor API.
|2017 and r163
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgl-versions-and-browser-support.adoc[WebGL Versions and Browser Support], xref:graphics-games/webgl/webgpu-overview-and-comparison.adoc[WebGPU: Overview and Comparison with WebGL]

|`THREE.Geometry`, `Face3` and `CubeGeometry` are the geometry classes.
|`BufferGeometry` only, with `BoxGeometry` for the cube.
|r66, r125 and r126
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-geometry.adoc[three.js Geometry]

|`THREE.ImageUtils.loadTexture` loads textures.
|`TextureLoader`, which returns the texture immediately and fills it when the image arrives.
|removed in r89
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-textures-and-color-management.adoc[three.js Textures and Colour Management]

|Raw WebGL examples need no context-loss handling and no cross-origin rules.
|A context can be lost at any time, and cross-origin images need CORS headers and `crossOrigin` before they can be textures.
|Always
|xref:graphics-games/webgl/context-loss-and-robustness.adoc[Context Loss and Robustness], xref:graphics-games/webgl/security-cors-and-privacy.adoc[WebGL Security, CORS and Privacy]
|===

== What to keep from the books

* The explanations of the scene graph, of cameras and projection, of texture mapping, of lighting models, of picking with a ray
  and of keyframe animation remain correct. Only the names of the calls around them have changed.
* The structure of the book projects (create a scene, a camera and a renderer, add objects, run a render loop) is exactly the
  structure of xref:graphics-games/webgl/threejs-getting-started.adoc[three.js: Getting Started].
* The raw WebGL chapters of PAR still describe the pipeline well; read them together with
  xref:graphics-games/webgl/rendering-pipeline.adoc[The WebGL Rendering Pipeline] and use WebGL 2 for the code.
* Where a book and the three.js manual or the Migration Guide disagree, follow the manual and the Migration Guide.
