= What Has Changed Since the Older Unreal Engine Guides
:description: Row-by-row corrections for the six Unreal books this sub-section was checked against, from the 2011 UDK guide to a 2023 UE 5.0 book, with what each says, what is current in Unreal Engine 5.8, the release or date of the change and the page to read, plus the UE3 to UE5 history and a procedure for moving an older project to 5.8.

The ideas in the older Unreal books are still sound: actors made of components, the gameplay framework, Blueprint graphs, the reflection macros, collision channels, material graphs, authority and replication. What has changed is much of what is typed and clicked: the input bindings, the cinematic, particle and physics tools, the lighting workflow, the toolchains, the Android steps, the licence and the addresses of the community sites. This page lists, book by book, the statements that are out of date and what to do today, so that you can keep reading the books for their explanations and use the pages of this sub-section for current steps. It documents Unreal Engine 5.8. The facts were checked on 5 November 2026 against Epic's release notes, requirements pages and migration guide and against Google Play's requirements page. Epic's licence, FAQ and news pages refused automated requests that day, so the licence figures below come from search excerpts of those pages. For the changes that affect all graphics libraries, not only Unreal Engine, see xref:graphics-games/whats-changed-since-older-guides.adoc[What Has Changed Since the Older Guides].

== From UE3 to UE5

.The versions of the books on the road to Unreal Engine 5.8
image::graphics-games-unreal-engine-whats-changed-version-ladder.svg["Seven steps from UDK 2011 with UnrealScript and Kismet, through UE 4.4 to 4.7, UE 4.7 to 4.11 and UE 4.20 to 4.22, to UE 5.0 with Nanite and Lumen, UE 5.7 with PCG and Substrate production-ready and UE 5.8 with MegaLights production-ready, with each step marked with the book that targets it",width=720]

Three breaks matter when you read an older guide. UE3 and UDK were scripted in UnrealScript and Kismet; UE4 dropped UnrealScript in 2014 for {cpp} and Blueprints, so nothing typed in a UDK book carries over. UE4 went free in 2015 with a royalty, so licence advice from before that date and the thresholds of the following years are both obsolete. UE5 arrived in 2022 with a new generation of systems, and Epic's migration guide lists what replaces what: Sequencer for Matinee, Chaos for PhysX as the default, Niagara for Cascade, World Partition for World Composition, and Lumen for Light Propagation Volumes and Distance-Field Global Illumination. The story in full is on xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/versions-licensing-and-history.adoc[Versions, Licensing and History].

Check the version of a tutorial before anything else. A UE 4.27 tutorial looks almost like a UE 5.8 one until it reaches lighting, input or particles, and UE 5.0 itself predates most of what this sub-section recommends.

== Moving an older project

.Storyboard: moving a UE4 project to Unreal Engine 5.8
image::graphics-games-unreal-engine-whats-changed-upgrade-storyboard.svg["Six panels: commit a backup, open the project in UE 5.8 with Open a Copy, regenerate project files and fix the build, replace Cascade, World Composition and legacy input, move the lighting to Lumen and Virtual Shadow Maps, and package and test on each target",width=720]

.Procedure: move a UE4 project to Unreal Engine 5.8
. Commit the project to source control, so that you can always go back. Once a project is upgraded, an older engine cannot open it.
. Open the project with UE 5.8 and choose *Open a Copy*. Epic's migration guide recommends this, and warns that *Convert in-place* and *Skip conversion* can corrupt or lose data.
. For a {cpp} project, regenerate the project files, compile the modules and fix the deprecation errors, starting with the constructors and with `TObjectPtr` members (xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/uobject-reflection-and-garbage-collection.adoc[UObject, Reflection and Garbage Collection]).
. Convert Cascade effects with the converter plugin (xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/niagara-vfx.adoc[Niagara VFX]), convert World Composition maps with `WorldPartitionConvertCommandlet` (xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/world-partition-and-pcg.adoc[World Partition and PCG]) and port the input (xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/enhanced-input.adoc[Enhanced Input]).
. Turn on Lumen and Virtual Shadow Maps in the project settings if the project did not get them, and review each level (xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/lighting-lumen-and-megalights.adoc[Lighting, Lumen and MegaLights]).
. Package for each target, test on the devices and profile again (xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/packaging-desktop.adoc[Packaging for Desktop], xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/android-and-mobile.adoc[Android and Mobile] and xref:graphics-games/unreal-engine/profiling-and-optimization.adoc[Profiling and Optimization]).

The migration guide says that projects from UE 4.0 to 4.26 load in the same or any newer version, and that very old projects may need fixes for deprecated references. A UE3 or UDK project has no such path: rebuild it.

== What is still correct in the books

.What to keep from the books and what to replace
image::graphics-games-unreal-engine-whats-changed-keep-and-replace.svg["Two columns, the ideas to keep such as actors and components, the gameplay framework, Blueprint graphs, reflection, collision, material graphs and replication, and what to replace such as input bindings, Matinee, Cascade, World Composition, static-first lighting, FObjectInitializer constructors, toolchains, licence advice and old links",width=720]

* The explanations of actors, components, the gameplay framework, Blueprint graphs and flow control, collision channels, traces and material graphs remain correct. Only some of the names around them have changed.
* The replication model in CAR and LIR, with authority, replicated properties and RPCs, is the model of UE 5.8, although Iris now sits underneath for projects that enable it.
* The UnrealScript class tree in COR explains where the classes of the gameplay framework came from.
* Where a book and Epic's documentation disagree, follow the documentation for the version you use.
