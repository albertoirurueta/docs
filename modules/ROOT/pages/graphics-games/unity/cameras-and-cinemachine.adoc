A camera decides what the player sees, and in a game about browsing a shelf of books it also decides how pleasant the browsing feels: how the view orbits the shelf, how it glides to a book that was picked, what happens when a bookcase
gets between the camera and the book. Unity gives you two layers for this. The *Camera* component is the lens: it turns the 3D scene into a 2D image with a projection, a field of view and clip planes. *Cinemachine* is a package of
procedural camera behaviors that moves that camera for you, with priorities, blends and collision avoidance, so most gameplay cameras need little or no camera code.

The facts were checked in November 2026 against the Unity 6.3 LTS manual (6000.3) and Cinemachine 3.1 (version 3.1.7 at the time of writing, the version in the Unity 6.3 package list). Unity 6.6 makes Cinemachine a core package. The examples run on every platform
and use the Universal Render Pipeline (URP) 3D template with the Input System. In the running example this is the "Cinemachine orbit camera" step: a camera that orbits the shelf with the pointer, blends to a close-up when a book is picked,
and shakes a little when a book hits the floor, working with the physics in xref:graphics-games/unity/physics-3d.adoc[3D Physics] and the actions of the xref:graphics-games/unity/input-system.adoc[Input System]. The Bookshelf scene uses the shared
xref:attachment$bookshelf.glb[bookshelf.glb] model. The mathematics of projection (view and projection matrices, the frustum) is in
xref:graphics-games/vectors-matrices-and-homogeneous-coordinates.adoc[Vectors, Matrices and Homogeneous Coordinates] and xref:graphics-games/coordinate-spaces-and-transforms.adoc[Coordinate Spaces and Transforms]; this page is the Unity-specific half.

== The Camera component

.The camera frustum: perspective and orthographic
image::graphics-games-unity-cameras-and-cinemachine-frustum.svg["A side view of a perspective frustum from a camera through the near plane to the far plane with a field of view angle and a book inside, above a rectangular orthographic volume with parallel edges and a size marker",width=720]

Every camera is a GameObject with a *Camera* component. The scene has at least one, and you can have many. The component produces an image by projecting everything inside its *view frustum*, the truncated pyramid (perspective) or box (orthographic)
bounded by the near and far clip planes. What is outside it is not drawn.

[cols="2,5"]
|===
|Property |What it does

|*Projection*
|*Perspective* makes distant objects smaller, as a real lens does; it is the default for 3D games. *Orthographic* removes perspective and is used for 2D games, maps, isometric views and UI-like overlays. Fog is rendered uniformly in orthographic mode.

|*Field of View*
|The vertical angle of the frustum in degrees for a perspective camera. The horizontal angle follows from the aspect ratio. Values around 60 to 75 are typical for first and third person; 30 to 40 gives the "telephoto" look suited to a book close-up.

|*Size*
|For an orthographic camera, half the height of the view in world units (`Camera.orthographicSize`); the width follows from the aspect ratio.

|*Physical Camera*
|Replaces the field of view with the real-world settings of a camera: focal length, sensor size, lens shift and gate fit. Use it for cinematic work and when matching footage.

|*Clipping Planes*
|*Near* and *Far*. Geometry closer than Near or farther than Far is clipped. The depth buffer has finite precision, so a huge Far-to-Near ratio produces z-fighting; use the largest Near and smallest Far that the scene allows.

|*Viewport Rect*
|The part of the screen the camera renders to, as a normalized rectangle. Two cameras with half-screen rects make a split screen.

|*Depth* (priority)
|Cameras render in increasing order; a camera with a higher value draws over a lower one.

|*Culling Mask*
|Which layers the camera renders. A viewmodel or a book inspection view can have its own layer and camera.

|*Output Texture* (`targetTexture`)
|Renders into a Render Texture instead of the screen: security monitors, a mirror, a minimap, a book preview.

|*Occlusion Culling* and *Dynamic Resolution*
|Skip hidden objects (needs baked occlusion data) and scale the render resolution to hold the frame rate.
|===

In URP most of these are the same, and the Inspector adds *Render Type* (Base or Overlay), *Renderer*, post-processing and shadow toggles, and the *Background Type* in place of the Built-In Render Pipeline's *Clear Flags*.
The Built-In Render Pipeline is deprecated as of Unity 6.5, so new projects use the URP component.

Source: https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/cameras/overview[Manual: Introduction to cameras], https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/cameras/camera-view/understanding-frustum[Manual: Introduction to the camera view] and https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/cameras/urp/camera-components-reference[Manual: Camera Inspector windows reference for URP]

=== The size of the frustum at a distance

For a perspective camera with vertical field of view \( \theta \), the visible height at distance \( d \) is

\[ h = 2 \, d \, \tan \left( \frac{ \theta }{ 2 } \right) \qquad w = h \cdot a \]

where \( a \) is the aspect ratio (width divided by height). This is how you frame a shelf of known height: to fit a 2 metre shelf with a 60 degree field of view, put the camera about 1.73 metres back from it. Keep this in mind when you change
the field of view for a zoom: it changes the framing as well as the perspective, which is why a "dolly zoom" moves the camera while it changes the field of view.

Source: https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/cameras/camera-view/frustum-size-at-distance[Manual: Calculate the size of the frustum at a distance]

=== Oblique, physical and other special views

An *oblique* frustum (set through `Camera.projectionMatrix` or `CalculateObliqueMatrix`) skews the near plane, which is how water reflections clip everything below the surface. A Physical Camera with *Lens Shift* offsets the
lens from the sensor and reframes the image without tilting, so an architectural view keeps vertical lines vertical. *Gate Fit* decides what happens when the sensor and screen aspect ratios differ.

Source: https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/cameras/camera-view/oblique-frustum[Manual: Make the camera perspective oblique] and https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/cameras/physical/lens-shift[Manual: Widen the view with Lens Shift]

== Screen, viewport and world space

A point can be described in three spaces, and `Camera` converts between them:

[cols="2,3,4"]
|===
|Space |Range |Conversion methods

|*Screen*
|Pixels. The lower left pixel is (0, 0); the upper right is (width minus 1, height minus 1). The `z` of a screen point is the distance in world units in front of the camera.
|`ScreenPointToRay`, `ScreenToWorldPoint`, `ScreenToViewportPoint`

|*Viewport*
|Normalized: (0, 0) is the lower left of the camera's viewport and (1, 1) the upper right; `z` is the distance from the camera.
|`ViewportPointToRay`, `ViewportToWorldPoint`, `WorldToViewportPoint`

|*World*
|Metres in the scene.
|`WorldToScreenPoint`, `WorldToViewportPoint`
|===

`ScreenPointToRay` returns a world-space ray that starts on the near plane and passes through the pixel; it is how a pointer picks an object (`BookPicker` in the xref:graphics-games/unity/physics-3d.adoc[3D Physics] page). `ScreenToWorldPoint` needs the depth in `z`, and
returns a point on a plane parallel to the camera at that depth. In an orthographic camera the depth hardly matters, which is why 2D games get away with a single value.

`Camera.main` returns the first enabled camera with the *MainCamera* tag. Unity caches the tagged cameras, so the call costs about the same as a `GetComponent`; keep a reference when you use it every frame. It returns null if no enabled camera has the tag, which
is a common source of null reference errors when the camera is disabled during a scene change.

Source: https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/camera/screenpointtoray[Scripting API: Camera.ScreenPointToRay], https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/camera/worldtoviewportpoint[Scripting API: Camera.WorldToViewportPoint] and https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/camera/main[Scripting API: Camera.main]

=== Is it in view?

To decide whether a book is in front of the camera (to pause a hover effect, to skip sound) you can test its bounds against the six frustum planes without a physics query:

[source,csharp]
----
using UnityEngine;

namespace Bookshelf.Cameras
{
    /// <summary>Reports whether the renderer's bounds are inside the camera frustum.</summary>
    [RequireComponent(typeof(Renderer))]
    public sealed class FrustumVisibility : MonoBehaviour
    {
        [SerializeField] private Camera _camera;

        private readonly Plane[] _planes = new Plane[6];
        private Renderer _renderer;

        public bool IsInView { get; private set; }

        private void Awake()
        {
            _renderer = GetComponent<Renderer>();
            if (_camera == null)
            {
                _camera = Camera.main;
            }
        }

        private void Update()
        {
            GeometryUtility.CalculateFrustumPlanes(_camera, _planes);
            IsInView = GeometryUtility.TestPlanesAABB(_planes, _renderer.bounds);
        }
    }
}
----

Source: https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/geometryutility/calculatefrustumplanes[Scripting API: GeometryUtility.CalculateFrustumPlanes] and https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/geometryutility/testplanesaabb[Scripting API: GeometryUtility.TestPlanesAABB]

The planes array is allocated once and reused, so the test does not create garbage. The test is conservative: it says "in view" if the bounding box touches the frustum, even when the object itself is hidden behind another. (Renderers already have `isVisible`, which tells you whether any camera drew them, and
occlusion culling goes further.)

== Multiple cameras

More than one camera is normal: a main camera, a minimap, a book inspector, a mirror, a UI camera. The options:

* *Depth and viewport.* Cameras draw in `depth` order into their viewport rectangles; a higher-depth camera draws after, and so over, the lower one. Viewport rects make split screens.
* *Render Textures.* A camera whose `targetTexture` is set draws into a texture that you put on a material or a UI image.
* *URP camera stacking.* In URP a *Base* camera renders the scene and any number of *Overlay* cameras render on top of it into the same target, in the order of the Base camera's *Camera Stack*. An Overlay camera does not clear colour, so it composes with the
  Base camera's image, and can clear depth so that its objects are never hidden by the world (a held book that must not poke through the shelf). Overlay cameras cost another culling and rendering pass, so use few.
* *Cinemachine.* Do not add more Unity cameras for different shots. One Unity camera, controlled by many CinemachineCameras, is the Cinemachine pattern.

[source,csharp]
----
using UnityEngine;
using UnityEngine.Rendering.Universal;

namespace Bookshelf.Cameras
{
    /// <summary>Adds an overlay camera to the base camera's stack, on top of the scene.</summary>
    public sealed class InspectOverlay : MonoBehaviour
    {
        [SerializeField] private Camera _baseCamera;
        [SerializeField] private Camera _overlayCamera;

        private void OnEnable()
        {
            UniversalAdditionalCameraData overlayData = _overlayCamera.GetUniversalAdditionalCameraData();
            overlayData.renderType = CameraRenderType.Overlay;

            UniversalAdditionalCameraData baseData = _baseCamera.GetUniversalAdditionalCameraData();
            if (!baseData.cameraStack.Contains(_overlayCamera))
            {
                baseData.cameraStack.Add(_overlayCamera);
            }
        }

        private void OnDisable()
        {
            if (_baseCamera != null)
            {
                _baseCamera.GetUniversalAdditionalCameraData().cameraStack.Remove(_overlayCamera);
            }
        }
    }
}
----

Source: https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/cameras/urp/camera-types-and-render-type[Manual: Camera render types in URP] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@17.3/api/UnityEngine.Rendering.Universal.UniversalAdditionalCameraData.html[URP API: UniversalAdditionalCameraData]

== A hand-written orbit camera

Before Cinemachine, it helps to write the simplest orbit camera once, because it shows what Cinemachine automates. The camera lives at a point on a sphere around a target, the pointer changes the two angles, the scroll wheel changes the radius,
and a sphere cast keeps the shelf from getting between the camera and the target.

[source,csharp]
----
using UnityEngine;
using UnityEngine.InputSystem;

namespace Bookshelf.Cameras
{
    /// <summary>A minimal orbit camera: yaw, pitch, zoom and shelf avoidance.</summary>
    public sealed class ManualOrbitCamera : MonoBehaviour
    {
        [SerializeField] private Transform _target;
        [SerializeField] private InputActionReference _look;    // Value action, Vector2 (pointer delta or stick)
        [SerializeField] private InputActionReference _zoom;    // Value action, Vector2 (scroll)
        [SerializeField] private LayerMask _obstacles;
        [SerializeField, Min(0.5f)] private float _distance = 3f;
        [SerializeField] private Vector2 _distanceRange = new Vector2(1f, 6f);
        [SerializeField] private Vector2 _pitchRange = new Vector2(-10f, 70f);
        [SerializeField, Min(0f)] private float _degreesPerUnit = 0.15f;
        [SerializeField, Min(0f)] private float _zoomStep = 0.002f;
        [SerializeField, Min(0f)] private float _smoothTime = 0.08f;
        [SerializeField, Min(0.01f)] private float _collisionRadius = 0.2f;

        private float _yaw;
        private float _pitch = 20f;
        private Vector3 _velocity;

        private void OnEnable()
        {
            _look.action.Enable();
            _zoom.action.Enable();
            _yaw = transform.eulerAngles.y;
        }

        private void LateUpdate()
        {
            if (_target == null)
            {
                return;
            }

            Vector2 look = _look.action.ReadValue<Vector2>();
            _yaw += look.x * _degreesPerUnit;
            _pitch = Mathf.Clamp(_pitch - look.y * _degreesPerUnit, _pitchRange.x, _pitchRange.y);

            float scroll = _zoom.action.ReadValue<Vector2>().y;
            _distance = Mathf.Clamp(_distance - scroll * _zoomStep, _distanceRange.x, _distanceRange.y);

            Vector3 pivot = _target.position;
            Vector3 direction = Quaternion.Euler(_pitch, _yaw, 0f) * Vector3.back;

            float distance = _distance;
            if (Physics.SphereCast(pivot, _collisionRadius, direction, out RaycastHit hit,
                    _distance, _obstacles, QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore))
            {
                distance = Mathf.Max(hit.distance, 0.2f);
            }

            Vector3 desired = pivot + direction * distance;
            transform.position = Vector3.SmoothDamp(transform.position, desired, ref _velocity, _smoothTime);

            Vector3 toTarget = pivot - transform.position;
            if (toTarget.sqrMagnitude > 0.0001f)
            {
                transform.rotation = Quaternion.LookRotation(toTarget, Vector3.up);
            }
        }
    }
}
----

Source: https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/monobehaviour/lateupdate[Scripting API: MonoBehaviour.LateUpdate] and https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/physics/spherecast[Scripting API: Physics.SphereCast]

The camera moves in `LateUpdate`, after every `Update` and animation has moved the target, so it never lags a frame behind. A mouse delta is already "per frame" and a stick value is "per second", which is why a production
rig reads them differently (Cinemachine's input controller has a *Cancel Delta Time* option for exactly this). This script is a good starting point; the Cinemachine version adds damping per axis, blends to other shots, shot quality and an obstacle strategy
without more code.

== Cinemachine 3

*Cinemachine* is a package (`com.unity.cinemachine`) of procedural camera control. Version 3 is a major rewrite of version 2: the namespace is `Unity.Cinemachine`, the Virtual Camera is now the *CinemachineCamera*, and the old
fields lose their `m_` prefix. Unity 6.3 uses Cinemachine 3.1; Unity 6.6 makes the package a core package.

=== The three essential elements

.Cinemachine Brain: priorities, blends and one Unity Camera
image::graphics-games-unity-cameras-and-cinemachine-brain-and-blends.svg["Three CinemachineCamera GameObjects with priorities feed one CinemachineBrain that picks the live one, applies the default blend or a custom blend and drives a single Unity Camera, with a small blend curve below",width=720]

A working setup has exactly three kinds of element:

. *One Unity Camera* (a GameObject with a `Camera` component). It is the only thing that renders.
. *A Cinemachine Brain* on that camera. It watches all active CinemachineCameras, chooses which one is live, and handles the transition (a cut or a blend) when the choice changes.
. *One or more CinemachineCameras.* Each is a separate GameObject, not nested in one another and without a Camera component. They are placeholders for a shot: when one is live it overrides the Unity camera's
  position, rotation, lens, post-processing profile and behavior over time.

Creating the first CinemachineCamera from the *GameObject > Cinemachine* menu adds a Brain to the Unity camera if it has none. CinemachineCameras are cheap, so a scene can hold dozens; a camera that is not live is in *Standby*
(it keeps following its target and updating, which is what makes a blend smooth), and a camera whose GameObject is deactivated is disabled and costs nothing. *Standby Update* (Never, Always, Round Robin) tunes how often standby cameras update.

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/concept-essential-elements.html[Cinemachine manual: Essential elements] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineBrain.html[Cinemachine manual: Cinemachine Brain]

=== How the Brain chooses the live camera

[mermaid]
....
flowchart TD
    A["All active CinemachineCameras"] --> B["Keep cameras whose Channel is in the Brain's Channel Mask"]
    B --> C{"Is a Timeline Cinemachine track active?"}
    C -- "Yes" --> D["The Timeline clip decides the live camera and the blend"]
    C -- "No" --> E["Highest Priority wins; a tie goes to the most recently activated"]
    E --> F{"Is the winner already live?"}
    F -- "Yes" --> G["No change"]
    F -- "No" --> H["Look up a blend: Custom Blends asset, then Default Blend"]
    H --> I["Blend (or cut) to the new camera"]
....

*Priority* is an integer (default 0, negatives allowed). A higher value wins, and among equals the camera activated most recently wins. Both cameras of a blend stay live until it ends, so a camera you just deactivated can still be live for the blend duration.
To change shots in a game, change priorities or activate and deactivate GameObjects, as the `ShelfCameraDirector` below does; to choreograph a cutscene, use Timeline's Cinemachine track, which ignores priorities (see xref:graphics-games/unity/timeline-and-cutscenes.adoc[Timeline and Cutscenes]).

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/concept-camera-control-transitions.html[Cinemachine manual: Camera control and transitions] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineCamera.html[Cinemachine manual: Cinemachine Camera component]

=== Behaviors: position, rotation and extensions

A CinemachineCamera has a *Tracking Target* (what to follow) and an optional *Look At Target* (what to aim at, if different), a *Lens* section that mirrors the Camera's, and *procedural behaviors* added as components
in three groups:

[cols="2,3,5"]
|===
|Group |Component |What it does

|Position Control
|*Follow*
|Keeps an offset from the target with per-axis damping and a binding mode (Lock To Target, World Space, Lazy Follow and others).

|
|*Orbital Follow*
|Puts the camera on an orbit surface around the target, driven by a Horizontal, a Vertical and a Radial axis. The orbit style is *Sphere* (one radius) or *Three Ring* (top, middle and bottom orbits joined by a spline). The figure below shows both axes.

|
|*Third Person Follow*
|An over-the-shoulder camera with a rig and a camera collision, usually combined with Third Person Aim.

|
|*Position Composer*
|Places the camera so that the target sits at a chosen position on screen, with dead zones and soft zones; used for 2D and side views.

|
|*Hard Lock To Target*, *Spline Dolly*
|Locks to the target's position, or moves along a spline.

|Rotation Control
|*Rotation Composer*
|Aims the camera at the target with a screen-space composition (dead zone, hard limits, lookahead) and damping.

|
|*Pan Tilt*, *Hard Look At*, *Rotate With Follow Target*, *Third Person Aim*
|Rotation from input angles, locked to the look at target, copied from the target, or aimed through the screen center.

|Noise
|*Basic Multi Channel Perlin*
|Adds handheld or vehicle shake from a noise profile.

|Extensions
|*Deoccluder*, *Decollider*, *Confiner 2D and 3D*, *Impulse Listener*, *Free Look Modifier*, *Group Framing*, *Recomposer*, *Storyboard*, *Volume Settings*
|Optional post-processing of the camera state: obstacle avoidance, keeping the camera inside a volume or polygon, reacting to impulses, tuning lens by vertical angle, framing a group of targets, and more.
|===

The menu entries combine these for you: *GameObject > Cinemachine > Targeted Cameras > Follow Camera* creates a camera with *Follow* and *Rotation Composer*, and *GameObject > Cinemachine > FreeLook Camera*
creates *Orbital Follow*, *Rotation Composer*, an *Input Axis Controller* and an optional *Free Look Modifier*.

.The orbit camera around a book: three rings
image::graphics-games-unity-cameras-and-cinemachine-orbit-camera.svg["A top view of a circular orbit around a target showing the horizontal angle, and a side view of three orbit rings, top, middle and bottom, joined by a curve on which the camera sits, with a radius that scales all rings",width=720]

*Cinemachine 3 has no separate FreeLook class.* A FreeLook camera is a CinemachineCamera with Orbital Follow and an input controller. The alternative is *Position Composer* with *Pan Tilt*, where Pan Tilt sets the viewing angle and
Position Composer frames the target on screen.

=== Blends

When a different camera becomes live the Brain either cuts or blends. A blend is not a fade: it smoothly animates the Unity camera's position, rotation and lens from one CinemachineCamera to the other, preserving the view of the target. How it is chosen:

. The Brain looks in its *Custom Blends* asset (a *Cinemachine Blender Settings* asset) for an entry matching the pair. Entries name the *From* and *To* cameras (names, not references; the name `ANY CAMERA` matches any). The most specific entry wins.
. If none matches, the *Default Blend* of the Brain applies: a *style* (Cut, Ease In Out, Ease In, Ease Out, Hard In, Hard Out, Linear, Custom curve) and a *time* in seconds.
. In Timeline, overlapping Cinemachine shots on the Cinemachine track define the blend by the size of the overlap.
. A camera's *Blend Hint* changes the algorithm, not the timing: spherical or cylindrical paths around the target, screen-space aiming, inheriting the current position, or freezing the outgoing camera.
. `CinemachineCore.GetBlendOverride` lets code override any blend at run time.

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/ControllingAndCustomizingBlends.html[Cinemachine manual: Control and customize blends] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineBlending.html[Cinemachine manual: Cinemachine Blender Settings asset]

== The Bookshelf camera scripts

=== ShelfCameraDirector: change shots by priority

[source,csharp]
----
using Unity.Cinemachine;
using UnityEngine;

namespace Bookshelf.Cameras
{
    /// <summary>Switches between the overview, orbit and close-up cameras by priority.</summary>
    public sealed class ShelfCameraDirector : MonoBehaviour
    {
        [SerializeField] private CinemachineBrain _brain;
        [SerializeField] private CinemachineCamera _overview;
        [SerializeField] private CinemachineCamera _orbit;
        [SerializeField] private CinemachineCamera _closeUp;
        [SerializeField, Range(10f, 60f)] private float _closeUpFieldOfView = 30f;
        [SerializeField, Min(0f)] private float _blendSeconds = 0.8f;

        private void Awake()
        {
            // Default Blend of the Brain: the style and time used when no custom blend matches.
            _brain.DefaultBlend = new CinemachineBlendDefinition(
                CinemachineBlendDefinition.Styles.EaseInOut, _blendSeconds);
            ShowOrbit();
        }

        public void ShowOverview() => Activate(_overview);

        public void ShowOrbit() => Activate(_orbit);

        public void FocusBook(Transform book)
        {
            _closeUp.Target.TrackingTarget = book;
            _closeUp.Lens.FieldOfView = _closeUpFieldOfView;
            Activate(_closeUp);
        }

        private void Activate(CinemachineCamera live)
        {
            _overview.Priority = live == _overview ? 20 : 0;
            _orbit.Priority = live == _orbit ? 20 : 0;
            _closeUp.Priority = live == _closeUp ? 20 : 0;
        }
    }
}
----

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/api/Unity.Cinemachine.CinemachineBrain.html[Cinemachine API: CinemachineBrain] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/api/Unity.Cinemachine.CinemachineCamera.html[Cinemachine API: CinemachineCamera]

`Priority` is a `PrioritySettings` struct that converts implicitly from an `int` (and enables itself); `Target` and `Lens` are public struct fields, so they can be assigned in place. The director does not move any camera: it only says which one is live,
and the Brain does the blend.

=== BookImpactShake: a camera nudge from physics

[source,csharp]
----
using Bookshelf.Gameplay;
using Unity.Cinemachine;
using UnityEngine;

namespace Bookshelf.Cameras
{
    /// <summary>Sends a Cinemachine impulse when a book hits something hard.</summary>
    [RequireComponent(typeof(CinemachineImpulseSource))]
    public sealed class BookImpactShake : MonoBehaviour
    {
        [SerializeField] private BookBody[] _books;
        [SerializeField, Min(0.1f)] private float _speedForFullShake = 6f;

        private CinemachineImpulseSource _source;

        private void Awake()
        {
            _source = GetComponent<CinemachineImpulseSource>();
        }

        private void OnEnable()
        {
            foreach (BookBody book in _books)
            {
                book.Impact += OnImpact;
            }
        }

        private void OnDisable()
        {
            foreach (BookBody book in _books)
            {
                if (book != null)
                {
                    book.Impact -= OnImpact;
                }
            }
        }

        private void OnImpact(BookBody book, float speed)
        {
            float force = Mathf.Clamp01(speed / _speedForFullShake);
            _source.GenerateImpulseWithForce(force);
        }
    }
}
----

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineImpulse.html[Cinemachine manual: Impulse] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/api/Unity.Cinemachine.CinemachineImpulseSource.html[Cinemachine API: CinemachineImpulseSource]

The shake needs both ends: this *Impulse Source* generates a signal (its *Impulse Definition* chooses the shape, duration and amplitude), and a *Cinemachine Impulse Listener* extension on the CinemachineCamera turns the signal into camera motion, filtered by channel.
The `BookBody` and its `Impact` event are defined in the xref:graphics-games/unity/physics-3d.adoc[3D Physics] page.

=== OrbitAxisDriver: drive the orbit from code

[source,csharp]
----
using Unity.Cinemachine;
using UnityEngine;

namespace Bookshelf.Cameras
{
    /// <summary>Sets the orbit angles from code, for example to face the front of a book.</summary>
    [RequireComponent(typeof(CinemachineOrbitalFollow))]
    public sealed class OrbitAxisDriver : MonoBehaviour
    {
        private CinemachineOrbitalFollow _orbital;

        private void Awake()
        {
            _orbital = GetComponent<CinemachineOrbitalFollow>();
        }

        /// <summary>Turns the camera to look at the front of a target whose forward is given.</summary>
        public void FaceFront(Vector3 targetForward)
        {
            // Horizontal Axis is a rotation about the up vector, in degrees.
            float yaw = Mathf.Atan2(-targetForward.x, -targetForward.z) * Mathf.Rad2Deg;
            _orbital.HorizontalAxis.Value = yaw;
        }

        public void SetZoom(float radialScale)
        {
            // Radial Axis scales the orbit radii; clamp to its Range.
            Vector2 range = _orbital.RadialAxis.Range;
            _orbital.RadialAxis.Value = Mathf.Clamp(radialScale, range.x, range.y);
        }
    }
}
----

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/api/Unity.Cinemachine.CinemachineOrbitalFollow.html[Cinemachine API: CinemachineOrbitalFollow] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/api/Unity.Cinemachine.InputAxis.html[Cinemachine API: InputAxis]

The axes are public fields of type `InputAxis`, a struct with `Value`, `Range`, `Center`, `Wrap` and recentering settings. The sign and the zero of the angle depend on the binding mode and the axis `Center`, so adjust the formula to your rig.

=== CameraEventLogger: react to shot changes

[source,csharp]
----
using Unity.Cinemachine;
using UnityEngine;

namespace Bookshelf.Cameras
{
    /// <summary>Logs shot changes and finished blends, as a base for HUD or audio reactions.</summary>
    public sealed class CameraEventLogger : MonoBehaviour
    {
        private void OnEnable()
        {
            CinemachineCore.CameraActivatedEvent.AddListener(OnCameraActivated);
            CinemachineCore.BlendFinishedEvent.AddListener(OnBlendFinished);
        }

        private void OnDisable()
        {
            CinemachineCore.CameraActivatedEvent.RemoveListener(OnCameraActivated);
            CinemachineCore.BlendFinishedEvent.RemoveListener(OnBlendFinished);
        }

        private static void OnCameraActivated(ICinemachineCamera.ActivationEventParams evt)
        {
            string from = evt.OutgoingCamera != null ? evt.OutgoingCamera.Name : "none";
            Debug.Log($"Shot change: {from} to {evt.IncomingCamera.Name} (cut: {evt.IsCut})");
        }

        private static void OnBlendFinished(ICinemachineMixer mixer, ICinemachineCamera camera)
        {
            Debug.Log($"Blend finished on {camera.Name}");
        }
    }
}
----

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineEvents.html[Cinemachine manual: Cinemachine Events]

Cinemachine events are global through `CinemachineCore` and carry every camera and brain; the *Cinemachine Camera Events* and *Cinemachine Brain Events* components filter them for one object and expose Unity Events for the Inspector.

== Input, 2D and groups

* *Input.* Cinemachine cameras do not read input themselves; they expose axes. The *Input Axis Controller* finds the axes of the sibling components (Orbital Follow, Pan Tilt and others) and drives them with the Input System (or the legacy Input Manager), with gain, acceleration and deceleration, and an option to ignore input
  while a blend is running. For several local players, or for custom control such as a touch slider, derive from `InputAxisControllerBase` and read a `PlayerInput`.
* *2D.* Use an orthographic Unity camera and a CinemachineCamera with *Position Composer*, add the *Confiner 2D* extension with a polygon to keep the view inside the level, and *Pixel Perfect* for pixel-art games. The confiner is a physics-free polygon test; see xref:graphics-games/unity/2d-games.adoc[2D Games] and xref:graphics-games/unity/physics-2d.adoc[2D Physics].
* *Groups.* A *Cinemachine Target Group* with *Group Framing* keeps several targets in view (two players, a book and the librarian).
* *Manager cameras.* *Clear Shot*, *State-Driven*, *Sequencer* and *Mixing* cameras contain child cameras and decide among them by shot quality, an Animator state, a timed list, or a weighted mix.

Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/CinemachineInputAxisController.html[Cinemachine manual: Input Axis Controller], https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/2d-and-top-down-games.html[Cinemachine manual: 2D and top-down games] and https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@3.1/manual/GroupingTargets.html[Cinemachine manual: Group targets]

== Common mistakes

* *Two Unity cameras with a Brain, or a Brain on the wrong object.* A Cinemachine setup has one Unity camera with one Brain; CinemachineCameras do not have Camera components.
* *Nesting CinemachineCameras.* They must be independent GameObjects.
* *Priorities that are never lowered.* Two cameras with the same high priority keep fighting; the most recently activated wins. Lower the old one when you raise the new one.
* *Moving the Unity camera by script while a Brain is on it.* The Brain overwrites the transform every frame. Move a CinemachineCamera, or its target.
* *`Camera.main` is null.* There is no enabled camera tagged *MainCamera*. Tag it, cache the reference, and handle a missing one.
* *Reading the pointer in the wrong space.* A screen point is in pixels, a viewport point is normalized; `ScreenPointToRay` takes pixels, and a `Vector3` input needs `z` set for `ScreenToWorldPoint`.
* *Far clip too large.* Z-fighting on the shelf boards; raise Near instead of lowering precision.
* *A Deoccluder with no colliders on the obstacles.* It uses raycasts, so it sees only colliders on the chosen layers.
* *Following a Rigidbody with an Update-driven camera.* The camera jitters. Interpolate the Rigidbody (see xref:graphics-games/unity/physics-3d.adoc[3D Physics]) and leave the Brain's *Update Method* on *Smart Update*, the recommended setting.
* *Using old Cinemachine 2 names.* `CinemachineVirtualCamera`, `m_Follow` and the `Cinemachine` namespace are gone; the namespace is `Unity.Cinemachine`. The upgrade guide lists the renamed components.
* *Mouse input scaled by `deltaTime`.* A mouse delta is already per frame; scaling it makes the camera slower at high frame rates (use *Cancel Delta Time* for such inputs).

== Performance notes

* Each CinemachineCamera is cheap, but standby cameras still update; use *Standby Update* to set Never or Round Robin for cameras that are rarely used, and deactivate the ones you do not need.
* The Deoccluder and the Third Person Follow collision use physics queries; limit their layer masks and avoid per-frame allocations in your own camera scripts.
* Extra Unity cameras are expensive, because each one culls and renders the scene again: avoid Overlay cameras and Render Texture cameras that are not needed, lower the render texture resolution, and disable cameras that are not visible.
* Use occlusion culling and the camera's *Culling Mask* to cut the work of a camera that sees only part of the scene, and keep Far no larger than necessary.
* The Profiler and the Frame Debugger show each camera's rendering pass (see xref:graphics-games/unity/profiling-and-optimization.adoc[Profiling and Optimization]).
