A COBOL program describes storage once and then looks at it in several ways. This page covers the four Data Division
features that describe the same bytes more than once or control where they sit: `REDEFINES` (another description of an
area), `RENAMES` (a regrouping of existing items under a level-66 name), `SYNCHRONIZED` (alignment of binary items, and the
slack bytes it adds) and `TYPEDEF`/`TYPE` (a named description that is reused, with the `STRONG` variant of Enterprise COBOL
6.5 that makes the compiler check it). The level numbers, group items and `FILLER` that all of them rely on are in
xref:programming-languages/cobol/data-division-and-level-numbers.adoc[Data Division and Level Numbers]; the `PICTURE` and
`USAGE` clauses that decide what a description means are in xref:programming-languages/cobol/picture-clause.adoc[The PICTURE Clause] and
xref:programming-languages/cobol/usage-and-numeric-representation.adoc[Usage and Numeric Representation]. The
Circulation layouts (`BOOKREC`, `LOANREC`) are the ones used throughout the section.

== REDEFINES

The `REDEFINES` clause lets several data description entries describe the same storage area (Enterprise COBOL Language
Reference, _REDEFINES clause_). Nothing is allocated or converted: both descriptions are in effect at all times, and the
program decides which one it uses at any point. The entry that introduces the area is the *redefined item* (the object,
`data-name-2`); the entry that carries the clause is the *redefining item* (the subject, `data-name-1`).

[source,text]
----
level-number  data-name-1  REDEFINES  data-name-2   (rest of the description)
level-number  FILLER       REDEFINES  data-name-2
----

=== The rules

The rules below are from the Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference; the right-hand column notes what GnuCOBOL 3.2 does
(each point was tried, and the diagnostics are shown later on this page).

[cols="3,2",options="header"]
|===
| Rule (Enterprise COBOL 6.5) | GnuCOBOL 3.2.0

| `REDEFINES` must be the first clause after the data name (or `FILLER`), or after the level number if the name is omitted.
| Same; the `free-redefines-position` dialect option controls the strictness.

| The redefining item and the redefined item have the same level in the hierarchy (the level numbers may differ). Neither
  may be level 66 or 88.
| Same.

| The redefined item may itself be a redefining item, but may not have an `OCCURS` clause (it may be inside a table, if the
  reference is not subscripted). Neither item may have `OCCURS DEPENDING ON`.
| `OCCURS` on the object is accepted with a warning (`should not have an OCCURS clause`).

| Neither the redefining item nor any item under it may have a `VALUE` clause.
| `VALUE` on a redefining item compiles with the warning `initial VALUE clause ignored`.

| Any usage is allowed on either side.
| Same.

| No entry with a lower level number may come between the two entries, and the redefinition ends at the next entry whose
  level number is equal to or lower than the redefining item.
| Same. A further item at the same level between the two entries is rejected: `REDEFINES must follow the original definition`.

| The redefining item may be larger than the redefined one, except for an `EXTERNAL` record.
| Larger is an error by default (`larger REDEFINES used`) and a warning with `-std=ibm`. For a level-01 item it is a warning
  (`-Wlarger-01-redefines`).

| Several redefinitions of one area are allowed, and each may name either the original entry or the previous redefinition.
| Naming the previous redefinition (`D REDEFINES B`) is an error by default (`+'B' is not the original definition+`) and is
  accepted with `-std=ibm`.

| A `TYPEDEF` item cannot be redefined, but items inside a group type definition can be.
| Not applicable (see the `TYPEDEF` section): the typedef name has no storage in GnuCOBOL either.
|===

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddered.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: REDEFINES clause] and https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/HTML/gnucobpg.html#REDEFINES[GnuCOBOL Programmer's Guide: REDEFINES]

=== Alternate views of one record

The classic use is one transaction area with a code that says which layout applies. The example gives the 30-byte
Circulation transaction three views: a loan or return (the `LOANREC` fields), a fine adjustment, and a free-text note. The
`TRAN-CODE` condition-names choose the view in an `EVALUATE`; the data is moved in as one literal for brevity.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. RD1.
      * One 30-byte transaction area, three alternate views.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  TRAN-AREA.
           05  TRAN-CODE           PIC X.
               88  TRAN-LOAN       VALUE "O" "R".
               88  TRAN-FINE-ADJ   VALUE "F".
               88  TRAN-NOTE       VALUE "N".
           05  TRAN-BODY           PIC X(29).
           05  LOAN-BODY REDEFINES TRAN-BODY.
               10  LB-MEMBER-ID    PIC 9(6).
               10  LB-BOOK-ID      PIC 9(6).
               10  LB-DATE         PIC 9(8).
               10  FILLER          PIC X(9).
           05  FINE-BODY REDEFINES TRAN-BODY.
               10  FB-MEMBER-ID    PIC 9(6).
               10  FB-AMOUNT       PIC S9(5)V99.
               10  FILLER          PIC X(16).
           05  NOTE-BODY REDEFINES TRAN-BODY.
               10  NB-TEXT         PIC X(29).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE "O00010000000120261020" TO TRAN-AREA
           PERFORM SHOW-TRAN
           MOVE "F0001010001250" TO TRAN-AREA
           PERFORM SHOW-TRAN
           MOVE "NCard replaced at desk" TO TRAN-AREA
           PERFORM SHOW-TRAN
           DISPLAY "area is " LENGTH OF TRAN-AREA " bytes"
           STOP RUN.
       SHOW-TRAN.
           EVALUATE TRUE
               WHEN TRAN-LOAN
                   DISPLAY "loan  member " LB-MEMBER-ID
                       " book " LB-BOOK-ID " date " LB-DATE
               WHEN TRAN-FINE-ADJ
                   DISPLAY "fine  member " FB-MEMBER-ID
                       " amount " FB-AMOUNT
               WHEN TRAN-NOTE
                   DISPLAY "note  " NB-TEXT
           END-EVALUATE.
----

The output is:

[source,text]
----
loan  member 000100 book 000001 date 20261020
fine  member 000101 amount +00012.50
note  Card replaced at desk        
area is 30 bytes
----

The three views are laid over `TRAN-BODY` byte for byte; no `MOVE` copies anything between them. The fine amount reads
`pass:c[+00012.50]` because GnuCOBOL's default `DISPLAY` shows a signed field with its sign and decimal point; with `-std=ibm` the same
field prints as `0001250+`, without the decimal point, which is closer to what z/OS shows. The `FILLER` items pad
a short view up to the size of the area, which is not required by the language but keeps the layouts the same length.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddered.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: REDEFINES clause]

=== A numeric view and a character view

The same bytes often need to be read as a number and as characters: a date split into year, month and day, or an input
field that must be tested with `IS NUMERIC` before it is used as a number. A level-01 item can redefine another level-01
item, as long as the two are adjacent; here the copybook record `LOAN-RECORD` is given a second layout, and a price is
given a character view.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. RD2.
      * Two views of one field: numeric and character.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       COPY LOANREC.
       01  LOAN-DATE-PARTS REDEFINES LOAN-RECORD.
           05  FILLER              PIC X(13).
           05  LDP-YEAR            PIC 9(4).
           05  LDP-MONTH           PIC 99.
           05  LDP-DAY             PIC 99.
           05  FILLER              PIC X(9).
       01  PRICE-AREA.
           05  PRICE-NUM           PIC 9(4)V99.
           05  PRICE-TEXT REDEFINES PRICE-NUM PIC X(6).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE "O00010000000120261020" TO LOAN-RECORD
           DISPLAY "date " LOAN-DATE
           DISPLAY "year " LDP-YEAR " month " LDP-MONTH
               " day " LDP-DAY
           MOVE 11 TO LDP-MONTH
           DISPLAY "date " LOAN-DATE " after changing the month"
           MOVE "12.50 " TO PRICE-TEXT
           IF PRICE-NUM IS NUMERIC
               DISPLAY "price is numeric: " PRICE-NUM
           ELSE
               DISPLAY "price text " PRICE-TEXT " is not numeric"
           END-IF
           MOVE "001250" TO PRICE-TEXT
           IF PRICE-NUM IS NUMERIC
               DISPLAY "price is numeric: " PRICE-NUM
           END-IF
           STOP RUN.
----

The output is:

[source,text]
----
date 20261020
year 2026 month 10 day 20
date 20261120 after changing the month
price text 12.50  is not numeric
price is numeric: 0012.50
----

Changing `LDP-MONTH` changed `LOAN-DATE` because they are the same bytes, which is the point and the danger of the clause.
The `IS NUMERIC` test is what makes the character view safe: text such as `12.50 ` is not a valid zoned decimal, and using
`PRICE-NUM` in arithmetic without the test would be an error on z/OS (a data exception, `S0C7`, covered with the other
numeric traps in xref:programming-languages/cobol/move-and-data-movement.adoc[MOVE and Data Movement]).

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddered.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: REDEFINES clause]

=== Several redefinitions of one area

One area may have any number of redefinitions. They may all name the original entry, or each may name the one before it
(the Language Reference shows both forms). The example uses both: `B` and `C` redefine `A`, and `D` redefines `B`. The `D`
form needs `-std=ibm` in GnuCOBOL 3.2.0, as the table above says.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. MULT1.
      * Several redefinitions of one area, one through a chain.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  REC.
           05  A               PIC 9999.
           05  B REDEFINES A   PIC 9V999.
           05  C REDEFINES A   PIC 99V99.
           05  D REDEFINES B   PIC 999V9.
       01  SHOW-B              PIC 9.999.
       01  SHOW-C              PIC 99.99.
       01  SHOW-D              PIC 999.9.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE 1234 TO A
           MOVE B TO SHOW-B
           MOVE C TO SHOW-C
           MOVE D TO SHOW-D
           DISPLAY "A=" A " B=" SHOW-B " C=" SHOW-C " D=" SHOW-D
           STOP RUN.
----

Compiled and run with `-std=ibm`, the output is:

[source,text]
----
A=1234 B=1.234 C=12.34 D=123.4
----

All four items hold the digits `1234`; only the implied decimal point differs, which is why the numeric-edited receivers
`SHOW-B`, `SHOW-C` and `SHOW-D` print `1.234`, `12.34` and `123.4`.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddered.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: REDEFINES clause]

=== Implicit redefinition under an FD

When more than one level-01 entry follows a file description (`FD`), each later record implicitly redefines the storage of
the first: the clause must not be written (Language Reference, _REDEFINES clause_). This is how a file with several
record layouts is declared, and it is why the program must test a record code before choosing a layout (the `LOAN-TYPE`
test here, or a record-type byte in a multi-record file). The example reads the Circulation `LOANTRAN` file and shows the
same record through the `LOANREC` layout and through a plain 30-character record.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. FD1.
      * Two level-01 records under one FD redefine each other
      * implicitly: no REDEFINES clause is written.
       ENVIRONMENT DIVISION.
       INPUT-OUTPUT SECTION.
       FILE-CONTROL.
           SELECT LOANTRAN ASSIGN TO "LOANTRAN"
               ORGANIZATION IS LINE SEQUENTIAL.
       DATA DIVISION.
       FILE SECTION.
       FD  LOANTRAN.
       COPY LOANREC.
       01  RAW-RECORD              PIC X(30).
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-EOF                  PIC X VALUE "N".
           88  AT-END              VALUE "Y".
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           OPEN INPUT LOANTRAN
           READ LOANTRAN
               AT END SET AT-END TO TRUE
           END-READ
           PERFORM UNTIL AT-END
               DISPLAY "raw  [" RAW-RECORD "]"
               DISPLAY "type " LOAN-TYPE " member " LOAN-MEMBER-ID
               READ LOANTRAN
                   AT END SET AT-END TO TRUE
               END-READ
           END-PERFORM
           CLOSE LOANTRAN
           STOP RUN.
----

The output is:

[source,text]
----
raw  [O00010000000120261020         ]
type O member 000100
raw  [O00010100000220261020         ]
type O member 000101
raw  [R00010000000120261020         ]
type R member 000100
raw  [O00010200000320261020         ]
type O member 000102
----

The file sections and the `SELECT` entry used here are explained in
xref:programming-languages/cobol/file-concepts-select-and-fd.adoc[File Concepts, SELECT and FD].

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddered.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: REDEFINES clause]

=== Redefining does not convert

`REDEFINES` never changes a bit. If the two descriptions use different usages, the program reads the same bytes as two
different values. The Language Reference gives the case of a two-digit `DISPLAY` item redefined as a binary item: adding
the first to a counter adds 8, and adding the second adds a number taken from the character bytes. The example repeats it
with neutral names and prints the bytes with `FUNCTION HEX-OF`.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. RD3.
      * Redefining changes the view, not the bits.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  B                   PIC 99 USAGE DISPLAY VALUE 8.
       01  C REDEFINES B       PIC S99 USAGE COMP-4.
       01  A                   PIC S9(5) USAGE COMP-5 VALUE 0.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           ADD B TO A
           DISPLAY "after ADD B: " A
           MOVE 0 TO A
           ADD C TO A
           DISPLAY "after ADD C: " A
           DISPLAY "bytes of B: " FUNCTION HEX-OF(B)
           DISPLAY "length of C: " LENGTH OF C
           STOP RUN.
----

With GnuCOBOL's default dialect the output is:

[source,text]
----
after ADD B: +0000000008
after ADD C: +0000000048
bytes of B: 3038
length of C: 1
----

Compiled with `-std=ibm` it is:

[source,text]
----
after ADD B: +0000000008
after ADD C: +0000012344
bytes of B: 3038
length of C: 2
----

The two runs differ because a `PIC S99 USAGE COMP-4` item is one byte long in GnuCOBOL's default dialect (the
`binary-size` option is `1-2-4-8`) and two bytes long in Enterprise COBOL and in the `-std=ibm` dialect. In the default
run `C` sees only the first byte, `0x30`, which is 48. In the `-std=ibm` run it sees `0x3038`, which is 12344. On z/OS the
two bytes of the value 8 are the EBCDIC digits `F0 F8`, and the same binary item is the value -3848, which is the figure
the Language Reference quotes. One program, three answers: the redefined area is the same, and the result depends on the
character code, the size of the binary item and the dialect. A redefinition that crosses usages is therefore a
portability decision, not a harmless alias (the rules for each usage are in
xref:programming-languages/cobol/usage-and-numeric-representation.adoc[Usage and Numeric Representation]).

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddered.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: REDEFINES clause considerations] and https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/doc/gnucobol.html[GnuCOBOL manual]

=== Moving between the two views

The Language Reference says the results are undefined if a redefining item is moved to the redefined item, or the redefined
item to the redefining item (`MOVE B TO C` when `B REDEFINES C`), because the sender and the receiver overlap. Move to
and from a separate work area instead. The `INITIALIZE` statement and the `VALUE` clause interact with redefinitions as
described in xref:programming-languages/cobol/value-initialize-and-condition-names.adoc[VALUE, INITIALIZE and Condition Names]:
a `VALUE` may appear on the original item (and under a redefined group), never on the redefining item.

=== Pitfalls, with the diagnostics

The next program breaks three of the rules deliberately: a `VALUE` on a redefining item, a redefined item that is a table,
and a `REDEFINES` that is not next to its object (`PAGE-OTHER` sits in between).

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. BAD1.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  FINE-AREA.
           05  FINE-NUM        PIC S9(5)V99.
           05  FINE-TEXT REDEFINES FINE-NUM PIC X(7) VALUE "0000000".
       01  SLOT-TABLE.
           05  SLOT            PIC X(4) OCCURS 5 TIMES.
           05  SLOT-ALL REDEFINES SLOT PIC X(20).
       01  PAGE-AREA.
           05  PAGE-NO         PIC 9(3).
           05  PAGE-HEAD REDEFINES PAGE-NO.
               10  PH-HUNDREDS PIC 9.
               10  PH-REST     PIC 99.
           05  PAGE-OTHER      PIC X(5).
           05  PAGE-LATE REDEFINES PAGE-NO PIC X(3).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           STOP RUN.
----

Deliberately wrong. `cobc -x -Wall` reports two warnings and one error:

[source,text]
----
bad1.cob:7: warning: initial VALUE clause ignored for REDEFINES item 'FINE-TEXT' [-Winitial-value]
    5 |        01  FINE-AREA.
    6 |            05  FINE-NUM        PIC S9(5)V99.
    7 >            05  FINE-TEXT REDEFINES FINE-NUM PIC X(7) VALUE "0000000".
    8 |        01  SLOT-TABLE.
    9 |            05  SLOT            PIC X(4) OCCURS 5 TIMES.
bad1.cob:10: warning: the original definition 'SLOT' should not have an OCCURS clause [-Wothers]
    8 |        01  SLOT-TABLE.
    9 |            05  SLOT            PIC X(4) OCCURS 5 TIMES.
   10 >            05  SLOT-ALL REDEFINES SLOT PIC X(20).
   11 |        01  PAGE-AREA.
   12 |            05  PAGE-NO         PIC 9(3).
bad1.cob:17: error: REDEFINES must follow the original definition
   15 |                10  PH-REST     PIC 99.
   16 |            05  PAGE-OTHER      PIC X(5).
   17 >            05  PAGE-LATE REDEFINES PAGE-NO PIC X(3).
   18 |        PROCEDURE DIVISION.
   19 |            STOP RUN.
----

The error is the one that stops the build; the two warnings are accepted in GnuCOBOL but are errors in Enterprise COBOL, so
a program that compiles here with them may not compile on z/OS. Treat every `-Wall` warning on a `REDEFINES` as a defect.

Size mismatch is the other trap. The same fragment of two sizes, compiled by default and with `-std=ibm`:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. BIG1.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  REC.
           05  A               PIC X(6).
           05  B REDEFINES A   PIC X(8).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           DISPLAY LENGTH OF REC
           STOP RUN.
----

Deliberately wrong for the default dialect. The default dialect rejects it:

[source,text]
----
big1.cob:7: error: larger REDEFINES used
    5 |        01  REC.
    6 |            05  A               PIC X(6).
    7 >            05  B REDEFINES A   PIC X(8).
    8 |        PROCEDURE DIVISION.
    9 |            DISPLAY LENGTH OF REC
big1.cob:7: note: size of 'B' larger than size of 'A' [-Wdialect]
----

and `-std=ibm` accepts it with this warning, after which the program runs and prints `8`, the length of the larger
redefining item, which is now the size of the whole record:

[source,text]
----
big1.cob:7: warning: larger REDEFINES used [-Wdialect]
    5 |        01  REC.
    6 |            05  A               PIC X(6).
    7 >            05  B REDEFINES A   PIC X(8).
    8 |        PROCEDURE DIVISION.
    9 |            DISPLAY LENGTH OF REC
big1.cob:7: note: size of 'B' larger than size of 'A' [-Wdialect]
----

The Language Reference allows it for a non-`EXTERNAL` item: the redefining item may use more storage than the redefined one
(its example is a six-byte `PIC X(6)` redefined by a national `PIC N(4)` of eight bytes). It is almost always a mistake in
a record layout, because the group is no longer the length the file or the copybook says it is.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddered.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: REDEFINES clause] and https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/HTML/gnucobpg.html#REDEFINES[GnuCOBOL Programmer's Guide: REDEFINES]

=== When not to use REDEFINES

Two modern alternatives avoid most of the risk:

* *Reference modification* (`LOAN-RECORD(14:4)`) reads a slice of a field without declaring a second layout. It has no
  name and no type, so it suits a one-off slice; a named `REDEFINES` suits a slice used in many places.
* A *separate receiving item* and a `MOVE` or `UNSTRING` makes a copy with a defined conversion. It costs a copy but
  has no overlap.

`REDEFINES` remains the right tool for multi-record layouts and for an alphanumeric view of a field that must be validated,
as in the examples. In new code, prefer a `TYPEDEF` (below) for a layout that is repeated, so the redefinition is written
once.

== RENAMES

The `RENAMES` clause, always written on a level-66 entry, gives a new name to one item or to a range of consecutive
elementary items in a record. The new item is an alternative, possibly overlapping, grouping; it occupies the same bytes
as the original items and has no storage of its own.

[source,text]
----
66  data-name-1  RENAMES  data-name-2  [ THROUGH | THRU  data-name-3 ]
----

The rules (Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference, _RENAMES clause_):

* Level-66 entries follow the last data description entry of the record, before the next `01` or `77`. A record may have
  several. They are written after the record they rename, and cannot rename a level-01, 77, 88 or another 66.
* `data-name-2` and `data-name-3` name elementary or group items of that record, without `OCCURS`; no item between them
  may have `OCCURS DEPENDING ON`. They may be qualified. They are different items.
* With `THRU`, the new item is an alphanumeric group running from the start of `data-name-2` to the end of `data-name-3`;
  `data-name-3` must not start or end before `data-name-2`, and may not be wholly inside it.
* Without `THRU`, the new item takes all the attributes of `data-name-2`: an elementary item stays elementary, with the
  same `PICTURE` and `USAGE`.
* `THROUGH` and `THRU` are equivalent. `data-name-1` can be qualified only by the level-01 name.
* A `TYPEDEF` item, or anything inside one, cannot be renamed, and a `RENAMES` entry cannot have the `TYPE`, `TYPEDEF` clauses.

The example gives the book record a key (`BOOK-ID` through `BOOK-ISBN`), a stock group, and a plain alias.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. RN1.
      * Level 66: alternative, overlapping groupings.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       COPY BOOKREC.
       66  BOOK-KEY       RENAMES BOOK-ID THRU BOOK-ISBN.
       66  BOOK-STOCK     RENAMES BOOK-COPIES THRU BOOK-PRICE.
       66  BOOK-ISBN-ALT  RENAMES BOOK-ISBN.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE 000001 TO BOOK-ID
           MOVE "9780000000017" TO BOOK-ISBN
           MOVE "A Small Guide to Rates" TO BOOK-TITLE
           MOVE 3 TO BOOK-COPIES
           MOVE 12.5 TO BOOK-PRICE
           DISPLAY "key    [" BOOK-KEY "]"
           DISPLAY "stock  [" BOOK-STOCK "]"
           DISPLAY "alt    [" BOOK-ISBN-ALT "]"
           DISPLAY "lengths " LENGTH OF BOOK-KEY " "

== SYNCHRONIZED

`SYNCHRONIZED` (abbreviated `SYNC`) asks the compiler to place a binary item on a natural storage boundary: two bytes
for a halfword, four for a word, eight for a doubleword. It is never required, and it can speed up arithmetic on some
hardware. It affects the *position* of the item, and therefore the layout of the record, never the item's length (Language
Reference, _SYNCHRONIZED clause_).

[source,text]
----
SYNCHRONIZED [ LEFT | RIGHT ]      SYNC is the short form
----

The rules:

* It may be written on an elementary item or on a level-01 group (every elementary item below is then synchronized). With
  `OCCURS`, each occurrence is synchronized. It cannot be combined with the `TYPE` clause.
* `LEFT` and `RIGHT` are syntax checked and do nothing, in Enterprise COBOL and in GnuCOBOL.
* For `DISPLAY`, `PACKED-DECIMAL`, `NATIONAL`, `UTF-8` and floating-point (external) items the clause is syntax checked and
  has no effect. `COMP-1` is aligned on a fullword, `COMP-2` on a doubleword, pointers and `INDEX` follow the `LP` option,
  and `COMP-3`, `COMP-4` and `COMP-5` are treated like `PACKED-DECIMAL` and `BINARY` respectively.
* In the `WORKING-STORAGE SECTION` each level-01 item starts on a doubleword boundary; in the `FILE SECTION` the compiler
  assumes level-01 records that contain synchronized items are doubleword aligned in the buffer.
* If the item is the first one under a `REDEFINES` item, the redefined area must already be properly aligned: the Language
  Reference's example is a four-byte item redefined by `PIC S9(9) BINARY SYNC`, which needs the four-byte item to start on a
  fullword.

=== Slack bytes

To put a synchronized item on its boundary the compiler inserts unused *slack bytes* in front of it (and, for a table of
groups, at the end of each occurrence so that every occurrence is aligned). The Language Reference gives the algorithm: add
up the bytes of all the elementary items before the binary item (including slack already added) and divide by `m`, which is 2
for a binary item of up to four digits, 4 for five digits or more, 4 for `COMP-1`, indexes and pointers, and 8 for `COMP-2`. If the
remainder `r` is not zero, `m - r` slack bytes are added. Slack bytes are included in the size of the enclosing group. A
file may also need *slack bytes between records* when blocked records contain synchronized items; the program supplies them
with a trailing `FILLER`. Enterprise COBOL's option `RULES(NOSLACKBYTES)` makes the compiler issue a warning wherever
`SYNC` forces slack bytes (the option is described in the Enterprise COBOL Programming Guide), which is a cheap way to find the records whose length is not
the sum of their fields.

The example reproduces the Language Reference record of three alphanumeric items (11 bytes) followed by three binary items. Without
`SYNC` the record is 19 bytes. With it, one slack byte goes before `B-1` and two before `B-2`, and the group is 22 bytes. The
hex dump shows the slack bytes, because `MOVE ALL "x"` fills the whole group, slack included, with `78`.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. SY1.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01 PLAIN-RECORD.
          05 A-1  PIC X(5).
          05 A-2  PIC X(3).
          05 A-3  PIC X(3).
          05 B-1  PIC S9999 USAGE COMP.
          05 B-2  PIC S99999 USAGE COMP.
          05 B-3  PIC S9999 USAGE COMP.
       01 SYNC-RECORD.
          05 A-1  PIC X(5).
          05 A-2  PIC X(3).
          05 A-3  PIC X(3).
          05 B-1  PIC S9999 USAGE COMP SYNCHRONIZED.
          05 B-2  PIC S99999 USAGE COMP SYNCHRONIZED.
          05 B-3  PIC S9999 USAGE COMP SYNCHRONIZED.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           DISPLAY "plain " LENGTH OF PLAIN-RECORD
           DISPLAY "sync  " LENGTH OF SYNC-RECORD
           MOVE ALL "x" TO SYNC-RECORD
           MOVE 1 TO B-1 OF SYNC-RECORD
           MOVE 2 TO B-2 OF SYNC-RECORD
           MOVE 3 TO B-3 OF SYNC-RECORD
           DISPLAY FUNCTION HEX-OF(SYNC-RECORD)
           STOP RUN.
----

The output is:

[source,text]
----

Reading the dump: eleven `78` bytes for the alphanumeric items, one `78` slack byte, `0001` for `B-1`, two `78` slack bytes, `00000002`
for `B-2` and `0003` for `B-3`. The sizes match the figures in the Language Reference (22 bytes).

A table of groups gets the end-of-occurrence slack bytes. Another Language Reference record, `WORK-RECORD`, with ten occurrences
of a group that holds two synchronized binary items, is 161 bytes and each occurrence is 16 bytes long (12 bytes of items, two
slack bytes before `COMP-PAY` and two at the end of the occurrence, which is what the manual's figures show):

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. SY2.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01 WORK-RECORD.
          05 WORK-CODE PIC X.
          05 COMP-TABLE OCCURS 10 TIMES.
             10 COMP-TYPE  PIC X.
             10 COMP-PAY   PIC S9(4)V99 COMP SYNC.
             10 COMP-HOURS PIC S9(3) COMP SYNC.
             10 COMP-NAME  PIC X(5).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           DISPLAY LENGTH OF WORK-RECORD " " LENGTH OF COMP-TABLE(1)
           MOVE ALL "x" TO WORK-RECORD
           MOVE 1 TO COMP-PAY(1)
           MOVE 2 TO COMP-HOURS(1)
           DISPLAY FUNCTION HEX-OF(WORK-RECORD(1:17))
           STOP RUN.
----

The output is:

[source,text]
----

7878787800000064000278787878787878
----

The 17 bytes shown are `WORK-CODE` (`78`), then the first occurrence: the type byte `78`, two slack bytes `7878`, `COMP-PAY` as `00000064` (the
value 1 with two implied decimals is stored as 100, hex `64`), `COMP-HOURS` as `0002`, and then the five bytes of `COMP-NAME` and the two end-of-occurrence slack bytes, all `78`. Both of these
examples give the same sizes as the manual, but GnuCOBOL does not follow the algorithm everywhere:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. SY3.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01 R1.
          05 A PIC X.
          05 B PIC S9(10) COMP SYNC.
       01 R2.
          05 A PIC X.
          05 B PIC S9(10) COMP-5 SYNC.
       01 R3.
          05 A PIC X.
          05 B PIC S9(4) COMP-3 SYNC.
       01 R4.
          05 A PIC X.
          05 B COMP-1 SYNC.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           DISPLAY LENGTH OF R1 " " LENGTH OF R2 " " LENGTH OF R3
               " " LENGTH OF R4
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
16 16 4 8
----

The four records are one `X` byte plus a synchronized item. A ten-digit binary item (eight bytes) is aligned on an
eight-byte boundary in GnuCOBOL (7 slack bytes, length 16, for both `COMP` and `COMP-5`), whereas the algorithm above, which uses `m = 4`
for items of five digits or more, would give 12 bytes (not run here, since the Language Reference algorithm is
for z/OS). A packed-decimal item is ignored (4 bytes) and `COMP-1` is aligned on a word (3 slack bytes, length 8), as in Enterprise
COBOL. Copybooks that mix `SYNC` with records written by other systems must therefore be checked on each compiler rather than
trusted.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlddesyn.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: SYNCHRONIZED clause] and https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/HTML/gnucobpg.html#SYNCRONIZED[GnuCOBOL Programmer's Guide: SYNCRONIZED]

=== When to use SYNC

For data that leaves the program, `SYNC` is a liability: records on files, in messages and in database columns have a fixed
byte layout, and slack bytes make that layout depend on the compiler. Leave it out of copybooks that describe external
records. Where it is used (in internal tables and work areas, to make binary arithmetic faster), measure first, because the
effect depends on the hardware and on options such as `TRUNC` and `ARCH`; the section's page on performance and compiler options returns to the subject, and the `TRUNC` options that matter more for binary items are described in
xref:programming-languages/cobol/usage-and-numeric-representation.adoc[Usage and Numeric Representation].

== TYPEDEF and TYPE

`TYPEDEF` creates a named description that can be reused with the `TYPE` clause, so that a layout such as a date or an amount is written
once. COBOL 2002 is the origin of the feature in the standard, and Enterprise COBOL for z/OS has had it since 6.5 (the
Language Reference documents it, and the PTF PH73209 of September 2026 added the `STRONG` form). GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 accepts
`TYPEDEF` and `TYPE` too, although its Programmer's Guide still lists `TYPEDEF` as a word that is not implemented (see
the table at the end of this section).

[source,text]
----
level-number  type-name  TYPEDEF [ STRONG ]  (description of the type)
level-number  data-name  TYPE [ TO ] type-name  [ OCCURS ... | VALUE ... ]
----

A type definition allocates no storage. An item defined with `TYPE type-name` takes the attributes of the type (the same
`PICTURE`, `USAGE`, group structure and subordinate names), and every item below a group type keeps its name.

=== Rules (Enterprise COBOL 6.5)

* A `TYPEDEF` may be an elementary item or a group. It may appear in the `WORKING-STORAGE`, `LOCAL-STORAGE`, `LINKAGE` or
  `FILE` sections, or in the optional `TYPE SECTION`, which comes first in the Data Division. In the `TYPE SECTION` every
  level-01 entry must have `TYPEDEF`.
* A type must be defined before it is used (no forward reference) and may not contain a `TYPE` clause that refers to itself,
  directly or indirectly. The scoping rules of type names are like those of data names.
* The `TYPE` clause cannot be used in the same entry as `PICTURE`, `USAGE`, `JUSTIFIED`, `BLANK WHEN ZERO`, `SIGN`, `SYNCHRONIZED`,
  `DYNAMIC` or `RENAMES`. It can be combined with `OCCURS`, `VALUE`, `GLOBAL`, `EXTERNAL` and `VOLATILE`. It can appear with `TYPEDEF`
  (to define a type from another type, for instance a table).
* The subject of a `TYPE` clause cannot be followed by subordinate entries. For an elementary type, level-88
  condition-names go in the type definition, not under the instances.
* A `VALUE` on the instance overrides the `VALUE` in the type at the same level. A `TYPEDEF` item cannot be redefined or
  renamed (items inside a group type can be redefined, and the redefinition becomes part of the type), cannot have `EXTERNAL`,
  `VOLATILE` or `RENAMES`, and the dependency object of an `OCCURS DEPENDING ON` in a group type cannot be inside the same type.
* A subordinate item can use `TYPE` with a level number as high as 49, as long as the whole item still has at most 49 levels.
* Enterprise COBOL adds qualification with `::` (for instance `INCOMING::PARTS(1)` or `WORK-AREA::NAME`) for items inside
  type instances and for index names; plain `OF` qualification still works.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/typedef.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: TYPEDEF clause] and https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/type.html[TYPE clause]

=== An example that runs on GnuCOBOL

The example defines an elementary amount type, a date group type and a status type with two condition-names, then uses
them in a loan row and in a table. It shows the instance `VALUE` clause, the 88-levels carried by the type and `OCCURS` on a
type instance. It compiles with no warnings by default.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. TY2.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01 MONEY-T TYPEDEF PIC S9(5)V99.
       01 DATE-T TYPEDEF.
          05 DT-YEAR  PIC 9(4).
          05 DT-MONTH PIC 99.
          05 DT-DAY   PIC 99.
       01 STATUS-T TYPEDEF PIC X.
          88 ST-ACTIVE VALUE "A".
          88 ST-GONE   VALUE "W".
       01 LOAN-ROW.
          05 LR-DUE   TYPE DATE-T.
          05 LR-FINE  TYPE MONEY-T VALUE 1.25.
          05 LR-ST    TYPE STATUS-T.
       01 DATE-LIST TYPE DATE-T OCCURS 3 TIMES.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE 20261105 TO LR-DUE
           MOVE 1 TO DT-MONTH OF DATE-LIST(2)
           SET ST-ACTIVE TO TRUE
           DISPLAY LR-DUE " " DT-YEAR OF LR-DUE " " LR-FINE " " LR-ST
           DISPLAY LENGTH OF LOAN-ROW " " LENGTH OF DATE-LIST
           STOP RUN.
----

The output is:

[source,text]

20261105 2026 +00001.25 A
16 8
----

`LOAN-ROW` is 16 bytes (8 for the date, 7 for the fine, 1 for the status). `LENGTH OF DATE-LIST` is 8, the length of one
table entry, because `LENGTH OF` a table name gives the length of a single occurrence; the table has three of them, 24
bytes in all. The fine was initialised by the instance `VALUE`, and `SET ST-ACTIVE
TO TRUE` set the 88-level that came from `STATUS-T`. The same source with `-std=ibm` also compiles, with the `DISPLAY` format difference seen
before.

=== STRONG types (Enterprise COBOL 6.5, PTF PH73209)

A type declared `TYPEDEF STRONG` is a *strong type*; its instances are *strongly-typed items*, and the compiler checks how they are
used. A type without `STRONG` is a *weak type* and behaves like the plain group it expands to. The rules, from the Language
Reference as documented for 6.5 (the strong form needs the PTF named above):

* `STRONG` is allowed only on a group type. The group, and every group below it, cannot have a `VALUE` clause or be a condition
  variable.
* Items inside a strong type cannot be redefined (implicitly or explicitly), renamed in whole or in part, or reference-modified
  (except elementary alphabetic, alphanumeric, UTF-8 and national items inside it).
* A strongly-typed group can be compared only with another group of the same type, and as a receiving item only in the
  contexts the manual lists: parameters and returning items, `INITIALIZE`, `JSON PARSE`, `MOVE`, `READ`, and the `FROM`
  phrase of `RELEASE`, `REWRITE` and `WRITE`, plus `RETURN`.
* In a `MOVE`, the sender must be an instance of the same type; for `WRITE`, `REWRITE` and `RELEASE` with `FROM`, the
  `FROM` item must be of the same type as the record.
* In a `CALL`, strongly-typed items can only be passed `BY CONTENT`.

last two statements are the compile-time errors the strong type exists to produce (the message numbers are not quoted

          05 DT-MONTH PIC 99.
          05 DT-DAY   PIC 99.
       01 D1 TYPE DATE-T.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE "20261105" TO D1
           STOP RUN.
----

Deliberately warns. The diagnostic is:

[source,text]
----
ty3.cob:5: warning: TYPEDEF STRONG is not implemented [-Wpending]
    3 |        DATA DIVISION.
    4 |        WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
    5 >        01 DATE-T TYPEDEF STRONG.
    6 |           05 DT-YEAR  PIC 9(4).
    7 |           05 DT-MONTH PIC 99.
----

The program compiles and runs; the `MOVE` of a literal into the strong group, which Enterprise COBOL would reject, is
accepted. A portable program must not rely on strong checking, and a program that does must be compiled on z/OS.

== Where the book is outdated

The book consulted for this section (Taulli, _Modern Mainframe Development_, 2022) introduces `REDEFINES` in its data-types chapter;
nothing is copied from it. Four points need correcting or updating when it is read today:

* Its `REDEFINES` sample carries a stray `PICTURE` keyword between the clause and the redefined name, and the size of the
  redefining item does not match the seven-character edited item it redefines. The correct form is `+REDEFINES data-name PICTURE ...+`
  with the keyword after the name, and a redefining item should have the length of the redefined item (or document why not).
* The book says binary items are not good for arithmetic because of rounding differences. Binary integers are exact. The real issues
  are truncation under the `TRUNC` option and decimal scaling, which also decide what a `REDEFINES` of a binary item sees, as the
  example above shows.
* `RENAMES`, `SYNCHRONIZED` and slack bytes are legacy layout tools that the book does not cover, and `TYPEDEF`/`TYPE`
  (Enterprise COBOL 6.5) and `STRONG` types (PTF PH73209) are newer than the book.
* Examples written for 2022-era compilers show `REDEFINES` as the way to build every table or variant record. With `TYPEDEF`,
  reference modification and `OCCURS` (in xref:programming-languages/cobol/tables-and-occurs.adoc[Tables and OCCURS]) it is only one of the
  tools.
