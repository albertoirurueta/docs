Business programs add, multiply and round money, and the whole point of COBOL's number model is that those operations are
exact in decimal. The data is stored as decimal digits (see
xref:programming-languages/cobol/usage-and-numeric-representation.adoc[USAGE and Numeric Representation]), a quantity such
as `24.90` is held as digits with an implied decimal point (see
xref:programming-languages/cobol/picture-clause.adoc[The PICTURE Clause]), and every arithmetic statement is defined in
terms of those decimal digits. This page covers the statements, the phrases they share, how big the temporary results are,
and where fixed-point decimal arithmetic stops and floating-point arithmetic starts.

== The arithmetic statements

COBOL has five arithmetic statements. `COMPUTE` evaluates a general arithmetic expression; the other four each perform a
single operation and are written in a form close to English. All five take the same optional phrases: `ROUNDED` after a
receiving item, and `ON SIZE ERROR` / `NOT ON SIZE ERROR` at the end, closed by the matching scope terminator
(`END-ADD`, `END-SUBTRACT`, `END-MULTIPLY`, `END-DIVIDE`, `END-COMPUTE`).

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Statement |Forms |Result
|`ADD`
|`ADD a b TO c d`; `ADD a b TO c GIVING d e`; `ADD CORRESPONDING g1 TO g2`
|All operands before `TO` are summed, and the sum is added to each receiver (format 1). With `GIVING` the sum of all the
operands, including the one after `TO`, is stored in each `GIVING` receiver, which takes no part in the sum.
|`SUBTRACT`
|`SUBTRACT a b FROM c d`; `SUBTRACT a b FROM c GIVING d`; `SUBTRACT CORRESPONDING g1 FROM g2`
|All operands before `FROM` are summed, and the sum is subtracted from each receiver, or from the single minuend with
`GIVING`.
|`MULTIPLY`
|`MULTIPLY a BY b c`; `MULTIPLY a BY b GIVING c d`
|`a` is multiplied by each receiver in turn (format 1) and the product replaces it, or the product of `a` and `b` is
stored in each `GIVING` receiver.
|`DIVIDE`
|`DIVIDE a INTO b c`; `DIVIDE a INTO b GIVING c`; `DIVIDE a BY b GIVING c`; `DIVIDE a INTO b GIVING c REMAINDER d`; `DIVIDE a BY b GIVING c REMAINDER d`
|`INTO` divides `a` into `b` (`b / a`), `BY` divides `a` by `b` (`a / b`). The five formats are those of the Language
Reference.
|`COMPUTE`
|`COMPUTE r1 r2 ROUNDED = expression`
|The value of the expression is stored in every receiver. The `=` can be written `EQUAL`.
|===

The operands of `ADD`, `SUBTRACT`, `MULTIPLY` and `DIVIDE` are elementary numeric items or numeric literals. A receiver after
`GIVING` (and the `REMAINDER` receiver) may also be a numeric-edited item, because it takes no part in the computation. The
receiver of `COMPUTE` may be numeric or numeric-edited. A group item is never a valid operand; it is only accepted by the
`CORRESPONDING` forms. The Enterprise COBOL Language Reference also states that if the operands of a statement share storage
(they overlap) the result is unpredictable.

The program below shows each statement once. The groups `BRANCH-A` and `BRANCH-B` have the same item names, which is what
`ADD CORRESPONDING` needs: it adds the elementary numeric items that have the same name in the two groups, and ignores the
rest (here `BRANCH-NAME`, which is alphanumeric).

Points worth noting in that run:

* `ADD WS-COPIES WS-OUT 10 GIVING WS-TOTAL-COPIES` is 7 + 1 + 10 = 18; with `GIVING`, `WS-COPIES` is not a receiver and stays 7.
* `SUBTRACT WS-PAID WS-OUT FROM WS-FINE GIVING WS-BALANCE` computes 12.50 - (10.00 + 1) = 1.50. Everything before `FROM` is
  added first, which is not the same as subtracting the operands one after the other from the minuend, although the
  result is the same here.
* `DIVIDE 4 INTO WS-VALUE` and `DIVIDE WS-VALUE BY 4` both give a quarter of `WS-VALUE`; only the position of the dividend
  differs.
* `DIVIDE 17 BY 7 GIVING WS-QUOTIENT REMAINDER WS-REMAINDER` gives 2 and 3. The quotient receiver is an integer, and the
  remainder is the dividend minus quotient times divisor. In formats 4 and 5 the Language Reference says that the quotient
  used to calculate the remainder is kept in an intermediate field and is truncated rather than rounded.
* The remainder phrase cannot be used with floating-point items in Enterprise COBOL. If the quotient overflows, neither the
  quotient nor the remainder is changed.

The Enterprise COBOL `ADD` and `SUBTRACT` formats can also name floating-point items and literals. `ADD CORRESPONDING` and
`SUBTRACT CORRESPONDING` do not apply to a pair of items when either one has a `REDEFINES` or `OCCURS` clause or is an
index, a pointer or `FILLER`. If one of the individual additions raises a size error, the `ON SIZE ERROR` imperative
statement runs only after all the other additions have been done.

When one statement is the same as another, prefer the shorter one: `ADD 1 TO COUNT-X` reads better than
`pass:c[COMPUTE COUNT-X = COUNT-X + 1]`, and the Programming Guide recommends `COMPUTE` for anything that combines several
operations, because one `COMPUTE` can replace a series of individual statements and can be more efficient.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/tasks/tpari34.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Programming Guide: Using COMPUTE and other arithmetic statements]

== COMPUTE and arithmetic expressions

An arithmetic expression is built from numeric literals, numeric data items and numeric intrinsic function references joined
by the operators in the table, with parentheses to group. The operators are evaluated in this order, and operators at the
same level go from left to right:

[cols="1,3,2",options="header"]
|===
|Order |Operator |Meaning
|1 |unary `pass:c[+]` and `-` |sign
|2 |`+**+` |exponentiation
|3 |`+*+` and `/` |multiplication and division
|4 |binary `pass:c[+]` and `-` |addition and subtraction
|===

Two consequences surprise programmers coming from other languages. A unary minus binds tighter than `+**+`, so
`pass:c[- A ** 2]` is `pass:c[(- A) ** 2]`, which is positive. And `+**+` is evaluated left to right, so
`pass:c[B ** C ** 2]` is `pass:c[(B ** C) ** 2]`, not `pass:c[B ** (C ** 2)]`. Operators must be surrounded by spaces:
a hyphen inside a name is part of the name (`WS-PRICE-WS-LIMIT` is one word, not a subtraction), as the diagnostics section
below shows. Write blanks around every `pass:c[+]`, `-`, `+*+`, `/` and `+**+`.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. COMPUTE1.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-A                 PIC S9(3)V99 VALUE 10.
       01  WS-B                 PIC S9(3)V99 VALUE 4.
       01  WS-C                 PIC S9(3)V99 VALUE 2.
       01  WS-D                 PIC S9(3)V99 VALUE 3.
       01  WS-R1                PIC S9(5)V99.
       01  WS-R2                PIC S9(5)V99.
       01  WS-P1                PIC S9(5).
       01  WS-P2                PIC S9(5).
       01  WS-P3                PIC S9(5)V9.
       01  WS-EDITED            PIC ZZ,ZZ9.99-.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = WS-A + WS-B * WS-C
           COMPUTE WS-R2 = (WS-A + WS-B) * WS-C
           DISPLAY "A + B * C   = " WS-R1
           DISPLAY "(A + B) * C = " WS-R2
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = WS-A - WS-B - WS-C
           DISPLAY "A - B - C   = " WS-R1
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = WS-A / WS-B / WS-C
           DISPLAY "A / B / C   = " WS-R1
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = - WS-A ** 2
           DISPLAY "- A ** 2    = " WS-R1
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = (- WS-A) ** 2
           DISPLAY "(- A) ** 2  = " WS-R1
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = WS-B ** WS-C ** 2
           DISPLAY "B ** C ** 2 = " WS-R1
           COMPUTE WS-P1 WS-P2 ROUNDED WS-P3 = WS-A / WS-B
           DISPLAY "A / B into 3 receivers = " WS-P1 " " WS-P2 " "
               WS-P3
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = WS-A + FUNCTION ABS (WS-C - WS-A)
           DISPLAY "A + ABS (C - A) = " WS-R1
           COMPUTE WS-EDITED = WS-C - WS-A * WS-D
           DISPLAY "edited receiver  [" WS-EDITED "]"
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
A + B * C   = +00018.00
(A + B) * C = +00028.00
A - B - C   = +00004.00
A / B / C   = +00001.25
- A ** 2    = +00100.00

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlpscomp.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: COMPUTE statement]; https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/tasks/tpari35.html[Programming Guide: Using arithmetic expressions]

The `COMPUTE WS-P1 WS-P2 ROUNDED WS-P3 = WS-A / WS-B` line shows that `ROUNDED` belongs to one receiver: 10 / 4 = 2.5 is
truncated to 2 in `WS-P1`, rounded to 3 in `WS-P2` and kept exactly in `WS-P3`. The edited receiver
`WS-EDITED` gets the value with the editing applied, which is how a report line is usually produced (see
xref:programming-languages/cobol/picture-clause.adoc[The PICTURE Clause]). Numeric intrinsic functions such as `FUNCTION ABS`
can appear in an expression; they are the subject of xref:programming-languages/cobol/intrinsic-functions.adoc[Intrinsic Functions].

With `cobc -std=ibm`, every multi-operand statement in this program produces the warning
`precision of result may change with arithmetic-osvs [-Warithmetic-osvs]`. That is the `arithmetic-osvs` dialect switch,
which GnuCOBOL turns on for IBM compatibility and which changes how intermediate results are sized (see the intermediate
results section). Use `-Wno-arithmetic-osvs` to silence it once you know what it means.

=== Exponentiation and its size errors

Exponentiation deserves its own rules. A fixed-point base raised to an integer power is computed by repeated
multiplication, a negative exponent divides one by the result, and an exponent with decimal places switches the whole
evaluation to floating point. Enterprise COBOL truncates a fixed-point exponent of more than nine significant digits to
nine. Three combinations are defined as size errors: zero raised to the power zero, zero raised to a negative power, and a
negative number raised to a fractional power. Enterprise COBOL's reaction depends on the `SIZE ERROR` phrase:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Expression |With `ON SIZE ERROR` |Without it
|zero raised to zero |the imperative statement runs |the result is 1 and a message is issued
|zero raised to a negative number |the imperative statement runs |the program is terminated abnormally
|negative number raised to a fractional power |the imperative statement runs |the absolute value of the base is used and a message is issued
|===

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlpdssiz.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: SIZE ERROR phrases]

GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 follows this for two of the three. With variables (a literal expression such as `+0 ** 0+` is folded by the
compiler, and the `ON SIZE ERROR` phrase was not executed in a test with literals), zero to the power zero and a negative
base to a fractional power both took the `ON SIZE ERROR` path, but zero to a negative power did not:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. EXPTEST.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-ZERO              PIC 9       VALUE 0.
       01  WS-EXP               PIC S9      VALUE -1.
       01  WS-NEG               PIC S9      VALUE -4.
       01  WS-HALF              PIC 9V9     VALUE 0.5.
       01  WS-R                 PIC S9(5)V99 VALUE 777.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           COMPUTE WS-R = WS-ZERO ** WS-ZERO
               ON SIZE ERROR DISPLAY "0**0 size error"
               NOT ON SIZE ERROR DISPLAY "0**0 ok " WS-R
           END-COMPUTE
           COMPUTE WS-R = WS-ZERO ** WS-EXP
               ON SIZE ERROR DISPLAY "0**-1 size error " WS-R
               NOT ON SIZE ERROR DISPLAY "0**-1 ok " WS-R
           END-COMPUTE
           COMPUTE WS-R = WS-NEG ** WS-HALF
               ON SIZE ERROR DISPLAY "(-4)**0.5 size error " WS-R
               NOT ON SIZE ERROR DISPLAY "(-4)**0.5 ok " WS-R
           END-COMPUTE
           COMPUTE WS-R = 2 ** 10
           DISPLAY "2**10 " WS-R
           COMPUTE WS-R = 2 ** -2
           DISPLAY "2**-2 " WS-R
           COMPUTE WS-R = 9 ** 0.5
           DISPLAY "9**0.5 " WS-R
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
0**0 size error
0**-1 ok +00000.00
(-4)**0.5 size error +00000.00
2**10 +01024.00
2**-2 +00000.25
9**0.5 +00003.00
----

Source: https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/HTML/gnucobpg.html[GnuCOBOL Programmer's Guide: ON SIZE ERROR]

The values on the last three lines are the ones to rely on: `+2 ** 10+` is 1024, `+2 ** -2+` is 0.25, and `+9 ** 0.5+` is the
square root 3. If portable behaviour for the three special cases matters, test the operands before the `COMPUTE`.

== ROUNDED and ROUNDED MODE

After the result of an operation has been aligned on the decimal point of the receiver, any extra low-order fraction digits
are discarded. That is truncation, and it is the default. With `ROUNDED` the receiver's last digit is increased by one
when the first discarded digit is five or more (in magnitude). That is _round half away from zero_, the rule most accountants
expect: 2.5 becomes 3 and -2.5 becomes -3.

Rounding happens before the size check, and Enterprise COBOL may carry one more integer and decimal place in the
intermediate result when `ROUNDED` is written, so that the carry is not lost. `ROUNDED` has no effect on a floating-point result, which
is always rounded to the nearest representable value, and in Enterprise COBOL it is not supported in `ARITH(EXTEND)` mode
for a receiver with 31 digits to the right of the decimal point (`PIC V31` or `PIC P(30)9`).

COBOL 2014 generalised the phrase to `ROUNDED MODE IS mode`, with eight modes. The GnuCOBOL manual defines the absence of
`ROUNDED` as `TRUNCATION` and a bare `ROUNDED` as `NEAREST-AWAY-FROM-ZERO`. GnuCOBOL 3.2 implements the modes, Enterprise
COBOL 6.5 does not: its Language Reference documents only the bare `ROUNDED` phrase with the half-away-from-zero rule.

[cols="2,5,2",options="header"]
|===
|Mode |Rule |2.5 and -2.5 to an integer
|`AWAY-FROM-ZERO` |Any discarded fraction rounds to the value of larger magnitude. |3 and -3
|`NEAREST-AWAY-FROM-ZERO` |Nearest value; a tie goes to the larger magnitude. The default for a bare `ROUNDED`. |3 and -3
|`NEAREST-EVEN` |Nearest value; a tie goes to the value whose last digit is even ("banker's rounding"). |2 and -2
|`NEAREST-TOWARD-ZERO` |Nearest value; a tie goes to the smaller magnitude. |2 and -2
|`PROHIBITED` |No rounding. If the value is not exact the `EC-SIZE-TRUNCATION` condition is set and the result is undefined. |undefined
|`TOWARD-GREATER` |Toward the nearest algebraically larger value. |3 and -2
|`TOWARD-LESSER` |Toward the nearest algebraically smaller value. |2 and -3
|`TRUNCATION` |Discard the fraction; the behaviour without `ROUNDED`. |2 and -2
|===

The program rounds seven values to an integer in each mode. The columns are, in order: the source, the statement without
`ROUNDED`, and then `AWAY-FROM-ZERO`, a bare `ROUNDED` (`NEAREST-AWAY-FROM-ZERO`), `NEAREST-EVEN`, `NEAREST-TOWARD-ZERO`,
`TOWARD-GREATER`, `TOWARD-LESSER` and `TRUNCATION`.

[source,cobol]
----

       DATA DIVISION.

       01  WS-SRC               PIC S9V999.
       01  WS-R-PLAIN           PIC S9.
       01  WS-R-AWAY            PIC S9.
       01  WS-R-NAFZ            PIC S9.
       01  WS-R-EVEN            PIC S9.
       01  WS-R-NTZ             PIC S9.
       01  WS-R-GREATER         PIC S9.
       01  WS-R-LESSER          PIC S9.
       01  WS-R-TRUNC           PIC S9.
       01  WS-SHOW              PIC -9.999.
       01  WS-I                 PIC 9.
       01  WS-VALUES.
           05  FILLER           PIC S9V999 VALUE +3.510.
           05  FILLER           PIC S9V999 VALUE +2.500.
           05  FILLER           PIC S9V999 VALUE +3.500.
           05  FILLER           PIC S9V999 VALUE +2.499.
           05  FILLER           PIC S9V999 VALUE -2.500.

                   = WS-SRC
               COMPUTE WS-R-NTZ ROUNDED MODE IS NEAREST-TOWARD-ZERO
                   = WS-SRC
               COMPUTE WS-R-GREATER ROUNDED MODE IS TOWARD-GREATER
                   = WS-SRC
               COMPUTE WS-R-LESSER ROUNDED MODE IS TOWARD-LESSER
                   = WS-SRC
               COMPUTE WS-R-TRUNC ROUNDED MODE IS TRUNCATION

Rows 2 and 3 are the ties, 2.500 and 3.500; reading across them shows the difference between the nearest modes. `NEAREST-EVEN`
sends both to the even integer (2 and 4), which is why it is preferred where thousands of roundings are summed: the errors
cancel instead of drifting upwards. `PROHIBITED` is not in the run because its result is undefined.

The default mode for a whole program can be changed in the `OPTIONS` paragraph of the `IDENTIFICATION DIVISION`, which
GnuCOBOL 3.2 supports with a `DEFAULT ROUNDED MODE IS` clause. It then applies to every `ROUNDED` phrase without a `MODE`:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. OPTS.
       OPTIONS.
           DEFAULT ROUNDED MODE IS NEAREST-EVEN.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-A                 PIC S9V9 VALUE +2.5.
       01  WS-B                 PIC S9.
       01  WS-C                 PIC S9.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           COMPUTE WS-B ROUNDED = WS-A
           COMPUTE WS-C = WS-A
           DISPLAY "ROUNDED default mode: " WS-B
           DISPLAY "no ROUNDED:           " WS-C
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
ROUNDED default mode: +2
no ROUNDED:           +2
----

Source: https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/HTML/gnucobpg.html[GnuCOBOL Programmer's Guide: IDENTIFICATION DIVISION (OPTIONS)]

The bare `ROUNDED` gave 2 because the default mode is now `NEAREST-EVEN`; the statement without `ROUNDED` truncates to 2 as
well. Two cautions from the same test. The manual's syntax diagram for `ROUNDED MODE` spells the modes `NEAREST-TOWARD-ZERO`
and `TOWARD-GREATER`, but its `OPTIONS` diagram spells them `NEAREST-TOWARDS-ZERO` and `TOWARDS-GREATER`; GnuCOBOL 3.2.0
accepted the first spelling and rejected the second in the `OPTIONS` paragraph. And the mode names are a dialect
matter: the same `rounded.cob` compiles and gives the same numbers with `-std=ibm`, but `-std=ibm-strict` rejects
`ROUNDED MODE` with `syntax error, unexpected Identifier`, which is the honest answer for Enterprise COBOL 6.5.

If you need a mode other than half-away-from-zero on z/OS, say banker's rounding, there are two ways. The first is to add
`ROUNDED` to a `COMPUTE` that carries enough decimals and test the dropped digits yourself. The second is the intrinsic
function route: the `FUNCTION INTEGER-PART`, `FUNCTION INTEGER` (greatest integer not greater than its argument) and
`FUNCTION REM` functions give the pieces of a hand-written rounding rule (see
xref:programming-languages/cobol/intrinsic-functions.adoc[Intrinsic Functions]). Which is easier depends on the case; what
you should not do is round by moving the value to a shorter field, since `MOVE` truncates.

== ON SIZE ERROR and NOT ON SIZE ERROR

A _size error_ happens when the absolute value of the result, after decimal point alignment, does not fit in the receiver,
and when a division by zero is attempted. Exponentiation adds the three cases listed above. The phrase applies to the final
result only, never to an intermediate result.

What happens depends on whether the phrase is written:

* Without `ON SIZE ERROR`, the result is truncated on the left by the usual rules for storing into a smaller field. The
  arithmetic statement does not complain. The Language Reference only says that "truncation rules apply" for this case.
* With `ON SIZE ERROR`, the receiver that overflowed is left unchanged, the imperative statement runs, and control passes
  to the end of the arithmetic statement; a `NOT ON SIZE ERROR` phrase is ignored. If there is no size error the
  `NOT ON SIZE ERROR` imperative statement runs instead.
* With several receivers, only the receivers that overflow are left alone; the others are stored, and the phrase runs once
  after all of them have been processed.
* With `ROUNDED`, rounding happens first, so a result such as 99.6 that rounds up to 100 is a size error for a `PIC 99` receiver.
* For `+DIVIDE ... REMAINDER+`, a size error in the quotient leaves both the quotient and the remainder unchanged, and a size
  error in the remainder leaves the remainder unchanged; you must test which happened.
* For a binary receiver (`BINARY`, `COMP`, `COMP-4`, `COMP-5`), the largest value the item can hold is the one implied by the
  decimal `PICTURE`, regardless of the `TRUNC` compiler option. `TRUNC` governs only what happens when no size-error
  phrase is present; see xref:programming-languages/cobol/usage-and-numeric-representation.adoc[USAGE and Numeric Representation].

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. SIZEERR.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-COPIES            PIC 9(3)    VALUE 995.
       01  WS-SMALL             PIC 99.
       01  WS-PAIR-A            PIC 99      VALUE 90.
       01  WS-PAIR-B            PIC 999     VALUE 90.
       01  WS-ZERO              PIC 9       VALUE 0.
       01  WS-QUOTIENT          PIC 9(3)    VALUE 777.
       01  WS-REMAINDER         PIC 9(3)    VALUE 888.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
      *    No ON SIZE ERROR: the high-order digits are lost
           ADD 10 TO WS-COPIES
           DISPLAY "1 no phrase, 995 + 10 in PIC 9(3):   " WS-COPIES
      *    ON SIZE ERROR: the receiving field is left alone
           MOVE 995 TO WS-COPIES
           ADD 10 TO WS-COPIES
               ON SIZE ERROR
                   DISPLAY "2 size error, field unchanged:       "
                       WS-COPIES
               NOT ON SIZE ERROR
                   DISPLAY "2 ok"
           END-ADD
      *    ROUNDED happens before the size check
           COMPUTE WS-SMALL ROUNDED = 99.6
               ON SIZE ERROR
                   DISPLAY "3 99.6 rounded to 100 does not fit PIC 99"
           END-COMPUTE
      *    A size error on one receiver leaves only that receiver alone
           ADD 15 TO WS-PAIR-A WS-PAIR-B
               ON SIZE ERROR
                   DISPLAY "4 PAIR-A " WS-PAIR-A " PAIR-B " WS-PAIR-B
           END-ADD
      *    Division by zero is a size error
           DIVIDE 10 BY WS-ZERO GIVING WS-QUOTIENT
               REMAINDER WS-REMAINDER
               ON SIZE ERROR
                   DISPLAY "5 divide by zero, quotient " WS-QUOTIENT
                       " remainder " WS-REMAINDER
           END-DIVIDE
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
1 no phrase, 995 + 10 in PIC 9(3):   005
2 size error, field unchanged:       995
3 99.6 rounded to 100 does not fit PIC 99
4 PAIR-A 90 PAIR-B 105
5 divide by zero, quotient 777 remainder 888
----

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlpdssiz.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: SIZE ERROR phrases]; https://gnucobol.sourceforge.io/HTML/gnucobpg.html[GnuCOBOL Programmer's Guide: ON SIZE ERROR + NOT ON SIZE ERROR]

Line 1 is the dangerous one: 995 + 10 = 1005 does not fit in three digits, nothing is reported, and the stored value is 005.
Line 4 shows that the two receivers of one `ADD` are treated separately in GnuCOBOL: `WS-PAIR-A` (`PIC 99`, 90 + 15 = 105)
is left at 90 while `WS-PAIR-B` (`PIC 999`) becomes 105. Line 5 is the division by zero: neither the quotient (777) nor the
remainder (888) is touched.

On z/OS, `ON SIZE ERROR` for fixed-point overflow and decimal overflow works only when the Language Environment run-time
option `TRAP(ON)` is in effect. Floating-point exponent overflow does not cause a size error: it
abends, and the way to intercept it is a user-written condition handler. The Programming Guide's example handles a division
by zero in a paragraph with `ON SIZE ERROR`, displays the operands and stops the run.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/tasks/tperr05.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Programming Guide: Handling errors in arithmetic operations]

What about a division by zero with no `ON SIZE ERROR` phrase at all? The GnuCOBOL manual says the run time prints an error and
aborts the program. GnuCOBOL 3.2.0 did not do that in a test with a `DIVIDE` of 120 by a zero field: the program carried on, the
receiver kept its old value and the exit status was 0, with or without `-debug`:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. DIVZERO.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-TOTAL             PIC 9(5)V99 VALUE 120.
       01  WS-COUNT             PIC 9(3)    VALUE 0.
       01  WS-AVERAGE           PIC 9(5)V99 VALUE 7.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           DIVIDE WS-TOTAL BY WS-COUNT GIVING WS-AVERAGE
           DISPLAY "average " WS-AVERAGE
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
average 00007.00
----

The lesson is the same on every platform: do not rely on a missing phrase to stop the program. Either test the divisor, or
write `ON SIZE ERROR` and decide there what the program does.

== Intermediate results and precision

A `COMPUTE` with several operators is evaluated as a series of two-operand steps, in precedence order, each storing its
result in a compiler-generated temporary field called an _intermediate result_. For `pass:c[COMPUTE Y = A + B * C - D / E + F ** G]`
Enterprise COBOL exponentiates `F` by `G`, multiplies `B` by `C`, divides `D` by `E`, then adds, subtracts and adds in turn.
Intermediate results also arise in `ADD` or `SUBTRACT` with several operands, in a statement with `GIVING` and several
receivers, in arithmetic inside a condition, and when `ROUNDED` is written.

How many digits a temporary field has is the compiler's decision, and it is the source of most unexpected results. In
Enterprise COBOL, for fixed-point data, the Programming Guide gives the theoretically possible size of each operation:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Operation |Integer places |Decimal places
|`pass:c[+]` or `-` |larger of the operands' integer places, plus 1 |larger of the operands' decimal places
|`+*+` |i1 + i2 |d1 + d2
|`/` |i2 + d1 |larger of (d2 - d1) and _dmax_
|===

_dmax_ is the largest number of decimal places among the receivers and the operands (excluding divisors and exponents). The
compiler never carries more than 30 digits in `ARITH(COMPAT)`, the default, or 31 in `ARITH(EXTEND)`. When a result needs
more, the compiler keeps the decimal places down to _dmax_ and lets the integer part use the remaining digits, and if even that
is too much the extra digits are truncated and a warning is issued (at run time, a message and the program goes on). The
lesson is to declare operands and receivers with enough decimal places for the accuracy you want. The classic trap is
division followed by multiplication.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. INTERM.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-A                 PIC 9(3) VALUE 10.
       01  WS-B                 PIC 9(3) VALUE 3.
       01  WS-C                 PIC 9(3) VALUE 3.
       01  WS-R1                PIC 9(3)V99.
       01  WS-R2                PIC 9(3)V99.
       01  WS-R3                PIC 9(3)V99.
       01  WS-R4                PIC 9(3).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           COMPUTE WS-R1 = WS-A / WS-B * WS-C
           COMPUTE WS-R2 = WS-A * WS-C / WS-B
           COMPUTE WS-R3 = (WS-A / WS-B) * WS-C
           COMPUTE WS-R4 ROUNDED = WS-A / WS-B * WS-C
           DISPLAY "A / B * C   = " WS-R1
           DISPLAY "A * C / B   = " WS-R2
           DISPLAY "(A / B) * C = " WS-R3
           DISPLAY "rounded     = " WS-R4
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
A / B * C   = 009.99
A * C / B   = 010.00
(A / B) * C = 009.99
rounded     = 010
----

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/concepts/cpinr02.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Programming Guide: Intermediate results and arithmetic precision]; https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/ref/rpinr04.html[Fixed-point data and intermediate results]

`+A / B * C+` divides first and stores 3.33 (two decimals, the receiver's), so the product is 9.99, not 10.00. Doing the
multiplication first, `+A * C / B+`, or changing the order of the operands, gives exactly 10.00. The parenthesised form is the
same as the first because the divide is already first. With a plain `ROUNDED` and an integer receiver, the default
GnuCOBOL arithmetic rounds the 9.99 to 10; a compiler that follows the Enterprise COBOL rules to the letter carries
no decimals at all when every operand and the receiver are integers, so the division gives 3 and the product 9. That is
what `-std=ibm` (which enables the `arithmetic-osvs` rules) printed for the same program:

[source,text]
----
A / B * C   = 00999
A * C / B   = 01000
(A / B) * C = 00999
rounded     = 009
----

Under `-std=ibm` `DISPLAY` shows implied-decimal fields without the decimal point, so `00999` is 9.99 and `01000` is 10.00,
while `009` is the integer 9. The compile also emitted four `-Warithmetic-osvs` warnings, one per `COMPUTE`. So the same
source gave 10 in the default dialect and 9 in the IBM-like dialect: for a program that must match the mainframe to the
penny, compile the test build with `-std=ibm`, write the multiplication before the division, and give the receiver the
decimals you need.

Two other rules from the Appendix are worth knowing. If a binary operation needs an intermediate result of more than
18 digits, the compiler converts the operands to internal decimal, performs the operation, and converts a binary result
back. And "the composite of operands" of an `ADD` or `SUBTRACT` (all the operands aligned on the decimal point and
superimposed) must not exceed 30 digits in `ARITH(COMPAT)` or 31 in `ARITH(EXTEND)`; `MULTIPLY` and `DIVIDE` apply the limit to the
receivers, and `COMPUTE` has no such limit. For example, `ADD A B TO C` with `A` as `9(7)V9(5)`, `B` as `9(11)V99` and `C`
as `9(12)V9(3)` has a composite of `9(12)V9(5)`, 17 digits.

== ARITH(COMPAT) and ARITH(EXTEND)

`ARITH` is an Enterprise COBOL compiler option (z/OS; not run here). With the default `ARITH(COMPAT)` the maximum size of a
`PICTURE` for packed-decimal, external-decimal and numeric-edited items is 18 digits, fixed-point literals can have 18
digits, `NUMVAL` and its relatives take 18-digit arguments, the maximum argument of `FACTORIAL` is 28, and fixed-point
intermediate results use at most 30 digits. With `ARITH(EXTEND)` all of those limits go up to 31 (29 for `FACTORIAL`), the
intermediate results use up to 31 digits, and floating-point intrinsic functions return extended-precision (128-bit)
results, and expressions with floating-point operands are evaluated in extended-precision floating point. `ARITH(EXTEND)`
costs run time and is chosen per program, either on the `CBL` or `PROCESS` statement or in the compile procedure:

[source,cobol]
----
       CBL ARITH(EXTEND)
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. BIGSUM.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-CHAR              PIC X(30)
                                VALUE "123456789012345678901234567890".
       01  WS-NUM               PIC 9(30).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE WS-CHAR TO WS-NUM
           DISPLAY WS-NUM
           GOBACK.
----

z/OS; not run here. With `ARITH(COMPAT)` the `PIC 9(30)` item is a syntax error, because 30 digits is more than the 18 allowed.

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/ui/up0012.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Programming Guide: ARITH]

GnuCOBOL has no `ARITH` option and needs none. Numeric items can have up to 38 digits (binary items up to 18 digits by default),
and in these tests the arithmetic behaved as if the equivalent of `ARITH(EXTEND)` were always on:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. WIDE.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-BASE              PIC 9(2)  VALUE 2.
       01  WS-POWER             PIC 9(3)  VALUE 100.
       01  WS-BIG               PIC 9(31).
       01  WS-MILLS             PIC 9(27)V9(4)
                                VALUE 1234567890123456789.1234.
       01  WS-DOUBLED           PIC 9(27)V9(4).
       01  WS-HUGE              PIC 9(38).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           COMPUTE WS-BIG = WS-BASE ** WS-POWER
           DISPLAY "2 ** 100       = " WS-BIG
           COMPUTE WS-DOUBLED = WS-MILLS * 2
           DISPLAY "wide fraction  = " WS-DOUBLED
           MOVE ALL "9" TO WS-HUGE
           ADD 1 TO WS-HUGE
               ON SIZE ERROR DISPLAY "38 digits is the limit"
           END-ADD
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
2 ** 100       = 1267650600228229401496703205376
wide fraction  = 000000002469135780246913578.2468

----

`+2 ** 100+` has 31 digits and fits a `PIC 9(31)`. The last two lines show a 31-digit item with four decimals and the first
size error of a 38-digit item. The same program also compiles with `-std=ibm` and `-std=ibm-strict`, which is a reminder that
GnuCOBOL's range is wider than Enterprise COBOL's even in the IBM dialects: code that depends on GnuCOBOL accepting a
`PIC 9(31)` will not compile on z/OS without `ARITH(EXTEND)`, and anything over 31 digits will not compile there at all.

== Fixed-point decimal versus floating point

Enterprise COBOL decides per operation whether to use fixed-point or floating-point arithmetic. An operation is
floating-point when an operand or the result is floating point (a floating-point literal or a `COMP-1`, `COMP-2` or
external floating-point item), or when an exponent has decimal places, or when an intrinsic function that returns floating
point is involved. Otherwise it is fixed-point: all operands and the result are fixed-point, no exponent has decimal places,
and nested expressions and function references are fixed-point as well. Comparisons are decided the same way, over both
comparands together. For example `pass:c[COMPUTE X = (N ** .5) + 1]` is a floating-point evaluation because of the fractional
exponent, while `ADD A TO B GIVING C` is a fixed-point one. In a floating-point evaluation `ROUNDED` is ignored.

Binary floating point cannot represent most decimal fractions exactly, which is why money is not kept in `COMP-1` or
`COMP-2`. The program adds a dime a thousand times, once into a double-precision floating-point item and once into packed
decimal:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. DECFLOAT.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-FLOAT-TOTAL       COMP-2 VALUE 0.
       01  WS-FLOAT-DIME        COMP-2 VALUE 0.1.
       01  WS-FIXED-TOTAL       PIC S9(5)V99 COMP-3 VALUE 0.
       01  WS-FIXED-DIME        PIC S9V99 COMP-3 VALUE 0.10.
       01  WS-I                 PIC 9(4) COMP.
       01  WS-SHOW              PIC -(5)9.9(16).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           PERFORM VARYING WS-I FROM 1 BY 1 UNTIL WS-I > 1000
               ADD WS-FLOAT-DIME TO WS-FLOAT-TOTAL
               ADD WS-FIXED-DIME TO WS-FIXED-TOTAL
           END-PERFORM
           MOVE WS-FLOAT-TOTAL TO WS-SHOW
           DISPLAY "COMP-2 total:  " WS-SHOW
           DISPLAY "COMP-2 raw:    " WS-FLOAT-TOTAL
           DISPLAY "COMP-3 total:  " WS-FIXED-TOTAL
           IF WS-FIXED-TOTAL = 100
               DISPLAY "fixed-point total is exactly 100"
           END-IF
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
COMP-2 total:      99.9999999999957367
COMP-2 raw:    99.99999999999574
COMP-3 total:  +00100.00
fixed-point total is exactly 100
----

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/concepts/cpari45.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Programming Guide: Fixed-point contrasted with floating-point arithmetic]

The floating-point total is 99.99999999999574 instead of 100. (The exact digits depend on the platform: GnuCOBOL does its
`COMP-2` arithmetic through a decimal intermediate, and on z/OS the same loop would be done with the hardware's own
floating-point format, so the error is of the same kind but not the same digits.) The packed-decimal total is exactly
100.00, and the `IF` is true. Comparing floating-point values with `=` is therefore a bug waiting to happen. If you must use
floating point, for example for a scientific calculation, compare with a tolerance, and move the final result to a
fixed-point field with `+COMPUTE ... ROUNDED+` before it is stored or printed. The Programming Guide also suggests floating
point to avoid truncation of intermediate results that exceed 30 digits, which is one of the few reasons to prefer it.

=== Decimal arithmetic in other languages

The same issue exists in every language with binary floating point, and the cure is the same: use a decimal type. Java's
`BigDecimal` does what COBOL's `PIC 9(n)V99` and `ROUNDED` do, with the rounding mode and scale explicit in the code; its
`RoundingMode.HALF_UP` corresponds to the bare `ROUNDED` and `HALF_EVEN` to `NEAREST-EVEN`. The page
xref:programming-languages/java/numbers-and-math.adoc[Numbers and Math] covers `BigDecimal`, `MathContext` and the
`new BigDecimal(double)` trap. Python's `decimal.Decimal` is the same idea; the page
xref:programming-languages/python/numbers.adoc[Numbers] covers it. In Python (3.9, run here), the same experiment as above is:

[source,python]
----
from decimal import Decimal, ROUND_HALF_EVEN, ROUND_HALF_UP

print(sum([0.1] * 1000))                       # float
print(sum([Decimal("0.10")] * 1000))           # decimal
print(Decimal("2.5").quantize(Decimal("1"), rounding=ROUND_HALF_EVEN),
      Decimal("2.5").quantize(Decimal("1"), rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP))
----

[source,text]
----
99.9999999999986
100.00
2 3
----

Source: https://docs.python.org/3/library/decimal.html[Python documentation: decimal]

The difference is that in COBOL the decimal type is the ordinary numeric data item, the scale is part of its declaration,
and all arithmetic on it is decimal by default; the other languages ask the programmer to select the type.

== The Circulation fine calculation

The nightly run assesses fines for late returns. The rules of this scenario are: a late return costs `0.35` per day; a fine
for one return is capped at `5.00`; members who joined before 2021 get a loyalty reduction of 12.5 percent; and the amount is
rounded to the cent and added to the member's fine total in `MEMBER-FINES`. The member records come from `MEMBMAST` through the
copybook `MEMBREC`; the days late would normally come from the loan file, and are set in the program here so that the
example is self-contained.

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. FINES.
       ENVIRONMENT DIVISION.
       INPUT-OUTPUT SECTION.
       FILE-CONTROL.
           SELECT MEMBER-FILE ASSIGN TO "MEMBMAST"
               ORGANIZATION IS LINE SEQUENTIAL.
       DATA DIVISION.
       FILE SECTION.
       FD  MEMBER-FILE.
           COPY MEMBREC.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-EOF               PIC X       VALUE "N".
           88  AT-END                       VALUE "Y".
       01  WS-DAILY-RATE        PIC 9V99    VALUE 0.35.
       01  WS-MAX-FINE          PIC 9(3)V99 VALUE 5.00.
       01  WS-LOYALTY-RATE      PIC V999    VALUE .125.
       01  WS-DAYS-LATE         PIC 9(3).
       01  WS-FINE              PIC 9(3)V99.
       01  WS-SHOW-BEFORE       PIC -(5)9.99.
       01  WS-SHOW-FINE         PIC ZZ9.99.
       01  WS-SHOW-AFTER        PIC -(5)9.99.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           OPEN INPUT MEMBER-FILE
           PERFORM UNTIL AT-END
               READ MEMBER-FILE
                   AT END SET AT-END TO TRUE
                   NOT AT END PERFORM ASSESS-FINE
               END-READ
           END-PERFORM
           CLOSE MEMBER-FILE
           STOP RUN.
       ASSESS-FINE.
           EVALUATE MEMBER-ID
               WHEN 100 MOVE 3  TO WS-DAYS-LATE
               WHEN 101 MOVE 20 TO WS-DAYS-LATE
               WHEN OTHER MOVE 7 TO WS-DAYS-LATE
           END-EVALUATE
           MOVE MEMBER-FINES TO WS-SHOW-BEFORE
           IF MEMBER-JOINED < 20210101
               COMPUTE WS-FINE ROUNDED =
                   WS-DAYS-LATE * WS-DAILY-RATE * (1 - WS-LOYALTY-RATE)
                   ON SIZE ERROR MOVE WS-MAX-FINE TO WS-FINE
               END-COMPUTE
           ELSE
               COMPUTE WS-FINE ROUNDED = WS-DAYS-LATE * WS-DAILY-RATE
                   ON SIZE ERROR MOVE WS-MAX-FINE TO WS-FINE
               END-COMPUTE
           END-IF
           IF WS-FINE > WS-MAX-FINE
               MOVE WS-MAX-FINE TO WS-FINE
           END-IF
           ADD WS-FINE TO MEMBER-FINES
               ON SIZE ERROR
                   DISPLAY MEMBER-ID " fine total does not fit"
           END-ADD
           MOVE WS-FINE TO WS-SHOW-FINE
           MOVE MEMBER-FINES TO WS-SHOW-AFTER
           DISPLAY MEMBER-ID " " MEMBER-NAME " days " WS-DAYS-LATE
               " before " WS-SHOW-BEFORE " fine " WS-SHOW-FINE
               " after " WS-SHOW-AFTER.
----

[source,text]
----
000100 ADA LOVELACE                   days 003 before      0.00 fine   0.92 after      0.92
000101 GRACE HOPPER                   days 020 before     12.50 fine   5.00 after     17.50
000102 ALAN TURING                    days 007 before     50.00 fine   2.14 after     52.14
----

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlpscomp.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Language Reference: COMPUTE statement]

Several decisions in the program are worth reading across. The rate, cap and loyalty percentage are items with a `VALUE`,
not literals scattered through the procedure code. The loyalty reduction is a fraction with its own `PICTURE`
(`V999`), so the expression `1 - WS-LOYALTY-RATE` is exact in decimal. The fine is computed in one `COMPUTE ROUNDED`, so the
only rounding is the final one: Ada's three days at 0.35 with the 12.5 percent reduction is 0.91875, rounded once to 0.92. The cap is an `IF` after the
computation; Grace's 20 days would be 7.00. The `+ADD ... ON SIZE ERROR+` protects the master record, since a member total that would
not fit in `S9(5)V99` is reported instead of truncated. All the other statements use a `PERFORM` and `EVALUATE` that are described in
xref:programming-languages/cobol/perform-and-structured-programming.adoc[PERFORM and Structured Programming] and
xref:programming-languages/cobol/conditions-if-and-evaluate.adoc[Conditions, IF and EVALUATE].

Under `-std=ibm` the program prints the same lines, preceded by the `-Warithmetic-osvs` warnings.

== Pitfalls and real diagnostics

The deliberately wrong program below collects the compile-time errors that new COBOL programmers meet most often:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. BAD.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-PRICE             PIC 9(4)V99 VALUE 24.90.
       01  WS-TAX               PIC 9(4)V99.

       01  WS-GROUP.

       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           COMPUTE WS-TAX = WS-PRICE-WS-LIMIT
           ADD WS-SHOWN TO WS-TAX
           COMPUTE WS-TAX = WS-PRICE + WS-NAME
           ADD 1 TO 5
           MULTIPLY WS-PRICE BY WS-GROUP
           STOP RUN.

* The first error is the missing spaces around a minus sign: `WS-PRICE-WS-LIMIT` is one name, and there is no such item.
* `WS-SHOWN` is numeric-edited, so it cannot be an operand (only a `GIVING` or `COMPUTE` receiver may be edited), and
  `WS-NAME` is alphanumeric. De-edit an edited item with `FUNCTION NUMVAL` or keep the number in a numeric item and edit a copy.
* `ADD 1 TO 5` has a literal as receiver, and `+MULTIPLY ... BY WS-GROUP+` has a group item as receiver. Only `CORRESPONDING`
  accepts groups.

The run-time pitfall is a numeric item that holds something that is not a number. A `REDEFINES` of a record read from a file
over a numeric field, or `MOVE SPACES` over a group that contains numeric items, leaves bytes in the field that are not
digits. On z/OS the arithmetic on such a field is the classic S0C7 data exception abend, and the `NUMCHECK` compiler option
can be used to catch it. GnuCOBOL does not abend by default; it treats the bytes as digits:

[source,cobol]
----
       IDENTIFICATION DIVISION.
       PROGRAM-ID. BADDATA.
       DATA DIVISION.
       WORKING-STORAGE SECTION.
       01  WS-RAW               PIC X(5) VALUE "  12 ".
       01  WS-NUM REDEFINES WS-RAW PIC 9(5).
       01  WS-TOTAL             PIC 9(5) VALUE 100.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
       MAIN-PARA.
           IF WS-NUM NOT NUMERIC
               DISPLAY "not numeric: " WS-RAW
           END-IF
           ADD WS-NUM TO WS-TOTAL
           DISPLAY "total " WS-TOTAL
           STOP RUN.
----

[source,text]
----
not numeric:   12 
total 00220
----

The five bytes `"  12 "` were read as the digits 0 0 1 2 0, which is 120, and the total became 220. The `IF WS-NUM NOT NUMERIC` test is
the way to catch the data before it is used. Compiled with `-debug`, GnuCOBOL stops at the `ADD`:

[source,text]
----
not numeric:   12 
libcob: baddata.cob:13: error: 'WS-NUM' (Type: NUMERIC DISPLAY) not numeric: '  12 '

 Last statement of "BADDATA" was ADD
	MAIN-PARA at baddata.cob:13
	ENTRY BADDATA at baddata.cob:9
 Started by ./baddata.out
----

Source: https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/pg/tasks/tpari30.html[Enterprise COBOL 6.5 Programming Guide: Checking for incompatible data (numeric class test)]

The statements that move data into numeric fields, and the S0C7 cases they lead to, are on
xref:programming-languages/cobol/move-and-data-movement.adoc[MOVE and Data Movement]; the compiler options that change the
behaviour (`TRUNC`, `NUMPROC`, `NUMCHECK`, `ARITH`) are on
xref:programming-languages/cobol/performance-and-compiler-options.adoc[Performance and Compiler Options]. A final list of habits
that avoid arithmetic bugs:

* Give every receiver enough integer digits for the largest value it can see and enough decimals for the accuracy needed.
* Write `ROUNDED` on the statement that produces the final amount, not on intermediate steps, and do multiplications
  before divisions.
* Put `ON SIZE ERROR` on every statement whose divisor or result size is not guaranteed, and have the imperative statement
  do something deliberate.
* Keep money in fixed-point decimal (zoned or packed), not in `COMP-1` or `COMP-2`.
* Test the dialect: a `cobc` default build and a `-std=ibm` build can differ in intermediate precision and `DISPLAY` format, and
  neither is the mainframe.

== Where the book is outdated

Tom Taulli's _Modern Mainframe Development_ introduces `ADD`, `SUBTRACT`, `MULTIPLY`, `DIVIDE` and `COMPUTE` at tutorial level,
and that part is still accurate: the statements and their basic forms have not changed. What it cannot show, because it was
written against Enterprise COBOL V6.3 as the latest release, is the current picture: 6.5 as the compiler, the COBOL 2014
`ROUNDED MODE` values (which Enterprise COBOL 6.5 still does not implement, but GnuCOBOL 3.2 does), the full rules for
intermediate results and `ARITH(EXTEND)`, the `TRAP(ON)` requirement for `ON SIZE ERROR` on z/OS, and the dialect differences
between GnuCOBOL and the mainframe compiler that matter when a program is developed on one and run on the other.
