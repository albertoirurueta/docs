This page describes how an organization administers Figma, as checked against the official help centre on 2026-10-30, in
the current interface (UI3). It concerns the account and security layer around every Figma product (Design, FigJam,
Slides and the rest), so it has no single GA or beta status: the controls below are generally available, but most of them
exist only on the Organization and Enterprise tiers, and a few only on Enterprise or on the Governance+ add-on. Each
section names its tier. Plans are named by tier only and this page quotes no prices. A "Content seat" announced at a
conference in 2025 does not appear on any current official page, so it is not documented here.

The running example is the *Bookshelf* team, which has outgrown a handful of shared files. Its design lead now needs to
sign people in through the company directory, add and remove contractors safely, and decide who may share files with
"anyone with the link". For the seat and plan model behind this, see
xref:ui-ux/figma/plans-seats-and-ai-credits.adoc[Plans, Seats and AI Credits]; for permissions on individual files, see
xref:ui-ux/figma/files-folders-teams-and-sharing.adoc[Files, Folders, Teams and Sharing].

== Where administration lives in UI3

Almost every control on this page sits in one place: open the file browser, choose your organization, and select *Admin* in
the left sidebar. The Admin area has tabs such as *People*, *Activity* and *Settings*, and the Settings tab groups controls
under headings such as *Login and provisioning*, *AI* and *Content*. The Actions menu, a command search, opens with
`Cmd+K` on macOS or `Ctrl+K` on Windows. It is a quick way to jump around the editor but is not an admin shortcut: admin
tasks are done in the Admin area of the file browser, not inside a design file.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039829474-Guide-to-managing-a-Figma-organization[Figma Learn: Guide to managing a Figma organization]

Figma renames headings from time to time, and the help pages themselves do not always agree: some describe SAML under
*Login and provisioning*, others under *Access > Authentication*. Treat the labels here as a dated snapshot and follow the
heading that your own admin view shows.

== Admin roles and scope

Every plan has at least one admin, but the kinds of admin depend on the tier.

.Admin roles and what each one covers
image::ui-ux-figma-administration-and-security-admin-scope.svg["Hierarchy of organization admin above workspace admins, team admins and billing group admins, with the plan each needs",width=720]

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4420557724439-Admins-in-Figma[Figma Learn: Admins in Figma]

. *Organization admin* (Organization and Enterprise tiers) is the highest level. It manages domains and the login and
  provisioning settings including SAML SSO and SCIM, seats for members and guests, activity logs, access to products,
  features and guests, organization-wide libraries, fonts, plugins and widgets, and billing. It can see all teams, visible
  and hidden.
. *Workspace admin* (Enterprise only) ranks below the organization admin. It performs limited actions in the workspaces it
  manages and acts as team admin for the teams in those workspaces that are not hidden.
. *Team admin* (Organization and Enterprise) manages one team: member permissions, default libraries, and the team name.
  Figma usually makes the person who created the team its admin.
. *Billing group admin* (Enterprise only) assigns people to a billing group, handles upgrade requests and moves people
  between free and paid seats.
. On Starter and Professional, *plan admins* all share one level of access; Professional plan admins also manage libraries,
  seats and billing.

Two points are easy to miss. Guests cannot be made organization admins, only members whose email matches a verified
organization domain can. An organization must always keep at least one organization admin, and only organization admins can
remove other organization admins. To give someone admin rights without adding to organization billing, the help page
suggests changing their seat to View, which yields a view-only admin.

=== Make a Bookshelf lead an organization admin

. In the file browser, select *Admin* in the left sidebar.
. Open the *People* tab.
. Find the design lead and open the menu at the end of the row.
. Choose *Grant admin access*.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4420115581079-Make-someone-an-organization-admin[Figma Learn: Make someone an organization admin]

== Domains and domain capture

A *domain* is the email domain that identifies your members. Anyone whose email matches a verified domain is a member; anyone
else who joins is a guest. Verifying a domain is the base for domain capture, SAML and SCIM.

*Domain capture* automatically adds people with a matching company email address to the organization with a View seat. For
existing Figma accounts, the personal files in *Drafts to move* move to the organization's Drafts, while files in Starter
or Professional teams stay where they are. Domain capture is available on Education (K-12), Organization, Government and
Enterprise, and only organization admins can manage it. A verified domain does not cover its subdomains: verify each one you
use.

=== Add and verify the Bookshelf domain

. In the file browser, select *Admin*, then the *Settings* tab.
. Under *Login and provisioning*, choose *Manage domains*.
. Choose *+ Domain*, enter the domain, and add it. It first appears as *Unverified*.
. Select *Unverified* and copy the text snippet Figma shows.
. At your DNS provider, create a TXT record using the name and value from that snippet.
. Return to the domains page, select *Unverified*, and choose *Verify*.
. To enable domain capture, open the *Domain capture* banner on the same page, turn on the toggle and save. All added
  domains must be verified first.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360045953273-Guide-to-domains-and-domain-capture[Figma Learn: Manage domain capture for an organization]

Two consequences deserve care. Per the same page, domain capture cannot be turned off from the admin settings; you contact
Figma support. And removing a captured domain converts its members to guests, which limits their access.

== Single sign-on with SAML

SAML SSO lets members sign in through your identity provider (IdP) such as Google Workspace, Okta, OneLogin or Microsoft
Entra ID instead of a Figma password. It is available on Education (K-12), Organization, Government and Enterprise, and only
organization admins can configure it. Figma supports SAML 2.0.

The authentication setting offers these choices:

* *Any method* (the default) lets members use Google SSO, SAML SSO or an email and password. Use it while you configure or
  change SAML so nobody is locked out.
* *Enforce Google SSO* requires a Google sign-in.
* *Enforce SAML SSO* requires sign-in through your IdP. Figma for Government requires SAML and does not offer email and
  password sign-in.

SAML applies to organization members only. Guests can always sign in with Google or an email and password, even when SAML is
required, which is one reason guest access needs its own controls (see below).

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052497994-Choose-organization-authentication-method[Figma Learn: Set login and authentication method]

For a supported IdP, the help pages say the Tenant ID is the main value you need. For a custom configuration you enter the
IdP's entity ID, its SSO target URL and its signing certificate in Figma, and copy Figma's service provider (SP) entity ID
and ACS URL into the IdP. The ACS URL is the entity ID with `/consume` appended. The name ID should use the email address
format. The values below are placeholders only; copy the real ones from your own admin view, since some older help pages
show a different URL scheme.

[source,text]
----
# Copy FROM Figma (Admin > Settings > SAML SSO) INTO your identity provider
SP Entity ID : <SP_ENTITY_ID_SHOWN_BY_FIGMA>
SP ACS URL   : <SP_ENTITY_ID_SHOWN_BY_FIGMA>/consume
NameID format: urn:oasis:names:tc:SAML:1.1:nameid-format:emailAddress

# Copy FROM your identity provider INTO Figma (custom "Other" provider)
IdP Entity ID   : <IDP_ENTITY_ID>
IdP SSO URL     : <IDP_SSO_TARGET_URL>
Signing cert    : <PASTE_PUBLIC_CERTIFICATE_HERE>
----

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040047774-Set-up-a-custom-SAML-configuration[Figma Learn: Set up a custom SAML configuration]

== Automatic provisioning with SCIM

Every SAML setup supports *just-in-time (JIT) provisioning*: an account is created or updated only when the person next logs
in. *SCIM* instead pushes changes as they happen, and also imports and deactivates users, syncs groups and, on Enterprise,
sets seat types. It is available on Education (K-12), Organization, Government and Enterprise and is set up by organization
admins, on top of SAML.

The setup, as the help page describes it:

. Find your Tenant ID in the admin settings, in the SAML SSO area.
. Build the SCIM base URL from the Tenant ID (shown below).
. Generate an API token in the SCIM provisioning area and copy it once into your IdP. With Governance+ and several IdPs, you
  generate tokens per IdP from the identity providers page instead.
. In the IdP, enable *Create Users*, *Update User Attributes* and *Deactivate Users*.
. Assign users or groups to the Figma application in the IdP.

[source,text]
----
# SCIM connection values (placeholders)
Base URL (Figma organizations): https://www.figma.com/scim/v2/<TENANT_ID>
Authentication                : bearer token, taken from the environment, e.g. $FIGMA_SCIM_TOKEN
Provisioning features         : Create Users, Update User Attributes, Deactivate Users
----

Never paste the generated token into a document, chat or repository; store it in the IdP's secret field or a secret
manager. Figma for Government uses a different base host, listed on the source page.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048514653-Set-up-automatic-provisioning-via-SCIM[Figma Learn: Set up automatic provisioning via SCIM]

Figma documents IdP-specific guides for Google Workspace, Okta, Microsoft Entra ID and OneLogin; for another IdP it does not
provide a custom guide, so configure SAML first and then ask the IdP's vendor about SCIM.

.Rolling out SSO and SCIM for the Bookshelf team
image::ui-ux-figma-administration-and-security-sso-storyboard.svg["Storyboard: verify the domain, configure SAML, test then enforce, and generate the SCIM token",width=720]

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052497994-Choose-organization-authentication-method[Figma Learn: Set login and authentication method]

=== Roll out SSO safely

. Verify the Bookshelf domain (see above).
. Configure SAML for your IdP while the setting is still *Any method*.
. Sign in with a test account through the IdP and confirm it lands in the organization.
. Only then switch the authentication setting to *Enforce SAML SSO*.
. Set up SCIM and assign the Bookshelf group in the IdP.
. If you must change the SAML configuration later, switch back to *Any method* first, as the help page advises.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052497994-Choose-organization-authentication-method[Figma Learn: Set login and authentication method]

== User groups

*User groups* are named sets of members and guests you can invite to a file, folder or team in one step, so access follows
membership. They are available on Organization and Enterprise. By default every member can create and manage groups, and
organization admins can manage all of them. On Enterprise, an admin can limit creation to organization admins in the
Settings tab under team and user group creation.

=== Create a group and share with it

. In the file browser, open *All workspaces* or *All teams*.
. In the *User group* section of the right sidebar, choose the plus icon.
. Name the group, for example Bookshelf Designers, and add people.
. Open a Bookshelf folder, choose *Share*, type the group name, choose *Can edit* or *Can view*, and invite.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/38020006913943-Create-and-manage-user-groups[Figma Learn: Create and manage user groups]

On Enterprise with SCIM, groups pushed from your IdP can be synced under *Admin > People > User groups* with *Sync from
IdP*. Once synced, membership is managed in the IdP and manual edits may be disabled. Enabling SCIM alone does not sync
groups; they must be pushed from the IdP first.

== Workspaces

A *workspace* groups teams, people and resources in a large organization. Workspaces are an Enterprise feature (they are also
listed for Education K-12 and Government), and only organization admins create them. You name the workspace, assign at
least one workspace admin, and may mark it hidden so it does not appear in *All workspaces* for non-members. Hiding a
workspace does not change who can open the teams, folders or files inside it. Separately, a *hidden team* does not appear in
search or the file browser, and people can join only by invitation; organization admins can still see all teams in Admin.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409612431383-Create-a-workspace[Figma Learn: Create a workspace]

Workspaces also let you override some organization defaults, for example link-sharing and AI settings, for just that
workspace.

== Activity logs

The *Activity* tab in Admin records how members and guests use organization resources. It is available on Education (K-12),
Organization, Government and Enterprise; Starter and Professional teams are not tracked. Only organization admins can view
it. Logging starts when the organization upgrades and events are not backfilled.

* Filter by member email, date range, event type and team.
* Each event shows the event type, member, time in UTC, product, team and IP address.
* *Get CSV* requests an export by email for the selected date range.
* Covered events include seat changes, AI credits, SSO, SCIM provisioning, domain management, sharing and workspace changes.
* Known gaps, per the help page: no login events for guests, and sessions closed without logging out are not recorded.
* The Activity Logs API gives programmatic access on Enterprise only. See
  xref:ui-ux/figma/rest-api-and-webhooks.adoc[REST API and Webhooks] for the developer platform in general.

The help page states no retention period or export size limit, so do not assume one; export what you need to keep.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040449533-View-and-export-activity-logs[Figma Learn: View and export activity logs]

=== Check who changed a sharing setting

. In the file browser, select *Admin*, then the *Activity* tab.
. Set the date range around the change.
. Filter by the member or event type.
. Read the event row, then use *Get CSV* if you need a record.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040449533-View-and-export-activity-logs[Figma Learn: View and export activity logs]

== AI and credit controls

Two separate things are controlled: whether AI features are available at all, and who may spend paid AI credits.

* *AI features on or off.* Only organization admins can change AI settings; workspace and team admins cannot. On Enterprise,
  go to *Admin > Settings > AI > AI features* and enable or disable AI for the whole organization, with per-workspace
  settings that override the organization default. When AI is off, members cannot use it in that organization or workspace
  but still can in drafts and in workspaces where it is on. On Organization, the help page says the control is an *Enable
  AI* option that exists only if AI was disabled during the beta.
* *Content training.* On Organization and Enterprise the help page states that content training is currently disabled and
  cannot be turned on; on Starter and Professional it is on by default and can be turned off by admins.
* *Paid credit limits per person.* In *Admin > People*, select a person and use the *AI credits* section to choose full
  access, a custom monthly limit or no access. Limits apply only to paid credits; the credits included with a seat are not
  affected. If a billing group also has a limit, whichever is reached first stops paid use. On Enterprise an
  organization-wide default can be set under *Admin > Settings > Billing*, and limits can be managed through SCIM, in which
  case Figma shows them but you change them in the IdP.

For what the credits are and how to read the balance, see xref:ui-ux/figma/plans-seats-and-ai-credits.adoc[Plans, Seats and AI Credits]; for the features themselves, xref:ui-ux/figma/figma-ai-tools.adoc[Figma AI Tools].

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/17725942479127-Manage-AI-settings-and-content-training-for-your-team-or-organization[Figma Learn: Manage AI settings and content training]

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/41944442159767-Set-AI-credit-limits[Figma Learn: Set AI credit limits]

== Guest and link controls

*Guests* are external collaborators, such as contractors or clients, whose email does not match an organization domain. Guest
access is available on Education (K-12), Organization, Government and Enterprise. Admins can open the *People* tab, filter
*User type* to guests, and choose *View guest access* to see the teams, folders and files a guest can reach plus their
recent activity, then decide whether to downgrade or remove them. Setting an expiration date on guests, individually or as a
default, is part of Governance+, an add-on for Enterprise (and Government); the Governance+ help page states no GA or beta
label, so ask Figma sales for current availability.

Public links are controlled in the Settings tab. Every organization allows public sharing by default, and the other options
are opt-in.

.Which tier unlocks each guest, link and AI control
image::ui-ux-figma-administration-and-security-sharing-controls.svg["Matrix of five controls across Organization, Enterprise and Enterprise with Governance+",width=720]

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/5726756336791-Manage-public-link-sharing-and-open-sessions[Figma Learn: Manage public link sharing and open sessions]

* *Disable public links* (Organization and Enterprise) removes the "Anyone with the link" options, so files become private
  while members can still invite people directly. It sits under the Content area on Organization and under *External access*
  on Enterprise.
* *Disable open sessions* (Organization and Enterprise) stops FigJam open sessions.
* *Require passwords*, *autogenerated passwords only* and *require link expirations* (Enterprise) tighten public links
  further. The expiry limit range is stated on the source page; confirm it there, since Figma's pages describe different maximums.
* *Workspace-specific settings* (Enterprise) override the organization defaults for a workspace's files.
* Changes to these settings are recorded in the activity log.

=== Lock down public links for a client review

. In the file browser, select *Admin*, then the *Settings* tab.
. Open the sharing area (*Content* on Organization, *External access* on Enterprise).
. Turn off public link sharing, or on Enterprise require a password and an expiry.
. Invite the client as a guest on the single Bookshelf folder instead, and set the minimum permission.
. Later, open *People*, filter to guests and use *View guest access* to confirm what the client can reach.

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/5726756336791-Manage-public-link-sharing-and-open-sessions[Figma Learn: Manage public link sharing and open sessions]

Source: https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4420556737175-View-guest-access[Figma Learn: View guest access]

== What older guides get wrong

Older books and videos describe an earlier model. Their "editor" and "viewer" roles, "paid Team plan" and per-feature plan
gating predate the Full, Dev, Collab and View seats, which have applied since 2025-03-11; the Bookshelf admin should pick
seats from the current table, not from those guides. They also predate the AI credit system and its admin limits, the
Figma agent and the Figma MCP server (see xref:ui-ux/figma/figma-mcp-server.adoc[Figma MCP Server]). The full list of
corrections is collected in xref:ui-ux/figma/whats-changed-since-older-guides.adoc[What's Changed Since Older Guides].
When you bring files in from other tools, see xref:ui-ux/figma/importing-and-migrating.adoc[Importing and Migrating].

== Common mistakes

* Enforcing SAML before testing it, which can lock out admins; stay on *Any method* until a test sign-in works.
* Assuming SAML or SCIM covers guests: it does not, so guests need their own review.
* Enabling domain capture without verifying every subdomain, or expecting to switch it off yourself later.
* Pasting a SCIM token or certificate into a ticket or chat; keep secrets in the IdP or a vault.
* Expecting a workspace or team admin to change AI settings; only organization admins can.
* Relying on a remembered limit (credits, link expiry, log retention): these change, so re-read the source page.
