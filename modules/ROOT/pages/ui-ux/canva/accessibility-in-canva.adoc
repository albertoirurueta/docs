This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center and Canva's accessibility statement on 2026-11-07. It covers the accessibility features of the Canva editor (web, desktop app and, where stated, the mobile apps) and how to use them so that the designs you publish can be read by people with low vision, blindness, colour blindness, motion sensitivity or a hearing loss, and by people who work with a keyboard or a screen reader. None of the Help Center articles used here restricts these features to a plan, so they are available on Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits accounts; plan details are on xref:ui-ux/canva/plans-pricing-and-ai-allowance.adoc[Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance] and prices are published at https://www.canva.com/pricing/. The checker, alt text, captions, reduce motion and screen reader support are generally available. The suggested alt text mentioned below is an AI feature, but the Help Center does not name an AI tier for it. The Help Center states that the Design Accessibility checker and the other tools help you reach an accessible result without guaranteeing compliance. For what the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) require and how to validate a result with external tools, read xref:web/accessibility.adoc[Web Accessibility]; this page does not repeat it and concentrates on where each control sits in Canva.

Canva states on its accessibility page that it supports WCAG 2.1 AA across its core product experiences, as documented in its Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT), and that the latest conformance report is published in the Canva Trust and Security Portal. That statement is about the Canva product itself. Whether the design you export is accessible depends on the choices you make in the design, which is the subject of this page.

The running example is *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma sub-section]. Its launch campaign is built in Canva across the pages of this sub-section. Here, the "Book of the week" post gets an accessibility pass before publishing, the launch flyer is exported as a tagged PDF, and the promo video and launch deck are prepared for viewers who need captions or reduced motion.

== Accessibility features at a glance

Canva spreads accessibility across three places: checks and structure inside a design, preferences in your account, and options in the viewer when someone watches or presents a design.

[cols="2,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Feature | What it does | Where it lives

| Design Accessibility | Detects text under 12 pt, low colour contrast and images without alt text, and offers fixes | File, Accessibility, Check design accessibility
| Alternative text | Describes an image or element for screen readers; can be marked decorative | More, Alternative text (or right-click, Alternative text)
| Text semantics | Tags each text box as a heading level or a paragraph for screen readers and tagged PDFs | File, Accessibility, Edit text semantics (desktop)
| Reading order | Controls the order in which a tagged PDF is read | Position, Layers, plus a PDF download setting
| Tagged PDF | Exports headings, lists, simple tables, images and reading order as tags | Share, Download, PDF with the Digital preset
| View-only captions | Auto-generated captions for spoken video and audio, shown to viewers | Account Accessibility settings, File, Accessibility, or the viewer bar
| Reduce motion | Limits animations, removes transitions, background animations, masks and overlays when viewing or presenting | Account Accessibility settings, or File, Accessibility
| Theme and High Color Contrast | Light, dark or system theme, and a stronger border in the editor header | Account Accessibility settings
| Autoplay videos | Chooses how videos play in your designs | Account Accessibility settings
| Screen reader support | Navigates and edits designs with keyboard shortcuts and a screen reader | Desktop browser and desktop app
| Screen reader language | Sets the language embedded in the design and in an exported PDF | File, Language
|===

== The Design Accessibility checker

Design Accessibility is Canva's built-in checker. You open it from the editor with File, then Accessibility, then Check design accessibility. A window opens in the editor with a summary of the problems grouped by category: typography, colour contrast and alternative text. Each issue appears as a card with a button that applies the fix. On a phone, the entry is in the three dots menu of the editor, under Accessibility. The checker appears only for designs you have permission to edit, so it is absent if you only have comment access.

.The Design Accessibility panel beside a draft Bookshelf post, with one card per category
image::ui-ux-canva-accessibility-in-canva-checker-panel.svg["Schematic of a draft post with three problems next to the Design Accessibility panel showing a typography card, a colour contrast card and an alternative text card",width=720]

. Typography: text with a font size below 12 pt can be hard to read for people with low vision. Raise it in the editor or select Increase font size to 12pt on the card. In presentations, social media, print, website and video designs you can select and fix several typography issues in one step.
. Color contrast: text whose colour is too close to its background, for example yellow on white, is flagged. Select one of the suggested colours on the card to apply it. If no suitable colour is found, change the font or the background colour yourself.
. Alternative text: an image without a description is flagged. Select Add alternative text on the card, type a description or tick Mark as decorative, and select Save. The same multi-select fixing exists here for the design types listed above.

The multi-select fixing is not available in Canva Docs. The Help Center says more automatically detected issue types will be added and invites you to report false positives to the support team.

=== What the checker does not do

The checker covers visual issues only: type size, colour contrast and missing alt text. It does not check heading structure, reading order, link text, captions or whether the content works with a screen reader. The Help Center states plainly that the tool is not a compliance tool and does not make a design WCAG A, AA or AAA compliant, because an automated checker cannot replace manual testing with assistive technologies. Treat a clean result as the start of a review, not its end.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-design-accessibility/[Canva Help Center: Use Design Accessibility]

== Colour contrast and type size

Contrast is the difference in brightness between text and the background behind it. The Help Center article does not publish the ratio the checker applies. WCAG 2.x level AA asks for at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text and 3 to 1 for large text, and the ratio is computed from the relative luminance of the two colours, as explained on xref:web/accessibility.adoc[Web Accessibility]. The figure uses the same formula on example colours, so you can see how far typical choices are from that bar: mid-grey on white and pale yellow on white fail by a wide margin, while the dark ink and the indigo of the Bookshelf palette pass comfortably.

.Text and background pairs that fail and pass a 4.5 to 1 ratio
image::ui-ux-canva-accessibility-in-canva-contrast-pass-fail.svg["Two columns of colour pairs: three light text and background pairs that fail, and three dark or saturated pairs that pass, each labelled with its contrast ratio",width=720]

Practical rules for the Bookshelf palette, defined on xref:ui-ux/canva/brand-kit-and-brand-voice.adoc[Brand Kit and Brand Voice]:

* Use the ink colour, not a mid-grey, for body text on white or on a pale tint.
* Put white text only on the saturated indigo or the dark amber, never on a pale tint of them.
* Do not carry meaning by colour alone: pair a red or green status with a word or an icon.
* Check text over photos and gradients after you place it, because the background changes under the letters. Add a solid or semi-transparent shape behind the text when the picture is busy.
* Keep body text at 12 pt or larger in every design, including print and video, as the checker expects.

Colour choices are discussed further on xref:ui-ux/canva/color-gradients-and-palettes.adoc[Colour, Gradients and Palettes], and type sizes on xref:ui-ux/canva/text-and-typography.adoc[Text and Typography].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-design-accessibility/[Canva Help Center: Use Design Accessibility]

== Alternative text and decorative images

Alternative text (alt text) is a short description of an image or element that screen readers announce in its place. It is available for Canva library images, uploaded images, videos, shapes, lines and graphics, and its limit is 250 characters. Some image and video formats, for example PNG, do not carry alt text.

To add it, select the element, then select More in the toolbar and Alternative text, type the description and select Save. On desktop you can also right-click the element and choose Alternative text; the editor can show a suggested description that you accept or edit. If the element adds no information, such as a background flourish, select Mark as decorative so that screen readers skip it. The same entry is reachable from the checker's alt text card.

=== Writing good alt text

The Help Center tips are to keep the description brief and accurate, to give the information that matters in context, and to use meaningful file names. It also mentions relevant keywords for search engines on published websites. For the Bookshelf cover, "Book cover of The Quiet Library on a wooden shelf" tells a reader more than "image1" and less than a full paragraph. Alt text is instance based: each copy of an image can have its own description, so write it for the place where that copy appears.

=== Where alt text survives and where it is lost

Alt text is carried by the PDF, PPTX and HTML exports, by the Canva design share link and by Presenter mode. It is not included in downloads of image and video formats such as PNG, MP4 or AVI, and it is not available in the Content Planner, so social media posts scheduled from Canva do not include alt text. Alt text embedded in imported PDFs and PowerPoint files is preserved and included in exports. Put the important description in the visible caption of a scheduled post as well, as described on xref:ui-ux/canva/social-media-and-content-planner.adoc[Social Media and the Content Planner].

.Destinations that keep alt text and destinations that lose it
image::ui-ux-canva-accessibility-in-canva-alt-text-exports.svg["Table-like figure listing PDF, PPTX, HTML, share link and Presenter mode as keeping alt text, and PNG, MP4 and Content Planner posts as losing it",width=720]

=== QR codes

Canva's QR code generator does not support alt text directly. To make a code accessible, create it, download it from Share, Download as a PNG or SVG file, upload it back into the design, add alt text that states its purpose or destination, for example "QR code to join the Bookshelf book club", and place a descriptive link or short URL near it for people who cannot scan.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-alt-text/[Canva Help Center: Create and edit alt text for your images and elements] and https://www.canva.com/help/create-accessible-pdfs/[Canva Help Center: Create accessible PDFs with Canva]

== Text semantics, reading order and tagged PDFs

A screen reader does not see a big bold line as a heading; it needs a tag. Canva exports tags into a PDF, and you control three things: the heading levels, the reading order and the export settings.

=== Text semantics

On desktop, open File, Accessibility, Edit text semantics and set each text box: H1 for the main title, normally one per design, H2 for main sections, H3 for subsections, lower levels in order without skipping, and Paragraph for body text. Applying semantics does not change how the text looks; it changes how it is identified by screen readers and in accessible PDF exports. Keep a multi-line heading in one text box and adjust the box width instead of pressing Enter, so it is not read as several headings. Editing text semantics is currently desktop only. In Canva Docs the tags follow the heading style you choose: Title is tagged h1, Subtitle h2, Heading h3, Subheading h4 and Section header h5. For older designs without semantic tags, Canva may estimate heading levels from visual hierarchy such as size and placement.

=== Reading order

Canva estimates the reading order automatically when you export a PDF, which works best for simple, linear layouts. To control it, open Position, then Layers, and arrange the layers so that the element to be read first, such as the H1 title, is at the bottom of the list and the next element sits directly above it. The layer order is applied to the PDF only when Match reading order to layer order is selected in the PDF download settings. Canva Docs does not offer reading order arrangement. Layer handling in general is explained on xref:ui-ux/canva/layers-alignment-and-arrangement.adoc[Layers, Alignment and Arrangement].

.Layer order, bottom to top, becomes the reading order of the tagged PDF
image::ui-ux-canva-accessibility-in-canva-reading-order.svg["Three schematic panels: a post with a title, cover, blurb and link, the layers list with the title at the bottom, and the resulting reading order of heading, image, paragraph and link",width=720]

=== Exporting a tagged PDF

Open Share, then Download, choose PDF with the Digital preset, and make sure Flatten PDF is unchecked, because flattening removes the accessibility tags. Open Advanced settings and turn on Match reading order to layers when you arranged the layers yourself, then Download. Content such as headings, paragraphs, simple tables, lists and images is tagged correctly, and tables, lists and text effects including curved text are tagged so that assistive technologies can interpret them. If the design contains paid elements the button reads Pay and download. Set the screen reader language first (File, Language) because it is embedded in the exported PDF. The wider download options are covered on xref:ui-ux/canva/downloading-and-file-formats.adoc[Downloading and File Formats].

=== Verify outside Canva

Test the PDF with a checker such as Adobe Acrobat's Accessibility Checker or the PDF Accessibility Checker (PAC), and try it with a screen reader such as NVDA on Windows or VoiceOver on Mac. The Help Center lists a known issue: built-in browser PDF readers and VoiceOver may skip content when the PDF is opened in macOS Preview or Safari, even when the tags look right in Adobe Acrobat, and recommends Adobe Acrobat for the most reliable result with NVDA, JAWS or VoiceOver. As an alternative to a PDF, you can publish the design as a Canva website, which keeps the headings, alt text and links you added; test it with a screen reader and an accessibility checking tool before sharing it. See xref:ui-ux/canva/websites.adoc[Websites].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/create-accessible-pdfs/[Canva Help Center: Create accessible PDFs with Canva] and https://www.canva.com/help/pdf-accessibility-features/[Canva Help Center: PDF accessibility features]

== Captions

Canva can show auto-generated captions for spoken-word video and audio. These view-only captions come from the Accessibility feature and the Show view-only captions button, and they are a different feature from Editable Captions, which are covered on xref:ui-ux/canva/video-editor-audio-and-captions.adoc[Video Editor, Audio and Captions]. You can turn them on in three places:

* for all media across Canva, in your account settings under Accessibility, with the Captions toggle, which is saved automatically;
* for one design, in the editor under File, Accessibility, Show view-only captions on media;
* for one video in edit mode, by selecting the video in the timeline, then More, then Show view-only captions.

Viewers can turn captions on while presenting or on a view or watch link by hovering over the bottom of the page and choosing Show view-only captions (select More first if you do not see it). Limits stated in the Help Center: captions can be viewed on Canva but not downloaded, they are auto-generated and cannot be edited, they take time to generate and can be wrong when the audio is unclear, and they are not yet supported for video on Canva websites. Canva lists more than fifty languages, from Afrikaans to Welsh, so check the list for yours.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/enable-captions/[Canva Help Center: Enable accessibility or view-only captions on videos and audio]

== Reduce motion and autoplay

Animations and transitions can disturb viewers with motion sensitivity or vestibular disorders. When Reduce motion is on, design animations are reduced, and transitions, background animations, masks and overlays are removed. There are three ways to set it:

. For every design you view or present: from the homepage, select your profile icon, then Settings, then Accessibility, open the Reduce motion when you view and present designs dropdown and choose Reduce motion on or Use my system preferences.
. For the design you are working in: File, Accessibility, Reduce motion; a checkmark shows that it is on.
. On a public view link, without a Canva account: hover over the bottom of the page, select More and then Reduce motion.

The limits are important when you design. Reduce motion affects playback only, not editing or exporting, so animations can still appear in downloaded video files. It applies only to your own account, so collaborators and audiences see animations according to their own settings. Some interactive elements may look simplified. For motion that must not be shown to anyone who is sensitive, keep the animation subtle or leave it out; see xref:ui-ux/canva/animation-and-transitions.adoc[Animation and Transitions].

The Autoplay videos dropdown, in the same Accessibility settings, lets you choose how videos play in your designs so that audio and video do not start by surprise. The same page offers a setting to keep on-screen messages visible longer.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/reduce-motion-settings/[Canva Help Center: Reduce motion settings for animations and transitions]

== Theme, high contrast and the account Accessibility settings

By default Canva follows your system appearance. From your profile choose Theme and pick Light, Sync with system or Dark, or open Your account, Accessibility and use the Theme dropdown. To improve visibility, switch on High Color Contrast in the Accessibility section: it removes the gradient from the editor header so that the border colour stands out. These preferences change how the editor looks for you; they do not change your design. The Accessibility section of the account settings also holds the Captions toggle, Autoplay videos, Reduce motion and the on-screen message time described above. Account-level settings are covered in xref:ui-ux/canva/account-homepage-and-navigation.adoc[Account, Homepage and Navigation].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/light-and-dark-mode-settings/[Canva Help Center: Light and dark mode settings]

== Keyboard and screen reader use in the editor

Canva supports screen readers in the editor on a desktop browser and the desktop app. Mobile support is described as experimental and may not be complete or consistent. Responsive design types such as Docs are not supported at this time. The recommended combinations are JAWS with Chrome, NVDA with Firefox and VoiceOver with Chrome.

The editor has two modes for a screen reader. Edit mode is the default when you have edit access; use focus mode in NVDA or turn off the virtual cursor in JAWS so that toolbars and shortcuts work. View mode is a read-only mode that is on by default without edit access and can be turned on from the menu bar; browse mode works well there. Navigation works with text, shape, image, video and line elements, tables, frames, groups, grids, sticky notes, fixed designs such as presentations, posters and social media, and unbounded designs such as whiteboards.

The editor is divided into surfaces you can move between: menu bar, side panel, canvas, editor toolbar, context menu, status bar, page navigator and share menu.

[cols="3,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| Action | macOS | Windows

| Move focus to the canvas | Command + F2 | Ctrl + F2
| Move focus to the editor toolbar (with a selection) | Command + F1 | Ctrl + F1
| Open the More actions (context) menu (with a selection) | Shift + F10 | Shift + F10
| Toggle the side panel | Command + / | Ctrl + /
| Move focus to the side panel | Command + F3 | Ctrl + F3
| Move to the next surface | Command + F6 | Ctrl + F6
| Move to the previous surface | Shift + Command + F6 | Shift + Ctrl + F6
| Open quick actions | / or Command + E | / or Ctrl + E
|===

On the canvas, Enter followed by the arrow keys moves between pages. Pressing Enter twice selects an element on the page; Tab and Shift + Tab, or Shift + W, A, S and D, move between elements, and Esc returns to page selection. When you select an element, the screen reader announces its type and details such as alt text, styling or position. The full list of shortcuts is on xref:ui-ux/canva/keyboard-shortcuts-and-quick-actions.adoc[Keyboard Shortcuts and Quick Actions].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/screen-reader-editor/[Canva Help Center: Use a screen reader in the Canva Editor]

== Desktop and mobile differences

* Text semantics can be edited on desktop only. Reading order is arranged with Position and Layers in the desktop editor.
* The checker, alt text, view-only captions, reduce motion and the account settings are available on both desktop and mobile; the menu entries differ, for example the checker sits in the three dots menu on a phone and the alt text entry in the toolbar under More.
* Screen reader support is for desktop browsers and the desktop app; mobile support is experimental.

== Procedures on the Bookshelf campaign

=== Fix accessibility issues in the "Book of the week" post

.Storyboard: run the checker, fix type size and contrast, describe the cover, set headings and reading order, export
image::ui-ux-canva-accessibility-in-canva-storyboard-fix-before-publishing.svg["Five-panel storyboard: run the checker, fix type size and contrast, describe or hide images, set headings and reading order, and export a tagged PDF",width=720]

. Open the *Bookshelf Book of the Week* design and select File, then Accessibility, then Check design accessibility.
. Read the summary of issues by category. Start with Typography and select Increase font size to 12pt on the blurb, or select all small text at once if the offer is shown.
. Open the Color contrast card and select one of the suggested colours for the grey title; the title now uses the ink colour of the brand kit.
. On the Alternative text card, select Add alternative text for the cover image, type "Book cover of The Quiet Library on a wooden shelf" and select Save.
. For the decorative line under the cover, select Add alternative text and tick Mark as decorative, then Save.
. Run Check design accessibility again until no issue cards remain, then review heading structure and reading order by hand, since the checker does not look at them.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-design-accessibility/[Canva Help Center: Use Design Accessibility]

=== Export the launch flyer as a tagged PDF

The flyer has a title, a cover image, a blurb and a link, like the post shown in the reading order figure.

. Open the *Bookshelf Launch Flyer* design and set the language with File, then Language, to the language of the text.
. Select File, Accessibility, Edit text semantics. Set the title to H1, the section headings to H2 and the blurb to Paragraph.
. Select Position in the toolbar, then Layers. Drag the title to the bottom of the list and place the cover image, blurb and link above it in the order they should be read.
. Select Share, then Download, and choose PDF with the Digital preset.
. Make sure Flatten PDF is unchecked, open Advanced settings and turn on Match reading order to layers, then select Download.
. Open the PDF in Adobe Acrobat, run its Accessibility Checker and listen to the first page with NVDA or VoiceOver to confirm headings, alt text, links and order.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/create-accessible-pdfs/[Canva Help Center: Create accessible PDFs with Canva]

=== Prepare the promo video and launch deck for all viewers

. In the *Bookshelf 30-second Promo* design, select File, Accessibility, Show view-only captions on media, or turn on Captions in your account Accessibility settings to apply it to all media.
. Share the view link and, as a viewer, hover over the bottom of the page and choose Show view-only captions to confirm they appear.
. Open the *Bookshelf Launch Deck*. Select File, then Accessibility, then Reduce motion to preview the deck with fewer animations and no transitions, and compare it with the setting off.
. In the account settings, under Accessibility, set Autoplay videos to a setting that suits your audience.
. Remember that these choices apply to playback in Canva for the person who set them; downloaded video files can still contain the animations.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/enable-captions/[Canva Help Center: Enable accessibility or view-only captions on videos and audio] and https://www.canva.com/help/reduce-motion-settings/[Canva Help Center: Reduce motion settings for animations and transitions]

=== Edit the post with a screen reader

. Open the design in a desktop browser with a supported pairing, for example NVDA with Firefox, and use focus mode.
. Press Ctrl + F2 (Command + F2 on macOS) to move focus to the canvas, then Enter and the arrow keys to choose the page.
. Press Enter twice to select the first element and Tab to move through the title, the cover and the blurb; the screen reader announces each element and its alt text.
. Press Ctrl + F1 (Command + F1) to move to the editor toolbar and use the arrow keys to change the text style; Esc returns to the page.
. Press Ctrl + F6 (Command + F6) to move to the next surface, for example the side panel, and Ctrl + / (Command + /) to open or close it.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/screen-reader-editor/[Canva Help Center: Use a screen reader in the Canva Editor]

== Common pitfalls

* Treating a clean checker result as proof of accessibility. It does not look at heading structure, reading order, link text or captions.
* Exporting a PDF with Flatten PDF checked. Flattening removes the accessibility tags.
* Arranging the layers but forgetting to turn on Match reading order to layers in the download settings, so the exported order is the automatic estimate.
* Placing the first element at the top of the Layers list. The bottom layer is read first.
* Pressing Enter to break a heading into lines, which can make a screen reader treat every line as a separate heading.
* Expecting alt text to appear on social posts scheduled from the Content Planner, or in PNG and MP4 downloads.
* Expecting view-only captions in a downloaded file. They are visible only on Canva.
* Relying on Reduce motion to protect everyone. It applies to the viewer who turned it on and not to exports.
* Opening the exported PDF in a browser or macOS Preview and judging it by what VoiceOver does there; test in Adobe Acrobat as the Help Center suggests.
* Using colour alone to carry meaning, or placing small text over a busy photograph.

== Where older guides are outdated

Older printed guides treat alt text as one of the element controls next to Lock and mention accessibility options only as optional account preferences. As checked on 2026-11-07, the current behaviour is different.

| Accessibility options are a few preferences chosen in the account settings | Settings, Accessibility now holds Captions, Autoplay videos, Reduce motion, theme and High Color Contrast, and longer on-screen messages; File, Accessibility holds the checker, text semantics, view-only captions and Reduce motion for a design | Help Center as checked 2026-11-07
