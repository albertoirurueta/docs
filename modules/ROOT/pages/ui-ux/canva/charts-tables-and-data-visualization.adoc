This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers the three ways to put numbers into a design: charts, tables and infographics. Charts and tables are part of the Canva editor and of Canva Docs. Magic Charts belong to Canva Sheets. Flourish is a separate third-party product that embeds into Canva. The Help Center articles on charts, tables and importing data do not restrict them to a plan, so treat them as generally available on Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits and check your own account. Three exceptions are worth knowing. Embedding Flourish visualisations and its Presenter features need Canva Business or Canva Enterprise, or a paid Flourish subscription. Adding chart styles to a Brand Kit is limited to roles with Brand Kit edit access (the Pro owner, or brand designers and administrators on team and enterprise plans). Magic Charts is documented together with an AI-usage notice, so check the AI usage article for your account (see xref:ui-ux/canva/plans-pricing-and-ai-allowance.adoc[Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance]). Prices are published at https://www.canva.com/pricing/ and not repeated here.

Canva is a continuously deployed service, so this page describes controls by name. Its drawings are schematic sketches of a generic editor, not screenshots, and use the neutral colours of this documentation.

The running example is *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma sub-section]. For its launch, the team charts the reading-challenge numbers, tabulates the challenge rules and combines both in a one-page infographic. The thinking behind a good chart (choosing the form, avoiding misleading axes, colour-blind safe palettes) is covered in xref:data-science/data-visualization.adoc[Data Visualization]; this page is about doing it in Canva.

== Charts: the editor's Charts panel

To add a chart, create or open a design, choose *Charts* in the editor side panel (if it is not there, choose *Apps* first, then *Charts*), and select a chart from the options. Canva advertises more than 30 chart options. The Help Center groups them by the question they answer.

.A chart in the editor with its data table and Customize tabs
image::ui-ux-canva-charts-tables-and-data-visualization-chart-panel.svg["Schematic of a bar chart on a page next to a side panel with Import data, a data table, Chart setup, Format column and the Customize text fields, with six numbered callouts",width=720]

[cols="2,3,2", options="header"]
|===
| Chart family | Best for | Data type

| Bar chart, column chart | Comparing categories | Categorical, numerical
| Line chart, area chart | Trends and change over time | Temporal, numerical
| Pie chart, donut chart | Proportions across a limited number of categories | Categorical
| Scatter plot, bubble plot | Relationships between variables, trends and outliers | Numerical
| Dot plot | Comparing individual points across categories, to show distribution and gaps | Categorical, numerical
| Hierarchy chart, bar race chart | Classification structures, and changes in several categories over time | Categorical, temporal
| Radar chart | Several variables, comparing several data sets | Numerical
| Statistical charts (histogram, funnel) | How data is distributed, and progress through stages | Numerical
| Infographic charts (progress, pictogram) | Infographics and storytelling | Categorical, numerical
|===

The Help Center suggests starting with the data type: categorical data (distinct categories) suits bars, columns, pie, donut and hierarchy charts; numerical data (measurable quantities) suits bars, lines, areas, scatter, bubble and statistical charts; temporal data suits lines, areas and bar races. Using the wrong chart can lead to misinterpretation.

== Chart data: type it, import it or connect it

Select the chart, choose *Edit* on the toolbar, and enter values in the *Data table* of the side panel. *Expand data table* shows it full screen. Use the *More* menu on a row or column to insert before or after, delete, or sort a column A-Z or Z-A. Hold Shift to select several cells, rows or columns, or drag across them.

To bring data in, choose *Import data* under Edit. You can pick *Sheets* (data from Canva Sheets), one of the listed data sources (Google Sheets, Salesforce, Google Analytics, HubSpot, World Bank) or *Upload data* for a CSV, XLSX or TSV file. For now, an import is limited to 1000 rows and 100 columns. Third-party sources ask you to sign in and grant Canva permission; then you choose the dataset and select *Done* to sync it with the chart. After loading, Data Actions can clean and organise the data before import. To keep a chart honest, cite where its numbers came from: under Edit, open the *Customize* tab, open the *Text* dropdown and fill in *Source* with a name and a URL.

The *Chart setup* area under the data table controls which data a chart displays and how colours, sizes or time periods are shown, and the available controls depend on the chart type. Only titles, subtitles, source text and axis titles can be restyled as text.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/set-up-chart-data/[Canva Help Center: Add and manage your chart data]

=== Format numbers

Raw numbers are hard to read. Choose a column, open its *More* menu (or the *Format* button in the floating toolbar) and select *Format column*. Choose a data type, number or text. For numbers, pick a style from the *Style* dropdown, including locale-specific formats such as German, Swedish and Indian; add a prefix (for example a currency symbol) or a suffix (for example % or units); and set up to five decimals, with *Reset* returning to automatic formatting. Typed numbers follow your document locale by default. A title and subtitle are set in the *Customize* tab for every chart type except infographic charts.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/format-chart-data/[Canva Help Center: Format chart data for clarity]

== On-brand chart colours

Chart colours can be tied to the Brand Kit. A person with Brand Kit editing access selects *Brand* on the homepage side panel, opens or creates a Brand Kit, scrolls to the *Charts* section and chooses *Add New* under *Colors* to set a custom palette. For now, only brand colours can be set up for charts. Anyone designing then adds a chart and opens the *Brand* tab in the side panel; selecting a Brand Kit with chart styles generates the chart in those colours, and each colour can still be adjusted from the floating toolbar. See xref:ui-ux/canva/brand-kit-and-brand-voice.adoc[Brand Kit and Brand Voice] for the rest of the kit. Remember that colour themes and gradients do not apply to charts (see xref:ui-ux/canva/color-gradients-and-palettes.adoc[Colour, Gradients and Palettes]); for accessible colour choices see xref:ui-ux/canva/accessibility-in-canva.adoc[Accessibility in Canva].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/set-up-on-brand-charts/[Canva Help Center: Set up on-brand chart styles]

== Magic Charts in Canva Sheets

In a spreadsheet, highlight the data, choose *Actions* in the floating toolbar, then *Magic Charts*, and pick a chart from the side panel. The chart is added to the sheet and updates as the linked data changes. After adding it, a *Chart Source* label under Data shows where the data comes from; select the chart, choose *Edit*, and use *More* beside the source title to edit the data source, open the linked source or *Disconnect from Source* (the chart then stops updating). The Help Center notes that AI usage limits apply. See xref:ui-ux/canva/sheets.adoc[Sheets].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/magic-charts/[Canva Help Center: Use Magic Charts]

== Flourish

Flourish is a data-visualisation tool for interactive and animated charts. To embed its visualisations in Canva you need Canva Business or Canva Enterprise, or a paid Flourish subscription. Open the Apps panel, search for the Flourish app and connect it with your Canva account; in Flourish, choose Export & publish, then Export to Canva design. Your Flourish charts then appear under *Projects* in the side panel, and *Edit* on the toolbar opens Flourish so you can change data or settings. Users on other plans can still make visualisations with a free Flourish account and embed them online. Flourish is available in English only. Access to the Presenter plan is controlled by your account administrator.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/set-up-flourish-with-canva/[Canva Help Center: Set up Flourish with your Canva account] and https://flourish.studio/[Flourish]

== Tables

Tables are for exact values and short comparisons. Choose *Elements* in the side panel, scroll to *Tables* and select one, or type `/table` anywhere in the design and say how many rows and columns you need. Double-click a cell to type. On desktop at canva.com you can copy cells from a spreadsheet and paste them into a table; the Help Center says that importing data directly into a table is still being worked on.

* *Rows and columns*: select the table, use the *More* icon to add rows or columns, and the icons that appear by a row or column to remove or move it.
* *Borders*: select the table, choose *Border* on the toolbar, pick which borders to edit, then a colour and a thickness.
* *Cell fill*: select a cell (hold Cmd on macOS or Ctrl on Windows to select several), use the colour tile on the toolbar, and pick a colour, a custom colour or a gradient.
* *Sizing*: select a single cell, then use *Size rows equally* or *Size columns equally* to even them out, or the "to content" options to fit text. You cannot type an exact row height or column width, and Docs does not support the equal-size and size-to-content options.
* *Merging*: select cells (Shift and click), right-click and choose *Merge cells*; right-click a merged cell and choose *Unmerge cell*. The steps work in Canva Sheets and in tables in designs.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/adding-and-deleting-tables/[Canva Help Center: Add and delete tables, rows, and columns], https://www.canva.com/help/formatting-tables/[Change table borders, colors, and size] and https://www.canva.com/help/merging-and-unmerging-cells/[Merge and unmerge table cells]

== Desktop and mobile

The Help Center shows a Desktop tab and a Mobile tab for the chart and table procedures, and the steps are the same in outline. Two limitations are stated for desktop only: pasting spreadsheet data into a table is available on desktop at canva.com, and merging uses a right-click. The Help Center shows no dedicated keyboard shortcuts for charts; the quick-actions slash command opens tables, and undo is Command + Z on macOS or Ctrl + Z on Windows (see xref:ui-ux/canva/keyboard-shortcuts-and-quick-actions.adoc[Keyboard Shortcuts and Quick Actions]).

== Procedures on the Bookshelf campaign

=== Chart the reading challenge from a CSV

. Open the *Bookshelf Reading Challenge* design and choose *Charts* in the side panel (or *Apps*, then *Charts*).
. Select a column chart, because the question is "how many books were read each month".
. With the chart selected, choose *Edit*, then *Import data*, then *Upload data*, and choose `books-per-month.csv`.
. Choose the dataset and select *Done*; the chart syncs with the file.
. Select the numeric column, open *More*, then *Format column*, and set a suffix such as " books" with zero decimals.
. In the *Customize* tab enter the title and subtitle, then under *Text* enter the *Source* name and the link to the reading log.
. Select the Bookshelf brand chart style from the *Brand* tab so the bars use the brand palette, and adjust the highlighted month from the floating toolbar.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/set-up-chart-data/[Canva Help Center: Add and manage your chart data]

=== Build the one-page "Reading challenge" infographic

. Create an *Infographic* design and pick a layout with space for a headline, one chart and one table.
. Add a chart from *Elements* or the Charts panel. For progress towards the yearly goal, add an *infographic chart* (progress or pictogram) and set its values, icons, spacing and colours.
. Add a table from *Elements*, then *Tables*, with the challenge tiers as rows.
. Select two cells in the header row, right-click and choose *Merge cells* to make one title cell spanning the table.
. Cite the data source on each chart and give the page a clear title.
. Run Design Accessibility from the File menu before sharing, to check colour contrast and alternative text.

== Common pitfalls

* Picking a chart by looks rather than by data type; the Help Center warns that a wrong chart can mislead.
* Importing more than 1000 rows or 100 columns; trim the data first.
* Expecting a chart to follow colour themes or gradients; neither applies to charts.
* Disconnecting a Magic Chart from its source and then wondering why it no longer updates.
* Expecting to type an exact row height or column width in a table; only the sizing options exist.
* Forgetting the source line; fill in Source under the Customize tab.
* Assuming Flourish embedding is free on every plan; it needs Business or Enterprise, or a paid Flourish plan.
