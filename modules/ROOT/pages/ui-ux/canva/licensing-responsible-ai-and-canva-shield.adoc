This page was checked against the official Canva policies and Help Center on 2026-11-07. It explains the rules that decide what you may publish when a design contains library content or AI-generated material: the Content License Agreement, the AI Product Terms and the Canva Shield trust, safety and privacy commitments. The rules apply to every plan, Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits, but what you pay, and a few rights, differ by plan: a Free account buys a Pro Content licence one design at a time, a subscription issues it automatically, Education accounts are limited to non-commercial use of Pro Content, and AI indemnification is offered to eligible Enterprise customers only. Which AI tools you can run, and how much, depends on the AI tier (Standard, Premium or Ultra) described on xref:ui-ux/canva/plans-pricing-and-ai-allowance.adoc[Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance]; the policies below apply to all of them. The policies are generally available and in force; the conversational Canva AI 2.0 assistant was launched as a research preview on 2026-04-16 and no general-availability date is published, so its availability varies by account.

This page is a practical reading guide, not legal advice. Contracts change, and the wording of the official documents always wins. Read the linked policies before relying on any summary here, and take local legal advice for anything with real stakes such as registering a trademark, selling merchandise or contracting with a client.

The running example is *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma sub-section]. Its launch campaign uses library photos and graphics, a logo and an AI-generated cover image. This page shows how to check that each of those can be published.

Several documents interact, and it helps to know which one answers which question.

The Content License Agreement says that exporting a design that contains library content binds you to it, together with the Privacy Policy and the Terms of Use. The AI Product Terms in force at the time of this check are stated to be effective as of 26 June 2026, and earlier versions are kept in Canva's policy archive. Because these documents are updated, a page like this one can only point you to them; the habit worth building is to reread the current version before a commercial launch.

== Content categories and the strictest-wins rule

Library content is sorted into four categories, from the least to the most restrictive: Free Content, Pro Content, Branded Content and Education Content. The agreement states that a design mixing categories follows the most restrictive one, so a single Branded or Education element changes what the whole design may be used for.

.The four content categories and the strictest-wins rule
image::ui-ux-canva-licensing-responsible-ai-and-canva-shield-content-categories.svg["Four stacked boxes from Free Content at the bottom to Education Content at the top with an arrow showing increasing restriction",width=720]

[cols="1,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Category | What the agreement says | What it means in practice

| Free Content | Licensed perpetually and non-exclusively; may be used in more than one design, downloaded on its own and used in templates for distribution or sale, with some conditions | The most flexible category, but a design that also contains Pro Content cannot use these extra freedoms. Items labelled CC0 or public domain follow their own licence
| Pro Content | A One Design Use licence: one Canva design per licence, and the content may not be copied, downloaded or distributed as a standalone item | A Free account pays a one-off fee per design to remove the watermark; a subscription issues a licence automatically on each export, including for a resized copy
| Branded Content | Provided with third-party brands and labelled as such in the content source information; personal use only unless that information says otherwise | Do not use it for merchandise or in a way that implies the brand endorses you
| Education Content | Labelled with a badge titled Resource; educational, non-commercial use only | Not for promotional work, even if the rest of the design is Free Content
|===

A licence is issued when you export a design, one for each piece of content in it. The permitted uses for ordinary Free and Pro content are broad: promotional and advertising projects including print, social media posts and profile images, school projects, books and book covers, online publications, and prints and posters for promotion or resale. Several limits matter for a book app launch:

* *Standalone use.* A photo used on its own, without other design elements, has restrictions; for example it cannot be printed on products for sale.
* *Pixel limit online.* Pro Content in an online publication such as a web page or ebook is limited to a maximum of 480,000 total pixels per un-edited file, with exceptions for a website published through Canva's dedicated website publishing and for a Canva-hosted design embedded in a third-party site. The agreement gives 600 by 800 pixels as an example.
* *Music.* Pro music can be used in online ads, but not in traditional paid-channel ads such as television, cinema, radio, podcasts or billboards, and it cannot be remixed or used as a standalone download. Popular Music has its own licence and cannot be used for any commercial purpose, including advertising.
* *Prohibited uses.* Among others: re-selling or redistributing content, removing copyright or trademark notices, using "Editorial Use Only" content commercially, and using content for machine-learning purposes.
* *Client work.* Section 4A lets you transfer a design containing Free or Pro content to a single client for their own use, under a written agreement that keeps you responsible for their compliance.
* *Education accounts.* Education users may only use Pro Content for educational, non-commercial purposes and need a separate account for commercial designs.

Source: https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/[Canva Content License Agreement]

=== Branded content from partners

Branded Content is provided by Canva in collaboration with third-party brands. The agreement includes a section on Disney-themed templates and elements, introduced on 18 October 2024 for Pro, Education and Nonprofit accounts, for personal or educational use only and with its own rules that prevail over the general terms. Treat any partner-branded template as personal use unless its source information says otherwise, and never place it in a campaign that promotes your own product.

Source: https://www.canva.com/licensing-explained/[Canva: Licensing explained]

== Logos, trademarks and selling templates

A trademark has to be a unique symbol used exclusively by its owner, and library content is licensed non-exclusively, so other Canva users may use the same item. The official guidance is therefore:

* You may not use Free or Pro library content in a trademark, with the exceptions of fonts, simple shapes and lines.
* To make a logo you can register, design it from scratch with Canva's fonts, simple shapes and lines, or upload an original graphic that you or a commissioned designer created.
* Logo templates are customisable by anyone, so rights to the result are non-exclusive and it cannot be registered as a trademark. They suit small or personal projects.
* Canva does not currently sell exclusive rights to a library graphic.
* Templates for sale: you may not use Pro Content in a template of any kind unless it is a template created for use on Canva; template designers are pointed to Canva's creators programme.

For Bookshelf, this means the logo built from upright book rectangles on a shelf line, drawn from simple shapes and the brand font, is suitable for registration work, while a stock book illustration dropped into the logo is not. Registration itself is a legal matter for a trademark professional. How Canva's own name and logo may be used is covered by the https://www.canva.com/policies/trademark-policy/[Canva Trademark Policy].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/trademarks-logo/[Canva Help Center: Trademark logos created on Canva]

== Responsible use of AI

The AI Product Terms govern Canva's AI-powered products. They start from a plain rule, "be a good human", and then add prohibitions on top of the Acceptable Use Policy. In the version in force on 2026-11-07, it is prohibited to use AI products to:

* mislead anyone that AI-generated content is human-generated
* remove, alter or disable provenance or metadata tags from AI-generated content, including C2PA metadata
* provide or obtain tailored advice that requires a licence, such as legal, medical or financial advice, without a qualified professional
* generate spam or malicious software
* implement fully automated decision making that is legally binding, has significant effects or otherwise breaks applicable laws

You also agree not to try to bypass the safety features and content protections. The Help Center's practical guide for Magic Studio turns this into do and do-not lists: use descriptive, generic prompts; keep rights to any image you edit; get permission before editing photos of a recognisable person; and do not reference known brands, characters or people without permission. It also recommends disclosing AI assistance when you share AI-created content and reviewing all generated work for third-party material and anything inappropriate.

Source: https://www.canva.com/policies/ai-product-terms/[Canva AI Product Terms]

=== Input, output and ownership

The terms make you responsible for your input, meaning what you type or upload, and for the output, meaning what the tool generates. By using the tools you state that you have the rights to your input. As between you and Canva, and to the maximum extent the law permits, you keep your input and own your output, with an important exception: output that modifies or incorporates library content stays under the licence for that content.

.Where ownership of AI-created work comes from, and where it does not
image::ui-ux-canva-licensing-responsible-ai-and-canva-shield-ai-output-ownership.svg["Flow from input to AI product to output, then a split between work generated by the AI alone that you own and work that incorporates library content that stays licensed",width=720]

The terms give two examples. If you use an AI tool to edit a photo from the Canva library, you do not own that output. If you generate a presentation that includes library photos, you own the presentation as a whole but not the individual photos, which remain licensed under the Content License Agreement. Three further points are worth remembering:

* *Uniqueness.* Outputs may not be unique, and other users may receive similar results from similar prompts. Your ownership does not extend to other users' outputs.
* *Copyright protection.* Owning output as between you and Canva is different from copyright protection. The Help Center notes that many jurisdictions, including the US, do not currently give copyright protection to AI-created works, so you can use them but may be unable to stop others from copying them. It recommends local legal advice about the impact on your plans.
* *Trademarks.* AI can help brainstorm a name, but search for availability before registering, and note that similar prompts can produce similar results, which makes AI-generated graphics a poor basis for a trademark. A trademark attorney is the right adviser.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-magic-studio-safely-and-legally/[Canva Help Center: How to use Magic Studio safely and legally]

=== Usage limits and technology partners

The terms say that Canva may impose usage limits, rate limits or fair-use limits on AI features, that these are operational controls rather than fixed entitlements, and that you may be able to buy additional usage such as an AI top-up add-on. Some products are not available in every country or language. Canva also uses technology partners for some AI products, and your input may be shared with them solely to provide the functionality; your privacy settings control whether Canva and its partners may use your data to improve AI services for everyone. The output of AI is not verified by Canva, and the terms advise independent professional advice before relying on it in a commercial context.

== Canva Shield

Canva Shield is the name Canva gives to its collection of trust, safety and privacy tools and commitments around AI. The official page presents four parts.

.The four parts of Canva Shield as presented on the official page
image::ui-ux-canva-licensing-responsible-ai-and-canva-shield-shield-layers.svg["Four columns labelled trust and safety, privacy settings, creators and indemnification, each with a short list of what it covers",width=720]

. *Trust and safety.* Automatic review of inputs and outputs, blocked terms, reporting options and machine-learning technology to prevent unsafe or inappropriate content. The AI Safety page adds that tools aim to prevent content about medical topics, self-harm and mental health, sexually explicit material and political topics including politicians and elections. AI products are for users aged 13 and over, except under teacher supervision in Canva Education.
. *Privacy settings.* You control whether Canva trains on your private content. Canva states that it handles data under its Privacy Policy and, where applicable, its Data Processing Addendum. The Trust Center says Canva does not use your content to improve AI-powered features unless that is consistent with your Privacy Settings, which you can review and change at any time.
. *Creators.* Canva commits more than 200 million US dollars to creators over the coming years, including an AI royalty programme.
. *Indemnification for Enterprise.* Eligible Canva Enterprise customers are indemnified for AI output, at no extra cost, when using Magic Studio products, in the rare event of an intellectual property claim. Eligibility is arranged with Canva sales; the official page does not list the criteria, so ask Canva rather than assuming that your account qualifies.

The Shield page also quotes usage statistics that change over time; this page does not repeat them. Indemnification is a contractual remedy for a covered claim and not a reason to skip review: the Help Center still says you are ultimately responsible for the content you create and should check that it contains no third-party material.

Source: https://www.canva.com/safe-ai-canva-shield/[Canva Shield: Ensuring safe AI use at Canva]

=== Reporting and false flags

Moderation sometimes flags a harmless prompt. The AI Safety page asks you to report both unsafe output and incorrect flags: use the Report button in the app for text-to-image results, or the AI feedback report form for other AI tools. Do not try to rephrase a prompt to get around a block; bypassing the safety systems is prohibited.

Source: https://www.canva.com/policies/ai-safety/[AI Safety at Canva]

=== Privacy settings and the Data Processing Addendum

Privacy Settings are an account-level page where you control whether your data can be used to improve AI-powered features. The Trust Center has a separate FAQ for Education users and for Business users, and it lists the Data Processing Addendum as applying to Canva Business and Canva Enterprise only. Team administrators should review the settings that apply to their organisation, and everyone else can open Privacy Settings from the account settings. Where those controls sit in the account menu moves between releases, so use the Privacy Settings link in the Trust Center rather than a menu path.

Source: https://www.canva.com/trust/privacy/[Canva Trust Center: Privacy]

== Procedure 1: check the licence of the Book of the week post

The Bookshelf launch post uses a library photo and a library graphic around the brand logo. Before scheduling it, check each library element.

.Storyboard for checking the licence of the launch post
image::ui-ux-canva-licensing-responsible-ai-and-canva-shield-storyboard-check-launch-post.svg["Five panels: spot library elements, open content source information, read the category, decide to keep or replace, export the post",width=720]

. Open the *Book of the week* post in the editor and note which elements come from the library rather than from your uploads or the Bookshelf brand kit.
. For each library element, hover over it in the Elements panel and open the three-dot menu to reach the content source information. The Content License Agreement describes this route; the editor is redrawn regularly, so if the menu looks different, follow the Help Center's current wording.
. Read the label. Free, Pro, Branded and Education labels each carry different rules, and CC0 or public-domain items point to their own licence.
. Decide. If the post is promotional, replace any Branded or Education item, and replace library artwork that you intend to place inside the logo itself.
. Export the post. The licence for each piece of content is issued at export, at no cost for Free content, and for Pro content by the one-off fee on a Free account or automatically with a subscription.

Source: https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/[Canva Content License Agreement]

== Procedure 2: generate, review and disclose an AI-made cover image

The campaign also needs an illustration of a reading nook for the story format. The steps below use any of Canva's image generation tools; the tools themselves are described on xref:ui-ux/canva/magic-studio-tools.adoc[Magic Studio Tools] and xref:ui-ux/canva/ai-image-and-video-generation.adoc[AI Image and Video Generation].

.Storyboard for generating, reviewing and disclosing an AI-made image
image::ui-ux-canva-licensing-responsible-ai-and-canva-shield-storyboard-review-ai-output.svg["Five panels: write a generic prompt, compare results, review the chosen image, keep report or regenerate, add a disclosure line",width=720]

. Write a descriptive, generic prompt, for example a cosy reading nook with warm light, without naming brands, characters or real people.
. Generate and compare the results. If you want similar results, use the tool's option for more like the one you prefer rather than rewording the prompt to evade a block.
. Review the chosen image closely for logos, lettering, faces or anything that looks like third-party material.
. If something is unsafe, use the Report button; if a harmless prompt was blocked, report the incorrect flag; otherwise regenerate or keep the image.
. Add a short line such as "illustration created with AI assistance" to the post caption or the first page of a longer publication, as the Help Center recommends. Do not remove provenance or metadata tags from the exported file.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-magic-studio-safely-and-legally/[Canva Help Center: How to use Magic Studio safely and legally]

== Desktop and mobile

The licence and AI rules are identical on every platform because they attach to the account and the content. The differences are in how you reach the information. Content source information, the Report button and the Privacy Settings page are reached through menus whose layout differs between the desktop editor, the web editor and the mobile apps, and Canva changes them between releases. Where a step in this page describes a hover or a three-dot menu, it describes the desktop editor.

== Common pitfalls

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Pitfall | What to do instead

| Treating one Pro licence as valid for every design | A Pro licence covers one Canva design. A resized copy needs its own licence, issued automatically with a subscription and paid for on a Free account
| Assuming a mixed design takes the loosest rule | The most restrictive category in the design applies to all of it
| Using a library graphic inside the logo | Use fonts, simple shapes and lines, or upload original artwork
| Assuming AI output is exclusively yours | Similar prompts can give similar results, and copyright protection depends on your jurisdiction
| Assuming ownership of AI edits to library photos | The photo stays under its licence, so you do not own that output
| Prompting with brand, character or celebrity names | Use generic descriptions and obtain permission when real people or brands matter
| Stripping provenance metadata from AI files | Prohibited by the AI Product Terms
| Relying on indemnification as a review substitute | It is a contractual remedy for eligible Enterprise customers and you remain responsible for the content
| Using Popular Music in a commercial video | It has a separate licence and cannot be used commercially, including for advertising
|===
