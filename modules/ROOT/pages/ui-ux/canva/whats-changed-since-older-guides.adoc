This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center, newsroom, pricing page and developer documentation on 2026-11-07. It covers the whole Canva product family on the web, the desktop app and the mobile apps, and it applies to every plan (Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits) because it is about vocabulary and locations, not about one feature. Where a change is plan-specific the linked page says so, and prices are never quoted here; they are published at https://www.canva.com/pricing/. No AI tier applies to the page itself. For status, Canva AI 2.0 launched as a research preview on 2026-04-16 and is documented in the Help Center without a preview label, but no general-availability date is published and availability varies by account, plan and region.

The two printed guides that this sub-section cross-checks, *Canva For Dummies* by Jesse Stay (John Wiley & Sons, 2025) and *Canva for Beginners* by Yellow Pen (2024), were written against the editor and plan structure of 2024 and early 2025. Much has moved since. This page does not reproduce either book. It states each outdated idea in its own words, gives the current position with a date, and links to the page of this sub-section that explains the topic in full. Every other page of the sub-section carries its own short "older guides" table for its topic; this page collects them all in one place.

The running example is *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma sub-section]. Its launch campaign is built across this sub-section, and the procedures below use it to show how to verify an old instruction before you follow it.

== Why older guides drift

Canva is a continuously deployed service, and four kinds of change make printed instructions go stale.

* *Renames*: a feature keeps working but its name or the name of its plan changes, so a search for the old name finds nothing.
* *Moves*: the feature is still there but sits somewhere else after a redesign, so the old click path ends in the wrong place.
* *Retirements*: the feature or the whole product was shut down, as happened to an external mockup site and a document-to-deck button.
* *Additions*: a whole product or capability appeared that the guides cannot mention, such as spreadsheets, forms, email design, a conversational assistant and the developer platform.

Concepts such as contrast, alignment, hierarchy and brand consistency do not age. The guides stay a good teacher of what to do; this page helps you find out where and under which name to do it today.

== The editor layout, before and after

The most visible change for a reader holding an older guide is the editor itself. The redesign that Canva calls the "glow up" replaced the fixed toolbar above the canvas with a floating quick actions toolbar that follows the selection, added an edit panel beside the canvas, merged shapes, lines, sticky notes, text, tables and drawing into a Tools tab, and gave Canva AI its own tab. Tab names and order differ between accounts, devices and releases, so treat the names in the figure as search terms rather than fixed positions.

.A generic editor before and after the redesign: separate Draw tab and fixed toolbar versus Tools tab, quick actions and edit panel
image::ui-ux-canva-whats-changed-since-older-guides-editor-old-vs-new.svg["Two schematic editors side by side: an older one with a fixed toolbar and a Draw tab, and the current one with a quick actions toolbar, an edit panel and a Tools tab",width=720]

On the phone the tabs are now Canva AI, Create, Templates, Your Designs and More, rolling out gradually; Home became Create and Projects became Your Designs. The Help Center also names two apps, Canva Classic and Canva Lite, that differ in features. The full tour is on xref:ui-ux/canva/editor-tour.adoc[Editor Tour].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/glow-up/[Canva Help Center: Get to know the glowed up editor and homepage]

== Procedure: check a Bookshelf tutorial against today's Canva

A blog post written in 2024 explains how to set up the *Bookshelf* launch post using "your Teams plan", the "Draw tab" and "Magic Switch". The storyboard checks each claim before you follow it.

.Five steps to verify an older tutorial and record the correction
image::ui-ux-canva-whats-changed-since-older-guides-storyboard-check-a-tutorial.svg["Five panels: list the claims, check the publication date, search the Help Center, compare with the editor, and record the old and new names with the check date",width=720]

. List every name the tutorial depends on: the subscription tier, each tool and each menu. For the Bookshelf post these are Teams, Draw and Magic Switch.
. Note the publication date of the tutorial. Anything older than the changes in the timeline above, and especially older than 2025-10-30 or 2026-04-16, deserves a check.
. Search the Help Center at https://www.canva.com/help/ for the current name of each tool, and read the article rather than a search snippet.
. Open the editor and compare the label and the location with the article. If the name or place differs, trust the editor and the Help Center over the tutorial.
. Write the correction down with the date, for example "Teams is now Business for new sign-ups; Draw is under Tools; Magic Switch is Magic Resize, checked 2026-11-07". Share it with the team in a pinned note of the campaign folder.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/[Canva Help Center]

== Where to find help

* The https://www.canva.com/help/[Help Center] is the first stop: articles for each feature, a search box and a Help Assistant for product questions. It also has a What's new in Canva section.
* https://www.canva.com/help/contact-us/[Contact Canva Support] explains how to reach support; support is faster when you are logged in to your account.
* The https://www.canva.com/community/[Canva community] pages describe community groups, Creator programs and Community Labs for educators, nonprofits and creators.
* https://www.canva.com/design-school/[Design School] offers courses, lessons and quick guides, and https://www.canva.com/learn/[Canva Learn] publishes design articles.
* Developers use the https://www.canva.dev/docs/apps/[Canva Developers documentation] and its changelogs.

== Pitfalls

* Treating a search result or an article without a date as proof that a feature is current. The Help Center articles often carry no change date, so confirm in the editor.
* Following a screenshot-based tutorial literally. Icons and the order of tabs differ between releases and devices.
* Assuming a feature seen in an announcement is in your account. Rollouts are gradual and depend on plan, language and region.
* Reading "research preview" as "generally available". Canva AI 2.0 has no published general-availability date.
* Treating mentions of Teams in Help Center articles as a sign that Teams is still sold to new customers.
* Quoting prices from a book. They differ by region and change; link https://www.canva.com/pricing/ instead.
