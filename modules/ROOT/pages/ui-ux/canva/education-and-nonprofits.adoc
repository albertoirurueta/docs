This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers the three programs that give schools, universities and charities access to Canva's premium features outside the standard Free, Pro, Business and Enterprise ladder described on xref:ui-ux/canva/plans-pricing-and-ai-allowance.adoc[Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance]: *Canva Education* for K-12 teachers, schools and their invited students, *Canva Campus* for higher education institutions, and *Canva for Nonprofits* for eligible charities. Education and Nonprofits are free for organizations that Canva verifies; Campus is a paid arrangement negotiated with Canva's sales team. Prices and thresholds are not repeated here; the public plan comparison is at https://www.canva.com/pricing/. All the features described on this page are generally available, although the Help Center notes that parts of the Learn Grid roll out gradually, so what you see can vary by region and account.

The page follows the running example of this sub-section. *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma sub-section], is used here by two imaginary organizations: a school teacher who runs the "Bookshelf Reading Club" class and assigns a "Book of the week" reading response, and a literacy charity, the "Bookshelf Readers Fund", that applies for the nonprofit program and then invites its volunteers.

== Which program fits which organization

The three programs are aimed at different kinds of organization, and the eligibility rules are strict and checked by Canva. Choosing the wrong one is the most common reason an application fails.

Some groups are explicitly not eligible for Canva Education. The Help Center lists, among others, certified teachers who are not currently teaching, public librarians, preschool and daycare assistants, colleges and universities including their lecturers, staff and students, continuing and adult education institutions, organizations that run extracurricular activities outside of school, early-stage education start-ups, parents, and homeschool educators who are not licensed or employed by a K-12 institution. Nonprofit organizations that do not deliver teaching directly are also excluded from Education, and are pointed to the nonprofit program, which has its own eligibility guidelines. Sports clubs, professional associations and businesses are examples of organization types that the nonprofit program does not accept.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/about-canva-for-education/[Canva Help Center: About Canva Education]

== Canva Education for K-12

Canva Education is free for eligible teachers and students anywhere in the world and is not restricted to any country. Access is tied to a verified role, not to a device or a paid seat. A teacher who signs up with a recognised school email address normally gets access immediately, although Canva may still ask to verify teaching credentials. Everyone else is reviewed by Canva after uploading evidence.

The Help Center describes Education as compliant with FERPA, COPPA and GDPR and as offering safe-for-school content, and states that teachers need to get parental consent for children below 13. Treat those statements as Canva's own claims and follow your school's or district's data-protection process before inviting students; the decision is yours and your institution's.

=== Apply for Canva Education

. Open the Canva Education sign-up page and sign up with a verified educational email domain. You can also sign up with your school or district's Clever, Microsoft or Google account.
. Follow the sign-up prompts and select that you are a Teacher.
. Select the type of school you teach at.
. Enter your first and last name, country, school name, address and school website.
. If you signed up with a verified educational email domain, access is granted right away. Otherwise upload a photo or scan of at least one document that shows your name, school name and teaching position, then select Submit.
. If you skipped the document step, you can submit it later from the link in the Help Center article. Recently signed-up teachers may also see a homepage alert, for 30 days, with a direct link to the application.

Teachers in Japan, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, the United States and South Korea only need to provide a copy of their teaching license; elsewhere the accepted documents depend on the location and can include a school ID that indicates teaching status or employment evidence. A school, district or institution can also ask Canva's Education team to roll Canva out to the whole organization.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/about-canva-for-education/[Canva Help Center: About Canva Education]

== Classes

A *class* is a group of students inside the school team. Classes let a teacher share learning material and collaborate with a specific group. Teachers and admins can create classes; students can join classes but cannot create them. There is no stated limit on the number of classes, so the Help Center suggests organizing them by grade, subject or teaching period. To let other teachers at your school find the team and request to join, adjust the team settings described in the Help Center article on school teams.

=== Create the Bookshelf Reading Club class

. On the homepage, select Classroom, or select your profile icon and then Settings.
. Open the Classes tab.
. Select Create class.
. Type the class name, for example "Reading Club 7B".
. Select Create. Then add students from the class page.

To rename a class later, open Settings, then the Class details tab, and select Edit under the name; teachers in a roster-managed school use My classes instead. From the Classroom homepage you can also select Classes, choose the class, open More under the People column and select Rename class. Students can only get Education access if a teacher invites them, which is done in Settings; see the Help Center article on inviting students and teachers to your class.

== Lessons and activities

A *lesson* is a folder of sequenced designs called *activities*. Teachers either start from a ready-to-teach lesson or build their own.

=== Use a ready-to-teach lesson

. On the homepage, search for the subject or topic. Relevant ready-to-teach lessons are listed. Alternatively open Templates and choose Education in the side panel, then select See all or filter by Resource Type, then Lessons.
. Select a lesson to see a preview with its grade, subject and designs.
. Select Use Lesson. It is saved to Projects with the lesson itself and the teacher answer keys.
. Open the lesson's main folder to view and edit the answer keys.

=== Build the Book of the week lesson from scratch

. On the homepage, select Projects.
. At the top right, select Add new, then Lesson. If you already have a folder, open it, select More and then Turn into a lesson.
. Rename the lesson and choose a banner from More, and write a description in the text box below the title.
. Select Add an activity to your lesson to add designs, such as the reading-response worksheet and a short quiz.
. Set each activity's Activity Experience: choose Resource when students only read it, or Solo work when each student should edit their own copy and share it back.
. Drag activities into the order you want students to follow them, and select Open Lesson to see the sequence in the editor, where the Navigation menu switches between activities.

To experience the lesson as a student does, open any activity and select View as student on the editor side panel; a banner confirms that you are viewing as a student, edits you make create a copy so the original is untouched, and Back to editing returns you to teacher mode.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/lessons-in-canva/[Canva Help Center: Publish lessons on Canva]

== Assignments

Once an activity exists, you can assign it to students by sharing a link or a code. Students need a Canva account and are prompted to sign up if they do not have one. Students receive an email that an assignment is waiting.

.How a link and a code differ, and how long each stays valid
image::ui-ux-canva-education-and-nonprofits-assignment-options.svg["Diagram of an assignment split into a shared link that stays active for 30 days and an assignment code that expires after 4 hours",width=720]

=== Assign the reading response

. Open the activity you want to assign.
. Select Assign in the top corner of the editor, or open Share and then Assign. Both open the same options panel.
. To share on another platform, select Share via link. Canva copies the link to the clipboard so you can paste it into an email, chat or your learning management system. The link stays active for 30 days and you can switch it off at any time with Deactivate link.
. To share a code, select Get assignment code in the Your assignment is ready window, then Copy code. The code expires after 4 hours.
. To assign a whole lesson instead, open the lesson, select Assign lesson, type the class or student names, optionally turn on the notification toggle and add a message, then select Assign. Copy the link in the confirmation if you want to send it yourself.

=== Review and track

From the homepage, select Classroom and open Assignments, where each assignment shows its title, the number of submissions ready for review and a progress bar. Select Create to start a new one or View all to see everything. Under Submissions, View all lists submissions that you can filter by class and by status. The Class dropdown appears only when you have classes, and shows All classes when you have just one. For a lesson, the Reporting tab summarizes how many students are Not started, In progress and Completed.

If you have edit access to another teacher's assignment design, granted by inviting you through the Share menu by name or email, you may see Add to my assignments, which adds it to your Classwork. Edit access through a share link may not show that option.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/publish-canva-assignments/[Canva Help Center: Share class assignments in Canva]

== Magic Activities and the Learn Grid

*Magic Activities* generates a learning activity from a prompt. The Help Center recommends the pattern "Students will be able to [action verb] about [specific topic]", a strong verb from Bloom's Taxonomy such as analyze, compare, evaluate, investigate, design, create or synthesize, and one specific learning goal per prompt. A good example is a prompt that asks students to evaluate a specific impact, whereas "learn about the environment" is too broad. Multistep or overly complex prompts confuse the result, so split them. Magic Activities works best for conceptual subjects such as humanities and sciences, and the Help Center asks teachers to review generated activities for age-appropriateness, accuracy and cultural sensitivity before classroom use.

For the Bookshelf class, a prompt such as "Students will be able to compare the narrators of two books from the reading list" gives a focused activity that you can then place in the lesson built above.

The *Learn Grid* is Canva's learning library. It holds two kinds of content: ready-to-teach resources, which are curriculum-mapped templates made for classrooms, and AI-generated content such as Create & Play games and Quiz Yourself quizzes. On Canva Education, select Education on the homepage; on other plans, select Templates and then the Education filter. Browse subject cards such as English Language Arts, Science, Mathematics or History, or combine filters for country, topic, grade, subject and category (for example presentation or worksheet). The country defaults to your location and applies the national curriculum automatically, and the Topic filter, which matches specific standards, is available in the United States, Australia, Indonesia and Mexico. The Learn Grid is available to everyone with a Canva account, but some resources are locked behind a premium plan.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/magic-activities/[Canva Help Center: Get better results with Magic Activities]

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-learn-grid/[Canva Help Center: Learn Grid]

== Classroom tools and LMS integrations

Education integrates with the tools classrooms already use: Canvas, Schoology, D2L, Moodle, Blackboard, Google Classroom and Microsoft Teams. Connecting an LMS such as Canvas, Blackboard, D2L, Moodle or Schoology is done by a school administrator, and the Help Center has a separate set-up guide for each. Students on Education also receive text or visual sticker comments as feedback, and eligible Education users get access to Affinity; K-12 schools that do not want Affinity can ask their Customer Success Manager to opt out, whereas higher education institutions ask the manager to turn it on.

== Canva Campus for higher education

Colleges and universities are not eligible for Canva Education. Canva Campus is the higher education offering. Its features, per the Help Center, are a free student team with premium features for all students, separate teams for staff and students, SSO to manage staff and student access, streamlined admin reporting and permissions, LMS integrations such as Canvas and Blackboard, and dedicated support. Students can create groups, create folders and submit work through the LMS. Faculty and staff can create groups, folders and Brand Kits, use LTI integrations to review student work, and use approval workflows, analytics dashboards and brand controls, while admins manage billing and SSO. The administration side is covered on xref:ui-ux/canva/administration-enterprise-and-security.adoc[Administration, Enterprise and Security].

An institution can begin with Canva Business for its staff and move to Campus once it reaches the license threshold, which is based on the total full-time student body size; at that point premium accounts for all students are unlocked at no additional cost. Staff pricing is aligned with Canva Enterprise, and details by institution size come from Canva's sales team.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/about-canva-for-campus/[Canva Help Center: About Canva Campus]

== Canva for Nonprofits

Canva for Nonprofits gives eligible organizations all the premium features of Canva Pro plus team and collaboration tools, at no cost. The Help Center lists premium images, videos, music and graphics, downloads with transparent backgrounds, an upgraded Brand Kit with more colour palettes and your own fonts and logos, publishing designs as brand templates, sharing designs and folders with your team, template share links and Affinity access. Brand building is covered on xref:ui-ux/canva/brand-kit-and-brand-voice.adoc[Brand Kit and Brand Voice] and brand templates on xref:ui-ux/canva/brand-templates-controls-and-approvals.adoc[Brand Templates, Controls and Approvals].

The free access applies to *one team of up to 50 members*. Additional seats are offered at 50% off on Canva Enterprise, for which you contact Canva's sales team. Nonprofit organizations that deliver teaching directly may qualify for Education instead; the two programs have separate guidelines and verification.

=== Apply as the Bookshelf Readers Fund

. Check the nonprofit eligibility guidelines for your country and organization category, such as Registered Nonprofit, Social Impact Organization or Public Health Organization.
. Make sure your Canva account has a valid email address. Use one that matches your organization's domain where possible.
. Read the program's terms and conditions.
. Fill out and submit the application form. If you chose "Non-profit or charity" while signing up, a homepage alert links to the form for 30 days. At any other time, open Settings, then the Billing tab, and select Upgrade to Canva for Nonprofits.
. Complete the verification process. Canva updates you by email within 72 hours; confirming your affiliation can take longer.
. After approval, invite your members to the single nonprofit team. Until the decision, the team can keep using Canva Free.

=== If the application is rejected

Canva explains the outcome by email. The usual causes are missing or unclear documentation, an organization type that does not match the selected category, an affiliation that could not be confirmed (a signed letter of affiliation or a volunteer ID showing your name, role and organization helps), or a mission that does not meet the program, as with sports clubs, professional associations and businesses. You can reapply at any time with corrected information, with no penalty. A "Your organization is already using Canva" error means a team for that organization already exists, so ask its administrator for access or contact Canva Support with the organization's name, domain or nonprofit registration.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/canva-for-nonprofits/[Canva Help Center: Canva for Nonprofits]

== Pitfalls

* Education is for K-12 only. University staff and students, parents and homeschool educators without a K-12 license do not qualify; higher education goes through Campus.
* Students cannot self-enrol in Education; a teacher has to invite them.
* A shared assignment link stops working after 30 days and an assignment code after 4 hours, so share a fresh one if students report a dead link.
* The nonprofit offer covers one team of up to 50 members; larger organizations need Enterprise seats.
* Always review AI-generated activities before using them with a class.
* AI availability for Education accounts may be limited by school administration, and Canva AI features vary by plan, as explained on xref:ui-ux/canva/canva-ai-assistant.adoc[Canva AI Assistant].
