This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers colour in Canva (web, desktop app and mobile apps): picking a colour for an element, text or background, building gradients, storing brand colours in palettes, applying a whole colour scheme with a colour theme, copying a visual style with Style Match and checking that text stays readable. The core colour tools are generally available on every plan, Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits. Gradients are free for all Canva users. The limits differ by plan: a Free Brand Kit holds one palette of up to three colours, whereas Pro, Teams, Business, Education, Enterprise and Nonprofits accounts can create 100 palettes of up to 100 colours each, and colour themes need one of those paid or free-premium plans. Prices are not repeated here; they are published at https://www.canva.com/pricing/. Style Match is an AI feature that draws on the Canva AI allowance (see xref:ui-ux/canva/plans-pricing-and-ai-allowance.adoc[Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance]); the Help Center does not label it with a tier, so check the AI usage article for your account.

Canva is a continuously deployed service and the Color panel moves between releases, so this page describes controls by name. Its drawings are deliberately schematic sketches of a generic editor, not screenshots, and their colours are the neutral ones of this documentation, not Canva's own.

The running example is *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma sub-section]. Its launch campaign is built in Canva. This page gives the "Book of the week" Instagram post a gradient background and recolours the five-page launch carousel with a brand colour theme. For the theory behind a good colour choice (the colour wheel, harmonies, contrast and hierarchy), see xref:ui-ux/canva/design-principles-in-canva.adoc[Design Principles in Canva], which also links the UX-process pages of the Figma sub-section instead of repeating them here.

== Where colour lives in the editor

Colour has three homes. The first is the *Color tile* in the quick actions toolbar: select an element, text or a background and the tile opens the Color panel for it. The panel shows your *Document colors* (the colours already used in the design), the default colours, a Gradients section and, when your account has a Brand Kit, your brand palettes. The second home is the *Brand* tab of the side panel, where palettes and colour themes are applied and, with edit access, managed (see xref:ui-ux/canva/brand-kit-and-brand-voice.adoc[Brand Kit and Brand Voice]). The third is the Transparency control of the toolbar, which fades a whole element rather than one colour. The editor layout itself is explained on xref:ui-ux/canva/editor-tour.adoc[Editor Tour].

The improved Color panel shows more default colours than before, with display names when you hover over them. Colours that come from a Brand Kit show the names given when the palette was created.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/creating-using-gradients/[Canva Help Center: Create and use color gradients]

== Solid colours and transparency

To change a colour, select the element or text, choose the Color tile on the toolbar and pick a swatch: a document colour, a default colour or a brand colour. Under Document colors the plus button adds a new colour; in a Brand Kit panel the plus icon also accepts a hex code. To make an element see-through, select it (hold Shift and click to select several), choose Transparency on the toolbar above the editor and drag the slider or type a value. For a solid fill, the colour selector flyout also has its own Transparency slider when that option is available. Transparency works on backgrounds, text, elements, photos and videos.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/transparency/[Canva Help Center: Adjust element transparency]

== Gradients

A gradient blends two or more colours. Canva applies gradients to text, backgrounds, shapes, table cells, frames and Brand Kits. In the Color panel, the Gradients section lists default gradient styles; hovering one shows its name, and selecting it applies it. To build your own, open the Color tile, use the plus under Document colors, switch to the Gradient tab and add gradient colours. You can add up to 10 colours. Select a colour tile and use the delete icon to remove it, drag the colour stops to rearrange them and choose a preset under Styles to change how the colours are laid out.

=== Fade a colour to transparent

A gradient can fade out gently. Select the element, open the Color tile, add a new colour under Document colors, go to the Gradient tab and select a colour stop, then lower its transparency to 0%. This is different from changing the transparency of the whole element. For a smooth fade, use the same base colour for the solid and the transparent stops; otherwise the blend can pass through a muddy intermediate shade.

=== Where gradients do not work

* Charts and sheet cells do not support gradients yet. A workaround is a separate gradient text box beside the chart.
* Poll and quiz elements do not support them either.
* Canva Docs does not support gradients on inline text; they work only in Design Blocks and Banners.
* Gradient text may show only the first colour for underline, strikethrough, superscript and subscript, and some text effects do not combine well with gradients.
* PDF and SVG exports keep gradient text, but macOS Preview can show it as a solid colour. For an accurate preview use Adobe apps, or export PNG or JPG.
* Colour themes (below) replace gradients on elements and text with solid colours.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/creating-using-gradients/[Canva Help Center: Create and use color gradients]

== Palettes

A palette is the set of colours that define your brand. Palettes live in the Brand Kit: from the homepage side panel select Brand, pick the Brand Kit if there are several, and select Colors. You can add a palette, add a colour to a palette, delete a colour, rename a palette and delete a palette; a Brand Kit must keep at least one palette. In a team, only owners, administrators and brand designers create and edit brand palettes. Palettes you create appear in the editor's Color panel, and for a team they are available to every member. Administrators on Teams, Business and Enterprise plans can set Brand Controls that restrict members to the brand palettes only.

=== CMYK values for print

For print, add the CMYK equivalents of a brand colour: under Colors select a colour tile and choose Add CMYK; for a new colour, add the colour first and then choose Add CMYK. Per the Help Center, only Pro users and Business or Teams administrators and brand designers can add CMYK colours to a team palette. Print settings are covered on xref:ui-ux/canva/frames-grids-guides-and-print-layout.adoc[Frames, Grids, Guides and Print Layout].

=== Managing brand colours while you design

You do not have to leave the editor. Select an element, open the Color tile, choose the Brand Kit (a dropdown appears when you have more than one) and select Edit. There you can add a colour with the plus icon, change one by picking a new colour or entering a hex code, delete one, and add, rename or delete a palette.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/color-palettes/[Canva Help Center: Set up and use Brand Kit color palettes]

== Colour themes

A palette says which colours are yours; a *colour theme* says how they are applied across a design. A theme assigns colours to backgrounds, elements and text at once, so one action recolours a page consistently. Themes are available on Pro, Teams, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits accounts that have Brand Kit access, and you need Brand Kit edit access to create or edit them (in a team Brand Kit, only owners, administrators and brand designers). You cannot create a theme from scratch. You create one from a suggestion that appears when your Brand Templates match your Brand Kit fonts and colours, or by generating themes from an existing design.

To apply a theme, open the design, select Brand in the side panel and, under Colors, hover over a theme and select Shuffle; select Shuffle again to preview other combinations, or use the theme card's More options to apply a specific combination. A theme applies to the current page by default; choose Apply to all pages to update the whole design.

Themes recolour backgrounds (including gradients), SVG graphics and text. They do not apply to PNG or JPG images, charts or locked elements, and gradients on elements or text are replaced with solid colours. Themes are not available in Canva Docs, whiteboards, emails or sheets, where you apply brand colours with the toolbar's colour picker instead. If an element does not change, check its type, unlock it or recolour it manually.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/color-themes/[Canva Help Center: Add and apply color themes]

== Style Match and Copy style

Two features copy a look from one element to another. *Copy style* copies formatting such as fonts, colours, filters and adjustments without AI. *Style Match* uses AI to recreate the look of one image on another, and each use consumes some Canva AI usage. To copy a style, select the source element, choose the Copy style icon on the toolbar, then choose Copy visual style (the art style) or Copy basic style (properties such as transparency and colour); the cursor becomes a paint roller, and you select the graphic or photo to restyle. To apply a ready-made style such as Cartoon, Sketch or 3D Art, select a graphic or photo, choose Edit, then Style Match in the side panel and See all; Remove undoes the style. You can apply a graphic's style to a photo. To reuse the look of a photo, an uploaded image or an image made with Canva's AI tools, select the image, choose Style in the toolbar, then Copy visual style, and select the image to restyle. If you see "You've reached the limit for now", wait 24 hours. Style Match is not available in Canva China, and applied to photos of people it can change facial features or skin tones, so review results.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/style-match/[Canva Help Center: Copy and match image styles]

== Contrast and accessibility

A beautiful scheme still fails if text cannot be read. Design Accessibility (File, then Accessibility, then Check Design Accessibility) groups issues by typography, colour contrast and alternative text. For colour contrast, it flags text whose colour is too close to its background, such as yellow on white, and offers suggested text colours you can apply from the flagged item; if none suit, change the font or background colour. The tool is not a compliance guarantee. See xref:ui-ux/canva/accessibility-in-canva.adoc[Accessibility in Canva] for the full checklist and xref:web/accessibility.adoc[Web accessibility] for the WCAG background.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/using-design-accessibility/[Canva Help Center: Use Design Accessibility]

== Colour tools on canva.com

Canva also publishes colour reference pages outside the editor, including a colour palette generator, a colour wheel with colour theory and colour meanings. They are web tools for exploring ideas, and they do not change the palettes in your Brand Kit.

Source: https://www.canva.com/colors/[Canva Colors]

== Desktop and mobile

The Color tile, gradient editing, palettes and themes follow the same steps in a browser, the desktop app and the mobile apps, where the help articles show a Mobile tab for each procedure. On a phone the toolbar is split into a floating toolbar for the selected element and a keyboard toolbar for text, so reach the Color tile from there. The Help Center shows no dedicated colour keyboard shortcuts; undo a colour change with Command + Z on macOS or Ctrl + Z on Windows.

== Procedures on the Bookshelf campaign

=== Give the "Book of the week" post a gradient background

. Open the *Bookshelf Book of the Week* Instagram post and select the background of the page.
. On the toolbar, select the Color tile.
. Under Document colors, select the plus to add a new colour, then open the Gradient tab.
. Under Gradient colors, add gradient colours with the plus (up to 10), set the first to the Bookshelf indigo and drag the stops to rearrange them.
. Choose a preset under Styles to change how the colours are laid out.
. To let the post fade out into the page, select the last stop and lower its transparency to 0%, keeping the same base colour for both stops.
. Select the title and check it against the new background with Design Accessibility.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/creating-using-gradients/[Canva Help Center: Create and use color gradients]

=== Recolour the launch carousel with a brand colour theme

. Open the *Bookshelf Launch Carousel* design, which has five pages.
. Select Brand in the side panel and open Colors. Confirm that the *Bookshelf* palette (indigo, amber, ink, tint and cream) is there; with edit access, add any missing colour.
. Scroll to Themes. If none exists, select Generate, search for the Book of the week post, select Generate color themes, choose a combination and select Add to Brand Kit.
. Hover over a theme and select Shuffle; select Shuffle again to preview other combinations.
. Select Apply to all pages so that all five pages share the scheme, then check that PNG or JPG images and charts, which a theme does not recolour, still look right.
. Press Command + Z (Ctrl + Z on Windows) if the result is not right.

== Common pitfalls

* Themes skip PNG and JPG images, charts and locked elements and turn gradients into solid colours; recolour those by hand.
* Gradients are not supported in charts, sheet cells, polls and quizzes, or in inline text in Canva Docs.
* A fade that looks muddy usually has different base colours on its solid and transparent stops.
* Themes apply to the current page unless you choose Apply to all pages.
* A Free Brand Kit holds one palette of up to three colours; the other plans allow far more.
* Only owners, administrators and brand designers edit team palettes and themes; members may see only brand palettes when Brand Controls are on.
* Gradient text can look solid in macOS Preview; export PNG or JPG for a faithful check.
* Style Match uses Canva AI usage and can alter people in photos; Copy style does not use AI.
