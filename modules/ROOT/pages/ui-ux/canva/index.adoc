*Canva* is a browser-based visual communication platform. Its _Visual Suite_ covers designs, presentations, docs, whiteboards, video, websites, sheets, code, forms and email, around brand tools and the Canva AI assistant. This sub-section is a practical, heavily illustrated guide to learning Canva and using it to produce on-brand visual content: the account, homepage and editor, design principles, every Visual Suite product, Canva AI, brand kits and templates, collaboration and administration, output formats and accessibility, apps and integrations, and the Canva Developers SDK. It belongs to the xref:ui-ux/index.adoc[UI / UX] section. Every page was checked against the official documentation on 2026-11-07 and describes the editor after its "glow up" redesign.

[discrete]
=== Scope

* *In scope*: every area of the official Canva documentation listed in the <<_coverage_map_official_documentation,coverage map>>, explained with original schematic figures, numbered procedures on one running scenario and a link to the official article each procedure derives from.
* *Also in scope*: the concepts taught by two published Canva books (see the <<_bibliography,bibliography>>). The books were used only to decide which concepts to cover and in what order. Every procedure is written against the current official documentation, and where a book is out of date the page says so in prose with dates, collected on xref:ui-ux/canva/whats-changed-since-older-guides.adoc[What Changed Since Older Guides]. No book text, figure, screenshot or template is reproduced.
* *Out of scope*: prices (pages name plans and AI tiers, never amounts; see https://www.canva.com/pricing/[Canva pricing]) and any third-party tool beyond a short, labelled ecosystem mention.
* *Figures*: all figures are original generic schematic drawings. They contain no Canva logo, icon, template design or help-centre image, in line with the https://www.canva.com/policies/trademark-policy/[Canva Trademark Policy].

=== Glossary

Short definitions of terms used throughout the sub-section.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
|Term |Meaning

|Visual Suite
|Canva's family of products that share one editor: designs, presentations, Docs, whiteboards, video and websites.

|Canva AI
|The conversational, agentic assistant introduced as Canva AI 2.0 on 2026-04-16, built on the Canva Design Model.

|Magic Studio
|The collective name of the individual AI tools such as Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Resize and Magic Eraser.

|Brand Kit
|A saved set of colours, fonts, logos and other assets that keeps designs on brand.

|Brand template
|A design published for a team to reuse, with locked regions that protect the brand.

|Bulk Create
|A feature that fills the fields of one design from the rows of a data file to produce many designs at once.

|Content Planner
|The calendar where designs are scheduled for publishing to connected social accounts.

|Bleed
|Extra artwork beyond the trim edge of a print design so that no white edge shows after cutting.

|Safe zone
|The inner area of a page where important content stays clear of the trim edge.

|Quick actions
|The toolbar and slash menu in the editor that put the next likely action one step away.

|AI allowance
|The monthly amount of Premium and Ultra AI usage included per member, extended by AI Pass or top-ups.

|Canva Developers SDK
|The developer platform with three surfaces: apps inside Canva, the REST APIs and the Canva MCP server.

|Intent
|The kind of experience a Canva app provides inside the editor, such as Design Editor or Data Connector.

|PKCE
|Proof Key for Code Exchange, the extension of OAuth 2.0 used by the authorization code flow of the REST APIs.

|MCP
|The Model Context Protocol, an open standard that lets AI assistants call tools such as Canva.

|Affinity
|The professional vector, photo and layout app from Canva, free to use with a Canva account.

=== How to read the figures

Procedures are illustrated as storyboards: a strip of numbered panels that shows a task as it progresses. Blue circles mark the steps, an amber circle marks the outcome and the caption under each panel names the action. Annotated interface diagrams use numbered callouts in the same way. Every figure is a schematic of a generic editor, not a screenshot, and each procedure is followed by a link to the official article it derives from.

=== Canva or Figma?

Both tools are browser-based and collaborative, but they answer different questions. Canva is built for visual communication: presentations, social posts, documents, video, websites and print, produced quickly from templates and kept on brand with brand kits. xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma] is built for product design: interface screens, components, design systems, prototypes and handoff to developers. Many teams use both, designing the product in Figma and producing its launch material in Canva, which is exactly what the Bookshelf scenario does. Canva can also import Figma files, as described on xref:ui-ux/canva/uploads-stock-media-and-imports.adoc[Uploads, Stock Media and Imports].

== Running example

Every example produces the launch campaign of *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma] sub-section. It is the same domain as the Express, Node.js, NestJS, FastAPI and Next.js sections of this site, so the design side, the marketing side and the code side meet. Across the pages the campaign grows in this order:

. A whiteboard brainstorm and a campaign brief Doc.
. The *Bookshelf brand kit*: colours and fonts from the Figma Bookshelf Design System, logo variants and a brand voice.
. A social campaign: an Instagram post and story, a LinkedIn banner and a YouTube thumbnail made with Magic Resize and scheduled in the Content Planner.
. A "Book of the week" brand template, filled by Bulk Create from a CSV file and translated into Spanish.
. A launch presentation for a book-club meetup, with presenter notes, a Canva Live Q&A, a quiz and a recorded version.
. A 30-second promo video with Beat Sync, captions and a voiceover.
. A one-page launch website on a custom domain with Magic SEO and analytics.
. A print flyer and bookmarks ordered through Canva Print, plus a phone mockup.
. A reading-challenge tracker in Sheets with Magic Formulas and Magic Insights, and a Canva Code reading-streak widget that uses it.
. A review form and a newsletter in Canva Email.
. Canva AI prompts throughout, and an accessibility check before publishing.
. Developers: a "Bookshelf Covers" app that inserts book covers, a REST script that autofills the brand template and exports PNG files, and the Canva MCP server driven from Claude Code.

Canva publishes no version numbers and its documentation changes often, so open an entry and confirm its content, plan names and limits before relying on it. Where this bibliography and a page seem to disagree, the official documentation wins.

=== Books

The books are copyrighted works and are not attached or reproduced here; each entry links to the official publisher page or listing. They were used only to select concepts and order topics. They predate several changes listed on xref:ui-ux/canva/whats-changed-since-older-guides.adoc[What Changed Since Older Guides].
