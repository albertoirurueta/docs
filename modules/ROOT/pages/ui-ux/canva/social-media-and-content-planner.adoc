This page was checked against the official Canva Help Center on 2026-11-07. It covers social media design and publishing in Canva: sizing and exporting for each network, connecting accounts, sharing a finished design straight to a network, and the *Content Planner*, the calendar that publishes designs automatically at a date and time you choose. Sharing to a network from the editor is open to every plan. Scheduling posts in the Content Planner is, according to the current Help Center article, available to Canva Pro, Canva Business, Canva Education and Canva for Nonprofits administrators and brand designers; older articles in the same Help Center still name Canva Teams, which Canva Business replaced for new customers on 2025-10-30. For Education, publishing is off by default and an admin must enable it. Prices are not repeated here; they are published at https://www.canva.com/pricing/, and plan details are on xref:ui-ux/canva/plans-pricing-and-ai-allowance.adoc[Plans, Pricing and AI Allowance]. The Content Planner is generally available. The one AI route on this page, Canva AI scheduled tasks, belongs to Canva AI 2.0, which launched as a research preview on 2026-04-16 and has no published general-availability date; its features draw on the AI allowance (Ultra tier for conversational design) and may not be visible on every account yet.

The running example is *Bookshelf*, the book catalogue and reading-list app designed in the xref:ui-ux/figma/index.adoc[Figma sub-section]. Its launch week needs an Instagram post, a LinkedIn banner and a Facebook Page post, all on schedule, with the whole team able to see the calendar. The page first explains how social publishing is organised, then builds that week step by step.

== Two ways to publish: share now or schedule

Canva has two publishing paths for social networks, and they differ in who can use them and in what they let you control.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| | Share from the editor | Schedule with the Content Planner

| Where
| *Share* in the editor's top bar, then the network (use *See all* if it is not listed)
| *More*, then *Content Planner* on the homepage side menu, or *Share*, *See all*, *Schedule* in the editor

| When it publishes
| Immediately
| At the date and time you pick

| Plans
| All plans, including Free
| Pro, Business, Education (admin must enable publishing) and Nonprofits, for administrators and brand designers

| Typical use
| One-off post, Instagram Stories and Reels from the mobile apps
| A planned campaign, the same post on a fixed slot every week
|===

Supported destinations change over time as networks update their integrations, so the Help Center tells you to open a design and select *Share* to see what your account currently offers. The Content Planner article lists these scheduling targets: Facebook Pages, Instagram Business accounts, X (formerly Twitter), LinkedIn, Pinterest, Slack and Tumblr. The sharing article also lists YouTube and TikTok as share destinations; YouTube publishes video designs only and cannot be scheduled from Canva.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/content-planner/[Canva Help Center: Schedule posts on Facebook, Pinterest, and more] and https://www.canva.com/help/sharing-social-media-limitations/[Canva Help Center: Sharing on social media or apps limitations]

== Designing for social media

A social campaign is a set of designs of different shapes that share one look. In Canva you start each from a social format in *Create a design* (for example an Instagram post, an Instagram story, a LinkedIn banner or a YouTube thumbnail), or you build one master design and convert it with Magic Resize, covered on xref:ui-ux/canva/resize-bulk-create-and-translate.adoc[Resize, Bulk Create and Translate]. The Bookshelf campaign uses the second route: one "Book of the week" square post, resized to a story, a banner and a thumbnail, with every colour and font coming from the brand kit on xref:ui-ux/canva/brand-kit-and-brand-voice.adoc[Brand Kit and Brand Voice].

Three habits keep a campaign coherent:

* *One visual system.* Fix the palette, two fonts and the logo placement first; apply the brand kit before resizing so every size inherits it. Layout principles are on xref:ui-ux/canva/design-principles-in-canva.adoc[Design Principles in Canva].
* *A series, not a single post.* A "storytelling series" is several posts that look related and read in order: the same grid, the same title position and a repeating accent colour. Duplicate pages inside one design, change the text and image, and keep the numbering visible. Note that a multi-page design cannot be scheduled as a whole in the Content Planner yet; copy each page into its own single-design document first.
* *Safe margins.* Networks crop and overlay interface elements on posts, profile images and covers, so keep the important content away from the edges and check the preview on mobile.

Networks also apply their own export limits. The Help Center lists them per destination, and they decide whether a post is accepted, so design and export with them in mind.

.Upload limits per destination, as listed by the Help Center on the check date
image::ui-ux-canva-social-media-and-content-planner-platform-limits.svg["Table of upload limits for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Pinterest, X, Slack, Tumblr and YouTube covering file types, size limits and text limits",width=720]

Two details deserve attention. LinkedIn accepts a JPG image, not a PNG, and Instagram accepts JPG and MP4, so a design with a transparent PNG look must still be exported flat. The Help Center gives two different hashtag figures for Instagram, a maximum of 30 in the general limits table and 20 in the scheduling article, so stay at 20 or fewer for scheduled captions.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/sharing-social-media-limitations/[Canva Help Center: Sharing on social media or apps limitations]

== Connecting accounts

A connection is an authorisation you give Canva to publish on your behalf; it is made the first time you share or schedule to a network, or at any time from the Content Planner menu with *Connect social account*. How many accounts you can link depends on your plan: Pro, Business and Enterprise users can connect up to 80 social media accounts per team in any mix (Facebook Pages, Instagram Business accounts, LinkedIn Pages and so on), while Free users link one account per platform at a time.

Each network has its own prerequisites.

* *Instagram.* Scheduling needs an Instagram Business account, not a Creator account, linked to a Facebook Page. Skipping the link causes errors. Only the Instagram Business accounts attached to the one Facebook account currently linked to Canva are offered, and multiple Facebook accounts are not supported yet.
* *Facebook.* Facebook restricts third-party apps from posting to personal profiles, so you can only schedule to Facebook Pages. You need the right role on the Page: Admin or Editor on a classic Page, or Page access with full or partial control on a new Page. If the Page belongs to a business account, turn on two-factor authentication on your personal account and confirm you are in the Business Asset Group for the Page. For a location Page, your account needs a direct role on that Page itself.
* *LinkedIn, Pinterest, X, Slack and Tumblr.* Log in to the account you want to use and allow the permissions Canva requests.

.How an Instagram Business account, a Facebook Page and the Content Planner connect
image::ui-ux-canva-social-media-and-content-planner-connect-accounts-flow.svg["Diagram showing an Instagram Business account linked to a Facebook Page, which you log in with to connect to the Content Planner, plus the Instagram and Facebook limits",width=720]

Each design can be scheduled to only one platform at a time, so a post that should go to three networks is scheduled three times, once per channel.

When a connection fails, the usual causes are a Facebook or Google account that already signed up for another Canva account (unlink it there first, after adding an email and password as an alternative login), an unverified Facebook email address, a Free plan limit of one account per platform, or an Education account whose publishing is off. Connection flows are run by the third-party networks, so the exact screens change.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/scheduling-posts-on-instagram/[Canva Help Center: Connect to Instagram to schedule posts], https://www.canva.com/help/scheduling-posts-on-facebook/[Canva Help Center: Connect to Facebook to schedule posts] and https://www.canva.com/help/cant-connect-social-media/[Canva Help Center: Can't connect my social media account]

== The Content Planner

The Content Planner is a calendar of posts. On the web it opens from the homepage side menu under *More*, then *Content Planner*; the *Add New* button creates a post, and a menu above the calendar offers *Share Content Planner* and *Calendar Options*, which includes *Manage social channels*. On mobile you can view the calendar, but you schedule from the editor.

.The Content Planner as a weekly calendar with channels, scheduled posts and post actions
image::ui-ux-canva-social-media-and-content-planner-content-planner-annotated.svg["Schematic of a content calendar with a channel list on the left, a week of scheduled posts as tiles, the Add New button and numbered notes for each area",width=720]

. *Add New*, then *Post*, starts a scheduled post from a recent design.
. *Share Content Planner* gives the team a view of selected accounts you own.
. *Calendar Options*, then *Manage social channels*, changes how each channel is shared.
. The channel list shows the accounts you or your team have connected.
. The calendar shows scheduled and published posts by day.
. Opening a post shows its caption, date and time and the *Post actions* menu.

Instagram has its own scheduling rules. You can schedule up to 25 posts in a rolling 24-hour period, a caption can carry up to 20 hashtags, and only single image or single video posts are supported: Instagram does not allow scheduling Stories, Reels or galleries. Scheduling to Instagram must be done from a desktop browser. Stories and Reels can still be shared from the Canva mobile apps when Instagram is installed on the same device.

Because a post is a live export, it can be edited afterwards. Opening it lets you change the caption, choose *Change date and time*, choose *Delete post*, or choose *Edit design* and then *Save and schedule* to republish the changed design. In a shared Content Planner you can only reschedule or delete posts of designs you own, so ask the design owner otherwise.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/content-planner/[Canva Help Center: Schedule posts on Facebook, Pinterest, and more] and https://www.canva.com/help/reschedule-delete-edit-scheduled-posts/[Canva Help Center: Reschedule, delete, or edit scheduled posts]

== Teams, sharing and approvals

Every team member has their own Content Planner view. When you connect an account you decide who can use it:

* *Private*: only you can see and schedule posts to the account.
* *Team-viewable*: teammates can see what has been published.
* *Team-publishable*: teammates can also publish to the account.

If an account is team-viewable or team-publishable, its scheduled and published posts appear in the Content Planner of every user with access. The permission applies to the whole team; individual settings are not available yet. You can only change team access to channels you own, meaning channels you connected yourself.

.The three visibility levels of a connected channel
image::ui-ux-canva-social-media-and-content-planner-team-visibility.svg["Diagram of a connected channel branching into Private, Team-viewable and Team-publishable access levels, with a note on what unsharing does",width=720]

Unsharing returns all scheduled posts, including ones created by teammates, to the owner, who becomes their owner and can delete and reschedule them. Because only the owner can unshare, do it before leaving a team. Roles and groups are covered on xref:ui-ux/canva/teams-roles-and-groups.adoc[Teams, Roles and Groups], and review and approval of designs on xref:ui-ux/canva/sharing-and-permissions.adoc[Sharing and Permissions].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/content-planner-sharing/[Canva Help Center: Sharing and unsharing my Content Planner]

== Procedures on the Bookshelf launch week

The three procedures below assume the Bookshelf brand kit is applied and the square "Book of the week" design exists. They use the desktop web editor, the only place where Instagram can be scheduled.

=== Connect the Bookshelf channels

. Open the Canva homepage and select *More* in the side menu, then *Content Planner*.
. Open the menu at the top right and select *Connect social account*.
. Choose *Facebook Page*, log in with the Facebook account that manages the Bookshelf Page, select the Page and grant the requested permissions.
. Repeat for Instagram, using the Business account linked to that same Facebook Page, then for the LinkedIn Page.
. When Canva asks who can use each account, choose *Team-viewable* for LinkedIn and *Team-publishable* for Instagram so a colleague can cover a day.
. Confirm that the three channels appear in the channel list of Content Planner.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/scheduling-posts-on-facebook/[Canva Help Center: Connect to Facebook to schedule posts] and https://www.canva.com/help/scheduling-posts-on-instagram/[Canva Help Center: Connect to Instagram to schedule posts]

=== Schedule the launch week

. Open the finished Instagram post design in the editor, select *Share*, then *See all*, then *Schedule*.
. In the calendar choose Tuesday, enter 09:00 and select *Next*.
. In the channel menu choose the Bookshelf Instagram account.
. Write the caption, for example the book title, a call to action and a handful of hashtags, keeping the total at 20 hashtags or fewer.
. Select *Schedule*.
. Repeat for the LinkedIn banner on Wednesday at 12:30, picking the LinkedIn Page, and for the Facebook Page post on Thursday at 18:00. Each design goes to one channel per scheduling pass.
. Open *Content Planner* and confirm that the three posts appear on their days.

.Storyboard: from finished designs to a scheduled launch week and a reconnect if a post fails
image::ui-ux-canva-social-media-and-content-planner-storyboard-schedule-launch-week.svg["Five-panel storyboard: open the design and choose Schedule, pick the channel, set the date time and caption, review the week in Content Planner, then handle published and failed posts",width=720]

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/content-planner/[Canva Help Center: Schedule posts on Facebook, Pinterest, and more]

=== Move a post and fix a failed one

. In *Content Planner*, select the Thursday post to open its scheduling view.
. Select *Post actions* next to the date and time, then *Change date and time*, pick Friday 18:00 and select *Done*.
. Edit the caption if needed and select *Save and schedule*.
. If a post fails to publish, Canva emails the reason. The usual causes are an expired connection, missing permissions on the network side, or a file type the network does not support.
. For an expired connection, open the link in the email, choose *Schedule* again for the date and time, select the platform and log in once more to refresh the connection.
. For missing permissions, check the network's app settings and make sure Canva is allowed to post and manage content for you.

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/reschedule-delete-edit-scheduled-posts/[Canva Help Center: Reschedule, delete, or edit scheduled posts] and https://www.canva.com/help/scheduled-post-fail/[Canva Help Center: My scheduled post didn't publish]

== Captions, bulk posting and AI

Captions are plain text typed at scheduling time. Canva AI and Magic Write can draft them, and the brand voice described on xref:ui-ux/canva/brand-kit-and-brand-voice.adoc[Brand Kit and Brand Voice] keeps the tone consistent; always review the text before scheduling. To publish many similar posts, fill a brand template from a spreadsheet with Bulk Create, then schedule the results one by one as described on xref:ui-ux/canva/resize-bulk-create-and-translate.adoc[Resize, Bulk Create and Translate].

Canva AI 2.0 has a separate feature called scheduled tasks. It does not publish to networks: it asks Canva AI to create or update a design on a repeating schedule. From the Canva AI homepage you describe the work and choose a schedule from the dropdown beside *Submit*, or open the panel, choose *Tasks* and then *New Task* with a name, a description and a date and time. Tasks run daily, weekly or monthly, each run creates a new chat, you are notified on completion, and up to 10 tasks can be scheduled at a time. If you do not see it, it is not available on your account yet. Canva AI is described on xref:ui-ux/canva/canva-ai-assistant.adoc[Canva AI Assistant]. Measuring results is the subject of xref:ui-ux/canva/canva-grow-and-marketing-analytics.adoc[Canva Grow and Marketing Analytics], and social tools outside Canva, such as dedicated schedulers, are discussed on xref:ui-ux/canva/apps-marketplace-and-integrations.adoc[Apps Marketplace and Integrations].

Source: https://www.canva.com/help/new-canva-ai/[Canva Help Center: What's new in Canva AI] and https://www.canva.com/help/using-canva-ai/[Canva Help Center: Get started with Canva AI]

== Common mistakes

* Scheduling an Instagram Creator account, or a Business account not linked to a Facebook Page, which fails with errors.
* Trying to schedule to a personal Facebook profile; only Pages work.
* Scheduling a whole multi-page document; copy the page into a single-design document first.
* Exporting a PNG for LinkedIn or Instagram, which accept JPG.
* Forgetting that an expired connection makes a scheduled post fail; the notification email explains the reason.
* Using the phone app to schedule Instagram, which must be done from a desktop browser.
