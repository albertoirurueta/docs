Written on 2026-10-01 against the NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (NIST AI 100-1) and the Generative AI Profile NIST
AI 600-1 (July 2024); see the xref:ai/security/index.adoc#_version_baseline[version baseline]. The mapping on this page is the
author's reading of the documents, not an official NIST crosswalk, and no assessment results are reported: the DocsAssistant is
a teaching scenario.

The OWASP lists tell you what attackers do; the NIST AI RMF tells an organisation how to manage AI risk as a whole. It is
voluntary and technology-neutral, which makes it the usual bridge between engineering controls and the policies an auditor or a
regulator asks about (see xref:ai/security/eu-ai-act.adoc[EU AI Act]).

== The four functions

The framework organises risk management into four functions that run continuously rather than once.

[mermaid]
....
flowchart LR
    G[GOVERN<br/>policies, roles, culture] --> MP[MAP<br/>context and risks]
    MP --> ME[MEASURE<br/>test, evaluate, monitor]
    ME --> MA[MANAGE<br/>prioritise, treat, respond]
    MA -. lessons .-> MP
    G -. applies to all .-> ME
    G -. applies to all .-> MA
....

[cols="1,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Function | Purpose | Typical evidence for a team

| Govern
| Cross-cutting. Accountability, risk tolerance, policies, third-party and supply-chain oversight, and a culture that allows
  incidents to be reported.
| Named owner, written risk appetite, approved-model list, incident procedure.

| Map
| Establish context: intended purpose, users, deployment setting, known limits, who can be harmed and how.
| System description, data inventory, threat model, list of foreseeable misuse.

| Measure
| Select and apply quantitative and qualitative methods to analyse and track risks and trustworthiness characteristics.
| Evaluation sets, red-team reports, monitoring dashboards.

| Manage
| Prioritise risks, decide how to treat them, plan responses and recovery, and communicate.
| Risk register, mitigation backlog, rollback and incident runbooks.
|===

== The Generative AI Profile (AI 600-1)

AI 600-1 is a companion profile that applies the four functions to generative AI. It lists twelve risks that are unique to or
made worse by generative AI, and for each one it proposes suggested actions grouped by function. The risks are:

[cols="3,4", options="header"]
|===
| AI 600-1 risk | What it means for an application like the DocsAssistant

| CBRN information or capabilities
| Easier access to information relevant to chemical, biological, radiological or nuclear harm. Mostly a model-provider
  concern; an application mitigates by narrowing its domain.

| Confabulation
| Confidently stated but false content (the profile's term for hallucination).

| Dangerous, violent or hateful content
| Output that incites, instructs or radicalises.

| Data privacy
| Leakage or inference of personal data, and unlawful processing of it.

| Environmental impacts
| Energy and resource cost of training and serving.

| Harmful bias and homogenization
| Unequal performance across groups and convergence of outputs.

| Human-AI configuration
| Over-reliance, automation bias and poorly designed oversight.

| Information integrity
| Mis- and disinformation, and difficulty telling synthetic content from authentic content.

| Information security
| Attacks on and through the system: prompt injection, data poisoning, exfiltration.

| Intellectual property
| Reproduction of protected material and uncertain licensing of training data.

| Obscene, degrading or abusive content
| Including non-consensual intimate or child sexual abuse material.

| Value chain and component integration
| Opaque third-party models, data and components.
|===

The suggested actions are numbered per function and risk in the document itself; read the profile for the exact wording and
identifiers rather than relying on a paraphrase. Typical themes are documenting provenance, testing with red teams and
structured public feedback, setting content and usage policies, monitoring after release, and having a way to disengage or
decommission a system.

== Mapping DocsAssistant controls

The table links each AI 600-1 risk to the OWASP 2026 identifier that usually causes it and to the control pages in this
section. A blank technical control means the risk is handled by organisational measures or by the model provider.

[cols="2,2,4,2", options="header"]
|===
| AI 600-1 risk | Related OWASP 2026 | DocsAssistant control | RMF function mostly involved

| Confabulation
| LLM07 Misinformation
| Grounding with citations, abstaining when retrieval is empty, see
  xref:ai/security/misinformation-and-hallucination-risk.adoc[Misinformation and Hallucination Risk]
| Measure, Manage

| Data privacy
| LLM02, LLM08
| Redaction, access-controlled retrieval, see
  xref:ai/security/sensitive-information-and-hidden-context.adoc[Sensitive Information and Hidden Context]
| Map, Manage

| Information security
| LLM01, LLM03, LLM09, LLM10
| Input and output guardrails, least-privilege tools, safe rendering, see
  xref:ai/security/prompt-injection.adoc[Prompt Injection] and xref:ai/security/guardrails.adoc[Guardrails]
| Measure, Manage

| Value chain and component integration
| LLM04, LLM05
| Pinned and verified models, ML-BOM, see xref:ai/security/supply-chain-security.adoc[Supply Chain Security]
| Govern, Map

| Human-AI configuration
| LLM03
| Approval gates for write actions, visible AI disclosure, see
  xref:ai/security/responsible-ai-practices.adoc[Responsible AI Practices]
| Govern, Manage

| Information integrity
| LLM07
| Labelling AI-generated answers; transparency duties in the xref:ai/security/eu-ai-act.adoc[EU AI Act]
| Govern

| Harmful bias and homogenization
| (none)
| Evaluation across user groups and languages, see xref:ai/security/responsible-ai-practices.adoc[Responsible AI Practices]
| Measure

| Intellectual property
| (none)
| Licence checks on indexed content, attribution of sources
| Govern, Map

| Dangerous, violent or hateful; obscene, degrading or abusive
| LLM01 (jailbreak)
| Moderation on input and output, red teaming, see xref:ai/security/ai-red-teaming.adoc[AI Red Teaming]
| Measure, Manage

| CBRN; environmental impacts
| (none)
| Domain restriction; model choice and caching, see xref:ai/llmops/cost-management.adoc[Cost Management]
| Map
|===

Measure and Manage depend on evidence that stays current, which is what xref:ai/security/llmsecops.adoc[LLMSecOps]
automates, and the Govern function corresponds to the practices in xref:ai/llmops/operational-governance.adoc[Operational
Governance]. For the cloud-platform view of the same ideas, see
xref:cloud/azure/governance-and-compliance.adoc[Azure Governance and Compliance].

== Using the framework without over-investing

* Start with Map: a one-page system description and threat model (xref:ai/security/trust-boundaries-and-threat-modeling.adoc[Trust Boundaries and Threat Modeling])
  already covers most of it.
* Pick a small number of Measure indicators you will actually track, and tie each to a threshold in Manage.
* Keep the mapping in the repository next to the code so it changes with the system.
