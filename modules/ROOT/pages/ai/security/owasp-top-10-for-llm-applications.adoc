Written on 2026-10-01 against the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications *2026* edition. The OWASP GenAI Security Project resource
page shows 2026-08-03 as its publication date and the source repository's README states 2026-08-04, so it is cited here as
August 2026; the 2025 edition is kept in the repository's `2025/` directory and the 2023 v1.1 edition is the one the book _The
Developer's Playbook for Large Language Model Security_ (Wilson) is written against. The LLM Top 10 index page on the OWASP site
may still present the 2025 edition, so link to the resource pages in the references rather than the index. The short names
below were read from the sources on the date above; the descriptions are paraphrased summaries, not OWASP text. See the
xref:ai/security/index.adoc#_version_baseline[version baseline] for the other frameworks.

The list ranks the risks the OWASP community judged most critical for applications built on LLMs. It is an awareness document,
not a standard and not a complete threat model: use it as a shared vocabulary and a review checklist, and pair it with the
threat model from xref:ai/security/trust-boundaries-and-threat-modeling.adoc[Trust Boundaries and Threat Modeling].

== The 2026 list

[cols="1,2,5,3", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Risk | Summary (paraphrased) | Treated on

| LLM01
| Prompt Injection
| Text that the model reads, from a user directly or from a document, web page or tool result indirectly, changes its behaviour against the developer's intent. Because instructions and data share one channel, it cannot be fully eliminated, only contained by layers.
| xref:ai/security/prompt-injection.adoc[Prompt Injection]

| LLM02
| Sensitive Information Disclosure
| The application reveals personal data, credentials, proprietary documents or training content to someone who should not see it, through the model's output or through over-broad retrieval.
| xref:ai/security/sensitive-information-and-hidden-context.adoc[Sensitive Information and Hidden Context]

| LLM03
| Excessive Agency
| The model is given more functions, permissions or autonomy than the task needs, so a wrong or manipulated decision becomes a harmful action.
| xref:ai/security/excessive-agency-and-tool-security.adoc[Excessive Agency and Tool Security]

| LLM04
| Supply Chain
| Models, datasets, adapters, libraries, plugins and servers obtained from third parties are tampered with, malicious or simply unverified.
| xref:ai/security/supply-chain-security.adoc[Supply Chain Security]

| LLM05
| Data and Model Poisoning
| Training, fine-tuning, embedding or ingestion data is manipulated to plant backdoors, bias or false content that later surfaces in answers.
| xref:ai/security/rag-and-vector-security.adoc[RAG and Vector Security]

| LLM06
| Unbounded Consumption
| Uncontrolled use of tokens, compute or calls leads to outages, runaway cost (denial of wallet) or model extraction.
| xref:ai/security/unbounded-consumption-and-denial-of-wallet.adoc[Unbounded Consumption and Denial of Wallet]

| LLM07
| Misinformation
| The model produces confident but false or unsupported output that users or downstream systems rely on, with legal, safety or reputational consequences.
| xref:ai/security/misinformation-and-hallucination-risk.adoc[Misinformation and Hallucination Risk]

| LLM08
| Hidden Context Exposure
| Content the developer meant to keep out of users' sight, such as system prompts, tool schemas, memory and other injected context, is extracted, and any secret placed there is lost. It replaces the 2025 entry named System Prompt Leakage.
| xref:ai/security/sensitive-information-and-hidden-context.adoc[Sensitive Information and Hidden Context]

| LLM09
| Vector and Embedding Weaknesses
| Weak access control, tenant mixing, poisoning or inversion in the vector store and embedding pipeline of a RAG system.
| xref:ai/security/rag-and-vector-security.adoc[RAG and Vector Security]

| LLM10
| Improper Output Handling
| Model output is passed to browsers, interpreters, databases or other systems without the validation that any untrusted input would receive.
| xref:ai/security/improper-output-handling.adoc[Improper Output Handling]
|===

== From 2023 to 2025 to 2026

Teams and books refer to different editions, so a finding labelled "LLM02" can mean three different things. The table maps the
risks across editions. Where an entry was merged, split or renamed the cell says so; treat the mapping as guidance for
translating labels, not as an exact equivalence, because the editions regrouped risks rather than renumbering them.
The 2023 (v1.1) and 2025 names were checked on 2026-10-01 against the official
https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10-2023-24/[2023-24 list] and https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/[2025 list].

Three practical points follow from the table. First, the 2023 "Insecure Plugin Design" concern did not disappear: it is
absorbed into excessive agency and supply chain, and it is why the tool and MCP guidance lives on
xref:ai/security/excessive-agency-and-tool-security.adoc[its own page]. Second, the 2025 entry for system prompt leakage was
widened in 2026 to cover everything hidden in the context, which is a reminder that the right design goal is *no secrets in
the prompt* rather than a prompt that resists extraction. Third, when you read a book or a vendor report, check its edition
before copying an identifier into your own register.

== Using the list in design reviews

A design review of an LLM feature does not need all ten risks every time; it needs the right questions asked of the diagram.
A workable routine for the DocsAssistant follows.

. *Start from the diagram, not the list.* List every place text from outside enters the context and every action the model can
  trigger. Those two inventories point to LLM01, LLM03 and LLM10 immediately.
. *Add the data stores.* Each store of documents or embeddings raises LLM02, LLM05 and LLM09; each hosted model, dataset or
  server raises LLM04.
. *Add the economics and the users.* A public endpoint raises LLM06; any user-facing factual claim raises LLM07; any prompt
  containing something confidential raises LLM08.
. *For each applicable risk record a control, a test and an owner* in the threat register, using the
  Attack, Defend, Test and Map structure of the risk pages.
. *Re-review on change.* New tools, new document sources, new channels or a new model version reopen the list.
. *Report in several vocabularies.* The same finding carries its OWASP LLM ID, its
  xref:ai/security/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications.adoc[Agentic ASI] ID where the system is an agent, its MITRE ATLAS
  technique and its NIST AI 600-1 risk, so it can be filed in whichever process the organisation uses.

The list also has limits worth stating in a review: it says nothing about fairness, accessibility, intellectual property or
regulation (see xref:ai/security/responsible-ai-practices.adoc[Responsible AI Practices] and
xref:ai/security/eu-ai-act.adoc[EU AI Act]), and it is silent on classic web risks, which still apply to every endpoint around
the model.
