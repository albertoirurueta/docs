Automatic Payment Program

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

A company with a few hundred suppliers does not pay invoices one at a time. It lets the system look through all open items, pick the ones that are due, group them into payments, post the payments and produce the files or forms the bank needs. That function is the automatic payment program, often called the payment run. This page explains its three steps (proposal, payment run, payment media), the parameters you enter, the rules that choose the payment method and the bank, what you can change before money moves, and what to do when a run fails. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The ideas are the same in both editions; the screens differ, and the page says where. The page ends with a worked run for the fictitious company code EX01.

Before you read

  • Accounts Payable: the supplier invoice that the run pays, and the terms of payment that give it a due date.

  • Open Item Management and Clearing: what an open item is and what clearing does to it.

  • Business Partners, Customers and Suppliers: where payment methods and bank details are kept in the master record.

  • Account Determination and Automatic Postings: how the system finds the accounts it posts to without a clerk choosing them.

Key terms

  • Automatic payment program: the program that selects due open items, creates payment documents and prepares the payment media.

  • Payment run: one execution of the program, identified by a run date and an identification.

  • Payment proposal: the list of payments the program would make, which you can check and change before anything is posted.

  • Payment method: how a payment is made, for example check, bank transfer or bank direct debit.

  • House bank: a bank at which your company code keeps an account.

  • Payment medium: the output of a run that goes to the bank or the payee, such as a file, a check or a payment advice.

  • Exception list: the open items that the program did not propose, with the reason for each.

What the program does and why it exists

SAP describes the payment program as able to process both outgoing and incoming payments, in Accounts Payable and in Accounts Receivable, domestic and foreign, for suppliers and customers. It creates the payment documents and supplies the data to the payment medium programs, which print a payment list or payment forms such as checks, or create files for the bank. This page concentrates on paying suppliers, the case most beginners meet first.

The help describes the work in three steps:

  1. Proposal. The program determines the open items to be paid and creates a proposal list. You can process that list online: change payment methods or banks, block items, or cancel payment blocks.

  2. Payment run. The program carries out the payment using the proposal. Only the items in the proposal are included. It posts the payment documents and provides the data for the forms, the data carriers, the payment advice notes and the payment summaries.

  3. Payment media. A print or file program prints the forms and creates the data carriers.

The split matters because step 1 changes nothing in the books. Everything you do while editing a proposal affects only the proposal; the invoices stay as they are. The money moves in step 2.

sequenceDiagram participant C as Accounting clerk participant P as Payment program participant L as Ledger and open items participant B as House bank C->>P: Enter run date, identification and parameters C->>P: Schedule the proposal P->>L: Read open items of the selected accounts P-->>C: Proposal list, exception list and proposal log C->>P: Edit the proposal (method, bank, blocks) C->>P: Schedule the payment run P->>L: Post payment documents and clear the invoices P-->>C: Payment log and payment list P->>B: Payment media (file or form)

Source: Executing the Payment Program (SAP Help Portal, on premise), How the Payment Program Works (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Overview of the Payment Run Procedure (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

What you need before the first run

The payment methods, house banks and forms you use must be defined before a run. SAP’s Customizing documentation describes the configuration as deciding what is paid (rules that select and group the open items), when (the due date, which you can refine), to whom (the payee), how (rules that choose a payment method) and from where (rules that choose a bank and bank account). Most of these settings are specific to a company code. The rules for controlling the program sit in four places: the customer or supplier master record, the line items, the configuration of the payment program, and the specifications you make for each payment run. The program also needs the document types, posting keys and accounts for its automatic postings. Requires an SAP system; not run here.

  • House banks. A house bank is a bank at which the company code has an account. It is defined under a house bank key and the accounts under an account ID, and each bank account has a G/L account. Bank master data holds the address and control data such as the SWIFT code, and the bank details are needed to print the payment forms.

  • Payment methods. A payment method is the procedure used to pay, for example check, transfer or bill of exchange. For suppliers SAP lists check, bank transfer, postal giro transfer, bills of exchange, check/bill of exchange and a bill of exchange payment request; for customers it lists bank collection, bank direct debit, return check, refund and bank switching. A payment method is defined first per country and then per company code, with the conditions for its use.

Sources: Customizing the Payment Program (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Payment Program Settings (SAP Help Portal, on premise), House Banks (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Payment Methods (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

The run parameters

A run is identified by two values: the date of execution and an alphanumeric identification. The identification is needed because several runs can be scheduled for the same day. In the classic system you reach the Automatic Payment Transactions screen from the SAP Easy Access menu under Accounting, Financial Accounting, Accounts receivable/Accounts payable, Periodic Processing, Payments. You enter the parameters on their own tab, or copy them from an earlier run.

Parameter What SAP’s help says

Posting date

The date printed on the payment media.

Documents entered up to

The date up to which documents are considered. The creation date, not the posting date, decides. Items entered later are not processed.

Due date

The date by which an open item must be due to be considered. Without a due date, the posting date counts as the due date.

Company code

The four-character company codes to pay.

Payment methods

The keys to use for every company code in this run. The sequence of the keys decides which method pays an item that has no method in the item or the master record.

Next posting date

The posting date of the next run. The program uses it to decide whether an open item is paid in this run or in the next.

Account restrictions

Optional: limit the run to specific customers or suppliers. Entering both account numbers of a business partner that is a customer and a supplier lets the program determine a joint balance.

If you enter, for example, the payment methods BTC, the program first checks whether a bill of exchange can be created, then a bank transfer, then a check. The Free selection tab accepts up to three more selection criteria on fields of the document and master data tables, and the Additional log tab lets you ask for more detailed logging of the due date check, the payment method selection and the payment document items for named accounts. SAP advises using the additional log only in exceptional situations or for testing, because it makes the log longer and the run slower.

On the Printout/data medium tab you enter one or more variants for each payment medium program used for a payment method, and variants for the payment proposal list (program RFZALI00) and the exception list (program RFZALI10).

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

Sources: Entering Payment Parameters (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Specifying the Payment Medium Program and Variants (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Which items are paid, by which method, from which bank

Selecting open items

The program selects items and pays them as late as possible without losing cash discount. SAP’s list of the factors:

  • The due date comes from the baseline date and the terms of payment in the open item. The program calculates the cash discount periods and the due date for net payment.

  • A company-code-specific grace period for payables can be added to the due date, so that payment is made later.

  • A minimum cash discount percentage can be set per company code for outgoing payments. If the item cannot reach it, the program pays on the net due date. Without a minimum, it pays with the highest possible cash discount.

  • You decide which special G/L transactions are included, by entering their indicators in the company code specifications. A down payment request can be paid by the program if its indicator is entered.

  • The next posting date of the run decides whether an item belongs to this run or the next.

Source: Selecting Open Items (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Choosing the payment method

A payment method in the open item takes precedence over one in the master record. When several methods are in the master record and none in the item, the program tries the methods of the run in the order you entered them and uses the first that passes the checks. Among the conditions SAP lists: the method must be in the master record or item and the master record must hold what the method needs (bank details for a transfer, for example); for foreign payments the method must allow payees or banks abroad; for foreign currency the method must allow it; and the payment amount must lie between the minimum and maximum set for the method. Then a permitted bank is determined.

Source: Selection of the Payment Method by the Payment Program (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Choosing the bank and the available amount

If several bank accounts serve the same payment method and currency, you can define the order in which the program uses them: when the funds of the first account are used up, it takes the second, and so on. You can also set per account the amount up to which payments or receipts may be made through it, for example to keep room for a payment the program does not make. In the Public Edition the Enter Available Amounts for Payment Program app lets an accounts payable accountant choose the house banks and amounts for exceptional or urgent payments; those settings override the bank determination configuration for the payment runs of the Manage Automatic Payments app.

Sources: Selecting Banks and Planning Available Amounts (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Handling Exceptional or Urgent Payments (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition)

Step 1: the payment proposal

You schedule the proposal with a start date and time or immediately. The status display shows which step the job is at. When it finishes, SAP says you should read the log: it tells you about configuration errors, and in that case no payment is possible. You correct the error, delete the proposal and create a new one.

Two lists help you judge the result. The proposal list shows the payments the program suggests. The exception list shows the blocked items and all open items the program did not propose. SAP names three kinds: blocked items, special G/L transactions (depending on the configuration), and items that could not be settled although they were due. Each exception has an error number and an error text. A due item that is missing from the proposal is therefore either on the exception list with a reason, or not yet due for this run.

Editing the proposal

You can divide the editing between clerks by the accounting clerk ID in the master record, so that several people work through a large run in parallel, and you can later display who changed what. At the payment level you can change the payment method, the house bank and the due date. A new method must be one set in the run parameters and must have what it needs in the master record, such as bank details; the new due date may not be before the run date, and apart from bills of exchange not more than 30 days after it. At the line item level you can block or unblock items, change the cash discount, and reassign an item to another payment or a new one. A blocked item moves to the exception group and the payment shrinks by its amount; only block indicators defined as editable in Customizing can be set or removed. Changes take effect only when you save them.

Sources: Creating the Payment Proposal (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Checking the Log (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Evaluating the Exception List (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Editing the Payment Proposal (Online) (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Changing Payments (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Changing Line Items (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Payment release list

The Payment Release List is an alternative to editing the proposal in the program. It lets several processors approve and release items, with an approval process based on dual control. SAP’s help says that once a Payment Release List application is activated, you can no longer edit payment proposals in the payment program, and that it is available only if you use Public Sector Management. Most companies therefore edit the proposal as described above.

Source: Payment Release List (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Step 2 and 3: payment run and payment media

Once you accept the proposal, you schedule the payment run. The help gives three ways to combine the programs: schedule the payment program first and the payment medium program later; schedule both together; or run the payment program and then print online. Before printing, check the payment log and the payment list, which you reach from the payment menu.

Scheduling both together needs the variants of the payment medium programs to exist, and you select the option with print programs. SAP adds a caution: the documents of the payment program are first saved to a log file and posted afterward. Some payment medium programs need information from the posted documents, so for those the medium programs should be scheduled separately for a later time, once all payment documents are posted. The status display shows how many documents were created and how many are posted.

Two families of programs produce the medium. The classic payment medium programs are named by country and payment method; SAP’s example is a program that generates bank transfers in ACH format for the USA. The Payment Medium Workbench is the generic tool that SAP says will gradually replace the classic programs, with better control, better performance for mass payments above 50,000 and better sorting of payment advice notes; not every classic format is supported yet. You choose between them per country and payment method in Customizing. With the Workbench, the data media go to the data medium exchange administration and the payment advice notes can be produced as IDoc, XML file, letter, e-mail or fax.

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

Sources: Running the Payment and Payment Medium Programs (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Scheduling the Payment Program and Payment Medium Programs Jointly (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Printout Control (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Payment Medium Workbench (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Creating Payment Media (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

The Public Edition: Manage Automatic Payments

In the Public Edition the run is handled by apps instead of the classic screen. SAP’s help names these:

App App ID What SAP’s help says

Manage Automatic Payments

F0770

Create, copy, display, edit and delete parameters; schedule payment proposals or schedule payments directly; create payment media automatically after scheduling; create payment advices; view and delete payment orders; search proposals and payments by identification, run date, creator and company code; view the proposal and payment lists; check the log, which shows whether the payment methods are valid and the house bank information is complete.

Revise Payment Proposals

F0771

Review proposals created in Manage Automatic Payments and view the open items. You can check the exceptions log, edit payments (method, partner bank account, bank account alias), block and unblock items and reallocate items among payments.

Manage Payment Media

F1868

Download, delete and analyze the payment media that each successful run creates. A medium has the status New, Downloaded or Processed; it becomes Processed when an electronic account statement is posted for it.

The proposal list and the payment list are PDF documents. The same form template is used for both by default. The list shows an overview with totals, the run date, the identification and the paying company code, and each payment with its number, bank account, method, currency and amount. To see them, open the proposal on the Proposals Processed tab or the payment on the Payments Processed tab, and use Export to PDF.

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

Sources: Manage Automatic Payments (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Revise Payment Proposals (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Manage Payment Media (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Payment Proposal Lists and Payment Lists (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition)

When a run fails

In the Public Edition SAP describes a rare case where a payment run stops unexpectedly and leaves the status Payment Cancelled or Payment Run Failed, or where the background job is deleted (Payment Run Job Deleted or Proposal Run Job Deleted). Then the payment is locked, which blocks the supplier or customer accounts and freezes the items. In the Manage Automatic Payments app you select the item on the Payments Processed tab, or the proposal on the Proposals Processed tab, and choose Delete Output or Delete Proposal. What happens next depends on the status:

  • Nothing posted and no payment orders: the data of the run is deleted and the status goes back, so you reschedule.

  • Payment orders created: delete the payment orders and create a new payment.

  • Payment documents posted: reverse the payment documents in the Manage Journal Entries app (F0717A), then create a new payment.

SAP advises acting on a failed run quickly, so that the job does not expire and the items are released.

Source: Resetting Unsuccessful Payments or Proposals (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition)

What older books show, and what has changed

The book Configuring SAP S/4HANA Finance, 3rd edition (2025), places the payment program in the accounts payable menu and names it with the transaction code F110, and it lists run date, identification, posting date, documents entered up to, company codes, payment methods, next posting date and accounts as the important fields. These match the parameters above, and the code is still how classic users call the program. The SAP help pages used for this page give the menu path and the screen name, Automatic Payment Transactions, instead of the code. The book SAP S/4HANA Financial Accounting Configuration, 2nd edition (2023), lists the code too. What is new since the classic program is the set of Public Edition apps F0770, F0771 and F1868, which split the work into a proposal and payment app, a revision app and a payment media app.

Common errors and pitfalls

  • Expecting the proposal to post anything. It posts nothing; only the payment run does.

  • Reading a missing item as a bug. Check the exception list for a block or a missing payment method, bank detail or master record specification, and check the next posting date.

  • Listing a method in the run that the supplier cannot use. The payment method must be in the master record or the item, and the master record must hold what the method needs, such as bank details, or the method is not used.

  • Setting the additional log on in production. It is meant for exceptional situations or tests, and slows the run.

  • Printing before the documents are posted. Some medium programs need the posted documents; schedule them separately.

  • Leaving a failed run alone. The accounts stay blocked and the job can expire; reset the run.

Summary

  • The automatic payment program pays due supplier and customer open items in three steps: proposal, payment run, payment media. Only the payment run posts.

  • A run is identified by a run date and an identification, and its parameters name the posting date, the company codes, the payment methods and the next posting date that decides which items are due now.

  • The program chooses the payment method and the house bank by rules; you can edit method, bank, due date, blocks and discounts in the proposal, and the exception list explains what was left out.

  • Payment media come from the classic programs or the Payment Medium Workbench. In the Public Edition the apps are Manage Automatic Payments, Revise Payment Proposals and Manage Payment Media.

  • In the worked run, supplier 1 receives one payment of EUR 9,920.00 after a discount of EUR 80.00, while a blocked item and a not yet due item stay open.
