Payment Terms, Cash Discount and Installment Plans

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Every invoice answers two questions before anyone pays it: when is it due, and is there a reward for paying early? In SAP both answers come from one small key, the payment terms (also called terms of payment) that sits on the invoice. The key holds the number of days, the discount percentages, and the rule for the date from which the days are counted. This page explains what is in the key, how the system turns it into a due date and a discount amount, how the same key can split one invoice into installments, which settings change the arithmetic, and how to judge a discount by the yearly rate it is worth. It follows the fictitious Example Trading Co. (company code EX01, euro) and uses made-up numbers throughout. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The ideas are the same in both; the names of the settings and apps differ, and the page says where.

Before you read

  • Accounts Payable: the supplier invoice and the outgoing payment, which this page goes deeper into.

  • Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management: the customer invoice, the incoming payment and dunning.

  • Open Item Management and Clearing: open items and clearing, which a payment with a discount relies on.

  • Account Determination and Automatic Postings: how the cash discount account is found.

Key terms

  • Payment terms: the key that holds the days, the cash discount percentages and the baseline date rule of an invoice.

  • Baseline date: the date from which the days of the payment terms are counted.

  • Cash discount: a percentage taken off the invoice when it is paid within an agreed period.

  • Discount base: the amount the discount percentage is applied to.

  • Installment payment: an invoice that is paid in several parts, each with its own payment terms.

  • Days in arrears: how many days an item is paid after its due date.

What a payment terms key holds

SAP’s help describes the key in two parts. For an individual payment it determines the valid cash discount rate with a maximum of three payment terms, namely the first cash discount period, the second cash discount period and the due date for net payment, and also a baseline date for the payment. For payment in installments it determines the installment terms instead, covered further down. If the key is stored in the customer or supplier master record, the system proposes it whenever you enter an item for that partner; the business partner page of this section shows where it sits in the company code data.

The clerk can also type the terms without a key. The days and percentages go into the Days/Perc. fields, two discount levels and the net due date at most. SAP’s example: to enter "2/10 Net 30", enter 10 days and 2 percent in the first two fields. Up to three decimal places are allowed in the percentage, for example 2.125.

Three ideas make up every key.

The baseline date

The terms count days, so the system needs a starting day. SAP’s help says the key contains the baseline date as of which the payment term days apply. On a line item, the baseline date and the net payment terms are what the system adds to get the due date: SAP’s own formula for the days in arrears is the baseline date for the due calculation plus the net payment terms period, or plus the cash discount days 1 when the customer took a discount.

What the help pages used here do not list are the options of the key’s baseline date default. One page, about the Prompt Payment Act function for US federal government, mentions that the document date can be selected as the default baseline date for invoices in the payment term Customizing. This page therefore does not list the other choices of the default; the Customizing activity that maintains the key shows them. The date chosen can differ from the posting date, which matters: an invoice dated 2026-05-04 and entered a few days later still counts its days from the date the key selects.

Sources: Calculation of Days in Arrears (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Using PPA: Scenario 1 - Ordinary Goods (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

What the discount is applied to

SAP’s help says that the Disc.Base field takes the amount the discount is based on, and that it does not have to equal the line item. When the field is empty, the system works the base out from two things. The first is whether the cash discount is calculated on the net or the gross amount (without or with tax); this is set in the system configuration. The second is whether G/L account line items were posted as without cash discount: the standard example is an invoice for goods and shipping, where the discount is valid for the goods only, so the shipping goes on its own line with the without cash discount field selected. The base is then the total of the lines where that field is not selected. The Disc. Amount field lets you type the discount directly and so overrides the system’s calculation. The help adds that the system calculates the discount when the document is posted.

The base amount per company code is a pair of settings. SAP’s page on the base amount says the tax base can include the cash discount (gross) or not (net), and that the base for the cash discount can be defined as well, per company code. Only some combinations are possible: gross discount base with gross tax base, net discount base with gross tax base, and net discount base with net tax base. A net tax base with a gross discount base works only for tax calculation with a jurisdiction code. The effect on the invoice of EX01:

Gross posting and net posting

On the supplier side, the document type decides how the cash discount is handled when the invoice is entered. SAP’s help for invoice verification describes two standard types. With a gross posting the discount is not considered when the invoice is entered, and only at payment is the discount posted to an account. With a net posting the expected discount is taken into account right away: the cost is posted minus the discount, and a cash discount clearing account holds the other side until payment. The accounts payable page covers the net posting for assets.

A consequence is the tax. SAP’s example posts an invoice of 100 with 15 percent tax net of a 5 percent discount: the input tax is debited with 15 when the invoice is posted, but only 95 (100 less 5 percent) is to be taxed, so the correct tax is . The difference of 0.75 is credited to the input tax account when the invoice is paid, and the system corrects the tax automatically.

Sources: Types of Cash Discount Clearing (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Example: Net Posting (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

What a discount is worth as a yearly rate

A discount of 2 percent for paying 20 days early sounds small. The yearly rate shows it is not. If you pay the discounted amount instead of the full amount, you give the supplier money for the number of days between the discount deadline and the net due date, and you gain a percentage of what you paid. Installment payments

A payment terms key can also describe installments. SAP’s help says the terms for installment payments are made up of individual payment terms, one for each installment. When you post an installment payment, the total amount is split into individual items for each installment and each one is calculated with its own payment term. When you enter such a key, the Days/Perc. fields remain empty and the text Due in is displayed.

SAP’s example for a supplier invoice posts 1,000.00 with posting key 31 on the supplier line and 40 on the expense line. Until the posting is simulated, the supplier line shows 0.00. The simulation then shows three supplier lines of 300.00, 400.00 and 300.00, and after posting the total amount is replaced by the individual installments. The accounts payable page of this section shows this example with its sums.

On the sales side, SAP’s installment plan example shows how the percentages work. A customer buys a washing machine for USD 1,000.00 and makes a down payment of 20 percent on February 1, then pays monthly installments of 13.33 percent over six months. How the terms change days in arrears

The payment terms also decide when a payment counts as late. SAP’s help gives the rule for incoming payments: if the customer does not take a discount, the date used is the baseline date for the due calculation plus the net payment terms period; if the customer takes a discount, whatever its size, it is the baseline date plus cash discount days 1. The days in arrears are the payment date minus that date, so a negative number means the payment was within the terms. With the terms of the running example (baseline date 2026-05-04):

Tolerances and the discount

A customer may pay a little too much or too little. SAP’s help says differences within the tolerance limits are posted automatically: the system adjusts the cash discount by the difference, or, if the discount cannot be adjusted, posts to a separate expense or revenue account. Tolerances are defined for the clerk (tolerance group for employees, which includes the maximum cash discount percentage a clerk may grant per line item) and separately for the business partner (which also carries grace days for cash discount deductions). When both exist, the lower limit applies.

SAP’s example: an invoice of EUR 100 with the net amount due within 60 days and a 3 percent discount if paid within 30 days. The clerk’s limits are EUR 5 and 0.5 percent for payment differences, adjusting the discount by EUR 2; the partner’s limits are EUR 100 and 10 percent, adjusting the discount by EUR 10. The system accepts and clears differences up to EUR 0.50, which is 0.5 percent of EUR 100 and the lowest limit. A maximum of EUR 2 can be adjusted through the cash discount posting.

The page on number ranges, field status variants and tolerance groups of this section introduces tolerance groups in general.

Sources: Tolerance Limits (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Difference Within Tolerance Limits (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Common errors and pitfalls

  • Counting days from the wrong date. The days run from the baseline date, which can differ from the posting date. Check the date the key selects.

  • Comparing a discount without its base. A gross base and a net base differ by the discount on the tax: EUR 28.50 in period 1 of the example.

  • Changing the terms after the system has adopted them. SAP warns that once adopted they are no longer changed automatically when the terms or the supplier change on an invoice, so check and reset them by hand.

  • Paying on the next payment day when the discount ends before it. The forecast app exists for this.

  • Expecting every installment to carry the default block of its own term. Only the defaults of the installment key apply.

  • Reading a discount as a small number. 2 percent for 10 days of credit is 74.49 percent a year as a simple rate.

Summary

  • A payment terms key holds up to two discount periods and a net due date, counted from a baseline date; it can also describe installments. It is proposed from the business partner or typed on the line.

  • The discount is the percentage of the discount base, and the base can be gross or net, minus lines marked without cash discount. In the example EUR 5,950.00 gives 178.50 (3 percent) and 119.00 (2 percent).

  • A payment inside a discount period clears the item in full, with the discount posted automatically to the account that Customizing defines.

  • A gross posting books the discount at payment; a net posting takes it into account at invoice entry, and tax is corrected at payment.

  • An installment key splits one invoice into items, each with its own terms, with rounding on the last item (333.30, 333.30, 333.40 in the example).

  • Days in arrears use the baseline date plus the net days, or plus the discount days 1 when a discount was taken.

  • As a yearly rate, 2 percent for 10 days is 74.49 percent (simple); the SAP apps measure taken against offered discounts.
