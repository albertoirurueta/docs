Fiscal Year Variants and Posting Periods

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

A company does not keep one endless list of postings. It cuts time into pieces, usually months, so that it can say how much it earned in March and close March before it starts April. SAP stores that calendar in a small setting called the fiscal year variant: it says where a financial year starts, how many pieces (posting periods) it has, and which piece a given date belongs to. A second set of switches then decides which pieces are currently open for posting. A newcomer meets this the first time a posting fails because "the period is closed", and every finance consultant meets it in the first week of an implementation. After reading this page you can read a fiscal year variant, work out the period and fiscal year of any posting date, and describe how a period is opened and closed. The page is written for SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The concepts are the same in both editions; the tools that maintain the settings differ, as the section on the two editions shows.

Before you read

  • Organizational Structures: what a company code is, because the fiscal year variant is assigned per company code.

Key terms

  • Fiscal year: the period, usually 12 months, for which a company produces its balance sheet and statements.

  • Fiscal year variant: the setting that defines the number of posting periods and special periods of a fiscal year and how a posting date maps to a period.

  • Posting period: one slice of the fiscal year, usually a month, with a start date and an end date.

  • Special period: an extra period (also called adjustment period) that subdivides the last regular period for year-end closing postings.

  • Year shift: the entry (-1, 0 or +1) that tells the system how to turn the calendar year of a posting date into the fiscal year.

  • Posting period variant: a named set of rules that says which posting periods are open, shared by one or more company codes.

Why periods exist

Three ideas explain the whole topic.

  1. A document is always posted on a date, its posting date, and the system turns that date into a fiscal year and a posting period. You never type the period for an ordinary posting.

  2. The period decides where the amounts land. SAP Help says that the general ledger stores the transaction figures of all accounts separately for each posting period and each special period, divided into debits and credits. A report for March reads the March figures.

  3. A period must be open before anything can be posted to it. Closing a period is how a company freezes its figures.

The first idea is the fiscal year variant, the second is the ledger’s period structure, and the third is the posting period control. The next sections take them in that order.

Source: SAP Help: Fiscal Year (on premise 2025 FPS02) and SAP Help: Determining Posting Periods When Posting

The fiscal year and the fiscal year variant

SAP Help defines the fiscal year as a time period, usually 12 months, for which a company must provide its inventory count and balance sheet. It may match the calendar year but does not have to, and in special cases it can be shorter than 12 months (a shortened fiscal year, described below). Defining the fiscal year is mandatory: you describe it in a fiscal year variant and assign that variant to each company code. Several company codes can share one variant.

The fiscal year variant contains the number of posting periods and the number of special periods. The documented limits are these.

  • The leading ledger of General Ledger Accounting can have a fiscal year of at most 12 posting periods and 4 special periods. The "12 plus 4" in the title of the example below is that maximum: 16 periods in all, the hard upper limit stated for the variant.

  • Non-leading ledgers and the Special Ledger component can define up to 366 posting periods (for example, a ledger of daily balances).

  • A ledger with more than 12 periods cannot receive postings directly with transaction FB01L; you can post only in ledger groups whose representative ledger has no more than 12 periods.

The fiscal year variant also appears in the controlling area. When you create a controlling area you specify its fiscal year variant too, and the variants of company code and controlling area may differ only in the number of special periods; they must describe the same time frame. SAP Help gives the example of a company code with 12 posting periods and four special periods that can be assigned to a controlling area whose variant has 12 posting periods and one special period, but not to a controlling area with 52 posting periods.

Source: SAP Help: Fiscal Year Variant (on premise 2025 FPS02) and SAP Help: Fiscal Year

Calendar and non-calendar fiscal years

When you define a variant you choose one of three shapes, which SAP Help lists under "Fiscal Year and Calendar Year".

Shape What it means

Corresponds to the calendar year

The year starts on January 1 and has 12 posting periods that are the calendar months. You do not define the periods; the system adopts the months. Fiscal year variant K4 is the calendar-year variant that SAP Help names in its examples.

Year-independent, but different from the calendar year

The year starts on another date, for example April 1. You define how many periods there are, the month and day on which each period ends, and a year shift for each period. Periods may also differ from calendar months.

Year-specific

Valid for one calendar year only. You set the year-specific indicator and enter the period ends again for each calendar year. The Public Edition documentation calls the same idea year-dependent.

How the system finds the period: month, day and year shift

For a non-calendar year the system needs two answers from a posting date: the period and the fiscal year. The first comes from the end-of-period month and day you enter for every period. The second comes from the year shift, one of -1, 0 or +1, which is added to the calendar year of the posting date.

Numbers first. Take a fiscal year that starts on April 1 and ends on March 31. SAP Help works through a posting date of February 3, 1999: the system determines posting period 11 in fiscal year 1998. February is the eleventh month counted from April, and 1999 minus 1 is 1998. When a period does not start on a month boundary, a calendar month can straddle two periods or two fiscal years. SAP Help’s example is a year that runs from April 16 to April 15: the stretch from December 16 to January 15 must be split into two rows of period 9, one with year shift 0 (December 20, 1998 gives period 9, fiscal year 1998) and one with year shift -1 (January 13, 1999 gives period 9, fiscal year 1998). The same page describes a week-based 4-4-5 fiscal calendar, with 52 or 53 weeks, as a year-specific variant.

Source: SAP Help: Fiscal Year and Calendar Year (on premise 2025 FPS02)

Why February must end on day 29

SAP Help carries a warning for the period-end days. For February, enter the day limit 29, not 28. With 29 the system finds the right period in a leap year; with 28, postings dated February 29 would fall into the next period, and if that period is not yet open the system issues an error message.

The shortened fiscal year

A company that starts business mid-year, or that switches from a calendar year to another fiscal year, needs a fiscal year with fewer than 12 months. SAP Help calls it a shortened fiscal year. It must always be flagged year-specific, because it is valid for one year only and is replaced by a complete fiscal year afterwards, and the shortened year and the complete years around it are defined in one fiscal year variant. If the company uses Asset Accounting, every fiscal year must start with period 001 so that depreciation can be calculated correctly. SAP Help adds that changes to these settings have far-reaching effects and refers to SAP Note 3224074 (an S-user login is needed to read SAP Notes).

Source: SAP Help: Shortened Fiscal Year (on premise 2025 FPS02)

Special periods 13 to 16

Special periods, also called adjustment periods, subdivide the last regular posting period for closing work, so that a company can create several supplementary financial statements for the same year. A fiscal year usually has 12 posting periods and General Ledger Accounting allows up to four special periods, numbered 13 to 16. Three rules matter in practice.

  • The posting date of a special-period posting must fall within the last regular posting period. A posting dated December 31 can go to period 013, 014, 015 or 016 of a calendar year.

  • The system cannot find a special period by itself: you enter it in the Period field of the document header.

  • If you need fewer than 12 regular periods, the unused numbers can serve as special periods, but you must state the number of special periods in the variant and the total may not exceed 16.

The Public Edition documentation says that an adjustment period must never overlap a normal period and that it typically ranges from 013 to 016.

Source: SAP Help: Special Periods (on premise 2025 FPS02) and SAP Help: Special Periods (Adjustment Periods) (Public Edition 2608)

Fiscal year variants and ledgers

The fiscal year variant is a property of the company code, but with parallel accounting it can also be set per ledger. SAP Help states these rules.

  • You can assign a separate fiscal year variant to each ledger.

  • Assign the same fiscal year variant to the controlling area and to the leading ledger for all company codes of that controlling area.

  • An extension ledger has to use the same fiscal year variant as the underlying ledger.

  • For the leading ledger, the variant of the company code and ledger equals the variant of the company code; for a non-leading ledger it can differ.

The Public Edition describes the business reason: a company may be required to use an April to March year for local reporting and a January to December year for group reporting. It assigns an alternative fiscal year variant to a non-leading ledger and the relevant company codes, and warns that a fiscal year can then be closed in one ledger while it is still open in the other, so that some period-end activities must be run per ledger and company code. The leading and non-leading ledgers themselves are the subject of the page on ledgers and currencies in this section.

The documentation also separates two reporting fields: Fiscal Year of Ledger (the year assigned to a ledger and company code) and Fiscal Year. Where a reporting app offers both for a company code and ledger, SAP recommends using only Fiscal Year of Ledger.

Source: SAP Help: Assignment of Fiscal Year Variants to a Ledger, SAP Help: Fiscal Year of Ledger vs Fiscal Year and SAP Help: Alternative Fiscal Year Variant (Public Edition 2608)

Opening and closing posting periods

A fiscal year variant says which periods exist; a separate control says which of them accept postings. SAP Help describes the usual practice: only the current posting period is open and all others are closed. At the end of the period it is closed and the next one is opened. During period-end closing, special periods can be open for closing postings. As many periods as you need can be open at once.

Posting period variants

A posting period variant says which posting periods are open. It can be shared by several company codes, so a company with many company codes opens and closes a period once for the variant instead of once per company code. SAP Help recommends this when you are responsible for many company codes. In the Public Edition the standard delivery assigns each posting period variant to just one company code, and a variant that you create can be assigned to a combination of ledger, company code and fiscal year variant, with several company codes allowed on one variant.

How finely a period can be opened

The on-premise documentation lists the ways to differentiate what is open. The rule that ties them together: for every period that must be open at all, you always enter account type + (valid for all account types) as the minimum entry, and more detailed entries are optional.

Dimension Effect

Posting period variant

Opens and closes periods for all company codes that use the variant at once.

Account type

A period can be open for customer accounts and closed for vendor accounts. Account type + is the entry for all types.

Account interval

A period is open only for postings to a range of G/L accounts. For subledger accounts, you enter the reconciliation account and the account type. Closing customers and vendors before the G/L accounts, after the balance confirmation, is the documented example.

User

An authorization group on the entry restricts the period to users who hold the authorization object F_BKPF_BUP (accounting document: authorizations for posting periods). The group acts only on time period 1.

CO-FI interval

A separate interval allows postings from Controlling to Financial Accounting through CO integration during closing. It is valid for no other postings and only for account type +.

The classic Customizing screen works with two time intervals, time period 1 and time period 2. In each interval you enter a lower period limit, an upper period limit and a fiscal year; a period is closed by choosing the entries so that it falls in none of the intervals.

The Manage Posting Periods app is the Fiori way of the same work. SAP Help lists its features: view open periods per posting period variant and per company code, shift open intervals manually, reset the adjustment periods and the CO periods, and schedule background jobs that open periods once or repeatedly. The Opening and Closing Posting Periods page gives the steps: search for the variant, select an item, choose Set Posting Periods and Open Periods, change the fiscal year period and save. Opening a posting period automatically closes the previous period, and recurring jobs can only be scheduled from the next posting period on; for the current period you can only run a job once or open and close by hand. The Public Edition adds that only a few normal periods should be open at a time, typically the current and the next or the previous and the current, and that you should assign only one fiscal year variant to a posting period variant. The Manage Posting Period Variants app edits the items of a variant: it copies, merges, splits, edits and deletes items and changes their authorization groups. In the Public Edition an item can be, for example, an account type and an account range such as assets 100 to 149.

One more detail from the app’s own page: if you enter a normal-period range that crosses calendar years, for example 2016012 to 2017001 with a variant that has special periods, the adjustment periods of the earlier year (2016013 to 2016016) are also open for manual posting. Check this before you open a range around a year change.

Common errors and pitfalls

  • Posting to a closed period. The posting date determines the period, and that period must be open. If it is not, the document is rejected. Ask the finance team to open the period in the Manage Posting Periods app, or have the authorization group arranged if the period is open only for selected users.

  • February limit 28. A variant with day limit 28 sends February 29 postings to the next period.

  • Controlling area and company code disagree. Their variants may differ only in the number of special periods.

  • Changing a variant late. In the Public Edition the variant cannot be changed after activation, and a year-dependent own variant needs the three previous fiscal years maintained too. SAP Help says that changing the settings of a shortened fiscal year has far-reaching effects.

  • Special period without a date in period 012. A special-period posting needs a posting date inside the last regular period, and the period typed in the header.

  • More than 12 periods in a ledger. Direct posting with FB01L is not possible; use a ledger group whose representative ledger has no more than 12 periods.

  • Older descriptions through Customizing only. Opening a period through the Customizing activity with its two intervals is the classic way. SAP S/4HANA adds the Manage Posting Periods app (F2293), which the Public Edition documentation names for opening and closing periods. Read a classic description as the background of the same rules.

Summary

  • A fiscal year variant defines how many posting periods a fiscal year has (at most 12 regular plus 4 special, 16 in all for the leading ledger), and it is assigned to each company code and controlling area.

  • A calendar-year variant such as K4 needs no period entries; a non-calendar variant needs the period end (month and day) and a year shift of -1, 0 or +1 for every period, and the fiscal year is the calendar year plus the shift.

  • February always gets the day limit 29, so that leap-year postings find the right period.

  • Special periods 013 to 016 subdivide the last regular period; you type them in the document header, and the posting date must lie in period 012 (or the last regular period).

  • A posting period variant opens and closes periods for many company codes at once and can differentiate by account type, account range and authorization group.

  • On premise, periods are maintained through Customizing and the Manage Posting Periods app (F2293); in the Public Edition the fiscal year variant is chosen once in SAP Central Business Configuration and cannot be changed after activation.
