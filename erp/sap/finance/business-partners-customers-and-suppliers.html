Business Partners as Customers and Suppliers

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Every sale on credit creates a receivable from a customer, and every purchase on credit creates a payable to a supplier. Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable keep one account per customer and per supplier, and each of those accounts hangs on a master record. In SAP S/4HANA that master record is no longer a stand-alone "customer" or "vendor" record: it is a business partner that has been given a customer role or a supplier role. This page explains the finance side of that arrangement: what the roles are, which finance data sits in the company code part of the record, what a reconciliation account is and why every customer and supplier needs one, which transaction and apps you use, why the familiar create transactions XK01 and FD01 no longer work as before, and what Customer/Supplier Integration (CVI) does behind the scenes. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The business partner model applies to both; transaction BP and the conversion topics apply to the on-premise side, and the Public Edition works through apps.

Before you read

  • Master Data and the Business Partner: the idea of one record with many roles, the category and the grouping. This page does not repeat it.

  • Finance Overview: the subledgers Accounts Payable and Accounts Receivable next to the general ledger.

  • Organizational Structures: what a company code is, because the finance data of a business partner is kept per company code.

  • Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts: what a G/L account and an account type are.

Key terms

  • Business partner: the central record of an organization, a person or a group, which can take several roles.

  • Business partner role: the view of a partner for one business function, such as customer or supplier in financial accounting.

  • Company code data: the part of a customer or supplier record that is kept separately for each company code.

  • Reconciliation account: the G/L account that the system posts to automatically whenever you post to a customer or supplier account.

  • Subledger: the detail accounts of one kind, such as all customer accounts, that sit behind one or more reconciliation accounts.

  • Customer/Supplier Integration (CVI): the synchronization that keeps the business partner and the customer or supplier record in step.

  • Account group: a classification of customer or supplier records that controls their number assignment and which fields they show.

  • Business partner grouping: a classification of business partners that decides the number range of the partner.

One partner, two kinds of account

SAP’s help for the business partner says that you can centrally manage master data for business partners, customers and suppliers, and that transaction BP is the single point of entry to create, edit and display all three, depending on the roles that are configured. The Public Edition says the same about its leading object: a business partner record exists only once, whatever business processes it takes part in, and when a company is both customer and supplier you need one record for both roles.

For finance this has a plain consequence. The partner holds the data that is true everywhere, such as name and address. A customer role and a supplier role add the data that Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable need. Posting still goes to a customer account or a supplier account, because those accounts are what the subledgers are made of; the business partner is the record that those accounts hang on.

Sources: Business Partner (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Business Partner (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition)

The finance roles

A role is the view of a business partner for one business function. SAP’s definition of the business partner role is that it describes rights and obligations a partner can take on in business transactions, and that the attributes of a role are tailored to the transaction behind it. The central data (name, address, bank details) is created only once, however many roles the partner has.

The help page of the Manage Business Partner Master Data app names the roles that matter for a beginner in finance:

Role What SAP’s help calls it

FLCU00

FI customer (the financial accounting view of a customer).

FLCU01

Sales customer.

FLVN00

FI supplier (the financial accounting view of a supplier).

FLVN01

Supplier (the purchasing view).

A partner can hold any combination. The app lets you delete a role only if it is not the only role of that category: the help says you can delete all but one role for the categories customer and supplier. Roles also have a validity period, which the Simplification List describes with an example: a partner with the prospect role can be moved to the sold-to-party role by closing the validity of the first and starting the second.

Two sources name the roles slightly differently. The Manage Business Partner Master Data app page calls the 00 roles the financial accounting views. One Simplification List item, which deals with specific fields for US federal financials, calls FLVN01 and FLCU01 the financial accounting part. This page follows the app page; in your own system, open the role list of transaction BP to see what each role is called.

Sources: Manage Business Partner Master Data (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Business Partner Role (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Simplification List for SAP S/4HANA 2025 (PDF)

What the finance data holds

SAP’s definition of the customer master record divides it into areas so that each company code and each sales organization can store its own information. The finance-relevant ones are:

  • General data: valid for all company codes, such as name, address and language.

  • Company code data: specific to one company code. SAP’s examples are the reconciliation account number, the payment terms and the dunning procedure.

  • Sales data (customers): per sales organization and distribution channel. This is not finance data.

  • Purchasing data (suppliers): per purchasing organization. Also not finance data.

SAP’s example makes the split concrete: a company with two company codes that trade with the same customer stores the address once, and each company code stores its own reconciliation account and payment terms in its own company code area. The supplier record follows the same pattern. SAP’s description of it lists the tax numbers, bank details, account control data such as the reconciliation account, and the payment methods and payment terms agreed with the supplier.

The field mapping pages of the on-premise help show how the company code fields of the old Customizing screens appear in the Fiori apps. Use them as the checklist of what finance data a record has:

Reconciliation accounts

A subledger such as Accounts Receivable holds a separate account per customer, but the balance sheet needs one number for "receivables". The reconciliation account connects the two. SAP states that when you post items to a subledger, the system automatically posts the same data to the general ledger at the same time. Each subledger has one or more reconciliation accounts in the general ledger, and these keep the balance sheet current without any transfer of totals from the subledgers. You must enter a reconciliation account in every customer and supplier record, in the company code data.

Two settings make it work:

  1. In the G/L account. You mark a G/L account as a reconciliation account by entering the account type it serves, for example customer or vendor, in the field Reconciliation account for account type. SAP’s example is the account "Receivables from goods and services" with the entry Customer. The account can then be assigned only to accounts of that type, and the system checks that the account named in a customer master record is allowed for customers.

  2. In the customer or supplier record. The company code data names the reconciliation account that the subledger account posts to.

SAP adds that you cannot post to a reconciliation account manually. That is the point of the construction: the subledger and the general ledger cannot drift apart, because the only way to change the reconciliation account is to post to a customer or supplier account.

What else a reconciliation account controls, from SAP’s descriptions:

  • Posting screens. You can use it to configure the screens for posting items to customer or supplier accounts, for example to hide fields for exchange rate hedges if you do not use them. This is the reason why the field status of customers and suppliers is partly set in the reconciliation account, as the page on number ranges and field status explains. The field status of the master record screens is a different setting: it comes from the account group’s screen layout, in the activity Define Account Groups with Screen Layout (Customers) or (Vendors) on premise.

  • Currencies. You can use it to say which currencies may be posted to the connected accounts. You usually enter the local currency in the master record, and postings are then possible in all currencies. If you enter a foreign currency key in the reconciliation account, postings to the connected accounts are possible only in that currency.

  • Tax code field. The reconciliation account’s tax category field decides whether the tax code field is hidden when you post to a customer or supplier account or whether it is ready for input. SAP lists three indicators: a hyphen allows only input tax codes, a plus sign only output tax codes and an asterisk all tax codes.

Changing a reconciliation account

You can change the reconciliation account in a customer or supplier record. If you do so after items were posted, the payables or receivables in the balance sheet are off, because the old items stay on the old reconciliation account. SAP says you must then adjust the account for the balance sheet at year end, using the regrouping program for changed reconciliation accounts: it posts the items from the old accounts to the new ones through temporary adjustment accounts and reverses those postings after the financial statements. The adjustment accounts and posting keys come from the Customizing activity Define Adjustment Accounts for Changed Reconciliation Accounts. SAP also recommends to protect the reconciliation account field with the field status or authorizations, so that nobody changes it by mistake.

The account for a single posting

The reconciliation account of the master record is the normal case. In sales, billing documents can be posted to alternative reconciliation accounts that the system finds with the condition technique, based on, for example, the sales organization and the account assignment group of the customer. The Public Edition help gives the account determination type for this as KOAB. A related on-premise page, about settlement management documents, adds that when a G/L account is determined this way, the system ignores the reconciliation account in the customer or supplier master record. The page on account determination and automatic postings shows how the condition technique finds an account.

Sources: Define Reconciliation Account for Account Type (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Defining Reconciliation Accounts (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Functions of the Reconciliation Account: Supplier/Vendor Master Records (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Reconciliation Accounts, tax category (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Changes to the Reconciliation Account (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Changing a Supplier/Vendor Master Record (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Reconciliation Account Determination (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Reconciliation Account Determination (SAP Help Portal, on premise, settlement management)

What replaced XK01 and FD01

In SAP ERP, a supplier was created with XK01 (centrally) or FK01 (accounting only), and a customer with XD01 (centrally) or FD01 (accounting only). The Simplification List item "Business Partner Approach" states what happens in SAP S/4HANA: the business partner is the mandatory, leading object, and transaction BP is the user interface. There is no separate customer or supplier interface, and the old transactions are not available. They are redirected to BP.

The list of redirected transactions in the Simplification List item includes the create, change, display, block and delete-flag transactions of the three families:

Family Redirected to BP

Customer (FI)

FD01, FD02, FD03, FD05, FD06

Customer (sales and central)

VD01, VD02, VD03, VD05, VD06, XD01, XD02, XD03, XD05, XD06

Supplier (FI, purchasing and central)

FK01, FK02, FK03, FK05, FK06, MK01, MK02, MK03, MK05, MK06, XK01, XK02, XK03, XK05, XK06

The same item names a short list of transactions that are obsolete and not redirected: MK12, MK18, MK19, XD07, XK07, V+21, V+22 and V+23. Another item of the Simplification List confirms the effect from the point of view of custom code: code that creates or changes customer or supplier master data must be changed so that the business partner is created or changed instead. The business process is not affected, only the transaction to use.

Because the business partner is the leading object, SAP’s guidance for integrations changes too. The Simplification List says the IDocs DEBMAS and CREMAS are not recommended for data integration between S/4HANA systems, and that business partner web services are the choice, with IDocs only when the source system has customer or supplier data and no business partner.

Sources: Simplification List for SAP S/4HANA 2025 (PDF), item S4TWL - Business Partner Approach (SAP Note 2265093, which needs a login and was not opened)

Customer/Supplier Integration (CVI)

The old customer and supplier tables still exist, because much of SAP’s own code and many interfaces read them. CVI is the mechanism that keeps them and the business partner consistent. SAP describes the process as bidirectional: you can process customer or supplier records from business partner maintenance, and data created in the old customer or supplier processing is populated to the business partner. The Customer, Supplier and Business Partner records are three synchronization objects in the configuration of master data synchronization.

When it matters:

  • In a conversion from SAP ERP. The conversion process and its pre-checks check whether CVI is completely introduced, and SAP states that a system without CVI is declined for the transition. All customers and suppliers, and the contacts that relate to them, must be converted to business partners, including those with a deletion flag. SAP recommends, but does not require, that the business partner ID and the customer or supplier ID are the same.

  • In a system that already runs on S/4HANA. Creating a business partner with a customer or supplier role creates the customer or supplier record at the same time, using the number assignment rules below.

The Simplification List names the preparation steps: implement the conversion pre-checks, activate the business function CA_BP_SOA, check the CVI Customizing (for example a missing business partner role category and the number assignments), check and maintain the business partner Customizing (for example missing tax types), check master data consistency with the report CVI_MIGRATION_PRECHK and clean up data such as a missing at sign in an email address. The data load itself runs in the Synchronization Cockpit (transaction MDS_LOAD_COCKPIT), where the Logs button and the Monitor tab help to find errors. A separate Compare Tool for Master Data Synchronization (transaction MDS_COMPARE_TOOL) compares customer or supplier data with business partner data at object level, to find inconsistencies before a migration. Each of these needs an SAP system; none was run for this page.

How numbers are assigned

When the customer or supplier record is created together with the partner, the number depends on the grouping of the business partner and the account group of the customer or supplier, each of which can use internal or external number assignment. The on-premise help describes the direction customer or supplier to business partner like this:

Account group Business partner grouping Result

Internal numbers

Internal numbers

The system generates both numbers.

Internal numbers

External numbers, number equivalence active

The customer or supplier number becomes the business partner number.

External numbers

Internal numbers

External numbers

External numbers, number equivalence active

For the direction business partner to customer or supplier, the help adds the Same Numbers indicator and, for flexible groupings, the Copy ID indicator (with an optional offset or prefix), which make the system copy the partner number into the customer or supplier number. The assignment of account groups to groupings is set in the Customizing activities of master data synchronization (below). SAP recommends, but does not require, that the numbers are identical.

What older books show, and what has changed

The book SAP S/4HANA Financial Accounting Configuration, 2nd edition (2023), lists FD01 to FD06 and FK01 to FK06 in its appendix of useful transaction codes as the transactions to create, change, display, block and flag customer and supplier master records. In SAP S/4HANA these are redirected to transaction BP, as the Simplification List says, so a reader who follows the appendix literally ends up in BP anyway. The book Configuring SAP S/4HANA Finance, 3rd edition (2025), describes the business partner approach as mandatory and BP as the one transaction, and its list of obsolete transactions maps FK01 to BP. Material written for SAP ERP describes customers and vendors as two separate master records with separate create transactions; that no longer holds.

Common errors and pitfalls

  • Looking for XK01 or FD01 to create an account. They are redirected to BP; create the business partner and select the role.

  • Creating a business partner without the finance role. A partner without the FLCU00 or FLVN00 role (or the customer or supplier role in the Fiori apps) has no finance data to fill in, such as the reconciliation account.

  • Choosing the wrong grouping. The business partner category cannot be changed afterwards, and neither can the grouping the partner was created with. Decide them at creation.

  • Treating the reconciliation account like a normal account. You cannot post to it manually; you post to the customer or supplier account and the system posts to it.

  • Changing the reconciliation account after posting. Plan the regrouping adjustment at year end, and restrict the field.

  • Archiving a customer or supplier that is still linked. SAP states that deletion of the customer or supplier record is not possible while it is connected to the business partner; the partner must be archived first, with its link.

  • Marking non-standard fields as required in a Public Edition account group. SAP warns that this triggers errors in the Fiori apps that the screen cannot resolve.

Summary

  • In SAP S/4HANA a customer or a supplier is a business partner with the roles for financial accounting (FLCU00, FLVN00 in SAP’s role list) and for sales or purchasing. General data is kept once; company code data is kept per company code.

  • The company code data holds the finance settings: the reconciliation account, payment terms, payment methods, house bank, tolerance group and clearing settings.

  • The reconciliation account is a G/L account marked with the account type it serves. Every posting to a customer or supplier account also posts to it automatically, and nobody can post to it manually.

  • XK01, FD01 and their relatives are redirected to transaction BP; the Public Edition offers the Maintain Business Partner app and the three Manage Master Data apps.

  • CVI keeps the business partner and the old customer and supplier records in step in both directions and decides how numbers are assigned. A conversion from SAP ERP needs it completely in place.

  • In the example, the one partner Nordwind Logistics has a customer balance of EUR 1,190.00 and a supplier balance of EUR 595.00, each mirrored in its reconciliation account.
