Cash Journal

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Most companies still handle a little physical cash: a petty cash box in the office, a till that takes small payments, a drawer that needs topping up from the bank. Someone has to write down every coin that goes in and out, and the accountant has to be sure the book balance matches the box. SAP’s tool for this is the cash journal, a small ledger of cash receipts and cash payments that keeps its own opening balance, receipt total, payment total and closing balance, and turns each entry into a normal accounting document. This page explains what the cash journal is, how it is set up, how an entry is entered, saved and posted, which special cases the help describes, and how one day of cash movements of the fictitious company code EX01 looks in the books. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The cash journal exists in both; the page says where the names differ.

Before you read

  • Accounting Basics: debit, credit and why every posting balances.

  • Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys: what an accounting document is and why it has a document type.

  • Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts: what a G/L account and its account type are.

  • Number Ranges, Field Status and Tolerance Groups: why every document gets a number from an interval.

Key terms

  • Cash journal: a Bank Accounting subledger for managing the cash in a business, with its own balances.

  • Business transaction: in the cash journal, a predefined kind of receipt or payment that fixes the accounts and the transaction type.

  • House bank: a bank at which the company holds an account, identified in the cash journal together with the account ID when cash moves to or from a bank account.

  • Accounting document: the document in Financial Accounting that a posted cash journal entry corresponds to.

  • Document type: the key that classifies a document and decides, among other things, which number range it uses.

  • Number range: the interval of document numbers from which the next number is taken.

What the cash journal is

SAP’s definition is brief: the cash journal is a Bank Accounting subledger for the management of cash in a business. The system calculates and displays the opening and closing balances and the totals of receipts and payments by itself. You can run several cash journals in one company code, and you can post to G/L accounts and also to supplier and customer accounts. SAP recommends a separate cash journal for each currency, although you can enter amounts in different currencies.

Two facts shape everything else on this page.

  • A cash journal belongs to one company code. SAP states that you can run several cash journals in one company code, but you cannot assign one cash journal to several company codes.

  • Entering an entry and posting it are two steps. In SAP’s words, you save the entries in the cash journal before they are transferred to Financial Accounting; after the transfer, the cash journal postings correspond to the FI documents. The Public Edition help adds that when entries are only saved, your accounting information is not updated, and the data is updated only after posting.

The cash journal can be used independently of other posting transactions. It is a single-screen transaction: you enter, display and change cash journal documents on one screen.

Setting up a cash journal

A cash journal is created in configuration, not in master data. On premise the settings are in Customizing for Financial Accounting under Bank Accounting, Business Transactions, Cash Journal. The pages SAP publishes for country versions show the activity names of that branch, and these names are the ones used here:

Financial Accounting
  Bank Accounting
    Business Transactions
      Cash Journal
        Define Document Types for Cash Journal Documents
        Set Up Cash Journal
        Create, Change, Delete Business Transactions
        Set Up Print Parameters for Cash Journal

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

In the Public Edition you find the settings with the search function of the configuration environment. The help lists five activities for the cash journal:

  • Maintain Business Transactions

  • Set Up Cash Journal

  • Define Amount Limit

  • Define Numbering Groups for Cash Journal

  • Define Number Ranges for Numbering Groups

The system comes with a cash journal for your company code, number 0001. To get more cash journals in the same company code, copy the standard cash journal in the Set Up Cash Journal activity and adjust it. The maximum amount you can enter per entry is controlled by the configuration settings of your company, which is the job of the Define Amount Limit activity.

One Customizing detail is worth knowing, and it comes from the pages for the Russian country version. The Set Up Cash Journal activity carries the document type for G/L account postings (DT GL act) and the document type for disbursements (DT Disb.), and the Russian setup uses separate document types for cash deposits and cash disbursements so that they are numbered separately, as local law requires. The general lesson: the document type of a cash journal posting is a setting of the cash journal, and the number range follows from the document type.

Every cash journal also needs a G/L account that holds the cash. The payment-process help explains the account types involved. A bank reconciliation account and its bank subaccounts are the special cash accounts SAP recommends for house bank connections, and the cash journal treats them in a particular way, as the next section shows.

Sources: Setting Up Cash Journals (SAP Help Portal, on premise, Russia), Preparation and Customizing for Cash Journal Printouts (SAP Help Portal, on premise, Russia), Post Cash Journal Entries (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Define G/L Accounts for Payment Processes (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Business transactions and posting

Each cash journal entry refers to a business transaction. A business transaction is defined in configuration, and the help says you may also define a new one while you are making entries in the cash journal. The business transaction has a transaction type. The help names four of them:

Type Name in the help Account to use

B

Payment to Bank Account

If the G/L account has the account type Cash Account with the subtype Bank Reconciliation Account or Bank Subaccount, both House Bank and Account ID must be filled before saving.

C

Receipt from Bank Account

Same rule as B.

R

Revenue

A G/L account that does not have the account type Cash Account with those subtypes.

E

Expense

The same restriction as R.

To enter an entry on premise, go to the cash journal screen of FBCJ, enter a company code and a cash journal number, and choose Continue. Choose the Receipts or the Payments tab, and enter a business transaction and an amount, and depending on the business transaction, either a customer or a supplier. These are the required entries; the others are optional. Then choose Enter to put the entry on the screen, Save to save it and Post to post it. The Public Edition names its tabs Cash Payments, Cash Receipts and Check Receipts.

For a cash journal document you can make CO account assignments, and the system can calculate taxes, including withholding taxes, from the business transaction, taking the tax jurisdiction code into account, for example. If your company code uses extended withholding tax, the help says to make the settings for posting withholding tax on payment under Withholding Tax, Extended Withholding Tax in Customizing before you use the cash journal. In the Public Edition, Registration for Indirect Taxation Abroad is fully supported: for company codes enabled for it you can enter a Tax Country/Region when you create cash journal entries.

Requires an SAP system; not run here.

Sources: Posting Documents in the Cash Journal (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Cash Journal (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Post Cash Journal Entries (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition)

Special cases the help describes

Check receipts and check deposits

A business can also receive a check at the till. The help describes how to handle it on the Check Receipts tab. You first define, in Customizing, additional business transactions for depositing checks at the bank, transaction type B. Then:

  1. Enter a check. Required are the business transaction, amount, check number, check issuer, bank key and bank account.

  2. Save it (with Save Selection for chosen entries, or Save for all) and post it (Post Selection or Post). The system checks the data to be posted.

  3. To deposit the checks, select the saved or posted checks and choose Present Checks. A dialog box confirms the number and total value of the checks, assigns them to a check lot with a sequential number and proposes a value date.

  4. Enter the business transaction for the deposit at the bank, then choose Save Lot or Post Lot, and print the check deposit list.

The system then determines the total posting for the deposit and shows it as a new business transaction item. The help warns that checks that have been saved and deposited can no longer be reversed, and that posting checks needs the appropriate authorization.

Splitting an entry

For each receipt or expense you can open a split dialog on each of the three tabs and give different account assignments, tax codes or business areas for each part. The system calculates the tax amounts in the dialog and, from the split, creates offsetting posting items, cash journal items and a summary tax item. A split business transaction shows an asterisk on the initial screen. This is a cash journal feature; the general idea of splitting a document by characteristics is explained on the page about Document Splitting.

Direct tax postings

In the cash journal you can post to tax accounts directly, so that, for example, import sales and purchases tax can be paid in cash. The tax code must be single-level, which means its costing sheet has only one active item, and a tax account must be defined for the business transaction in Customizing.

Printing, reversing, deleting

You can print all posted entries of the displayed period as a cash journal, and print receipts for saved entries; the print form is chosen in Customizing. With the appropriate authorization you can delete saved entries, and you can display all documents deleted within a period. The Public Edition app also lets you reverse a cash journal entry, and the output of cash journal receipts (print queue, output type FI_CASH_JOURNAL_RECEIPT and form template FI_CASHJOURNAL_RECEIPT) is available only in the Public Edition releases of the app; the help says it is configured with the Output Parameter Determination app.

Sources: Cash Journal: Depositing Checks (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Document Split in the Cash Journal (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Direct Tax Postings in the Cash Journal (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Configuring the Output of Cash Journal Receipts (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Post Cash Journal Entries (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition)

Country versions, audit access and archiving

Several local requirements are met by country versions rather than by the standard cash journal. Examples in the help: for Russia, where the law prescribes the layout and numbering of cash vouchers, a local version provides the cash voucher register (form KO-3) and cash journal (form KO-4) and lets several users work in the same cash journal at once; the Public Edition offers receipt templates for Poland and a cash journal report for Romania; for Hungary an app calculates a weighted average exchange rate every working day for cash journals that use a foreign currency. If your company code is in one of these countries, read the country page; the rest of this page describes the standard function.

To restrict an external auditor to a period, the on-premise help describes an authorization check scenario named EXTERNAL_AUDIT in transaction SACF, a dedicated role and the authorization object S_BRWS_TIM; the Public Edition help points to Auditor Direct Access for Professional Service for the same purpose.

Old cash journal documents can be archived with the archiving object FI_TCJ_DOC. SAP states that only completely posted, deleted and reversed cash journal documents can be archived; if the selection contains a document that does not meet this, no other document of the same cash journal and company code can be archived either.

Sources: Cash Journal (SAP Help Portal, on premise, Russia), Calculate Exchange Rates for Cash Journals (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Post Cash Journal Entries, Poland (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Cash Journal Report, Romania (SAP Help Portal, Public Edition), Time Restriction on Displaying Cash Journal Entries (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Archiving Cash Journal Documents Using FI_TCJ_DOC (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Worked example: one day in the petty cash journal of EX01

Company code EX01 keeps its books in euro (EUR), uses British pounds (GBP) as group currency, and writes ledgers 0L and 2L. It runs one cash journal, number 0001, in EUR, with a cash G/L account that stands for the petty cash box. All amounts are made up, and tax is left out to keep the example short. The postings that the cash journal produces are ordinary accounting documents, so the ledgers are updated as the page on the Universal Journal explains; this page does not repeat that.

The box holds EUR 500.00 at the start of the day. Three entries are made:

  1. A small cash sale of EUR 120.00 (business transaction type R, Revenue).

  2. Office supplies bought for EUR 45.50 (type E, Expense).

  3. EUR 300.00 taken to the bank (type B, Payment to Bank Account). The offsetting account is a bank subaccount, so the House Bank and Account ID fields must be filled before saving.

Common errors and pitfalls

  • Assuming a saved entry is in the books. Saving updates the cash journal; the accounting information is updated only after posting.

  • Using a bank subaccount without house bank data. For types B and C the House Bank and Account ID must be filled, otherwise the entry cannot be saved.

  • Using a revenue or expense type with a bank reconciliation or bank subaccount. Types R and E need a G/L account that is not a cash account of those subtypes.

  • Trying to share one cash journal between company codes. It is not possible; create one per company code.

  • Expecting to undo a deposited check. Saved and deposited checks can no longer be reversed.

  • Mixing currencies in one journal. SAP recommends one cash journal per currency.

  • Forgetting the amount limit. The maximum amount per entry is a configuration setting in the Public Edition, so an entry above it is not accepted.

What older books show, and what has changed

The book SAP S/4HANA Financial Accounting Configuration, 2nd edition (2023), devotes a chapter to the cash journal and walks through the same configuration steps: setting up a cash journal, creating the G/L accounts, an amount limit, number range intervals, business transactions and print parameters. Its concepts match the help. Three things differ from the current help pages used on this page.

  • The book describes the cash journal as a part of SAP R/3. In S/4HANA the same function is reached through the Post Cash Journal Entries transaction on premise and the app of the same name in the Public Edition.

  • For numbering, the book copies the number range intervals of a delivered company code. The Public Edition help lists the activities Define Numbering Groups for Cash Journal and Define Number Ranges for Numbering Groups.

  • The book teaches Customizing paths only. The Public Edition help points to the search function of the configuration environment instead of a menu path, and it describes two apps, one for posting and one for display.

Summary

  • The cash journal is a Bank Accounting subledger that tracks cash receipts and payments and shows the opening balance, receipt and payment totals and the closing balance. It belongs to one company code; a company code can have several cash journals, and SAP recommends one per currency.

  • An entry is first saved in the cash journal and then posted, which creates the accounting document. A saved entry does not yet update the books.

  • Each entry refers to a business transaction with a type: B and C for moves to and from a bank account (house bank and account ID required for bank subaccounts), R and E for revenue and expense.

  • On premise the tool is FBCJ; in the Public Edition it is the Post Cash Journal Entries app and, for display, the Cash Journal app. Setup is in the Bank Accounting Customizing branch on premise and in five configuration activities in the Public Edition.

  • Checks are deposited in lots, entries can be split, taxes can be posted directly, and country versions add statutory reports and forms.

  • In the worked example for EX01, EUR 500.00 plus receipts of EUR 120.00 minus payments of EUR 345.50 leaves a closing balance of EUR 274.50, equal to the net movement of the cash account.
