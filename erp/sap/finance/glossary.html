Finance Glossary

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This glossary explains, in plain words, the finance and accounting terms used across the Finance part of the SAP section, such as general ledger, cost center, posting period and clearing, and links each one to the page that teaches it. Use it when a Finance page uses a word you have not met. General SAP terms are in the SAP Glossary. Example values on the Finance pages, such as the profit centers EX-RETAIL and EX-WHOLE, are made up. Terms are listed alphabetically.

A

Account assignment object

The thing a cost is charged to. Every cost posting in Controlling names one, such as a cost center or an internal order, so the cost can later be reported against it. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Account determination

The set of rules that lets the system choose the G/L account for a line it creates by itself, so that nobody has to enter the account by hand each time. The rules read data such as the material, the kind of line and the chart of accounts. See Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

Account group

A label on a customer, supplier or G/L account record that decides how the record is created: which number interval it takes and which fields appear on its screens. Records of the same kind therefore look and number the same way. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

Account key

A short key attached to a pricing condition type, such as a discount or a freight charge, so that its amount is posted to its own revenue or deduction account instead of the general sales account. See Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

Accounting Business Transaction Interface (ABTI)

The interface in Accounting that takes business data handed over by other applications, such as a sales or purchasing document, and turns it into journal entries. It is the path that substitution and validation rules for those postings run on. See Substitutions, Validations and BAdIs.

Accounting document

The stored record of one posting. It has a header with data for the whole document and at least two line items that balance, and it keeps its own number so the posting can always be found again. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

Accounting principle

A set of valuation rules for the books, such as IFRS, US GAAP or a local standard. The same business event can be valued differently under each principle, so each one is kept in its own ledger. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Accounts payable

The part of accounting that records what the company owes its suppliers. Each supplier invoice creates a payable that stays open until a payment settles it. See Accounts Payable.

Accounts Receivable (FI-AR)

The part of accounting that records what customers owe the company. Each customer invoice becomes an open item until the customer’s payment clears it. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Accrual

Recording a cost or a revenue in the period it belongs to, even when the invoice or the money arrives later. It keeps each period’s result from being distorted by the timing of paperwork. See Period-End Closing.

Acquisition and production cost (APC)

The cost at which an asset is first recorded, including what it took to buy or build it, before any depreciation is taken off. Subtracting the accumulated depreciation gives the net book value. See Asset Accounting.

Activity type

A measurable service that a cost center provides, such as machine hours or repair hours. Giving it a price per unit lets the cost of the service be charged to the objects that use it. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Actual costing

An optional calculation at the end of a period that replaces the preliminary standard price of a material with a price based on what the material really cost. It is run by the Material Ledger. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Advanced Financial Closing

SAP’s separately licensed solution for the closing process. It runs task lists for the period-end close and can coordinate tasks across several systems. See Period-End Closing.

Allocation

The period-end passing of costs, or of activity quantities, from sender objects to receiver objects, for example sharing the cost of a service department among the departments that use it. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Allocation cycle

The definition of one allocation run. It has a header with its validity and settings and one or more segments that hold the actual senders, receivers and rules. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Allocation segment

The part of an allocation cycle that holds the senders, the receivers and the rules for one allocation. A cycle can contain several segments, each passing on a different set of costs. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

App ID

The identifier of an app in the SAP Fiori apps reference library, such as F0718. It identifies the app unambiguously even when its name differs between releases or languages. See Finance Transactions and Fiori Apps.

Application Interface Framework (AIF)

An SAP component whose monitor shows the messages that finance services process asynchronously, so you can see which ones arrived, failed or still wait. It helps you follow a message that was accepted but not yet posted. See Finance APIs.

Asset

Something the company owns or controls that is expected to bring future benefit, such as cash, stock or equipment. Assets are listed on one side of the balance sheet. See Accounting Basics.

Asset retirement

Removing an asset, or a part of it, from the asset portfolio, for example by selling or scrapping it. The system takes the asset’s value off the books and posts any gain or loss. See Asset Accounting.

Assigned value

The amount counted against a budget. It includes commitments, such as open purchase orders, as well as costs already posted, so the budget is used up before the invoices arrive. See Planning and Budgeting.

Attributed account assignment

A profitability segment that the system works out for a line item from the item’s other account assignments when the journal entry is created. It shows up in margin analysis reports only. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

Automatic payment program

The program that pays due invoices in bulk. It selects the open items that are due, proposes the payments, creates the payment documents and prepares the output for the bank. See Automatic Payment Program.

Automatic posting

A line that the system adds to a document by itself, for example for tax, a cash discount or an exchange rate difference. Account determination decides which account it uses. See Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

Availability control

A check that compares the funds already used with the budget of an object and reacts when a limit is reached, either with a warning or by stopping the posting. See Planning and Budgeting.

B

Balance sheet

The statement that shows what the company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities) and what is left for the owners (equity) at one date. See Accounting Basics.

Bank clearing account

An interim account that a two-step posting rule uses between the bank account and the customer or supplier account. It collects the bank amounts until the matching open items are cleared. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

Bank statement

The bank’s list of the movements and the balance of one account for a period. Matching it against your own postings shows which payments have really gone through. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

BAPI

A documented function module that other programs call to read or change business data without touching the tables directly. BAPI_ACC_DOCUMENT_POST, which posts an accounting document, is one example. See Posting from ABAP: BAPI_ACC_DOCUMENT_POST.

Baseline date

The date from which the days of the payment terms are counted. It is often the invoice date or the posting date, and it fixes when the cash discount periods and the due date end. See Payment Terms, Cash Discount and Installment Plans.

Budget

The approved, binding cost limit for an object such as an internal order or a cost center. Unlike a plan, which is an expectation, a budget can be enforced by availability control. See Planning and Budgeting.

Business area

An older, optional unit of external accounting for a business segment or area of responsibility. In current releases the segment and the profit center cover this need. See Organizational Units in Finance.

Business context

The circumstances a substitution or validation rule is defined for, such as Journal Entry Item or Coding Block. It decides which fields are available inside the rule. See Substitutions, Validations and BAdIs.

Business function

A switch in the Switch Framework that turns on a group of features. Some business functions cannot be turned off again once they are active. See Universal Parallel Accounting.

Business partner

The single master record for a customer, a supplier or a contact. One partner can take several roles, so its address and bank data are kept once. See Business Partners as Customers and Suppliers.

Business partner grouping

A classification of business partners that decides which number range the partner’s number is taken from. See Business Partners as Customers and Suppliers.

Business partner role

The view of a business partner for one business function, such as customer or supplier in financial accounting. Each role holds the data that function needs. See Business Partners as Customers and Suppliers.

Business transaction

A predefined kind of business event, such as a vendor invoice, a payment or a cash receipt. In document splitting it tells the rules what kind of document they see, and in the cash journal it fixes the accounts of a receipt or payment. See Document Splitting.

C

Callup point

In the classic tools, the position in the processing of a posting at which a validation or substitution is called, for example at the document header, at a line item or at the complete document. See Substitutions, Validations and BAdIs.

Cardinality

How many units of one kind can belong to one unit of another kind, written n : 1 or 1 : n. For example, many company codes can belong to one controlling area. See Organizational Units in Finance.

Cash discount

A percentage taken off an invoice when it is paid within an agreed period. It rewards quick payment and is defined in the payment terms. See Payment Terms, Cash Discount and Installment Plans.

Cash journal

A Bank Accounting subledger for the cash a business keeps on hand. It records each receipt and payment as a cash journal entry and keeps its own balances. See Cash Journal.

Cash position

An overview of how much money the company has, shown by bank, company, country and currency. It is built from the bank balances and is used to decide where cash is available. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

Characteristic

A level at which you analyze profitability, such as sales organization, region, product or customer. A combination of characteristic values forms a profitability segment. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

Chart of accounts

The list of G/L accounts that one or more company codes use. Each account has a number and a name, and sharing one chart makes the books of the company codes comparable. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

Chart of depreciation

A country-specific directory of depreciation areas and depreciation keys, assigned to company codes. It decides how assets are depreciated in those company codes. See Asset Accounting.

Check indicator

A setting on a tax code that decides whether the system gives a warning or an error when a manually entered tax amount differs from the calculated one. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Clean core

The rule of extending SAP only through released interfaces and extension points, never by changing standard code or tables. It keeps the system easy to upgrade. See Posting from ABAP: BAPI_ACC_DOCUMENT_POST.

Clearing

Marking open items that add up to zero as settled, for example an invoice and the payment that pays it. Cleared items no longer count as open. See Open Item Management and Clearing.

Clearing document

The document the system creates when items are cleared. Its number is entered in every cleared item so you can see which items were settled together. See Open Item Management and Clearing.

CO-PA derivation

The tool that fills further characteristics of a profitability segment from the data that is already available, for example finding the product group from the product. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

Coding block

The account assignment fields of a line, such as cost center, project and internal order. They say which objects the amount is charged to. See The Universal Journal (ACDOCA).

Commitment

A purchase requisition or purchase order that will cause a cost later but has not been posted as a cost yet. Counting commitments lets a budget reflect spending that is already decided. See Planning and Budgeting.

Communication arrangement

The Public Edition setting that switches a communication scenario on for one communication system and one user. Without an arrangement, the scenario’s services cannot be called. See Finance APIs.

Communication scenario

SAP’s design-time description of which inbound and outbound services two partners use and how they authenticate. It is the template from which a communication arrangement is created. See Finance APIs.

Company code

The smallest organizational unit for which a complete, independent set of accounts can be kept. It is the legal unit that produces the annual accounts. See Organizational Units in Finance.

Company code data

The part of a customer or supplier record that is kept separately for each company code, such as the reconciliation account and the payment terms. See Business Partners as Customers and Suppliers.

Condition technique

The search method that tries condition tables from the most specific to the most general until it finds a matching record. It is used wherever the system must pick a value depending on several conditions, such as an account for a pricing condition. See Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

Condition type

One line in a calculation procedure. In tax calculation it is a tax type with its base amount and account key. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Consolidation unit

The entity whose reported data is consolidated in Group Reporting. In the usual case it is one company. See Group Reporting and Consolidation.

Contra node

A node of a financial statement version paired with another node. It holds the same accounts with the opposite debit or credit setting, so that each account shows on the side that matches its balance. See Financial Statement Versions.

Contribution margin

What remains of revenue after subtracting the costs you choose at one level of the margin structure. It shows how much a product or customer contributes toward covering the other costs. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

Control level

The strictness of a validation rule: Error stops the user, Warning lets the user continue. See Substitutions, Validations and BAdIs.

Controlling
Controlling (CO)

The application area for management accounting. It shows where costs and revenues arise inside the company and why, to support planning and decisions. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Controlling area

The organizational unit within which a complete cost accounting is done in a closed system. Cost centers, orders and other Controlling objects belong to one controlling area. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Controlling area currency

The currency in which cost accounting is done. It is also the default currency of the Controlling objects of the controlling area. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Cost center

A clearly delimited place in the organization where costs occur, such as a department. It belongs to one controlling area and is where costs are collected and monitored. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Cost center group

A collection of cost centers that reflects part of the structure of the organization. Groups make it easy to report on or process several cost centers at once. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Cost component split

The breakdown of a cost estimate into groups such as raw materials, production and overhead. It shows what a product’s cost is made of. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Cost element

A classification of a cost or revenue for management accounting. In SAP S/4HANA a cost element is a type of G/L account. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Cost element category

The setting on a cost element that decides which business transactions may use it, for example whether it can be used for postings from financial accounting or for allocations. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Cost element group

A hierarchy of cost elements used to build report rows and to process several cost elements in one step. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Cost estimate

A calculation of what it costs to make a product, built from the quantities of materials and activities and their prices. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Costing-based profitability analysis

The older form of profitability analysis that stores values in value fields and needs reconciliation with Financial Accounting. Margin analysis is the account-based form that replaced it in SAP S/4HANA. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

Country chart of accounts

An optional chart of accounts that holds the accounts a country’s legal reporting needs. The Public Edition calls it the alternative or local chart of accounts. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

Credit

The right side of an account in double-entry bookkeeping. A credit increases liabilities, equity and revenue and decreases assets and expenses. See Accounting Basics.

Credit exposure

The amount of a customer’s credit limit that has already been used up, for example by open invoices and orders not yet invoiced. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Credit limit

The amount a customer may owe before the credit checks react. It is the ceiling against which the credit exposure is compared. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Credit management

Monitoring how much each customer owes against an agreed limit, so that sales to a customer can be warned about or stopped when the limit is exceeded. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Currency type

The purpose of a currency in a ledger, such as company code currency or group currency, together with its translation settings. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Customer invoice

The document that makes a customer owe money. It creates an open item on the customer account that stays open until the customer pays. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Customer/Supplier Integration (CVI)

The synchronization that keeps the business partner record in step with the customer or supplier record. It lets older customer and supplier data live together with the business partner. See Business Partners as Customers and Suppliers.

D

Data monitor

The app in which the tasks that bring and check unit data are run for each consolidation unit in Group Reporting. See Group Reporting and Consolidation.

Days in arrears

How many days an item is past its due date. The dunning run uses it, after any grace days, to decide whether to remind the customer. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Debit

The left side of an account in double-entry bookkeeping. A debit increases assets and expenses and decreases liabilities, equity and revenue. See Accounting Basics.

Default profit center

A normal profit center used to collect postings until they are moved to the right profit centers. It is also called a clearing profit center. See Profit Center Accounting.

Depreciation area

A set of values and rules kept in parallel for each asset, for example one for the balance sheet and one for taxes. Each area depreciates the same asset under its own rules. See Asset Accounting.

Depreciation key

The combination of calculation rules, such as the method and the useful life, that the system uses to compute depreciation automatically. See Asset Accounting.

Direct quotation

An exchange rate written as units of the To currency for one unit of the From currency. For example, 0.85 GBP for 1 EUR is a direct quotation of EUR to GBP. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Discount base

The amount that the cash discount percentage is applied to, for example the invoice amount with or without tax. See Payment Terms, Cash Discount and Installment Plans.

Distribution rule

The part of a settlement rule that names one receiver, the share it receives and the period the rule applies to. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Document header

The part of a document with the data that holds for the whole document, such as the dates and the document type. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

Document number

The number that identifies one accounting document within a company code and a fiscal year. It is taken from the number range of the document type. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

Document principle

The rule that every posting leaves a numbered document behind instead of changing a balance directly. It lets any balance be traced back to the documents behind it. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

Document splitting

The function that divides the lines of a document by chosen dimensions, such as profit center or segment, when it is posted. It adds clearing lines where a dimension would otherwise not balance. See Document Splitting.

Document splitting characteristic

A dimension, such as profit center or segment, for which documents are split so that each value of it balances on its own. See Document Splitting.

Document splitting rule

The setting that says, for one business transaction, which item categories are split and from which lines the account assignments are taken. See Document Splitting.

Document type

A two-character key in the document header that classifies the document, limits the account types it may post to and selects its number range. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

Double-entry bookkeeping

Recording every business event in at least two accounts, with equal debits and credits, so that the books always balance. See Accounting Basics.

Down payment

Money paid or received before the goods or services are delivered. It is shown separately from normal open items until the final invoice settles it. See Special G/L Transactions.

Down payment request

A reminder document that asks for a down payment, or triggers its automatic payment. It does not change any balance by itself. See Special G/L Transactions.

Dummy profit center

A default value of a controlling area that receives postings to objects without a profit center, in the classic design. See Profit Center Accounting.

Dunning

The automatic reminder process for overdue open items. It groups the overdue items of a customer and sends a reminder whose tone depends on how far behind the payment is. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Dunning block

A key on an account or an item that keeps it out of the dunning run, for example while a dispute is being sorted out. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Dunning level

The step of the dunning procedure that an item has reached. It decides how firm the reminder text is. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Dunning procedure

The setting that says how a business partner is dunned: how often, after how many days and how many times. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

E

Electronic bank statement

A bank statement that enters the system digitally, as a file or through a bank connection, and cannot be edited. The system posts its items by rules instead of by hand. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

Embedded analytics

Reporting tools that run inside SAP S/4HANA on its own data, without copying the data to another system. See Financial Reporting and Embedded Analytics.

Entry view

The document as it was typed or handed over by the source application, before document splitting. See Document Splitting.

Equity

What is left for the owners after the liabilities are subtracted from the assets. See Accounting Basics.

Event

The exact moment and place in a business process where a substitution or validation rule runs, for example when a journal entry item is checked. See Substitutions, Validations and BAdIs.

Exception list

The list of open items that the automatic payment program did not propose, with the reason for each one. See Automatic Payment Program.

Exchange rate difference

The gain or loss that arises when an amount in a foreign currency is booked or settled at a different rate than before. It is posted automatically to its own account. See Open Item Management and Clearing.

Exchange rate type

A key that separates the rates of the same currency pair by purpose, for example one for average rates and another for bank rates. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Expense

The cost used up to earn the revenue of a period. See Accounting Basics.

Extension ledger

A ledger that reads through to an underlying ledger and stores only its own postings or adjustments. It avoids copying all the data of the underlying ledger. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

External number assignment

A way of numbering documents in which the user, or a feeder system, supplies the number, which must be unique within its number range. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

F

Feature comparison

A table in the SAP help that lists transactions and apps side by side, with what each of them can do, so you can see what replaces an old transaction. See Finance Transactions and Fiori Apps.

Field status group

A named set of field settings that decides, for each field of a posting screen, whether it is hidden, required or optional. A G/L account points to one. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

Field status variant

A container for field status groups, assigned to one or more company codes. The G/L accounts of those company codes pick their field status group from it. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

Finance API

A published, supported interface of SAP S/4HANA Finance, either an OData service or a SOAP service, that outside programs use to read or post finance data. See Finance APIs.

Financial Accounting (FI)

The application area that keeps the legal books of each company code and reports to the outside world. See Finance Overview.

Financial Closing cockpit

The task-list tool delivered with SAP S/4HANA that plans, runs and monitors the tasks of a period-end close. See Period-End Closing.

Financial statement version
Financial statement version (FSV)

A hierarchy that groups G/L accounts into the lines of a balance sheet and a profit and loss statement. It lets the same accounts be reported in a layout you choose. See Financial Statement Versions.

Fiscal year

The period, usually 12 months, for which a company produces its balance sheet and statements. It need not match the calendar year. See Fiscal Year Variants and Posting Periods.

Fiscal year variant

The setting that defines how many posting periods and special periods a fiscal year has and how a posting date maps to a period. See Fiscal Year Variants and Posting Periods.

Fit-to-standard

A workshop approach in which the business sees the standard process first and states only what does not fit. It replaces writing every requirement in advance. See Finance Implementation, Migration and Testing.

Fixed asset

A long-lived item the company owns and uses, such as a machine or a building. It is recorded in Asset Accounting with its own master record. See Asset Accounting.

Foreign currency valuation

Revaluing foreign currency balances and open items at the exchange rate of the key date at period end, so the books show their current value. See Period-End Closing.

FS item

A financial statement item: the account of the consolidation chart of accounts to which G/L accounts are mapped in Group Reporting. See Group Reporting and Consolidation.

FSV node

One element of a financial statement version, also called a financial statement item, that holds a group of G/L accounts. See Financial Statement Versions.

G

G/L account

One account of the general ledger, kept as a master record, such as a bank or a revenue account. Postings add up on it. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

G/L account type

The classification of a G/L account as balance sheet, nonoperating expense or income, primary costs or revenue, or secondary costs or cash. It decides how the account is used. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

General ledger
General ledger (FI-GL)

The central record of all accounts of a company code. The totals of the subledgers, such as accounts payable, feed it. See Finance Overview.

General ledger view

A document can look different in each ledger, because split lines and clearing lines are added. The general ledger view is the document as one ledger stores it, including those extra lines. See Document Splitting.

Global hierarchy

A hierarchy is a tree that groups items for reporting. A global hierarchy is one that is maintained in the app Manage Global Hierarchies and shared by many reports; the financial statement version is one type of such hierarchy. See Financial Statement Versions.

Goods receipt

The posting that records that ordered goods have arrived at the company. It is the first step of a purchase and is later matched with the supplier invoice. See Accounts Payable.

GR/IR clearing account

A holding account between the arrival of goods and the invoice for them. It carries the value of goods received but not yet invoiced, or invoiced but not yet received, and it should return to zero once both have been posted. See Accounts Payable.

Group chart of accounts

A chart of accounts whose accounts are shared by the whole corporate group. Because every company can map its own accounts to it, the results of all companies can be reported together. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

Group Reporting

The SAP S/4HANA application for financial consolidation, which combines the figures of several companies of a group into the results of the group as a whole. See Group Reporting and Consolidation.

Group reporting preparation ledger

An accounting ledger that you configure so that the data needed for group reporting is kept in General Ledger Accounting, ready to be passed to the consolidation. See Group Reporting and Consolidation.

Group valuation

Valuing assets and materials from the point of view of the whole group, where profits made between group companies are eliminated. With Universal Parallel Accounting it has its own ledger. See Universal Parallel Accounting.

Guarantee

A promise to stand in for someone else’s debt, either made by the company to a customer or received from a supplier. It is recorded as a special posting that does not change the normal balances. See Special G/L Transactions.

H

Held document

A document you save temporarily while you are still entering it. It has no document number and changes no account balance until you complete and post it. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

House bank

A bank at which your company code keeps an account. It is set up once and identified by a bank ID, so that the system knows where payments are made from. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

House bank account

The representation of one real bank account of the company in Financial Accounting. It is identified by an account ID under its house bank and is linked to a G/L account. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

I

Incoming payment

Money received from a customer. It is posted to the bank and used to clear the open invoices that it pays. See Accounts Receivable, Dunning and Credit Management.

Indirect quotation

A way of writing an exchange rate. Instead of saying how much of the second currency one unit of the first currency buys, it states how many units of the first currency make one unit of the second. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Input tax

Tax that a company pays on its purchases and expenses. It can normally claim this tax back from the tax office. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Installment payment

An invoice that is paid in several parts instead of one amount. Each part has its own payment terms and due date. See Payment Terms, Cash Discount and Installment Plans.

Integrated planning

A way of planning in which the plan values are also written as plan line items and passed on, so that other applications can use them. See Planning and Budgeting.

Internal number assignment

A way of numbering documents in which the system picks the next free number of the number range by itself, so that nobody has to supply or check it. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

Internal order

A Controlling object for a specific job or task, such as a trade fair or a repair. It is used to plan, collect and finally settle the costs of that task. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Item category

A predefined class, such as vendor, expense or tax, to which every account is assigned. The splitting rules use it to know what kind of line they are looking at. See Document Splitting.

J

Journal entry

One posting that records one business event. It has a header and at least two lines, and the debits equal the credits. In SAP S/4HANA this is the name for what used to be called an accounting document. See Accounting Basics.

K

Key date

The date on which a closing run evaluates the data. It is usually the last day of the period being closed. See Period-End Closing.

L

Leading ledger

The main ledger, which is updated in every company code and feeds Controlling. In the standard system it is the ledger with the key 0L. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Ledger

A complete set of books in General Ledger Accounting, kept for one accounting principle, for example the rules of one country. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Ledger group

A named combination of ledgers. A posting or a closing step can address all ledgers of the group together instead of one at a time. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Ledger-specific valuation

Calculating a value, such as depreciation or a material price, separately for each ledger, so that every ledger follows its own accounting principle. See Universal Parallel Accounting.

Liability

Something the company owes to others, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier invoice. See Accounting Basics.

Line item

One line of a journal entry, with one account, one amount and a debit or credit side. A journal entry is made of at least two line items that balance. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

Line item display

A list of the individual postings on an account, as opposed to only its balance. It lets you see which items are open and which are cleared. See Open Item Management and Clearing.

Liquidity forecast

A forecast of the cash that is expected to come in and go out in the coming days, together with the expected closing balance of the bank accounts. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

M

Margin analysis

The form of Profitability Analysis that stores costs and revenues in accounts, so that it is permanently reconciled with Financial Accounting. It shows how profitable products, customers or markets are. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

Material Ledger
Material ledger

The component that values material stock and can keep those values in several currencies and valuations. In SAP S/4HANA it is also called Material Subledger. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Migration cockpit

The SAP Fiori app Migrate Your Data - Migration Cockpit, which loads business data from another system into SAP S/4HANA following predefined migration objects. See Finance Implementation, Migration and Testing.

Migration key date

The date, set per company code, up to which the loaded balances and open items are valid. Postings after it are made in the new system. See Finance Implementation, Migration and Testing.

Migration object

The description of how the data of one kind of business object, such as G/L account balances, is moved into the new system. See Finance Implementation, Migration and Testing.

Movement type

The three-digit key that says what kind of goods movement is posted, for example 261 for a material issue to a production order. It helps decide which accounts are posted automatically. See Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

N

Negative posting

A posting entered with an amount whose sign is opposite to the usual sign of its debit or credit side. It reduces the balance instead of increasing it. See Finance APIs.

Net book value (NBV)

The value an asset still has in the books: its acquisition and production cost (APC) minus the depreciation accumulated so far. See Asset Accounting.

Non-deductible input tax

The part of an input tax that the company cannot claim back from the tax office. It therefore becomes a cost. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Non-leading ledger

A parallel ledger that you activate for the company codes that need to follow another accounting principle in addition to the leading ledger. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Noted item

An entry that is kept only as a reminder, for example for a down payment that is still requested. It does not update any account balance. See Special G/L Transactions.

Number range

A named series of numbers, with its limits, from which the documents of one or more document types take their numbers. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

Number range interval

The from and to numbers, and the fiscal year they apply to, that make up a number range for one company code. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

O

Object currency

The currency kept in the master data of one Controlling object, such as a cost center. See Controlling Overview and Cost Elements.

Open item

A line on an account that still has to be cleared by another line, for example an invoice that has not been paid yet. See Open Item Management and Clearing.

Open item management

A setting that makes an account hold items that must be cleared. The balance of the account is then always the total of its open items. See Open Item Management and Clearing.

Operating chart of accounts

The chart of accounts used for the daily postings in a company code and in the controlling area. Every company code has exactly one. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

Operating concern

The organizational unit for which the sales market has a uniform structure. It defines the characteristics, such as product or customer, that profitability analysis can use. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

Order status

The processing state of an order, such as created or released. It decides which business transactions are allowed on the order. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Outgoing payment

The posting that pays a supplier. It clears the open invoices that it settles. See Accounts Payable.

Output tax

Tax that a company charges on its sales. The company passes it on to the tax office. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

P

Parallel accounting

Keeping the books under more than one accounting principle at the same time, for example for the group and for local law. See Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates.

Parked document

A saved but incomplete document that already has a document number. It changes no balance until it is completed and posted. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

Payment block

A mark on an invoice or on a supplier account that keeps the payment program from paying it. See Accounts Payable.

Payment medium

The output of a payment run that goes to the bank or the payee, such as a payment file, a check or a payment advice. See Automatic Payment Program.

Payment method

How a payment is made, for example by check, bank transfer or bank direct debit. See Automatic Payment Program.

Payment proposal

The list of payments that the payment program would make. You can check and change it before anything is posted. See Automatic Payment Program.

Payment run

One execution of the automatic payment program, identified by a run date and an identification. See Automatic Payment Program.

Period-end closing

The ordered set of periodic activities that correct and complete the books of a period before the period is closed to further postings. See Period-End Closing.

Periodic unit price

The actual price that actual costing calculates for a material for one closed period, based on what the material really cost in that period. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Plan version

A separate copy of plan data, so that alternative plans can be kept side by side and compared. See Planning and Budgeting.

Plan/actual variance

The difference between the actual costs and the planned costs of an object such as a cost center. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Planning

Entering the expected costs, activities, prices or statistical key figures for a period, so that actual values can be compared with them later. See Planning and Budgeting.

Posting key

A two-digit key on each line of a document. It fixes the account type, the debit or credit side and the layout of the entry screen. See Journal Entries, Document Types and Posting Keys.

Posting period

One slice of the fiscal year, usually a month, with a start date and an end date. A posting is made into the period of its posting date, and only open periods accept postings. See Fiscal Year Variants and Posting Periods.

Posting period variant

A named set of rules that says which posting periods are open. One or more company codes can share it. See Fiscal Year Variants and Posting Periods.

Posting rule

The older rule for processing a bank statement. It says exactly which accounts an item posts to and whether it clears open items. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

Prediction ledger

The extension ledger in which the predicted entries are written. It is structured like the ledger of actual data. See Predictive Accounting.

Predictive accounting

Using the newest data from outside Finance, such as Sales, to predict accounting results before the actual postings exist. See Predictive Accounting.

Predictive journal entry

An entry in the prediction ledger that shows what a future actual entry is expected to look like. See Predictive Accounting.

Price control

The setting in the material master that says whether a material is valued at a standard price (S) or at a moving average price (V). See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Price difference

The part of a posting that exceeds or falls short of the standard price of a material. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Processing rule

The newer rule, with conditions and actions, for processing bank statement items. SAP recommends it instead of posting rules. See Bank Accounting and Cash Management.

Profit and loss statement

The statement of revenue and expenses over a period, also called the income statement. It shows whether the company made a profit or a loss. See Accounting Basics.

Profit center

An organizational unit that reflects a management-oriented structure of the company for internal control. Its result can be determined and analyzed on its own. See Profit Center Accounting.

Profit Center Accounting

The part of accounting that determines profit and loss by profit center, either by period accounting or by the cost-of-sales approach. See Profit Center Accounting.

Profit center derivation

The rules by which a line item receives a profit center from the object it is posted to. See Profit Center Accounting.

Profit center group

A tree of profit centers, used for reporting, allocations and planning. See Profit Center Accounting.

Profit center valuation

A valuation approach in which goods that move between profit centers are valued at transfer prices. See Profit Center Accounting.

Profitability segment

One combination of values of the characteristics of an operating concern, for example one product sold to one customer group. It corresponds to a market segment. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

R

Receiver

In an allocation, the object that is charged with the allocated value, for example a cost center. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Reconciliation

Comparing two sources of the same figure, here the old and the new system, and explaining every difference. See Finance Implementation, Migration and Testing.

Reconciliation account

A general ledger account that the system posts to automatically whenever you post to a customer or supplier account. The ledger therefore always shows the total of the subledger. See Business Partners as Customers and Suppliers.

Reduction posting

A predictive entry that cancels the amount of an earlier prediction once the actual entry exists. See Predictive Accounting.

Reference key

The object key that a posting coming from another application carries, so that the source document can be found again from the accounting entry. See Posting from ABAP: BAPI_ACC_DOCUMENT_POST.

Release Universal Journal

The older task that copies data from accounting to group reporting. It is the classic way of integrating the two. See Group Reporting and Consolidation.

Retained earnings account

The equity account that receives the balance of the profit and loss accounts at year end, so that the result of the year is carried forward. See Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts.

Revenue

The value of the goods and services sold in a period. See Accounting Basics.

S

SAP Fiori app

A task-focused web application that you start from a tile on the launchpad. See Finance Transactions and Fiori Apps.

SAP Fiori apps reference library

SAP’s public catalog of apps, with their name, ID, release availability and technical data. See Finance Transactions and Fiori Apps.

SAP GUI

The older desktop-style user interface of SAP systems, in which you open screens by typing transaction codes. See Finance Transactions and Fiori Apps.

Segment

A division of a company for which you can create financial statements for external reporting. See Organizational Units in Finance.

Sender

In an allocation, the object that holds the value to be passed on, for example a cost center together with an account. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Settlement

The transfer of the costs collected on a sender object, such as an order, to its receivers. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Settlement profile

The settings that control settlement for a kind of sender, such as which receivers are allowed. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Settlement rule

The definition of where the costs of a sender are settled. It is made up of one or more distribution rules. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Simplification List

SAP’s list of what changed between SAP ERP and SAP S/4HANA, including transactions that are redirected or obsolete. See Finance Transactions and Fiori Apps.

Source document

The business document, here a sales order, from which predictions are derived. See Predictive Accounting.

Special G/L indicator

The one-letter code that marks a line item as a special G/L transaction and says which kind it is. See Special G/L Transactions.

Special G/L transaction

A posting to a customer or supplier account that is shown separately in the general ledger and in the subledger, for example a down payment. It is not handled like an ordinary invoice. See Special G/L Transactions.

Special node

One of seven nodes that the system creates in every financial statement version. Each carries a fixed meaning in reporting. See Financial Statement Versions.

Special period

An extra period, also called an adjustment period, that subdivides the last regular period so that year-end closing postings can be made separately. See Fiscal Year Variants and Posting Periods.

Staging table

A table that the migration cockpit creates for each migration object. You fill it from a template file before the data is loaded. See Finance Implementation, Migration and Testing.

Standard cost estimate

The cost estimate, normally made once a year, whose result becomes the standard price of a material. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Standard hierarchy

The one tree of cost centers (and, separately, of profit centers) that every such object must belong to. See Organizational Units in Finance.

Statistical key figure

A number kept on a Controlling object that is not an amount, for example the number of employees or the floor area. It is often used as a tracing factor. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Statistical posting

A posting whose offsetting entry always goes to the same preset account, such as a guarantee. See Special G/L Transactions.

Subledger

A detailed ledger, such as accounts payable or asset accounting, whose totals feed the general ledger. See Finance Overview.

Substitution

A rule that derives, replaces or clears the value of a field while data is being entered. See Substitutions, Validations and BAdIs.

Supplier invoice

The bill from a supplier, entered in the system so that it becomes a payable. See Accounts Payable.

T

T-account

A drawing of one account as a letter T, with the debits on the left and the credits on the right. See Accounting Basics.

Target tax code

The tax code that a deferred tax is moved to when the tax is recognized. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Tax calculation procedure

The country-specific list of tax types (condition types) and the rules that calculate and post them. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Tax code

A short key that stands for a tax rate and its rules. It is entered on a line item so that the system can check, calculate and post the tax. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Tax jurisdiction code

A multi-level key for the taxing authorities of a location. Together with the tax code it is used to find the tax amount (United States and Brazil). See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Tax type

The kind of tax that a tax code carries, such as input tax, output tax or non-deductible input tax. It decides how the amount is calculated and posted. See Tax Codes and Tax Calculation.

Technical clearing account for integrated asset acquisition

The account that sits between the supplier invoice and the asset posting in an integrated asset acquisition. It always ends at zero. See Asset Accounting.

Terms of payment
Payment terms

The agreement on when an invoice is due and with which cash discount it can be paid early. In the system it is a key that holds the days, the cash discount percentages and the baseline date rule. See Payment Terms, Cash Discount and Installment Plans.

Tolerance group

A named set of limits, kept for a company code, for the amounts a user may post or the differences the system may accept. See Number Ranges, Field Status Variants and Tolerance Groups.

Tolerance limit

The usage level or absolute amount at which availability control issues a warning or an error. See Planning and Budgeting.

Tracing factor

The number that decides what share of the sender value each receiver gets, for example floor area or hours used. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Transaction code

A short name, such as FB50, that opens one screen of the SAP GUI. See Finance Transactions and Fiori Apps.

Transaction key

A three-character key, such as BSX, that names the kind of line in a goods movement or invoice, so that Customizing can assign an account to it. See Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

Trial balance

A list of all account balances, used to check that the total of the debits equals the total of the credits. See Accounting Basics.

True account assignment

A line item whose profitability segment was determined by the sending application or entered by hand. It is available to follow-on processes. See Margin Analysis (Profitability Analysis).

U

Universal Allocation

The group of apps in current releases for defining, running and checking allocations. See Allocations: Assessment, Distribution and Activity Allocation.

Universal Journal

The book of original entry for Financial Accounting and Controlling in SAP S/4HANA. Its line items are stored once, in the table ACDOCA, instead of in separate tables for each area. See The Universal Journal (ACDOCA).

Universal Parallel Accounting

The capability, switched on by one business function, that makes the subledger processes of Asset Accounting, Controlling and the Material Ledger work per ledger. See Universal Parallel Accounting.

V

Validation

A rule that checks the values entered and raises a warning or an error when they break the rule. See Substitutions, Validations and BAdIs.

Validity period

A date range for which one version of a cost center’s master data holds. See Cost Centers and Internal Orders.

Valuation class

A setting in the accounting data of a material that groups materials which post to the same accounts. See Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

Variance

The difference between the costs a production order should have had (target costs) and the costs charged to it. See Product Costing and the Material Ledger.

Y

Year shift

The entry (-1, 0 or +1) that tells the system how to turn the calendar year of a posting date into the fiscal year. See Fiscal Year Variants and Posting Periods.

Z

Zero balance clearing

The extra lines the system adds so that each balancing dimension of a document nets to zero. See Document Splitting.
