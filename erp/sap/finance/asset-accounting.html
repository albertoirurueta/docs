Asset Accounting

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

A company owns things it uses for many years: a delivery van, a machine, a building. Accounting does not treat the price of such a thing as a cost on the day it is bought. It records the thing as an asset and then spreads its cost over the years in which it is used, a step called depreciation. Asset Accounting is the part of SAP S/4HANA that keeps that subsidiary ledger of fixed assets: it holds each asset’s master record, records its purchase, sale and transfer, calculates its depreciation and posts the results to the general ledger. This page explains the structure (chart of depreciation, depreciation areas, depreciation keys), the business transactions, the four depreciation calculations that SAP documents with formulas, and a worked example for the fictitious company code EX01. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The accounting ideas apply to both. The classic SAP GUI transactions are the on-premise tools, and the Public Edition works through SAP Fiori apps, as each section says.

Before you read

  • Accounting Basics: debit, credit and the rule that every posting balances.

  • Ledgers, Currencies and Exchange Rates: the ledgers 0L and 2L, accounting principles and the currencies of the running example.

  • Universal Parallel Accounting: why one asset can carry several values at once.

  • Accounts Payable: the supplier invoice that an asset purchase is posted with.

Key terms

  • Fixed asset: a long-lived item the company owns and uses, recorded in Asset Accounting with its own master record.

  • Acquisition and production cost (APC): the cost at which an asset is recorded, before any depreciation.

  • Net book value (NBV): the APC minus the accumulated depreciation.

  • Chart of depreciation: a country-specific directory of depreciation areas and depreciation keys, assigned to company codes.

  • Depreciation area: a set of values and rules kept in parallel for each asset, for example one for the balance sheet and one for taxes.

  • Depreciation key: the combination of calculation rules that the system uses to compute depreciation automatically.

  • Technical clearing account for integrated asset acquisition: the account that sits between the supplier invoice and the asset posting and always ends at zero.

  • Asset retirement: the removal of an asset, or part of one, from the asset portfolio, for example by sale or scrapping.

What Asset Accounting does

Asset Accounting (application component FI-AA) is a subsidiary ledger of the general ledger, the same way Accounts Payable is for suppliers. Each fixed asset has a master record, and each business transaction on an asset (a purchase, a sale, a transfer, a period of depreciation) changes the asset’s values and posts to the general ledger. SAP’s help names the asset accountant as the target group of its documentation.

Two ideas from SAP’s description matter at the start.

  • Depreciation is calculated, not entered. Every asset transaction immediately changes the forecast depreciation, but it does not immediately update the depreciation and value adjustment accounts. The planned depreciation is posted to Financial Accounting when you run the periodic depreciation posting run, which posts the planned depreciation for each posting level and asset as a lump sum. The run creates collective documents, not one document for each asset, and its update run can only be started in the background. The migration guide names the program of the posting run RAPOST2000.

  • Asset Accounting is integrated. A transaction on a supplier account in Accounts Payable and a transaction on an asset account each influence the matching G/L accounts directly, so the subsidiary ledgers always reconcile with the general ledger.

Sources: Posting Depreciation (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Migrating from Classic to New Asset Accounting (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

New Asset Accounting and what changed

SAP S/4HANA uses what SAP calls new Asset Accounting. SAP’s migration guide for it covers customers who used classic Asset Accounting until now, and states the target as SAP S/4HANA on premise 1511 or higher. The guide says the system is based on new General Ledger Accounting, that new depreciation calculation from the Financials Extension (EA-FIN) is a mandatory prerequisite of the migration, and that asset values are written to the universal journal entry. It also states two consequences that explain why older descriptions of Asset Accounting read differently:

  • From the moment SAP S/4HANA is installed you can no longer post in Asset Accounting. Posting is possible again only after the migration has finished successfully, so the guide demands a complete period-end closing in Asset Accounting before installation.

  • The guide lists components that cannot be combined with new Asset Accounting, among them the Lease Accounting Engine and classic Real Estate Management (Flexible Real Estate Management is the alternative the guide names).

The periodic asset postings that classic Asset Accounting ran with program RAPERB2000 are not needed after the migration: the guide says the program is no longer available. So a description of classic Asset Accounting that centers on a periodic posting of asset values describes a process that no longer exists in this form. In S/4HANA the values of an asset are posted in real time (see the posting options below).

Source: Migrating from Classic to New Asset Accounting (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Chart of depreciation, depreciation areas and depreciation keys

Chart of depreciation

A chart of depreciation manages the legal requirements for depreciating and valuing assets. It is usually country-specific and defined independently of the other organizational units. SAP delivers sample charts for each country, and you cannot use these directly: you copy a reference chart to create your own and delete the depreciation areas you do not need.

A chart of depreciation is a directory of depreciation areas organized by business requirements, plus the depreciation keys for automatic depreciation. Each company code defined in Asset Accounting is assigned to exactly one chart of depreciation. SAP recommends keeping the number of charts in use as small as possible, so that companies with the same valuation rules share one. The assignment of a company code to a chart of accounts is independent of its assignment to a chart of depreciation. This is the same idea as the chart of accounts of the Charts of Accounts and G/L Accounts page, one level further down.

One detail of copying: when you copy a chart of depreciation, the system also copies depreciation-area-specific restrictions on transaction types from classic Asset Accounting (table TABWA). SAP says you no longer need to post with restricted transaction types in Asset Accounting, with the exception of revaluations and investment support, and tells you to check and delete these restrictions in a new chart.

Sources: Chart of Depreciation (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Depreciation areas

Depreciation areas let the system calculate different values in parallel for each asset, for different purposes: the balance sheet, cost accounting or tax. A chart of depreciation can hold up to 99 depreciation areas, each identified by a two-digit numeric key, and each area belongs to one chart. You enter the asset-specific depreciation terms for each area either in the asset class or directly in the asset master record. This makes it possible, as SAP’s example says, to use straight-line depreciation for internal accounting and declining-balance depreciation for the balance sheet.

Each area can post its values to its own balance sheet and income statement accounts. You set the accounts per depreciation area through the account determination key defined in Customizing and entered in the asset class. This is the same account determination idea that the Account Determination and Automatic Postings page explains for goods movements and billing. You should decide which types of valuation need their own areas before you implement Asset Accounting. New depreciation areas can still be opened while the system is live.

Sources: Depreciation Areas (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Depreciation areas, accounting principles and ledgers

In S/4HANA the link between depreciation areas and parallel accounting is explicit. SAP’s help says Asset Accounting handles parallel accounting with depreciation areas: you define the areas needed for each accounting principle and mark an area for a principle by assigning the accounting principle to it. There are two scenarios, the ledger approach and the accounts approach. In the ledger approach, as in General Ledger Accounting, each principle or valuation is a separate ledger, the same accounts are generally used, the areas have equal status and separate documents are posted for each principle with full values, not delta values. In the accounts approach the valuations sit on different accounts of the same ledger.

For each valuation there is exactly one area that posts to the general ledger in real time and manages the APC. That area has the posting option Area Posts in Realtime in the ledger approach. The other areas of the valuation can be statistical or adopt their values from it. SAP’s example chart (ledger approach) lists the areas below. The numbers and the principle names (IFRS and HGB, the German commercial code) are SAP’s own example values, not those of EX01.

Depreciation keys

The depreciation key holds the value settings the system needs to determine depreciation amounts. It combines the calculation rules for the automatically calculated depreciation types: ordinary depreciation, special depreciation and the calculation of interest. You assign calculation methods to each key, and you define keys and methods in Customizing for Asset Accounting under Depreciation, Valuation Methods, Depreciation Methods. Keys are defined at the level of the chart of depreciation, so they are available in every company code that uses the chart, and the standard charts contain keys predefined for country-specific needs.

A key can divide the depreciation period into phases. When you enter a changeover method for a phase, the system moves to the next phase as soon as the event named by the changeover method has occurred and then uses the calculation of that phase.

To see all influencing factors of a key (calculation methods, phases and so on), SAP provides the Depreciation Key: Display Details app. You open it from the SAP Fiori launchpad or from the smart link of the Depreciation Key field in Manage Fixed Assets. SAP’s page also describes an AI-generated explanation of a depreciation key in natural language, reachable from the same smart link through Generate Explanation. Like any AI-generated text, check it against the key’s real settings.

Sources: Depreciation Key (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

The asset master record and the app that replaced AS01

An asset needs a master record before it can be bought. SAP’s procedure for creating assets has these steps: determine the asset class (or add a subnumber to an existing main asset number), enter the company code, fill the tabs (general data such as description and capitalization date, time-dependent assignments such as cost center, allocations, origin, and more), and then enter the valuation information for each depreciation area: the depreciation key, the useful life in years or periods and the start date of depreciation calculation. Areas that the asset does not need are deactivated, and the asset is saved. You can create several assets of the same kind at once if the configurable entry screen is activated in Customizing.

The tools differ between the two editions.

Edition Tool for creating, changing and displaying assets Notes

On premise

The classic transactions AS01 (create), AS02 (change) and AS03 (display), and the Manage Fixed Assets app

SAP’s help names AS01, AS02 and AS03 as the Create, Change and Display Asset transactions. Its page for Manage Fixed Assets says the app cannot be used while the business function Universal Parallel Accounting (FINS_PARALLEL_ACCOUNTING_BF) is active, and names the app Manage Fixed Assets (F3425) for that case.

Public Edition

F3425A is the successor of the older app F3425, which SAP’s What’s New states has been available since 2602 and is deprecated as of 2608, to be deleted in a future release. In Manage Fixed Assets you see all data of an asset in one place, with a life cycle chart, key figures such as APC, accumulated depreciation and net book value, all depreciation areas, related journal entries, notes and attachments. SAP says fixed assets are usually created and capitalized automatically in the integrated procurement and invoicing processes if the solution is configured for it.

Acquisition, retirement and transfer

Acquisition: the integrated posting

SAP’s help describes an external acquisition posted with the supplier invoice in one transaction, called an integrated asset acquisition. It saves data entry, and the postings reach the supplier and asset accounts together. On premise the path is Posting, Acquisition, External Acquisition, With Vendor, and the example in the help enters the document in transaction F-90. Other routes into an asset exist (the table of contents of SAP’s help lists acquisition through purchasing, from internal activity and from extraordinary revenue); this page follows the manual route because its accounting is the easiest to see.

A step beginners miss: the system splits the business transaction into an operational part and a valuating part.

  • The operational part is the supplier invoice. The system posts it for all accounting principles against the technical clearing account for integrated asset acquisition.

  • The valuating part is the asset posting that capitalizes the asset. For each accounting principle assigned in the chart of depreciation, the system generates an accounting-principle-specific document, also posted against the technical clearing account.

As a result the technical clearing account has a zero balance for every ledger and account assignment object. Because the system must guarantee that, manual postings to it are not possible, and it does not appear in the balance sheet.

flowchart TD A["Enter one document:

Setup for it (requires an SAP system; not run here): a transaction type for asset acquisitions, and a technical clearing account, created as an asset reconciliation account in the chart of accounts and in the relevant company codes and entered in the Customizing activity Specify Technical Clearing Account for Integrated Asset Acquisition. In the SAP document for gross posting, document type AA posts without deducting cash discount and AN posts net. You can simulate the posting beforehand in the entry view, the general ledger view or the asset view, which also lets you switch the accounting principle and the display currency.

Sources: Integrated Asset Acquisition Posting (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Example 1: Acquisition, Integrated Posting (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Worked example: an acquisition with a different value in each ledger

Retirement

Asset retirement removes an asset or part of one from the asset portfolio. SAP’s help distinguishes: a sale with revenue posted against a customer, a sale with revenue posted against a clearing account, scrapping without revenue, and a sale to an affiliated company. In a sale with a customer the system posts the receivable, the revenue and the retirement in one step: you enter the revenue posting first and then the retirement. The revenue account needs a field status variant in which the Asset Retirement field is optional or required. With the standard transaction types the system posts the gain or loss and the revenue clearing automatically when the transaction type has the gain-and-loss indicator set. A retirement without revenue creates a loss posting for the net book value retired.

A retirement can be complete or partial. For a partial retirement you enter the APC retired, a retirement percentage or a quantity. SAP adds that for a complete retirement of an asset acquired in earlier years you must select a transaction type meant for prior-year acquisitions, and that you cannot post transactions with a value date before the value date of the last retirement without first reversing the later retirements.

The system also retires the proportional depreciation. Here is SAP’s idea with EX01 numbers. The van of the example above is depreciated straight-line from an APC of 15,000.00 over five years, 3,000.00 a year, and was acquired on 1 January of the year before last. On 1 July of the current year EX01 retires 50 percent of the APC.

The depreciation retired is 3,000 + 750 = 3,750. The APC retired is 7,500, so the net book value retired is 7,500 - 3,750 = 3,750. If EX01 sells that part for EUR 5,000.00 (a made-up price), the gain is 5,000 - 3,750 = 1,250. SAP’s help gives the proportional values the transaction type 290 in the standard transaction types. Whether the system calculates the current-year half exactly like this depends on the period control of the asset, so read the example as arithmetic of the principle, not as system output. Posting a retirement requires an SAP system; not run here.

Source: Retirement (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Transfer

An asset cannot change company code by editing its master record. SAP’s help for intercompany transfers (between company codes) says that for each asset you either create a new master record in the target company code or use an existing one, and that the identity of the asset is kept through the inventory number in the master record. For the two companies an intercompany transfer is a retirement and an acquisition. For the group it typically balances to zero in the group asset history sheet. A client-internal transfer can be posted in one step (automatic intercompany transfer). A cross-client transfer uses the manual transfer, where the retirement transfer and the acquisition transfer are posted in two separate steps and can be integrated with Accounts Payable and Accounts Receivable. A transfer within one company code, an intracompany transfer, has its own function in the help. The standard transaction types for transfers are 390 and 395 (transfer retirement and transfer acquisition).

Sources: Asset Transfer (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Retirement (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

How depreciation is calculated

The next four formulas are the calculation rules that SAP documents in its help for the standard depreciation methods. In a depreciation key each one is combined with a base value, a changeover rule and period control. These figures are annual amounts for full years. Real systems also calculate pro rata for the period of the first and last year, a detail that depends on the key and is not shown here.

Straight-line over the total useful life

The asset is depreciated by the same amount each year over its useful life, where is the acquisition and production cost and the expected useful life in years:

SAP’s example: APC 1,000 and a useful life of 10 gives 1,000 / 10 = 100 a year. A post-capitalization or subsequent acquisition raises the depreciation and lengthens the time until the book value reaches zero. After the planned life, some depreciation keys allow depreciation below book value zero: SAP’s example is that in the 11th year of use the system would use 1/11 = 9.0909 percent instead of the 10 percent of the first ten years.

Source: Straight-Line Depreciation over Total Useful Life (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Straight-line from the book value over the remaining life

The net book value is spread evenly over the remaining life. Unlike depreciation over the total life, a post-capitalization or subsequent acquisition does not extend the expected useful life. With the net book value and the remaining useful life in years:

SAP’s example: APC 1,000, useful life 10, net book value 500, remaining life 5. Then the depreciation is 500 / 5 = 100. SAP warns that a post-capitalization after the expected useful life has expired causes problems here, so the changeover key must provide another method after the end of life. In the depreciation key the method is represented by a percentage rate computed from the remaining life with net book value as the base value.

Source: Straight-Line from the Book Value over Remaining Useful Life (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

Declining balance, with minimum and maximum rate and changeover

The declining-balance method depreciates by a falling amount: a constant percentage rate, found from the useful life and a multiplication factor, is applied to the falling net book value:

where is the factor. SAP’s example: APC 1,000, useful life 10, net book value 700, factor 3, so 700 times (100 percent / 10 times 3) = 210. A short useful life can give a very large rate, so you can set a maximum rate as a ceiling, and a minimum rate keeps the rate from sinking below a level when the life is very long.

For mathematical reasons the net book value never reaches zero with this method, so the system changes over to straight-line or complete depreciation. SAP lists these conditions: the declining-balance amount falls below the straight-line amount, the net book value falls below a percentage of the acquisition value, below a fixed amount, or below the straight-line depreciation. The changeover method is a setting in the depreciation key. SAP’s help lists the changeover methods, including changeover when the net book value falls below a given percentage of the acquisition value, below a changeover amount set for the depreciation area, or below the straight-line rate, and changeover after the end of the planned useful life. Customer-defined changeover is possible through BAdI methods of the FAA_DC_CUSTOMER enhancement, which SAP describes in transaction SE18. Normally the system checks the changeover for whole fiscal years.

Sources: Declining-Balance Method of Depreciation (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Declining-Balance Method (SAP Help Portal, on premise), Changeover Method (SAP Help Portal, on premise)

There is no remaining life after the planned end of life, so the method cannot be used then, and you can change over to another method. SAP does not allow acquisitions after the depreciation start year, or post-capitalization, with this method: such acquisitions are created as subnumbers of the asset, and the acquisition year must equal the depreciation start year.

Common errors and pitfalls

  • Expecting depreciation to appear in the general ledger straight after a transaction. Forecast depreciation changes at once, but the accounts are updated only by the periodic posting run.

  • Posting by hand to the technical clearing account. The system must keep it at zero for each principle, so manual postings are not possible.

  • Using a copied sample chart of depreciation unchanged. SAP says the delivered charts cannot be used directly, and a copy can carry transaction type restrictions that you no longer need.

  • Using sum-of-the-years'-digits depreciation and then adding a post-capitalization. The method does not allow it. Create the subsequent acquisition as a subnumber.

  • Expecting declining-balance depreciation to reach zero. It cannot, which is why the depreciation key needs a changeover.

  • Retiring an asset acquired in earlier years with a current-year transaction type. Choose the prior-year transaction type for a complete retirement.

  • Posting an earlier value date after a retirement. Reverse the later retirements first.

  • Opening F3425 in a Public Edition tenant on a new project. SAP deprecated it as of 2608 in favor of F3425A.

Summary

  • Asset Accounting is the subsidiary ledger of fixed assets. Each asset has a master record, its transactions post to the general ledger, and its depreciation is calculated by the system and posted by a periodic run in collective documents.

  • A chart of depreciation, assigned to the company code, holds up to 99 depreciation areas and the depreciation keys. Each area keeps one set of values for one purpose, and in S/4HANA each accounting principle is represented by its own areas and its own ledger.

  • An integrated acquisition posts the supplier invoice and the capitalization together through a technical clearing account that stays at zero, which is how one asset can have a different APC in each ledger.

  • Retirement and transfer are transactions on the asset: a retirement also takes out the proportional depreciation, and an intercompany transfer is a retirement for one company code and an acquisition for the other.

  • Straight-line is APC / n, remaining life is NBV / remaining years, declining balance is NBV times the rate from useful life and factor with a changeover, and sum-of-the-years'-digits uses the remaining life divided by the sum of the remaining lives.

  • On premise the asset master data uses AS01, AS02 and AS03 and Manage Fixed Assets. In the Public Edition the app is Manage Fixed Assets F3425A, the successor of F3425.
