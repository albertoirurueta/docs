Finance Implementation, Migration and Testing

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Every finance consultant eventually works on a project, and the finance part of a project has its own questions. Which of the company’s legal entities and currencies must the new system handle? Which of the standard processes fit, and where does the company need something different? How do the balances, the unpaid invoices and the fixed assets of the old system get into the new one so that the books still add up? How do we prove that the new system calculates and posts correctly, and what do we check on the last weekend before the business starts to use it? This page answers those questions for finance. It is the finance companion of the general page Implementation Projects and Go-Live, which explains the method (SAP Activate, the three ways to move to SAP S/4HANA, cutover and hypercare) and which this page does not repeat. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02, which covers the on-premise edition and SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition, and against SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. Where the two differ, the text says so. All numbers belong to the fictitious Example Trading Co., company code EX01.

Before you read

  • Implementation Projects and Go-Live: the project phases, the migration paths and the test stages this page applies to finance.

  • Accounting Basics: debit, credit and why every posting balances.

  • Finance Overview: what the finance components are and how they relate.

  • Open Item Management and Clearing: what an open item is, because the migration loads open items, not documents.

  • Universal Journal: where every loaded balance ends up.

Key terms

  • Fit-to-standard: a workshop approach in which the business sees the standard process and states only what does not fit.

  • Migration cockpit: the SAP Fiori app Migrate Your Data - Migration Cockpit that loads business data into SAP S/4HANA.

  • Migration object: the description of how the data of one business object, such as G/L account balances, is moved.

  • Staging table: a table the cockpit creates for each migration object, which you fill from a template file before the data is loaded.

  • Migration key date: the date, set per company code, up to which the loaded balances and open items are valid.

  • Reconciliation account: the general ledger account that sums up a customer or supplier subledger.

  • Open item: a posting on an account that has not been cleared yet.

  • Reconciliation: comparing two sources of the same figure, here the old and the new system, and explaining every difference.

What is specific to a finance project

A finance project differs from other parts of an SAP project in three ways. First, finance is where everything ends up. A mistake in the chart of accounts, the ledger setup or the fiscal year variant is visible in every module and, because the books are legally audited, it is expensive to fix after postings exist. Second, finance data is not just master data. Balances and open items are numbers that must equal the old books to the cent on a given date. Third, finance has fixed calendar constraints: a fiscal year end, a tax filing date, an auditor. The general page already uses the book Configuring SAP S/4HANA Finance (3rd edition, 2025) for practical advice on team structure and go-live dates, and that advice applies here too.

Fit/gap in finance

The general page describes fit-to-standard: the business watches the standard process and names only the gaps. For finance the gaps usually fall into three groups. A configuration gap is solved by settings, for example a second ledger. An extension gap needs a custom field, a custom report or a validation rule, and the page Key User Extensibility describes the tools. A localization gap comes from a country law, such as a statutory report or a tax format, and is solved by the country version that SAP delivers or by a partner. The book names the same split: it first analyzes the requirements of a global template and then the localization fit/gap per country. Record every gap with its group and an owner, because a finance project that collects its gaps in one list can decide early which ones are worth a customization.

Source: SAP Learning: Analyzing each phase of SAP Activate

Data migration in finance

The migration cockpit and its two approaches (staging tables and direct transfer) are explained on the general page and on the ABAP page Batch Input and Data Migration. This section covers what is special for finance data. In the Public Edition, SAP documents the app Migrate Your Data - Migration Cockpit with the app ID F3473, and the on-premise page for the same app shows the same ID.

What finance does and does not migrate

SAP states the principle plainly in the documentation of the finance objects: no documents are migrated, only balances and open items are taken over. The old accounting documents stay in the old system (or in an archive), and the new system starts with the balances that result from them. This has a practical consequence for the project: the old system must stay readable for audits and queries, for as long as the company needs to answer questions about old documents. A second option exists for reporting. The object FI - Historical balance loads historical G/L account balances into the universal journal for comparison reports, and the migrated balances are reversed on the migration key date so that they have no effect on the current balances. SAP says it supports up to 33 fiscal years in the past and that the migrated documents themselves cannot be displayed.

SAP’s recommended approach follows from that principle: clear as many open items on payables, receivables and outgoing checks as possible in the old system before the migration. Fewer open items mean fewer numbers to load and fewer to reconcile. From the bank accounts only the balance needs to be loaded. From the tax accounts nothing needs to be loaded, because a normal VAT account is at zero when the migration takes place, with an exception for deferred tax. The cash account is special: its balance is migrated as part of the balance of a bank account and is posted later in the cash journal as the opening balance, because a posting to the cash account through the migration object does not update the cash journal.

The migration key date and the legacy data transfer status

Before any finance object can be loaded, you set a migration key date for each company code. SAP says this one date is used by every migration object that posts into financial accounting, with the exception of the fixed asset objects, which have their own transfer date in Asset Accounting. You maintain it in the transaction FINS_MIG_CTRL_1 (Define Settings for Legacy Data Transfer), which the object documentation also offers as an app of the same name. The same place holds the legacy data transfer status, which has three values:

  • In Preparation: the migration key date is still unclear, and transactional data cannot be migrated.

  • Ongoing: required for the initial data transfer, and transactional data can be migrated.

  • Completed: set after the initial data transfer has finished, and transactional data can no longer be migrated.

The status is a safety switch. Setting it to Completed after the final load closes the door to another load into the books by accident. Two rules follow. The migration key date is the date the loaded balances are valid for, so it is normally the last day of a posting period, and the old system’s balances must be taken at the same date. And a test migration in the quality system must use a status and a key date of its own, because a status in the productive system that is still Ongoing after go-live is a finding for the auditors.

The setting is configuration, so it requires an SAP system; not run here.

Source: FI - G/L account balance and open/line item (on premise) and FI - Historical balance (on premise)

The load order

The objects depend on each other. SAP lists "predecessor objects" for each: the G/L account object before any balance, the customer and supplier objects before the open items, and the cost center and profit center objects where an account assignment is needed. The exchange rates are a predecessor as well. A sensible order for finance therefore looks like this.

flowchart TD A["Set migration key date and status Ongoing"] --> B["Master data: G/L accounts, cost centers, profit centers, exchange rates, banks"] B --> C["Business partners: customers and suppliers"] C --> D["Open items: accounts payable and accounts receivable"] B --> E["G/L balances and open items on G/L accounts"] B --> F["Fixed assets with balances"] D --> G["Reconcile every subledger to its reconciliation account"] E --> G F --> H["Reconcile assets to the asset balance sheet accounts"] G --> I["Check that the transfer offset accounts net to zero"] H --> I I --> J["Set status Completed"]

G/L balances and open items

The object FI - G/L account balance and open/line item is restricted to four currency fields: the document (transaction) currency, the company code currency, the group currency and one freely defined currency. SAP’s rules for its template, which you should know before you build a mapping, are these:

  • One line per document in the file. The offsetting entry is generated automatically on the offsetting G/L account.

  • The posting key is controlled by the sign of the amount. A positive amount debits the account (posting key 40), and a negative amount credits it (posting key 50), with the opposite posting on the offsetting account.

  • The key fields company code, ledger group, reference document number, line item number and G/L account are used for a duplicate check. The line item number is only an additional key and is not migrated.

  • Postings to an account with open item management are possible only with an empty ledger group, so different balances for different ledgers need an account without open item management. Otherwise the system reports a message from the posting service.

  • Out of scope: several line items in one document, an external document number, open tax items (open tax must be settled before the migration), automatic tax calculation and posting to an extension ledger.

  • Fixed asset balances must not be loaded with this object. The file must contain no records for asset reconciliation accounts, because the asset objects load them.

  • Currency: either provide all currencies and amounts in the template, in which case they are taken as they are, whatever exchange rates exist, or leave the second local and the group currency empty and let the system translate with the valid exchange rate. SAP recommends providing all of them so that the amounts match their currencies.

The object documentation names initial offsetting accounts, which SAP’s test script Data Migration to SAP S/4HANA from Staging (2Q2) describes: separate offset accounts for initial FI-AR, FI-AP, other G/L and other open item G/L postings. These are account numbers of SAP’s delivered content. On premise, you create your own offset accounts in your chart of accounts. The point of the design is that the balance of all these offset accounts must be zero once everything is loaded, which is the check at the end of this section.

Open items on accounts payable and receivable

The open-item objects load the unpaid supplier and customer items. A few rules from the documentation matter for planning. The posting key is derived from the account type and from the sign: for suppliers, a negative amount is an invoice (posting key 31) and a positive amount a credit memo (posting key 21). One line per document is entered and the offsetting entry is generated. The object does not take over down payments and down payment requests, items with a special G/L indicator that leads to a noted item, bills of exchange, or open items whose tax attributes belong to a country other than the company code’s country. Items of that kind are posted by hand after the migration. The page Special G/L Transactions explains what they are, which is a good reason to find out early how many exist in the old system.

Fixed assets

The book calls fixed assets one of the more complex migration objects, and the SAP documentation contains requirements that cannot be met on the weekend itself. You need:

  • The previous fiscal year closed in Asset Accounting.

  • A legacy data transfer segment with the transfer date, the document type and the legacy data transfer status, defined in the Customizing of Asset Accounting under Asset Data Transfer, Parameters for Data Transfer, Define Transfer Date and Additional Parameters.

  • An offsetting account for the legacy data transfer, defined in the Customizing of Asset Accounting under Define Offsetting Account for Legacy Data Transfer.

  • The asset classes assigned to the right number range intervals before the migration starts.

  • Customizing for the depreciation areas, because the settings decide which values are accepted. By default, acquisition values must be positive and depreciation values negative, and the acquisition value must exceed the depreciation (3,000 of acquisition cost with -10,000 of depreciation is rejected).

  • For a transfer during the year, the last posted depreciation period for each asset, so that the system knows from which period to post depreciation.

How the migration treats the values depends on when you transfer. At a fiscal year end, the accumulated acquisition cost and accumulated depreciation are transferred. During the year, the transactions from the start of the year up to the transfer date are transferred as well, and by default also the depreciation already posted. When the new system posts the asset values, the asset balances in the general ledger are updated automatically, so no separate load of asset reconciliation accounts is needed. This is a real change from older systems, where asset values and G/L balances were loaded separately and reconciled by hand. Assets under construction and deactivated assets are not supported by this object. SAP’s workaround for a deactivated asset is to transfer it as active and then post a complete retirement.

SAP recommends a pretest with a small sample (for example one asset per asset class) using the manual transactions AS91 (create master data for a legacy asset), ABLDT (post transfer values) and AB01 (transfer during the year). Wrong transfer values cannot be edited: reverse the transfer document and post it again.

All of this configuration and these transactions require an SAP system; not run here. SAP names the app Make Company Code Settings for the option to leave out the posted depreciation of the transfer year.

Source: Fixed asset (incl. balances and transactions) (on premise), FI - Accounts payable open item (on premise) and FI - Accounts receivable open item (on premise)

Reconciliation

Reconciliation is the proof that the new books equal the old ones. It has two parts, and a finance project should treat both as deliverables with a named owner and a signature.

The first part compares the new system with the old one, account by account, as of the migration key date. The second part checks the internal consistency of the new system: every subledger must equal its reconciliation account, and the transfer offset accounts must net to zero. Because the offsetting entries are generated by the migration, a clean set of loads leaves the offset accounts with debits and credits that cancel. In words: all loaded balances together must be balanced on the offset accounts. If they are not, at least one object was loaded with a missing or wrong line, and the offset account that is out of balance tells you which kind of object to look at.

Go-live checks for finance

The general page describes the cutover plan, the dress rehearsal and hypercare. These are the checks that belong to finance on the go-live weekend, collected from the object documentation and from the book.

Check What to verify

Migration status

The legacy data transfer status is Completed for every company code that was loaded, and the migration key date is the agreed one. No further load is possible by mistake.

Offset accounts

The offset accounts of the migration net to zero, as in the worked example.

Reconciliation

Every subledger equals its reconciliation account, the G/L balances equal the signed-off legacy trial balance, and the asset reconciliation is documented and signed.

Posting periods and calendar

The periods that users need are open, and the periods that must stay closed are closed. See the page Fiscal Year and Posting Periods.

Number ranges

Number ranges for documents and assets are set for the new fiscal year. The general page notes the book’s reminder that number ranges are not transported.

Tax codes and account determination

Tax codes work in a test posting, and a purchase and a sale find their accounts, as described in Account Determination and Automatic Postings.

Bank and payments

The bank accounts are connected, and a payment run in test mode proposes the open items that are due. See Automatic Payment Program.

Cash

The cash account balance was loaded as part of the bank balance and the opening balance in the cash journal is posted. See Cash Journal.

Background jobs and interfaces

The periodic jobs, such as the depreciation run, are scheduled, and the interfaces to banks, tax authorities and other systems are tested.

Sign-off

Each functional lead and each business expert signs the validation in writing, and project management has a plan for the case that a critical issue appears.

What older books show, and what has changed

The book Configuring SAP S/4HANA Finance, 3rd edition (2025), describes SAP S/4HANA 2023. For the project chapters its picture still holds: a global template followed by localization fit/gap, a migration that loads balances and open items rather than documents, unit, integration and acceptance testing, and validation and hypercare after go-live. The book itself says that the migration cockpit replaced the load tools of older releases, and that fixed assets no longer need a separate load of the general ledger, which matches the official documentation. Details that change from release to release are the list of migration objects, their names and approaches (the page Available Migration Objects is the current list), and the apps that SAP names for validation. The book SAP S/4HANA Financial Accounting Configuration, 2nd edition (2023), by Okungbowa, concentrates on configuration. Books written for SAP ERP describe the load of finance data with legacy tools such as batch input and LSMW, and with separate loads for assets and the general ledger. Treat these as history. For a current project use the migration cockpit and read the object documentation of your release.

Summary

  • A finance project starts with decisions on legal entities, chart of accounts, ledgers, currencies, fiscal calendar, subledgers, assets, controlling and tax. Collect the gaps against the standard in groups: configuration, extension and localization.

  • The migration cockpit loads balances and open items, not documents. The finance objects are G/L accounts, G/L balances and open items, accounts payable and receivable open items, fixed assets and historical balances.

  • Set the migration key date and the legacy data transfer status first. Load master data before balances and balances before reconciliation, and set the status to Completed after the final load.

  • Reconciliation has two parts: new system against old books, and the new system against itself (subledgers against reconciliation accounts, offset accounts netting to zero).

  • Write finance test cases with expected accounts and amounts, and finish with the go-live checks for the status, the offset accounts, the periods, the number ranges, tax and signed validations.
