Master Data and the Business Partner

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Every business keeps a few lists that change slowly and that everything else refers to: who its customers and suppliers are, which products it buys and sells, and what each product costs for each customer. In SAP these lists are called master data. A newcomer meets master data in the first hour, because nothing can be ordered, delivered or invoiced until the customer, the product and the price exist as records. This page explains what master data is and how it differs from transactional and configuration data, how SAP S/4HANA stores every customer, supplier and contact once as a business partner with one or more roles, what the product master and the pricing condition records hold, and how a program can read a business partner through a documented web service. It is written for SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (October 2026) in the on premise and Private Edition forms, and for SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608. The business partner concept, the product master and the condition technique apply to both. The SAP GUI transactions named below belong to the on premise and Private Edition forms, and each fact says which form it was read in.

Before you read

  • Organizational structures, the page Organizational Structures on client, company code, plant and sales area. Master data is stored per level of that structure, so it helps to know the levels first.

  • Getting Started: what an SAP system is, if the words "client" and "system" are new.

Key terms

  • Master data: records that describe the lasting things a business deals with, such as customers, suppliers and products, and that are reused by many transactions.

  • Transactional data: records of one business event, such as a sales order or an invoice, that refer to master data.

  • Configuration data: the settings, also called Customizing, that decide how the standard software behaves in one company.

  • Business partner: the single central record for a person, an organization or a group that the company has a business interest in.

  • Business partner role: the part a business partner plays, such as customer or supplier, which decides which extra data is kept for it.

  • Business partner category: the type of a business partner, which is person, organization or group, and which cannot be changed later.

  • Customer/Supplier Integration (CVI): the link that keeps the old customer and supplier master records and the business partner in step.

  • Product master: the master record of a material, which holds the data that purchasing, inventory, sales and production need about it.

  • Condition record: a stored price, discount or surcharge that is valid for a given customer, product and period.

  • OData: a web protocol, used by SAP APIs, that addresses data as collections and entities in a URL.

Three kinds of data

A running SAP system holds three kinds of data, and a newcomer who keeps them apart understands most of what the screens ask for. SAP’s learning material says that data is commonly categorized as configuration, master and transactional, and the plain-words versions below follow that split.

Kind What it describes Example in the expense claim story How often it changes

Configuration data

How the software behaves in this company: which organizational units exist, which document types are allowed, how a number range is split.

The rule that an expense claim needs approval above a limit.

Rarely, through a controlled change that is transported from the development system to the test and production systems.

Master data

The lasting things the business deals with: a customer, a supplier, a product, a price.

The employee who files the claim and the supplier who is reimbursed, each stored once.

Occasionally, by a person with the right role, such as a master data specialist.

Transactional data

One business event and its documents.

A single claim with its amount, its status and its posting.

Constantly, by the processes themselves.

The difference matters in practice. Configuration data travels in Customizing requests, as described in Packages, Transports, gCTS and abapGit. Master data is created in the production system by the people who run the business, so it does not travel in a transport and has to be loaded by a migration or an interface when a new system goes live. Transactional data refers to master data: a sales order does not contain the customer’s address, it points to the customer. That is why a wrong master record hurts so widely, and why SAP talks about master data quality as part of keeping the system clean.

The running example of the ABAP pages shows the same split. The table ZEXP_CLAIM of DDIC Tables and Structures is transactional: one row per claim. It carries an employee_id, a currency code and an amount, and each of those is a pointer to something that lives in master data or configuration. Where an employee is kept as master data is a question for the HR pages of this section, and the currency belongs to the configuration side.

Source: SAP Learning: Introducing the Clean Core Approach

The business partner: one record, many roles

Before SAP S/4HANA, a customer and a supplier were two separate kinds of record, and a company that sold to and bought from the same firm kept it twice. The business partner replaces that. SAP’s definition is that a business partner is an organization (a company or subsidiary), a person, or a group of people or organizations in which your company has a business interest. You enter and manage it centrally, which pays off most when one partner has several roles, such as sold-to party and ship-to party. A partner can be created in one role and take on further roles over the course of the business relationship. The general data, which does not depend on the function, is entered once, so redundant entry and storage is avoided.

The Public Edition documentation states the same idea in terms of a leading object. A business partner record exists only once, regardless of the business processes it is involved in. When a company is both a customer and a supplier, one partner record is the single point of entry for both roles, and the specific data is created by assigning the customer and the supplier role. That page lists customer, supplier and employee as examples of roles.

An invented example makes this concrete. A haulage firm called Nordwind Logistics buys fuel cards from your company and also sells you freight services. In the old model that is one customer record and one supplier record with the address typed twice. In the business partner model it is one business partner, with one name and one address, which has a customer role and a supplier role. If the firm moves, the address is changed once.

flowchart TB BP["Business partner: one record with name, address and number"] CAT["Category: person, organization or group"] FLCU00["Role FLCU00: customer, financial accounting data"] FLCU01["Role FLCU01: customer, sales data"] FLVN00["Role FLVN00: supplier, financial accounting data"] FLVN01["Role FLVN01: supplier, purchasing data"] CP["Role: contact person"] BP --- CAT BP --> FLCU00 BP --> FLCU01 BP --> FLVN00 BP --> FLVN01 BP --> CP

The role names in the diagram come from the on premise help page for the Manage Business Partner Master Data app, which names FLCU00 as the FI (financial accounting) customer, FLCU01 as the sales customer, FLVN00 as the FI supplier and FLVN01 as the purchasing supplier, next to the contact person. A partner can hold several of these at once, and the help page lists the cases the app allows: for the role categories customer and supplier you can delete a role from a partner only if it is not the only role of that category. Each role opens the extra data for one business function. From a partner that has the customer role, the app navigates to the customer app for sales data, and from one with the supplier role to the purchasing data.

Source: SAP Help: SAP Business Partner (on premise 2025 FPS02)

Source: SAP Help: Business Partner (Public Edition 2608)

Category, roles and relationships

Three ideas structure the business partner data, and the on premise help page names each of them.

  • The business partner category says whether the partner is a natural person (a private individual), an organization (a company, a department, a club or an association) or a group (a married couple, for instance). The category decides which name fields exist: an organization has a name, a legal form and an industry, a person has first and last name and gender, and a group has two names. SAP states that no other categories can be created and that the category cannot be changed afterwards, so choosing it is the one decision at creation that cannot be undone.

  • The business partner role is the view of the partner for one business function. A role brings its own data and hides the rest.

  • A business partner relationship links two business partners, and each link has a relationship category. A contact person at a supplier is the everyday case: the person is a partner of category person, the company is a partner of category organization, and a relationship connects them.

Source: SAP Help: Business Partner Category

The business partner number and the grouping

Every business partner gets a number. When it is created, it is assigned to a grouping, a classification that decides which number range the number comes from, and SAP states that the assignment cannot be changed afterwards. The grouping, the number ranges and the field status rules that decide which fields are mandatory per role are configuration data, so they are set in Customizing and differ from one company to the next. A newcomer should treat the grouping as a decision that the implementation team has already taken and read it as a property of each partner.

Source: SAP Help: BusinessPartner service node (APIs for Business Partner)

Customer/Supplier Integration

Old customer and supplier records did not disappear when SAP S/4HANA arrived, because much older code and many interfaces still use them. The Customer/Supplier Integration, written CVI and called Customer/Vendor Integration in older material, keeps both in step with the business partner. The on premise help page makes three points. The recognized term is CVI. Only customers with the integration in place can move to SAP S/4HANA, and for a conversion all customers and suppliers must be converted to business partners, including the ones already in use and the contacts that relate to them. And the business partner is the leading object when customer or supplier master data is created: you can maintain customer and supplier master records from the business partner maintenance, as well as maintain data from the customer and supplier processing into the business partner. The page points to an SAP Note for the conversion activities. That note needs a login and was not opened for this page, so the conversion steps are left to the implementation and migration page of this section.

Source: SAP Help: Business Partner Approach (Customer/Supplier Integration)

Working with business partners on the screen

Two tools create and change business partners in the on premise and Private Edition forms.

The classic one is the SAP GUI transaction BP, which the help page for the United States localization calls Maintain Business Partner. It opens a search, for example by supplier number, a list of the hits, and a detail view with tabs such as Address and Identification. A field named Create in BP role lets the user pick the role for a new partner, for example FLVN00, which that page names Supplier (Fin.Accounting). Choosing Person on the start screen creates a partner of the category person. A role’s data, such as the company code data of a supplier, is reached from the More menu. The page uses a withholding tax setup as its example, and the steps above are only the generic part of it.

The other tool is the Fiori app Manage Business Partner Master Data. SAP’s page says you need the business role Master Data Specialist - Business Partner Specialist (technical name SAP_BR_BUPA_MASTER_SPECIALIST), and that the app can create, change, search, display and copy business partner master data. It saves changes as a draft, so a browser that closes in the middle of an entry does not lose the work, and the draft is deleted when the data is saved into the master tables. When you copy a partner, everything is copied except the business partner number, and the copy gets a new number. The app reads the CDS view I_BusinessPartner.

Source: SAP Help: Manage Business Partner Master Data

Source: SAP Help: Maintain Business Partners (United States localization)

Reading business partners through API_BUSINESS_PARTNER

Programs outside the SAP system, such as a web shop or a data warehouse, read and write business partners through a web service instead of the screen. SAP delivers the service API_BUSINESS_PARTNER as an OData API. The Public Edition page gives its technical name and says that it lets you create, display, update and delete data related to business partner, customer and supplier objects, that it supports deep entities for POST and batch processing, and that it is published on the SAP Business Accelerator Hub. The on premise page for the same service adds that integration is supported through OData, IDoc and SOAP interfaces, and that inbound and outbound interfaces exist. This page uses only the OData service.

OData addresses data by URL. The path ends in an entity collection such as A_BusinessPartner, and the standard options after a question mark narrow the answer. The OData version 2 URI conventions define $top as selecting only the first N items of the set, $select as returning a subset of the properties, and $expand as including related entries inline. The service documentation names these entities and navigation properties:

Name on the SAP help page Meaning

A_BusinessPartner

The entity with the basic data of a partner. Its properties include BusinessPartner (the number), BusinessPartnerCategory, BusinessPartnerFullName, BusinessPartnerGrouping, BusinessPartnerIsBlocked, Customer, Supplier and SearchTerm1.

to_Customer, to_Supplier

Navigation properties from A_BusinessPartner to the customer and supplier data, with a cardinality of zero or one each.

to_BusinessPartnerRole

Navigation to the roles of the partner, zero or more.

to_BusinessPartnerAddress, to_BusinessPartnerBank, to_BusinessPartnerContact, to_BusinessPartnerTax, to_BuPaIdentification, to_BuPaIndustry

Navigations to the addresses, bank details, contacts, tax numbers, identifications and industries of a partner, zero or more each.

The Customer property is read only and shows the customer’s key when the customer role has been added, and Supplier shows the supplier’s key when the supplier role exists. That is how an API client can tell from one read whether a partner already works as a customer, a supplier or both.

The help page lists these sample paths. The GET and POST paths use /sap/opu/odata/SAP/ and the $batch path on the Public Edition page uses /sap/opu/odata/sap/. The path of your own system is shown in its service catalog, so take the spelling from there.

Operation Sample path from the help page

GET one partner

/sap/opu/odata/SAP/API_BUSINESS_PARTNER/A_BusinessPartner(BusinessPartner='900023122')

POST a new partner

/sap/opu/odata/SAP/API_BUSINESS_PARTNER/A_BusinessPartner

PATCH, PUT, MERGE

/sap/opu/odata/SAP/API_BUSINESS_PARTNER/A_BusinessPartner(BusinessPartner='900023122')

DELETE a child entity

/sap/opu/odata/SAP/API_BUSINESS_PARTNER/A_BuPaIndustry(IndustrySector='022',IndustrySystemType='0002',BusinessPartner='15444')

A first read of the collection combines the documented collection path with the standard $top and $select options. The property names in $select are the ones listed in the table above.

GET /sap/opu/odata/SAP/API_BUSINESS_PARTNER/A_BusinessPartner?$top=5&$select=BusinessPartner,BusinessPartnerCategory,BusinessPartnerFullName,BusinessPartnerGrouping

This request requires an SAP system; not run here. The collection path is the one the POST row of the SAP table shows, and the three query options are those of the OData version 2 conventions. The help page does not show this exact request, and no response is shown here because none was obtained. What SAP documents is the shape of a successful answer: an entry for the partner with links named after the navigation properties above and the properties of the entity, for example the category, the grouping and the search terms. A failing request returns an error with a code and a message. The documented example of a wrong category has the message Business partner category does not exist; use 1, 2 or 3, which agrees with the three categories person, organization and group. The page does not say which number stands for which category.

To follow one partner into its roles, the related entities are reached with a navigation path or with $expand. SAP’s own read examples use the navigation form, for instance A_BusinessPartner followed by a key and then to_BuPaIndustry with the key of the industry entry.

GET /sap/opu/odata/SAP/API_BUSINESS_PARTNER/A_BusinessPartner?$top=5&$expand=to_BusinessPartnerRole

This request requires an SAP system; not run here. It uses the documented navigation property to_BusinessPartnerRole and the standard $expand option, and the name of the role property inside that entity is deliberately not given because the pages read for this guide do not list it. Setting up the user and the connection that may call the service is an administrator task that this page does not cover.

Released interfaces and clean core

The API is a released interface, which is the point of the clean core approach: an extension reads and writes business partners through this service and does not read the database tables underneath, so SAP can change the tables without breaking the extension. The SAP Learning course lists "using released APIs only" among its best practices for clean core extensibility, next to zero modifications and the use of key user extensibility. The ABAP side of the same idea is on Clean Core and Cloud Readiness. The Fiori app above, for its part, reads the CDS view I_BusinessPartner instead of the tables.

Source: SAP Learning: Introducing the Clean Core Approach

The product master

The product master holds the data of the things a company buys, makes, stores and sells. SAP’s definition is that it contains information on all the materials a company procures or produces, stores and sells, and that it is the central source for material-specific data, kept in individual material master records. The words product and material name the same object here, and the on premise pages use both. The point of one central record is that purchasing, inventory management, invoice verification, sales and distribution, and production planning all read the same record, so the data is not stored several times. The Customizing for the material master lets a company adjust the record to its needs.

A material master record is not filled all at once. The help page for creating a record says that you create it when no record exists for the material, and that you extend it when a record exists but the data for your department is missing, or exists for a different plant. The record therefore grows by organizational level: general data on the material itself, then data per plant, per storage location or per sales area, which is where the organizational structures of the previous page come in. Companies typically create the record centrally, where a central body enters a minimum of the data and the departments add theirs in change mode, or decentrally, where each department creates its own part.

The material type decides which kind of data a record can hold. SAP’s list of standard material types gives each an internal code. A short selection, all taken from that page, shows the principle:

Material type Code What the page says about the data in its records

Raw materials

ROH

Always procured externally and processed. They hold purchasing data but not sales data, since they cannot be sold.

Semifinished products

HALB

Can be procured externally and manufactured in-house, then processed further. They can hold purchasing and work scheduling data.

Finished products

FERT

Produced in-house. Because purchasing cannot order them, the record contains no purchasing data.

Trading goods

HAWA

Always procured externally and then sold. They can hold purchasing and sales data.

Services

DIEN

Performed internally or procured externally. They cannot be stored or transported.

In the on premise forms SAP names two ways to create a product: the Manage Product Master Data Fiori app and the classic SAP GUI app Create Material. Public Edition has the Manage Product Master Data app, Fiori ID F1602, which is used to search, display, edit, create and copy products and to mark a product for deletion. The role to assign is PRODMASTER_SPECIALIST. The Public Edition page says a product can have several plants, and that a deletion mark set at the client level marks the whole product, while one set at plant level also marks the corresponding storage location and valuation type. That nesting is the organizational structure at work.

Conditions: prices that are master data

A price list that applies to every customer is simple. Real prices depend on who buys, what, how much and when, and SAP stores them as master data too. SAP’s pricing documentation says that pricing means the calculation of prices for customers or suppliers and of costs for internal purposes. A condition is a set of circumstances that apply when a price is calculated. Its example is a particular customer ordering a certain quantity of a particular product on a certain day: the customer, the product, the quantity and the date are the variable factors that decide the final price, and the information about each factor can be stored as master data in the form of condition records.

Three words keep the picture straight, and the page defines each:

  • The condition type is the kind of price element, set up in configuration. It controls what a condition record may contain, for example the proposed validity period or the calculation type, such as a percentage or an amount per quantity.

  • The condition record is the master data. It holds the amount or ratio that is valid for a validity period and for given price-relevant attributes, such as product and business partner, and it can hold scale lines in which the amount depends on a quantity or value.

  • The price condition is the result inside one business document. The system copies the data of the record that is valid on the date of the document item, and the pricing step saves only the price conditions.

The method by which the system finds the right record is the condition technique. During order processing it determines the net price the customer should be charged and which discounts and surcharges apply.

An invented calculation shows how the pieces meet. Suppose a condition record says that a product costs 12.00 per piece for orders up to 99 pieces and 11.40 per piece from 100 pieces, valid for the whole of one year, and another record gives one customer a 5 percent discount for the same product. A customer without a discount who orders 150 pieces is priced at 150 times 11.40, which is 1,710.00. The customer with the discount pays 5 percent less, which is 1,624.50. The two figures come from two records, one for the price scale and one for the discount, and from the order, which holds only the quantity and the references. The values and the number of records are invented for illustration, and the page does not give them.

In the on premise forms, the SAP GUI transaction VK11 creates condition records and VK12 changes them, as the help page for the United States localization states while describing sales tax records. Which condition types a company has and which fields a record is keyed by (the access sequence and the condition tables) are configuration data, so they differ per implementation and belong to the page on customizing in this section.

Source: SAP Help: Pricing and Conditions

Source: SAP Help: Maintaining SD Condition Records (United States localization)

Where master data comes from and how it stays clean

A new company does not type its master data by hand. It loads it, and a later page of this section covers the migration tools. Day to day, a small group of specialists keeps it up. The business roles named in the apps above, the Master Data Specialist - Business Partner Specialist for business partners and the role PRODMASTER_SPECIALIST for products, express that split: the apps are assigned to specialists. SAP states the quality goal as part of clean core: integrating and extending the system so that it stays cloud-compliant, with optimal master data quality and strong process governance. For a newcomer the practical rules are short. Search before you create, so that the same firm does not become two partners. Create the partner in the role you need and add roles later instead of copying a partner. Remember that a category cannot be changed after the partner exists. And never mix master data into a transport, because the production system owns it.

Summary

  • Master data describes lasting things, such as partners, products and prices. Transactional data records events and refers to master data, and configuration data sets how the software behaves and travels in Customizing requests.

  • The business partner is the leading object in SAP S/4HANA. One record holds a customer, a supplier or both, through roles such as FLCU00, FLCU01, FLVN00 and FLVN01, and the general data is stored only once.

  • A partner has a category of person, organization or group that cannot be changed, a number from a number range chosen by its grouping, and relationships to other partners.

  • Customer/Supplier Integration keeps the old customer and supplier records in step with the business partner, and a conversion to SAP S/4HANA requires that all of them are converted.

  • On premise and Private Edition forms offer the transaction BP and the Manage Business Partner Master Data app. The Public Edition and the on premise forms both expose the OData service API_BUSINESS_PARTNER with A_BusinessPartner and its navigation properties.

  • The product master is one central record per material, grown by organizational level, whose material type decides which data it can hold. Prices are master data too, as condition records that the condition technique finds during pricing.
