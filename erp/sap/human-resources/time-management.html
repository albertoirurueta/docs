Time Management Basics

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Every payroll question about an hourly worker or a salaried employee on leave starts with a simpler one: when was this person supposed to work, and what actually happened? SAP Personnel Time Management answers it in three layers. First it describes the planned working time of each employee with work schedules. Then it records what differs from the plan or what really happened, as absences such as leave and attendances such as extra work. Finally it keeps time accounts, called absence quotas, that say how many days of leave an employee may still take. A newcomer meets these layers when an HR administrator records a leave request, when an employee asks how many vacation days remain, or when payroll needs the hours of an hourly employee. This page explains the three layers with the employees of Example Trading Co. and works out one calculation in full, the pro rata accrual of a leave entitlement. It is written against SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 on premise and Private Edition, where Personnel Time Management comes with SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA (H4S4). The same concepts exist in the older SAP ERP HCM, and SAP SuccessFactors has its own time products that are outside this page.

Before you read

  • HR and Payroll Basics: what the HR processes are and where time data ends up.

  • Enterprise and Personnel Structure: the personnel area, personnel subarea and employee subgroup that the work schedule groupings refer to.

  • Infotypes: what an infotype, a subtype and a validity period are, because absences, attendances and quotas are infotypes.

Key terms

  • Daily work schedule: the working time of one day, with start, end, breaks and planned hours.

  • Period work schedule: a repeating sequence of daily work schedules, such as one week Monday to Friday or a rotating shift plan.

  • Work schedule rule: the object that says which period work schedule applies to which employees, on which public holiday calendar, and from which day of the period it starts.

  • Personal work schedule: the work schedule of one employee after the individual absences, attendances and substitutions are added.

  • Absence: a period inside the planned working time in which the employee is not at work, such as leave, recorded in infotype 2001.

  • Attendance: a recorded working time or a deviation from the planned working time, recorded in infotype 2002.

  • Absence quota: a time account that holds an entitlement, in days or hours, from which absences are deducted, kept in infotype 2006.

  • Time management status: the setting in infotype 0007 that says whether and how an employee is processed by time evaluation.

The running example: Example Trading Co.

All HR pages on this site use one fictitious business, Example Trading Co. All keys, names, numbers and dates are invented example values chosen for this site, including the work schedule codes below. They are not values delivered by SAP, and a real system defines its own. Dates are written DD.MM.YYYY, as an SAP screen shows them.

  • 00001234 Ana Torres, Sales Manager, hired 01.03.2024, and 00001235 Ben Okoye, Sales Clerk, hired 01.09.2024, are salaried staff (employee subgroup AA, personnel subarea 0001 "Head office"). They work Monday to Friday from 08:00 to 16:30 with an unpaid break of 30 minutes, which is 8 paid hours a day and 40 hours a week. Their annual leave entitlement is 30 days.

  • 00001236 Chloe Martin, Warehouse Clerk, hired 15.01.2025, is hourly staff (employee subgroup AB, personnel subarea 0002 "Warehouse"). She works in shifts, also 8 paid hours a day, and has an annual leave entitlement of 28 days.

Layer 1: work schedules say when people should work

A work schedule is built from blocks, in the way a week of a school timetable is built from lessons. The SAP help describes four elements, from the smallest to the largest:

  1. The daily work schedule is the smallest unit. It holds the start and end of work, the breaks and the planned hours of one day, and there is also a daily work schedule for a day off with 0 planned hours.

  2. The period work schedule is a defined sequence of daily work schedules that repeats after a set period: a day, a week, a month or a year. It can cover one week, as in a flextime model, or a whole rotating shift cycle of several weeks.

  3. The work schedule rule assigns a period work schedule to a group of employees. It says which period work schedule is used, on which public holiday calendar and from which day of the period the schedule is generated, so that one period work schedule can serve many rules, for example three rules for early, late and night starting points of one rotating shift pattern.

  4. The work schedule itself is generated by the system when it applies the rule to a calendar.

On top of these comes the personal work schedule, which the system builds for each employee when individual time data from the Absences (2001), Attendances (2002) and Substitutions (2003) infotypes is added to the general work schedule.

Layer 2: recording what happened

Planned working time is only half of the picture. Time Data Recording and Administration, the help portal component that records and manages time data, supports two recording methods:

  • Recording exceptions to the work schedule. Only data that differs from the employee’s work schedule is recorded, such as an illness, a schedule substitution or leave. For everything else the planned working time counts as worked. This fits salaried staff such as Ana and Ben.

  • Additional recording of actual times. All attendance times are recorded, in addition to the exceptions. The actual times can be recorded automatically at time recording terminals or entered by hand in Attendances (2002) and Absences (2001). The book calls this positive time recording. This fits hourly staff such as Chloe, whose pay depends on the hours she really worked.

Which method applies to an employee is stored in the Time Management status in Planned Working Time (0007). The help lists the statuses:

Status Meaning in the help Example use

Employees are not selected for time evaluation. Their time wage types are determined in the time data processing of payroll.

Chloe: her actual hours are evaluated and the resulting time wage types go to payroll.

Not used in the example.

Two infotypes record the events themselves:

  • Absences (2001) records periods during planned working time in which the employee is not on site and does not work for the company, such as leave, illness with a certificate or time off from overtime. Each kind is an absence type, a subtype of 2001. Paid leave is paid and is deducted from a leave entitlement. Unpaid leave is not deducted from a quota and reduces the pay of an employee who receives a fixed monthly salary, so the two types are always valued differently. When you enter an absence, the system calculates its duration in days and hours.

  • Attendances (2002) records an employee’s actual times, deviations from the personal work schedule, and activities such as seminars or business trips.

Ana’s leave in August extends the shared example of the Infotypes page and uses the same numbers. She takes paid leave from Monday 10.08.2026 to Friday 14.08.2026. Her personal work schedule shows 5 working days of 8.0 planned hours, so the absence is 5 days or 40.0 hours, and the five days are deducted from her leave quota.

Entering an absence is done with the personnel administration transaction PA30 on a system; it requires an SAP system and is not run here. The absence, attendance and quota infotypes also have reports. For example, Displaying Absence Quota Information (report RPTQTA10) lists, for selected employees, the quota, the entitlement, the quota used up to a key date and the total quota remaining.

Source: Time Data Recording and Administration, The Time Management Status, Absences Infotype (2001), Absences/Attendances, Displaying Absence Quota Information (Report RPTQTA10)

Layer 3: absence quotas are time accounts

An absence quota is a time account. Think of a bank account that holds days instead of money: deposits are the entitlement, withdrawals are the absences. The help describes the Absence Quotas infotype (2006) as the place for employees' annual leave, for time in lieu accounts and for paid and unpaid absence entitlements. Several facts matter for a beginner:

  • A quota can be entered by hand, proposed by the system as a default when an employee is hired, or accrued automatically.

  • Quotas with the same content, such as leave, are grouped into an absence quota type in Customizing. The quota type is the subtype of 2006, and the account is kept in days or hours depending on the type.

  • A quota has a validity period and a deduction period. They need not be the same. In the help’s example a quota that is valid for one leave year can be deducted for three more months, and expires after that if it is not transferred to another quota.

  • When an employee takes leave, the system deducts the time from the quota until it is used up. Only if Customizing allows a negative balance can it be deducted further.

  • The quota record shows the entitlement (the total entitlement for the period), the requested part (absences recorded for past or future dates), the compensated part (already paid out) and the used part (compensated or deducted by an absence), and the remainder (what can still be taken or compensated).

Ana’s 2026 leave quota is therefore a record in 2006 with the leave quota type, validity 01.01.2026 to 31.12.2026, and an entitlement of 30 days. After her five days of leave the remainder is 30 - 5 = 25 days. If the quota type is kept in hours, the same state reads 30 x 8.0 = 240.0 hours of entitlement, 5 x 8.0 = 40.0 hours used and 240.0 - 40.0 = 200.0 hours remaining.

Accruing a leave entitlement pro rata

An annual entitlement of 30 days is given for a full year. An employee who joins in March has not been there for the whole leave year, so many enterprises grant only a share. The SAP help lists this as a business requirement of automatic quota accrual: a proportionate absence entitlement, for example according to the time worked in the previous period, or a proportionate annual leave entitlement for each month or payroll period. The system enters the result in Absence Quotas (2006) as a deductible quota.

The accrual rules are defined in Customizing, and they control the validity period, the deduction period and the amount of the entitlement. There are three ways to run the accrual: the function QUOTA in time evaluation, the report Generate Absence Quotas (RPTQTA00), and default values when a record of 2006 is created. The help does not give one fixed formula, because the amount comes from the rules a company defines. For understanding the idea, this page uses the simplest rule: the share of the period that the employee was employed.

Here the base is the full-period entitlement (30 or 28 days), the worked days are the calendar days of the accrual period in which the employee was employed, and the period days are all calendar days of the period, here the 365 or 366 days of the year. This is an illustration of the principle, not the exact rule set of an SAP system.

For Ana and Ben a full year, such as 2026 for Ana, needs no calculation: the worked days equal the period days, the fraction is 1 and the entitlement is the full 30 days. Rounding is also a rule the company chooses. The table rounds to two decimals, and a company that grants whole or half days would, for example, round Chloe’s 26.93 to 27.0.

The help also names periodic accrual, a proportionate entitlement for each month or payroll period. The shares of the periods are then added up in the quota during the year; how the periods are weighted is again a rule of the company.

If time evaluation accrues the quota, a correction cannot be typed over it in 2006. The help says that automatically accrued quotas are not changed by hand in Absence Quotas (2006); the Quota Corrections infotype (2013) is used to change accrual entitlements generated by time evaluation. Its help example is an employee who joins from another company with a guaranteed one-off entitlement of ten days of leave, which is added to the leave quota. The quota overview (transaction PT50) shows the calculated quota credits and offers correcting and compensating them. All of this requires an SAP system and is not run here.

Source: Automatic Accrual of Absence Quotas, Quota Corrections Infotype (2013), Quota Maintenance in Time Evaluation

From time data to payroll

Time Management feeds payroll. Time evaluation (the report RPTIME00, treated on the next page of this section) compares the recorded time data with the work schedule, determines approved overtime, accrues absence entitlements and forms wage types, and payroll uses the time wage types to calculate the gross wage. For Ana, the leave week does not reduce her monthly pay because paid leave is paid, while an unpaid week would. For Chloe, the hours that time evaluation finds become time wage types that payroll values. As a plain illustration with her hourly rate of 14.00 EUR, 8.0 hours on one day are worth 8.0 x 14.00 = 112.00 EUR.

Source: Evaluating Time Data

What older books show, and what has changed

The book SAP ERP HCM: Technical Principles and Programming, 1st edition (2013), treats time management as a core data area of HCM, next to personnel administration. It describes time evaluation with the report RPTIME00 and the quota generation program RPTQTA00, mentions schemas for positive and for exception time recording, and shows how to prorate an absence quota accrual with a custom proration factor in a customer exit. The concepts of this page, work schedules, absences, attendances and quotas, are the same as in the book.

What has changed is the product around them. The book describes SAP ERP HCM; in SAP S/4HANA the same functions come as SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA (H4S4), a separately licensed component, and SAP SuccessFactors is the cloud alternative with a different data model. The current help for Quota Corrections (2013), Automatic Accrual of Absence Quotas and the quota overview is published for SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02. The book’s customer exit and screen details were not compared with the current release, so check any enhancement against the in-system documentation of your release before you use it.

Source: SAP ERP HCM: Technical Principles and Programming (SAP PRESS, publisher page; the consulted copy is the 1st edition, the page lists the 2nd)

Summary

  • Planned working time is built from blocks: a daily work schedule, a period work schedule that repeats, and a work schedule rule that assigns the period work schedule to employees and a public holiday calendar. The personal work schedule of an employee adds absences, attendances and substitutions.

  • Time can be recorded as exceptions to the work schedule (Ana and Ben, status 9 in infotype 0007) or as all actual times (Chloe, status 1 or 2).

  • Absences (2001) such as paid leave are deducted from absence quotas (2006), the time accounts of leave and time off. Unpaid leave is not deducted from a quota.

  • Quotas have a validity period and a deduction period, and show entitlement, used, remainder, requested and compensated.

  • A leave entitlement can be accrued pro rata. With the illustration entitlement = base x worked days / period days, Ben gets 30 x 122 / 366 = 10.00 days for 2024, Ana 30 x 306 / 366 = 25.08 days and Chloe 28 x 351 / 365 = 26.93 days for 2025. Real accrual rules are defined in Customizing.

  • Ana’s 5 days of leave from 10.08.2026 to 14.08.2026 leave 30 - 5 = 25 days in her 2026 quota.
