Employee Central Payroll

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Some companies keep their people data in the cloud, in SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central, but still need a payroll run that calculates gross pay, deductions and net pay. Employee Central Payroll (short name ECP) is the payroll that SAP offers for that case: a payroll system that SAP runs for the customer and that reads its people data from Employee Central. A newcomer meets it in projects that move payroll to the cloud, in job descriptions ("ECP consultant") and in support tickets about replication errors. This page explains in plain words what ECP is, how a person such as Ana Torres gets from Employee Central into it, how the payroll result gets back, which tools watch the flow (the Data Replication Monitor and the Payroll Control Center) and which settings a project makes. After reading it you can describe the whole flow and name the right tool when a record does not arrive. It is written against SAP SuccessFactors release 2H 2026 (2611), as described in the product documentation for Employee Central Payroll. That documentation is titled for the version "based on SAP Human Capital Management for SAP ERP HCM"; no page read for this one says that ECP is based on SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA (H4S4), so this page does not state that. Every step below happens in an SAP SuccessFactors tenant or an Employee Central Payroll system that the customer owns, so nothing on this page was run.

Before you read

  • SAP HCM, H4S4 and SuccessFactors: the product options and the core hybrid model.

  • The Payroll Process: payroll area, payroll control record and payroll status.

  • Infotypes: the record types that replication fills in the payroll system.

  • Personnel Actions: how hires and terminations are recorded in the payroll system.

  • Posting Payroll to Accounting: what happens to the payroll result after the run.

Key terms

  • Employee Central Payroll: a payroll system that SAP runs for the customer and that reads its people data from Employee Central.

  • Point-to-point replication: the direct copy of employee data from Employee Central into Employee Central Payroll, with no middleware in between.

  • Compound Employee API: the Employee Central interface that delivers a person’s data, with all its history, in one structured answer.

  • Full transmission start date: the date from which effective-dated data is sent to payroll.

  • Data Replication Monitor: the Employee Central screen that shows, per employee, whether the copy to payroll worked.

  • Payroll Control Center: a set of applications that checks data quality and steers the payroll process.

  • Payroll control record: the record in the payroll system that holds the current payroll period and status.

The example used on this page

The page uses the made-up business of this section, Example Trading Co. (company code EX01, currency EUR). Ana Torres (personnel number 00001234, Sales Manager, hired 01.03.2024) is the featured employee, Ben Okoye (00001235) is a second salaried employee and Chloe Martin (00001236, hired 15.01.2025) is paid by the hour. Their codes and values are invented for this site and are not SAP-delivered values. Payroll area E1 ("Monthly EX01", one period per calendar month) is the example’s choice, and so is the statement that Chloe is paid by the hour inside that monthly payroll area. In Employee Central the same group of people would be a pay group; the mapping is shown in the terminology table below.

What Employee Central Payroll is, and what it is not

SAP’s integration guide for Employee Central Payroll states the main rule at its start: Employee Central is the mandatory system of record for Employee Central Payroll, and all HR events are managed in Employee Central. Payroll-relevant data is either replicated to payroll or maintained in Employee Central using mashups. In plain words:

  • The hire, the job change, the pay change and the termination of Ana are entered once, in Employee Central.

  • ECP does not become a second place where people are hired. It receives a copy of the data it needs, calculates pay, and sends confirmations and results back.

  • The payroll data is kept in the payroll system, which keeps the structures a payroll consultant knows from classic SAP HCM: personnel numbers, infotypes, payroll areas, wage types and a payroll control record. The guide’s terminology page lists the translations between the two worlds:

With the example, Ana’s Employee Central pay component for basic pay becomes a wage type in ECP, and the pay group "Monthly EX01" corresponds to payroll area E1. The guide also states that a partner-provided country version that SAP does not deliver as standard needs a separate SAP Note (the supported countries page points to the Country/Region Specifics guide for the list of countries).

Step 1 to 3: point-to-point replication

The pull flow. The guide’s description says that during point-to-point replication the Employee Central Payroll system calls the Employee Central Compound Employee API to read employee master data and replicate it into Employee Central Payroll. The replication is driven by a report in ECP: its transaction is HRSFEC_PTP_EE_REPL, which the guide also reaches through the SAP Easy Access path Human Resources, Personnel Management, Employee Central Payroll, Replication from Employee Central to Employee Central Payroll, Start Master Data Replication. The report that runs as a background job is named RP_HRSFEC_PTP_EE_REPLICATION. The guide also describes a push replication option, with setup pages in Employee Central and in ECP; this page covers only the pull flow.

Two queries per run. Each run of the report consists of two queries. The first asks for the employees that match the selection criteria of the configuration and were modified after the replication date and time that is stored in the table HRSFEC_PTP_LMOD. The second asks for the employees that match the criteria and that were marked in the Employee Central Data Replication Monitor. When the employees have been replicated, logs are written and the overall status (Successful or Failed) is sent back to Employee Central as a confirmation message.

Configuration. In the guide’s Customizing activity, "Configure Compound Employee API Query", under Personnel Management, Integration Settings for SuccessFactors Employee Central Payroll, Configuration of Point-to-Point Replication, the project creates a configuration that decides what is requested. The guide lists these settings:

Setting What the guide says

Individual or combined configuration

Several individual configurations can be combined with a flag, so one job covers them all.

SELECT clause segments

The minimum set is compensation information, employment information, job information, person and personal information. Two technical segments are added automatically during replication.

Filters

Query parameters for company, countries or regions, employee class, compensation pay group and employment assignment class limit the people that are processed. The standard filter length is 255 characters.

Full transmission start date (FTSD)

The date from which employees with effective-dated master data are listed. The guide recommends moving it forward regularly to limit the amount of data and improve performance.

Multiple actions per day

An optional flag that returns all job information records when a person has several job changes on one day.

Employment end date filter

By default terminated employments are filtered out of the API response; the filter can be switched off, but the guide recommends this only in exceptional situations.

Source: Configuring the Point-to-Point Replication from Employee Central to Employee Central Payroll

Scheduling. The guide schedules the report as a periodic background job through transaction SM36 with the step program RP_HRSFEC_PTP_EE_REPLICATION and a variant. It recommends a 15 minute interval and states that the minimum is 5 minutes. Before the job is released, a test run for one external person ID shows the application log (transaction SLG1 displays it), and errors are corrected first.

Source: Scheduling Employee Master Data Replication from Employee Central to Employee Central Payroll

What Ana’s data becomes. The first transfer of an employee is the initial load. The guide says it assigns a personnel number (PERNR) to each employee and stores the assignment in the table HRSFEC_D_EEKEYMP. In the example the number assigned to Ana is 00001234. Employee Central and ECP have their own data models, so before the initial load a project decides how to treat the history of employee data and what to do with employees and employments that payroll will not account. The mapping page adds an important rule: infotypes that have been filled by replication must not be changed manually in ECP, because the next replication run overwrites the manual change.

Sources: Transferring Replicated Employee Data to Employee Central Payroll and Employee Master Data Replication Mapping Information

Recommended practices for hires and terminations. The guide advises, among other points, to rehire with a new employment rather than the old one, not to add termination and hire or rehire events on the same day (with an exception for no-shows) and not to use customer-specific actions that change the employment status. For Ana’s hire date 01.03.2024 this means the hire is entered once in Employee Central with the usual hire event and is replicated as a personnel action in ECP. The guide requires the actions and reasons to be set up in two tables of the payroll system and a mapping for the events in a third; the table names are given in the guide.

Source: Recommended Practices For Employee Master Data Replication

When the payroll is running: the payroll control record

Replication and payroll meet at the payroll control record. In the ECP system, the guide explains, the payroll control record (transaction PA03) holds the current payroll period and the earlier periods that retroactive accounting may touch. It locks employee master data against changes during a payroll run for the past and present; changes in the future are still possible. The payroll status therefore decides whether replication may write:

Payroll status What the guide says about replication

Exit Payroll

The standard allows replication from Employee Central.

Released for Payroll

The standard does not allow replication.

Released for Correction

The standard does not allow replication either. No error is raised when the changes are in the future or the infotypes are not payroll relevant.

With the example: while the March 2026 payroll for E1 is released, a bank account change that the payroll administrator enters for Ben in Employee Central waits. The guide names two ways to send such a change during the correction phase: select Send Data in the Data Replication Monitor, or activate the switch ONGO in the ECP system. After the exit, the next regular replication run carries the change. In Employee Central the user is also told the earliest possible next date on which payroll-specific changes can be replicated (the guide refers to the business rule "Get Next Possible Date for Changes"). The page The Payroll Process explains the statuses themselves.

Source: Payroll Control Record

Watching the flow: monitor and reports

When a record does not arrive, the guide offers these tools:

  • Data Replication Monitor (in Employee Central). It shows the replication status per employee for the content type Employee Master Data. Selecting the status Failed shows the detailed error message, which comes from the application log of ECP (transaction SLG1). After the cause is corrected, a replication can be triggered again for one or more employees.

  • Replication status report (in ECP). Transaction HRSFEC_REPL_STATUS shows the status of replicated data, the service messages, and an export to a spreadsheet; from it a user can open the application log, the web service utilities (transaction SRTUTIL), start a corrected replication of the same configuration, and compare the current and last successful XML. The guide recommends a copy of the template role SAP_HR_EC_EEMDR_STATUS_MONITOR (made with transaction PFCG) and states that authorization for infotype 0008 (basic pay) is needed to see the XML, because the replication content is sensitive.

  • Consistency check report (in ECP). Transaction HRSFEC_REPL_CONSIST compares the ECP log with the Data Replication Monitor for a period to find inconsistencies caused by an interrupted replication, and offers Reprocess. The guide states that it does not compare all Employee Central and ECP data.

Sources: Using the Employee Central Data Replication Monitor, Checking Status of Replicated Employee Master Data and Using the Consistency Check Report for Replication Data

Step 6: payroll results back to Employee Central

Employee Central Advanced Reporting needs the payroll results, so they travel back. The guide says to transfer the results from ECP to Employee Central, and recommends scheduling the replication as part of the normal payroll process after payroll completion, and also for off-cycle runs. The report RP_HRSFEC_ExportPayrollResults selects a period, organizational units, the wage types to export (through a subapplication) and cumulated or detailed results. It transfers them automatically (OData) or by a file. The guide states that the standard report does not support custom fields and that deleting replicated results in Employee Central is noncritical, because they can be replicated again. For Ana, this means her March 2026 result (gross 4,000.00 and the wage types the project selected) appears in the reports of Employee Central without anybody typing it there.

Sources: Payroll Results in Employee Central and Replicating Payroll Results

The Payroll Control Center

The guide describes the Payroll Control Center as a suite of applications that controls the payroll process. It detects a change event (an administrator changes master data, or data arrives from another program), validates the change against the company’s policy, and lets users do the following:

  • validate master data and payroll results against the company’s own policies before the payroll run;

  • distribute detected issues, called alerts, to the responsible teams of payroll administrators;

  • solve issues, for example with predefined solutions;

  • monitor the statistics of issues and key performance indicators over time.

When a payroll process is repeated, the payroll process manager can choose to repeat the steps for all employees or only for the erroneous employees. The terminology page names this mechanism a shadow process. An alert entity is typically the personnel number, so a validation rule raises one alert per personnel number whose data does not comply. As an example in the guide’s wording, a rule can alert on a salary increase above a limit. With Ana’s data a rule of that kind would catch a pay increase that was keyed with a wrong digit before the March run, and only that employee is repeated. The implementation page recommends configuring it with the Manage Configuration application and the Configuration Workbench, using transaction PFCG for authorizations, and copying the sample objects that SAP delivers instead of using them directly. A prerequisite is that the Enable Payroll Control Center option is selected in Provisioning, which customers do not access themselves: they contact the implementation partner or the account executive.

The guide also lists the application names, which differ between the on-premise systems and ECP: for example Process Management and Alert Management in SAP ERP and SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA correspond to "My Processes", and "My Alerts", "Team Alerts" and "Unassigned Alerts" in ECP.

Sources: Payroll Control Center: Process Management, Terminology in Payroll Control Center, Implement Payroll Control Center and Application Names in SAP ERP, SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA and Employee Central Payroll

Prerequisites and migration

The prerequisites page requires Employee Central core to be set up first, with permissions for restricted access to the Compound Employee API and for the Employee Central OData APIs. It also requires the ECP system to be on the latest support package, a check of the business function in transaction SFW5, and the SAP GUI for HR administrators, who are the only people who sign in to ECP. Many setup steps need Provisioning, which customers do not use themselves. For a move from an old payroll system, the guide describes a migration and initial data load phase in which the production environment is transferred and the overlap between the go-live of ECP and the shutdown of the old system must be covered by a planned cutoff scenario.

Sources: Prerequisites for Employee Central Payroll and Migration and Initial Data Load

What older books show, and what has changed

The book SAP ERP HCM: Technical Principles and Programming, 1st edition (2013), by Dirk Liepold and Steve Ritter, has a chapter on integrating SuccessFactors with SAP ERP HCM. It describes an add-on installed in the on-premise system with two reports that extract employee data toward Employee Central, optionally through middleware, with its own logging tables. The direction is the reverse of what this page shows: the on-premise HR system was the source and the cloud system the target. In today’s documentation Employee Central is the system of record and Employee Central Payroll pulls the data through the Compound Employee API. The book does not mention Employee Central Payroll, which the 2013 text does not contain, and a reader must use current documentation for every setting named on this page.

Summary

  • Employee Central Payroll is a payroll that SAP runs for the customer; Employee Central stays the system of record and all HR events are entered there.

  • The default flow is point-to-point replication: a periodic ECP report (a 15 minute interval is recommended) calls the Compound Employee API and confirms each employee’s status back to the Data Replication Monitor.

  • Replication respects the payroll control record: while a payroll is released, master data changes wait unless an administrator forces them through.

  • Payroll postings reach accounting through ALE or web services depending on the accounting system, and payroll results go back to Employee Central with a report.

  • The Payroll Control Center checks master data and results against company policy and repeats the process only for erroneous employees.

  • The documentation read for this page is the version based on SAP ERP HCM; this page does not claim that ECP is based on H4S4.
