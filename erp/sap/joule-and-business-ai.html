Joule and Business AI

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

SAP now adds artificial intelligence to its business software in three places that a newcomer meets quickly. The first is Joule, a chat-style helper that sits inside SAP applications and answers questions or carries out tasks. The second is the generative AI hub, a service on SAP Business Technology Platform (SAP BTP) that developers use to call large language models from their own applications under company controls. The third is a public catalog of AI agents on the SAP Business Accelerator Hub. This page explains what each of the three is, how they relate, and what a developer or a consultant can do with them. It is written from SAP’s official documentation read on 2026-11-13, for SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608, SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS02 (on premise and Private Edition) and the services on SAP BTP. Joule and the generative AI hub are cloud services, so they apply wherever the SAP BTP account and the connected SAP system allow it, not to the SAP system alone. None of the listings on this page was run: they need an SAP BTP account, and no account was used for this page.

About the name of the platform: the product documentation still calls the platform SAP Business Technology Platform (SAP BTP) in every service guide read for this page, and this page does the same. SAP’s product page states that SAP Business Technology Platform capabilities are now a core part of SAP Business AI Platform, so the same services may be presented under that newer name.

Before you read

  • What Is SAP: the products that Joule and the AI services sit next to.

  • SAP BTP Account Model: global accounts, subaccounts and entitlements, which decide who may use an AI service.

  • SAP Integration Suite and Business Accelerator Hub: the public catalog this page returns to in the last section.

  • What Is an LLM: the general idea of a large language model, which this page does not repeat.

Key terms

  • Joule: SAP’s AI solution that brings assistants and agents into the flow of work.

  • Joule agent: a purpose-built piece of software that carries out a business task in several steps.

  • Generative AI hub: the part of SAP AI Core that gives governed access to generative AI models.

  • SAP AI Core: the SAP BTP service that runs and manages AI workloads.

  • Orchestration: the generative AI hub service that chains templating, grounding, masking and filtering around a model call.

  • Grounding: giving a model your own documents as context at the moment it answers.

  • Data masking: replacing personal data in a prompt before it reaches the model.

  • SAP BTP: SAP’s cloud platform, where these services run.

The three pieces at a glance

A large language model is a program that reads text and writes text. On its own it knows only what it learned during training. It does not know your open purchase orders, and it will happily invent an answer when it is unsure. Business AI at SAP is mostly the work of connecting such models to company data safely. The three pieces below solve different parts of that problem for different people.

Piece Who uses it What it is for

Joule

Business users, and developers who extend it

Ask in plain language and get answers or actions inside SAP applications

Generative AI hub in SAP AI Core

Developers

Call language models from your own application, with grounding, masking and filtering

AI agents on the SAP Business Accelerator Hub

Developers and architects

Browse the agents that SAP and partners offer

Joule: assistants and agents

SAP’s service guide describes Joule as bringing "AI assistants and agents together" to streamline workflows. It is an AI solution embedded into the flow of work across SAP and non-SAP systems. The user meets it as a conversational interface inside the SAP application they already use, and the guide says the web client renders answers with SAP Fiori controls. It also states that Joule is a multi-tenant application that runs in SAP BTP Cloud Foundry: each customer is a separate tenant and the data of tenants is isolated.

SAP’s learning material separates two roles that are easy to mix up:

  • An assistant talks to the person. It interprets what the person wants and coordinates the work.

  • An agent does the work. SAP’s lesson describes a Joule agent as a purpose-built software system for one business task, which plans the steps, invokes skills, interacts with SAP systems and checks the results.

The same lesson names three modes in which agents run. In conversational mode the person asks in Joule. In embedded mode the agent works inside an existing application. In system-triggered mode a business event or a schedule starts it. It also says that agents act with the identity and the permissions of the user, so an agent can only do what that person is entitled to do. That is the same authorization idea as in Users, Roles and Authorizations: the AI does not get a back door past the roles.

For a running example, imagine an employee who asks Joule about the status of an expense claim that is stored in the table ZEXP_CLAIM of this series. Joule does not read the table itself. It reaches the claim through the SAP application and the employee’s own roles, so a colleague without the right role gets no answer.

Setting Joule up and extending it

Joule is not something a developer installs on a laptop. The service guide lists, among its sections, customer-managed provisioning ("To start using Joule, you must complete some steps in SAP BTP cockpit"), setup and integration with SAP solutions, integration with SAP Build Work Zone, a security guide and a monitoring and troubleshooting guide. All of these are done by an administrator in an SAP BTP account.

For developers, the Joule Development Guide describes how to develop an assistant with the Joule Studio command line tool and the Joule Studio code editor, and how to compile it and deploy it into a tenant on SAP BTP. The guide states that building and deploying capabilities for Joule requires the role of an extensibility developer. SAP’s learning material adds that agents can also be built in Joule Studio, which is the low-code side of the same idea. This page does not walk through Joule Studio, because that needs an SAP BTP account.

Governing agents: the SAP AI Agent Hub

As the number of agents grows, companies need one place to see them. SAP’s learning lesson describes the SAP AI Agent Hub as a central view of the agents in a landscape: SAP-built agents, custom agents built in Joule Studio and agents from other providers. It lists five lifecycle stages: discover and provision, plan and build, observe and analyze, secure and govern, and optimize and decommission. The lesson states that every decision of an agent is logged and traceable. The lesson is training material, so check the current product documentation for the licensing of the hub before relying on it.

Source: What Is Joule?, Joule Development, Getting to Know Joule Agents

The generative AI hub in SAP AI Core

Joule is a finished product. The generative AI hub is a toolbox. SAP’s documentation defines it as a capability of SAP AI Core and SAP AI Launchpad that gives access to, and orchestration of, generative AI models within a governed runtime. SAP AI Core is the runtime and lifecycle foundation for AI scenarios on SAP BTP, and the documentation says the hub does not replace it: "it extends it", with model execution and resource management handled by SAP AI Core. With the hub you can access supported models, submit prompts, integrate generative AI into business applications and operate those workloads under enterprise governance.

Models are not all made by SAP. The table of contents of the documentation lists model providers such as OpenAI, Mistral, Cohere, NVIDIA, Perplexity, AWS Bedrock and Vertex AI, and SAP’s own models such as SAP-RPT-1 and SAP-ABAP-1. Which model is available depends on the region, and the documentation has a page for that. A page like this one cannot give a current list, so look at "Supported Models" in the documentation.

Orchestration: one call, several safeguards

A language model should not receive raw company data. The generative AI hub therefore offers an orchestration service, which runs on SAP AI Core under the global scenario named orchestration. SAP describes it as unified access to many models through a harmonized API, so the client code does not change when the model changes. Its key features are:

Module What it does, in the words of the documentation

Templating

Composes prompts with placeholders that are filled in during inference

Content filtering

Restricts the type of content passed to and received from the models

Data masking

Anonymizes or pseudonymizes data before it is passed to a model

Grounding

Integrates external, domain-specific or real-time data as context

Translation

Adds translation for input and output

Source: Orchestration

In a basic scenario the modules form a pipeline that runs with a single API call, and the response of one module is the input of the next. SAP states that the order of the pipeline is fixed centrally in orchestration, and that the caller configures each module and leaves out the optional ones. Only the templating and the model configuration are mandatory. For a new application, SAP recommends the SAP Cloud SDK for AI to call orchestration.

Data masking

Data masking is the module that matters most to a payroll or expense scenario. It has two methods. Anonymization replaces personal data of the chosen categories with the placeholder MASKED_ENTITY, and the original is not kept, so it cannot be restored. Pseudonymization uses the placeholder MASKED_ENTITY_ID, and the masked values can be restored in the response. The documentation lists entity categories such as person name, e-mail, phone number, address, IBAN and national identity number. It also warns that the masking service relies on automatic detection and "can’t guarantee" that all personal information is found, and that anonymizing can remove context the model needs. The practical rule: masking reduces the risk, it does not replace a decision about which data may leave your landscape at all.

Grounding

Grounding is how a model gets to know your documents. SAP explains that pretrained models have no access to your proprietary or real-time data, and that grounding connects the model to external sources, retrieves relevant information when a question arrives, and supplies it as context. To prepare the documents, the module splits them into chunks, turns the chunks into vectors (embeddings) and stores them in a vector database. At question time the question is turned into a vector, similar content is searched and the result is handed to the model. This is the pattern called retrieval augmented generation, which Citations and Grounding explains in the AI section. The documentation lists the sources for documents: Microsoft SharePoint, AWS S3, SFTP, SAP Build Work Zone, SAP Document Management service, ServiceNow and Google Drive, with a maximum of 8000 documents per pipeline. Documents come in either through a pipeline from such a repository or directly as chunks through a vector API.

A request to orchestration

The listing below is a reduced request in the documented structure: a config with modules, the mandatory prompt_templating module with its model, the optional masking module, and placeholder_values that fill the placeholders. The documented example in the source uses more modules and a longer prompt. The expense-claim wording is this page’s own. The model name is only an example of the form: use a model that your region offers.

curl --request POST \
  --url $ORCH_DEPLOYMENT_URL/v2/completion \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  --header "AI-Resource-Group: $RESOURCE_GROUP" \
  --header 'content-type: application/json' \
  --data '{
  "config": {
    "modules": {
      "prompt_templating": {
        "prompt": {
          "template": [
            { "role": "system", "content": "Summarize the expense claim in one sentence." },
            { "role": "user", "content": "{{?claim-text}}" }
          ]
        },
        "model": { "name": "gpt-4o" }
      },
      "masking": {
        "providers": [
          {
            "type": "sap_data_privacy_integration",
            "method": "pseudonymization",
            "entities": [ { "type": "profile-person" }, { "type": "profile-email" } ]
          }
        ]
      }
    }
  },
  "placeholder_values": { "claim-text": "Claim EXP-1001 from Jane Doe (jane.doe@example.com): taxi to the client site." }
}'

Source: structure and field names from Orchestration Workflow V2 and the masking entities from Data Masking; requires an SAP BTP account; not run here.

The variables are the deployment URL of your running orchestration deployment, a token created from the service key of your SAP AI Core instance, and the ID of the resource group that holds the deployment, exactly as the documentation lists them. The endpoint /v2/completion is the one of orchestration version 2. SAP notes that anyone migrating a workflow from version 1 must change the endpoint and the payload, and the documentation marks version 1 as deprecated. Older articles and books that show version 1 requests are therefore outdated in this respect.

With pseudonymization, the model sees placeholders instead of the name and the e-mail address, and the response has them put back. The person’s name never reaches the model provider in readable form, which is the point of the module.

AI agents on the SAP Business Accelerator Hub

The SAP Business Accelerator Hub, covered in SAP Integration Suite and Business Accelerator Hub, is the public catalog of APIs, events and integrations. On 2026-11-13 its public start page showed an Artificial Intelligence area with a tile "AI Agents" ("Unlock the Power of AI Agents") that counted 117 AI agents, and two API packages: SAP AI Core (10 APIs) and SAP AI Services (7 APIs). The link of the tile leads to the agents listing, but opening it asked for a sign-in, and no account was used for this page. So this page does not describe what an individual agent entry contains. Counts change over time.

Where the AI section of this site continues

Joule and the generative AI hub are SAP’s way to use the ideas that the AI section of this site explains in general: AI agents, grounding with your own documents, guardrails and the Model Context Protocol. Read those pages for the concepts. Read this page to see which SAP service carries each idea.

Summary

  • Joule is SAP’s AI solution with assistants, which talk to the user, and agents, which carry out multi-step tasks. It runs as a multi-tenant service in SAP BTP Cloud Foundry, and agents act with the permissions of the user.

  • Developers extend Joule with Joule Studio, which needs an SAP BTP account and the extensibility developer role.

  • The generative AI hub is part of SAP AI Core. Its orchestration service gives one harmonized API to many models, with templating, content filtering, data masking, grounding and translation modules.

  • Data masking offers anonymization and pseudonymization, and SAP warns that it cannot guarantee that every piece of personal data is found.

  • Grounding gives a model your own documents as context through chunks, embeddings and a vector database.

  • The SAP Business Accelerator Hub has a public AI Agents catalog; its listing needs a sign-in. The documentation still names the platform SAP BTP, while SAP now presents its capabilities as part of SAP Business AI Platform.
