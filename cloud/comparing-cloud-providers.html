Comparing Cloud Providers

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page compares the three public cloud providers documented in this section, AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, on popularity, ecosystem, learning curve, pricing, availability regions and a few other metrics. It ends with a recommendation by workload type and target region, plus a decision flowchart. All figures are a snapshot as of 2026-10-26: each number is linked to a source listed in the Bibliography, and the data files behind the tables and figures are kept in the repository under scripts/cloud-comparison-charts/data.

The scope is deliberately limited to the three providers above. Other providers, such as Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, Alibaba Cloud and IBM Cloud, exist and matter in some markets, but they are mentioned for context only and are not scored. The Gartner positioning in Analyst positioning notes, for example, that Alibaba Cloud was also named a Leader in 2026.

Methodology

Sources, dates and missing data

Every row of every data file has a source id and a retrieval date (2026-10-26, the snapshot date). Primary and official sources are preferred: the providers' earnings releases, official pricing pages and price-list files, official region and service-availability pages, SLA documents, and recognised analyst and survey publications. Source ids appear next to each table or figure as links into the Bibliography, which lists every source with its publication or access date. Blogs and unsourced aggregators are not used for numbers.

A value for which no reliable public source was found is shown as n/a (not available). It is never plotted as zero and never estimated. Where a figure had to come from a secondary source because the primary page could not be read, it is labelled as secondary in the text:

  • JetBrains State of Developer Ecosystem 2025 is taken from an AWS Insider report, because the JetBrains page returned an access error.

  • Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026 usage, admired and desired figures are taken from a Virtualization Review article dated 2026-10-06, because the survey page text had no per-provider figures.

  • Canalys (Omdia) quarterly shares come from search-engine excerpts of the publisher pages, which returned an access error when fetched, so some provider shares are missing.

  • Gartner Magic Quadrant positioning for 2020 to 2022 comes from press coverage, and the 2025 and 2026 positioning of AWS and Google Cloud comes from the providers' own statements. The Gartner reports themselves are not public.

Caveats that apply to the whole page

  • Synergy Research Group and Canalys (Omdia) market-share series are different series with different scopes and methods and must not be spliced together.

  • Synergy changed its treatment of Microsoft from the third quarter of 2024: some Azure revenue was reclassified as software as a service, which lowered Microsoft’s share from that release onward. The share series has a break at that point.

  • Revenue segments are not like for like: AWS reports segment net sales, Google Cloud reports a segment that includes Google Workspace, Microsoft reports Intelligent Cloud, which is broader than Azure, and discloses Azure only as a growth rate.

  • Developer surveys measure different populations and wordings, so their percentages are not comparable across surveys.

  • A region or an availability zone is not defined identically by the three providers, and the Azure count used here is the number of regions listed on the Microsoft regions page, which may include regions that are not open to every customer.

  • Prices are list prices on the retrieval date, on demand, in US dollars, without taxes, discounts, free allowances or support plans. They are not a quote.

Criteria and score derivation

The radar chart in Scorecard uses six axes scored from 0 to 10, where 10 means best of the three providers on that axis (not a theoretical maximum). Four axes are computed from the data files by a script, and two are editorial. The rule for each axis is:

Axis Kind Rule

Popularity

Computed

10 times the mean of (Synergy 2026-Q2 share divided by the largest share) and (Stack Overflow 2026 usage divided by the largest usage)

Ecosystem breadth

Computed

10 times the mean of (service categories with a service, out of the 31 mapped) and (official SDK languages divided by the largest count)

Ease of use

Editorial

Judgement from trial credit and length, Stack Overflow admired score, size of the first-party learning catalogue and certification path size

Price competitiveness

Computed

10 times the mean over the four reference architectures of (cheapest monthly list-price total divided by own total)

Regional coverage

Computed

10 times the mean of (generally available public regions divided by the most), (their availability zones divided by the most) and (sovereign categories available out of China, government and sovereign, divided by 3). Region and zone counts come from the footprint data on the same basis for all three providers: China, government and sovereign partitions are left out of the counts, because they are scored by the third term

AI offering

Editorial, low confidence

Judgement from managed ML platform, foundation-model platform, prebuilt AI services and AI certifications; no model-quality or market data was fetched

The scores, the evidence behind each one and the computed-versus-editorial split are given in Scorecard. The data files and their refresh procedure are documented in the repository (scripts/cloud-comparison-charts/data/NOTES.md), and the figures are regenerated from them with npm run charts:cloud-comparison.

Integration strengths

The sources used measure breadth and tooling, not integration quality, so the strengths below are limited to what they show. Claims about, for example, Microsoft 365 or .NET integration are not quantified by any dataset here and are not scored.

  • AWS: the widest mapped catalogue (31 of 31 categories), the most official SDK languages (11), the most downloaded Terraform provider (about 8.1 billion cumulative downloads against 2.1 billion for Azure and 2.7 billion for Google Cloud), and AWS Ground Station, for which Microsoft states that Azure has no managed equivalent and which the Google comparison page does not list. Sources: svc-ms-aws-index, sdkref-aws, terraform-registry.

  • Azure: Microsoft Entra ID is the mapped identity service, Azure DevOps and GitHub Actions are the mapped CI/CD services, and Azure Arc and Azure Local cover hybrid and edge, a category for which the Google comparison page lists no equivalent. Sources: svc-ms-aws-index, svc-ms-compute.
