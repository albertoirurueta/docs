Graphics APIs and Game Engines Comparison

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

This page compares the six libraries documented in this section: OpenGL 4.6 (with OpenGL ES 3.2), WebGL 2.0 (with three.js and WebGPU as context), Metal 4, Vulkan 1.4, Unity 6.3 LTS and Unreal Engine 5.8. Every number was fetched on 2026-11-01 and every claim about a feature is explained on the concept pages of this section. The approach follows the E-commerce Platforms Comparison, the Documentation Tools Comparison and the Cloud Providers Comparison of this site: data kept in CSV files, charts generated from them, and a stated source and date for every figure. The page does not name a winner, because the right choice depends on the target platforms, the team and what has to be shipped; the last sections turn the data into recommendations by purpose. For the criteria in prose see Choosing an API or Engine.

Scope, methodology and snapshot date

Scope. The six libraries are not the same kind of product. OpenGL, WebGL, Metal and Vulkan are graphics APIs: they give the program access to the GPU and nothing else. Unity and Unreal Engine are game engines: they add an editor, an asset pipeline, scripting, physics, audio and build targets on top of one or more graphics APIs. The definitions are in Graphics APIs vs Game Engines. Comparisons are therefore made within each group (four APIs, two engines) and across the two groups only on the axes where the comparison makes sense, such as platform reach, tooling or licensing.

Snapshot date. All values were fetched on 2026-11-01 from the sources in References and in scripts/graphics-games-comparison-charts/data/sources.csv; every row of the CSV files carries its own fetch date. Time series end at the latest complete period at that date: 2025 for yearly series and the quarter to date (2026-Q4) for npm.

Basis of every number.

  • Counts (questions, stars, contributors, downloads) are measured values from the sources named in each figure.

  • The engine-share chart is survey data from the JetBrains State of GameDev 2025 report, not a market share. The GDC 2026 State of the Industry report page could not be opened on the check date, so no percentage from it is cited.

  • A missing value is shown as n/a and is never drawn as zero. Unity and Unreal Engine have no public source repository, so they are n/a in the GitHub charts.

  • The radar scores are editorial. Each of the 60 scores has a recorded basis and source in radar-scores.csv and follows the rubric below; they summarise the evidence and are not a benchmark.

Strengths and weaknesses

OpenGL. Strengths: a huge body of books, tutorials and code; royalty-free; runs on old and new GPUs; a gentle state machine that makes pipeline concepts visible. Weaknesses: development stopped at version 4.6 in 2017; Apple froze it at 4.1 and deprecated it; implicit synchronisation limits multi-threading; no standard hardware ray tracing.

WebGL. Strengths: runs in every current browser with no install; the JavaScript ecosystem (three.js, Babylon.js) supplies scene graphs and loaders; links share instantly. Weaknesses: a sandboxed subset of OpenGL ES 3.0 without compute shaders; browser overhead and limits on memory; WebGPU is the successor and WebGL receives little new functionality.

Metal. Strengths: explicit but approachable API; first-party tools in Xcode; direct integration with Apple GPUs, MetalFX and machine learning; the way to render on visionOS. Weaknesses: Apple platforms only; the Metal 4 generation needs recent hardware and operating systems; RenderDoc does not capture it.

Vulkan. Strengths: the highest performance ceiling on desktop and Android; explicit multi-threaded submission; royalty-free; strong validation layers and tools. Weaknesses: the steepest learning curve and the most code before a first triangle; extensions and device differences; Apple platforms need MoltenVK or KosmicKrisp.

Unity. Strengths: fast iteration in C#, the Asset Store, the broadest platform reach including mobile and web, XR packages. Weaknesses: proprietary with seat licences above a revenue threshold; the render pipeline landscape is changing (the Built-in pipeline is deprecated, URP is the main investment); the engine owns the frame, which limits deep renderer changes.

Unreal Engine. Strengths: high-fidelity rendering out of the box (Nanite, Lumen, MegaLights), Blueprints and C++, full source access, mature tools. Weaknesses: heavy projects and long build times, a 5% royalty above US$1M of product revenue, limited browser support, a steep C++ framework.

Community activity: Stack Overflow questions

Figure 1. Stack Overflow questions per year for the graphics API tags, 2010 to 2025

The opengl tag peaked in 2014 at 5,150 questions and has fallen every year since; vulkan grew until 2022, and metal has no values before 2014 because the tag did not exist yet. Every tag falls steeply from 2023, mirroring the decline of the whole site, so the trend says little about the popularity of a library. By total, the opengl tag holds 37,925 questions, three.js 20,865, opengl-es 14,004, webgl 6,733, vulkan 2,531 and metal 2,309.

Figure 2. Stack Overflow questions per year for the game engine tags, 2010 to 2025

Unity questions are an order of magnitude more numerous than Unreal Engine questions (76,776 in total for unity-game-engine against 2,277 for unreal-engine4 and 705 for unreal-engine5). Unreal Engine has its own forums and Epic community, so the tag undercounts its community. A tag that did not exist in a year is n/a.

Recommendations by purpose

  • Learning graphics. OpenGL 4.x or WebGL 2 to see the pipeline as a state machine, then Vulkan or WebGPU to learn explicit resources.

  • A browser product. WebGL 2 with three.js, with WebGPU as an upgrade path.

  • An Apple-only app. Metal for custom rendering; higher-level frameworks for everything else.

  • A cross-platform renderer. Vulkan with a Metal backend or a portability layer, keeping a thin abstraction of your own (see Cross-Platform Translation Layers).

  • An indie or mobile game. Unity: the largest community and the widest platform reach for a small team.

  • A AAA-style game. Unreal Engine, where the renderer, tools and source access save years of work.

  • XR. An engine with OpenXR support, or Metal on visionOS for Apple-only experiences.

Limitations of the data

  • Stack Overflow’s decline since 2023 affects every tag; the metal tag is generic and also covers other topics.

  • Stars, contributors and downloads measure interest and activity, not the number of shipped products.

  • npm counts include continuous integration and mirror traffic.

  • Survey data has few AAA respondents and no stated sample size on the page.

  • APIs and engines are different kinds of product; no figure adds them together.

  • The radar scores summarise the evidence of the snapshot date and reflect the weighting of the rubric.
