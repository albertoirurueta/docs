B2B

This content was generated with the assistance of AI and should be verified against the official documentation before being relied on in production.

Shopify B2B lets a merchant sell to other businesses from the same store that sells to consumers: a buyer logs in as a contact of a company, picks one of the company’s locations, and then sees the products, prices, quantity limits and payment terms that the merchant agreed for that location. This page was written against the Admin GraphQL API and the Storefront API 2026-10, the Customer Account API schema 2026-04 (the newest schema that could be downloaded without a store, shipped in @shopify/hydrogen-react 2026.4.4) and Shopify CLI 5.0.0, and every fact was checked on 2026-10-27. B2B features depend on the plan, and the plan rules changed with the Winter '26 and Spring '26 Editions, so the section Plans, availability and limits lists what is known and where it comes from.

The page covers the four building blocks and the places where they surface: the data model (companies, locations, contacts and roles), catalogs with their price lists, publications and quantity rules, payment terms and deposits, and draft orders with imported orders; then the storefront side (Liquid in themes, and the Customer Account and Storefront APIs for headless), checkout customization with Shopify Functions, events, and a list of the mistakes that cost the most time. It builds on the Admin GraphQL API page (versioning, cost-based throttling, pagination), on Authentication and Access Scopes for the tokens that sign the requests, and on Markets and Internationalization, because a company location is also a market condition and catalogs are shared between Markets and B2B. The running example is Pocket Coffee, a small specialty-coffee shop that opens a wholesale channel: the invented company Cafe Brasa SL (a cafe with a Madrid and a Berlin location) buys 1 kg bags of single-origin beans at a negotiated price, in multiples of two bags, on 30-day terms with a 20 percent deposit.

Every Admin GraphQL, Storefront and Customer Account document on the page was validated against the downloaded schema, and every set of variables was checked against the variable definitions of its operation. The Liquid snippets passed shopify theme check and were rendered with a stand-in engine. The Shopify Function was built and run offline with the CLI’s Function runner. Nothing on this page was executed against a Shopify store: B2B resources need a shop on a plan that supports them and an access token, and no account or credential was created for this page. The section What was run and what was not lists exactly what each check covered.

B2B on Shopify at a glance

Shopify B2B is not a separate product line with its own API. It is a set of objects and settings that sit on top of the ordinary store model, and each layer has its own entry point:

Layer What it does Where you reach it

Companies, locations, contacts

Identify the business customer, its branches and the people who buy for it. A contact is a customer record linked to a company.

Admin GraphQL: Company, CompanyLocation, CompanyContact, CompanyContactRole

Catalogs, price lists, publications

Decide which products a location sees and what it pays, including fixed prices, volume breaks and quantity rules.

Admin GraphQL: CompanyLocationCatalog, PriceList, Publication, QuantityRule, QuantityPriceBreak

Payment terms and deposits

Decide when the company pays: at once, within a number of days, on a date or on fulfillment, with an optional deposit.

Admin GraphQL: PaymentTermsTemplate, BuyerExperienceConfiguration, PaymentTerms; Functions: paymentTermsSet

Orders and draft orders

A merchant or an app quotes and invoices a purchase, or imports history; the order records the purchasing company.

Admin GraphQL: DraftOrder, Order, PurchasingCompany

Storefront

Show the buyer’s location, prices and rules in a theme or a headless storefront.

Liquid objects company, company_location, customer.b2b?; Storefront API @inContext(buyer:); Customer Account API

Checkout

Apply the location’s terms, review rules and validation to the B2B checkout.

Source: Apps and B2B

The developer guide describes a typical lifecycle in four steps. A merchant creates a company with its contacts and locations, and may create draft orders for a contact to review. A contact logs in with an account attached to the company, selects products, reviews draft orders and completes checkout. Shopify creates the transaction and the order. An app then uses the Admin API for the integrations around it: order management interfaces, B2B fulfillment, loyalty, or CRM synchronization.

A store can serve consumers and companies together or only companies. The developer guides use the names "blended" and "dedicated" for the two setups; in code the difference is whether the storefront hides prices, the cart and the order buttons from visitors who are not logged in as a B2B buyer. Both setups use the same objects, and the section on Themes shows the Liquid that implements a dedicated store.

Customer accounts

B2B only works with the new customer accounts. A store still on legacy customer accounts cannot give buyers a B2B session, and a headless storefront must replace its legacy login code with the Customer Account API authorization flow before it can contextualize requests. Legacy customer accounts are deprecated in favour of new customer accounts, as the Authentication and Access Scopes page explains.

The data model

The B2B objects of the Admin API and how they hang together.

Three objects model the customer, and one more joins them:

Object Meaning

Company

The business that buys. Fields include name, note, externalId (the key of the company in an ERP or CRM), customerSince, mainContact, defaultRole, contactRoles, contacts, locations, orders, draftOrders, ordersCount, totalSpent and metafields.

CompanyLocation

A branch of the company, and the object that carries almost all commercial settings: billingAddress, shippingAddress, currency (derived from the shipping address), locale, externalId, note, taxSettings, buyerExperienceConfiguration, catalogs, roleAssignments, staffMemberAssignments, storeCreditAccounts, orders and draftOrders.

CompanyContact

A person who buys on behalf of the company: title, locale, isMainContact, the linked customer, and its roleAssignments. The contact is not a separate login; it points at an ordinary Customer, which is the account that logs in.

CompanyContactRoleAssignment

One row of "this contact has this role at this location". A contact can act only at locations where it holds a role, so creating a contact is not enough to let it order.

CompanyContactRole

A named role defined on the company (id, name, note). Company.contactRoles lists them and Company.defaultRole is the one proposed by default.

Source: Company object reference (fields read from the 2026-10 introspection)

Orders and draft orders refer to all three through the PurchasingEntity union, which is either a Customer or a PurchasingCompany (company, location and contact). A B2B order can be tied to the company, to one location or to one contact. Catalogs, however, attach to a location only, which is why price differences between branches of the same company are modelled with several locations, not several contacts.

On the storefront side the types are deliberately small. The Storefront API Company has id, name, externalId, timestamps and metafields; CompanyLocation adds locale; CompanyContact has only title and locale; and PurchasingCompany ties them to a cart. The Customer Account API exposes Customer.companyContacts, and from a contact its company, locations, orders, draftOrders and status (DISABLED or ENABLED), and from a location its addresses, tax identifier, buyerExperienceConfiguration, payment instruments and orders.

The tutorial stresses that a location must be added to attach information such as tax exemptions, and that a contact must exist before any order or draft order can be created for the company. Tax settings

Tax exemptions belong to the location, not the company, because two branches may be taxed differently (the guide’s example is a company with branches that follow different billing and taxation rules).       taxSettings {

        taxRegistrationId

Catalogs, price lists and publications

Three objects, one idea

A catalog answers two questions for a context: which products can the buyer see, and what do they cost. It does so by pointing at two optional objects. A publication is the set of products and collections visible in the catalog. A price list holds prices for variants: a percentage adjustment of the base price (parent), plus fixed prices for individual variants, plus quantity rules and price breaks. Catalog is an interface with three implementations, chosen by the context:

Type Context Created with

Several catalogs on one location

A company location can be in several catalogs, and the rules for combining them are simple. A buyer sees the products that are published in at least one applicable catalog, and when several catalogs price the same variant, the lowest listed price applies. The developer docs build a scaling pattern on this: keep pricing-only catalogs (one per price list) and publication-only catalogs (one per assortment) and assign several to each location. A merchant with 50 pricing tiers and 10 assortments then manages 60 catalogs instead of 500 full ones. The pattern is shared with Markets, and on plans below Plus the limit of three B2B catalogs applies, so it matters most on Plus.

The model below encodes only the documented rules, so a configuration can be reasoned about before it is sent to a shop. It is a local illustration, not Shopify’s engine: it does not know about rounding, exchange rules or market inheritance, and how price breaks of one catalog combine with the lower price of another is not documented, so it takes the lowest result.

const base = { 'PC-COL-WHL-1KG': 29.9, 'PC-BRA-WHL-1KG': 29.9, 'PC-ETH-WHL-1KG': 33.9 };

const catalogs = [
  {
    title: 'Wholesale beans 1 kg',
    locations: ['Madrid', 'Berlin'],
    publication: ['PC-COL-WHL-1KG', 'PC-BRA-WHL-1KG'],
    priceList: {
      decreasePercent: 20,
      fixed: { 'PC-BRA-WHL-1KG': 22.0 },
      breaks: { 'PC-COL-WHL-1KG': [{ min: 10, price: 21.5 }, { min: 20, price: 20.0 }] },
      rules: { 'PC-COL-WHL-1KG': { minimum: 4, increment: 2, maximum: 100 } },
    },
  },
  {
    title: 'Spring promotion (pricing only)',
    locations: ['Madrid'],
    publication: null,
    priceList: { decreasePercent: 30, fixed: {}, breaks: {}, rules: {} },
  },
];

const round2 = (n) => Math.round(n * 100) / 100;

export function resolve(location, sku, quantity) {
  const applicable = catalogs.filter((c) => c.locations.includes(location));
  const visible = applicable.some((c) => c.publication && c.publication.includes(sku));
  if (!visible) return { sku, location, visible: false };

  const rule = applicable.map((c) => c.priceList.rules[sku]).find(Boolean)
    ?? { minimum: 1, increment: 1, maximum: null };
  const quantityAllowed =
    quantity >= rule.minimum &&
    (rule.maximum === null || quantity <= rule.maximum) &&
    quantity % rule.increment === 0;

  const candidates = applicable.map((c) => {
    const pl = c.priceList;
    let unit = pl.fixed[sku] ?? round2(base[sku] * (1 - pl.decreasePercent / 100));
    for (const b of (pl.breaks[sku] ?? []).sort((x, y) => x.min - y.min)) if (quantity >= b.min) unit = b.price;
    return { catalog: c.title, unit };
  });
  const best = candidates.reduce((a, b) => (b.unit < a.unit ? b : a));
  return { sku, location, visible, quantity, quantityAllowed, candidates, unit: best.unit, from: best.catalog,
           line: round2(best.unit * quantity) };
}

const MINIMUM_SUBTOTAL = 500;
const NET_DAYS = 30;
const DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE = 20;

/**
 * @param {import("../generated/api").RunInput} input
 * @returns {import("../generated/api").CartPaymentMethodsTransformRunResult}
 */
export function run(input) {
  const company = input.cart.buyerIdentity?.purchasingCompany;
  const subtotal = parseFloat(input.cart.cost.subtotalAmount.amount);

  // Retail buyers and small company orders keep the default behaviour: pay now.
  if (!company || subtotal < MINIMUM_SUBTOTAL) {
    return { operations: [] };
  }

  return {
    operations: [
      {
        paymentTermsSet: {
          paymentTerms: {
            net: {
              dueInDays: NET_DAYS,
              deposit: { percentage: DEPOSIT_PERCENTAGE },
            },
          },
        },
      },
    ],
  };
}

Two practical points follow from the guide. In a dedicated B2B store, hide prices, the cart icon, add-to-cart buttons and other calls to action from visitors who are not B2B buyers, either with the theme editor’s store contextualization settings or with customer.b2b?; in a blended store keep the default prices for consumers. And for a Theme Store theme that must stay eligible for automatic upgrades, do not edit core theme files: hide the existing section or block and add a Custom Liquid block that does the conditional, as the guide advises. Some areas, such as the cart icon in the header, are not individual blocks, so a code change may be unavoidable there.

Pages that show B2B prices must not be cached and served to other buyers, so custom caches (CDN rules, Hydrogen loaders) must treat these responses as private.

Headless storefronts

The buyer’s path from login to a B2B checkout in a headless storefront.

A headless B2B storefront has four jobs, taken from the developer guide: obtain the buyer’s access token, obtain a company location ID, contextualize product queries to get B2B prices, volume prices and quantity rules, and contextualize the cart so that checkout is a B2B checkout. Without contextualization the store’s base prices and publishing are used, and the buyer sees none of the catalogs, payment terms or other B2B setup.

Step 1 and 2: the token and the buyer’s locations

The Customer Account API authorization flow produces an access_token for the customer; this is the customerAccessToken of the later steps. With that token, a Customer Account API query returns the company contacts of the customer and the locations they can buy for. If the result has one location, keep its ID. If it has several, build a location selector, store the chosen ID and redirect there after login. A location is shown with its address and the payment terms it will use, so the buyer knows what the checkout will do.

query BuyerLocations {
  customer {
    id
    displayName
    companyContacts(first: 5) {
      nodes {
        id
        title
        company {
          id
          name
        }
        locations(first: 20) {
          nodes {
            id
            name
            shippingAddress {
              countryCode
              city
            }
            buyerExperienceConfiguration {
              paymentTermsTemplate {
                name
                dueInDays
              }
            }
          }
        }
      }
    }
  }
}

This query was validated against the Customer Account API schema 2026-04 from @shopify/hydrogen-react 2026.4.4, because the schema for 2026-10 is not served for download; the fields it uses are in the 2026-10 reference pages as well.

Changing the buyer identity of a cart that already has lines can make it invalid. Products not published for the new location are removed, and products that stay but fall under different quantity rules are left unchanged. Reading the cart does not fail, but updating it may, and a buyer who goes to checkout with an invalid cart sees the problems as an error. The codes to handle in userErrors and warnings:

Code Meaning

MISSING_CUSTOMER_ACCESS_TOKEN

The customer access token is required when a company location is set.

INVALID_COMPANY_LOCATION, BUYER_CANNOT_PURCHASE_FOR_COMPANY_LOCATION

The location was not found or is not allowed, or the buyer cannot purchase for it (the second code was added to CartErrorCode on 2025-05-10).

MINIMUM_NOT_MET, MAXIMUM_EXCEEDED, INVALID_INCREMENT

A cart line breaks the variant’s quantity rule.

PRODUCT_UNAVAILABLE_IN_BUYER_LOCATION (warning)

A line is not published for the buyer’s location.

MERCHANDISE_SELLING_PLAN_NOT_APPLICABLE_ON_COMPANY_LOCATION (warning)

Checkout and Functions

The B2B checkout is the standard Shopify checkout with the location’s settings applied: the terms and deposit from buyerExperienceConfiguration, the quantity rules and catalog prices of the cart, tax exemptions of the location, and, with checkoutToDraft, the creation of a draft order for merchant review in place of an order. Customization goes through the same extension points as any checkout, with B2B hooks in each:

  • Functions receive cart.buyerIdentity.purchasingCompany in their input, as the payment terms Function above shows, so discounts, delivery customizations, payment customizations and validations can depend on the company or the location.

  • Cart and Checkout Validation Functions can validate the billing address and the PO number since API version 2026-04 (changelog of 2026-04-01). They return errors on the field targets $.cart.billingAddress.{field} and $.cart.poNumber, so a rule such as "company orders require a PO number" no longer needs client-side code. The inputs are null outside Cart and Checkout Validation.

  • A Function can also impose a review requirement with the orderReviewAdd operation; the checkout is then submitted as a draft that the merchant approves. The Winter '26 Edition presents this as rules on order value and products, for Plus.

  • Checkout UI extensions can read metafields owned by the COMPANY and COMPANY_LOCATION of a B2B checkout, besides those on the cart, customer, product and shop; owner_type is optional in 2026-10 and becomes required in 2027-01.

  • The Winter '26 Edition also lists checkout and customer account customization per market and for B2B buyers in the theme editor, and pickup in store as a delivery option for B2B.

Mistakes that cost the most time

  • Creating a contact and expecting it to order. Without a role assignment at a location there is no permission; the Storefront cart reports BUYER_CANNOT_PURCHASE_FOR_COMPANY_LOCATION.

  • Giving the app only write_products and write_draft_orders and then wondering why company mutations fail. Company objects need read_companies and write_companies, or read_customers and write_customers.

  • Copying a payment terms template ID from a tutorial. IDs are specific to a shop, so read them with paymentTermsTemplates.

  • Skipping the publication on a catalog and finding that buyers see nothing (older guide) or whatever the sales channel makes available (2026-10 reference). Decide on purpose, and test with a real buyer on a development store of the target plan.

  • Using several catalogs on a location and assuming the first wins. The lowest listed price wins, and a campaign catalog that is cheaper than the negotiated one silently overrides the negotiation.

  • Sending priceListFixedPricesAdd with more than 250 prices, or expecting it to merge: it replaces the fixed price of a variant that already has one.

  • Changing a catalog’s price list or publication and leaving the previous object orphaned. Delete it, or use deleteDependentResources when deleting the catalog.

  • Importing orders without priceSet. The API fills the current variant price and the history is wrong.

  • Rendering buyer-specific data from a shared cache.

  • Customer accounts: B2B does not work with legacy customer accounts. Headless login code must move to the Customer Account API.

  • Building a subscription or pre-order flow for B2B. It is not supported, and the cart returns a warning.
